# Grade 7 English - Complete Study Guide

## 1. Reading Comprehension Strategies

Read the passage twice: first for thegist, second for detail. Underline names, dates, and turning words (but, however, therefore).

### Finding the Main Idea
- The main idea is usually in the opening sentence or the closing sentence of a paragraph.
- Ask: "What is this paragraph mostly about?" Answer in under 10 words.
- Example: A paragraph describing a boy practising cricket daily despite rain -> main idea: dedication to practice.

### Inference Practice
- An inference = evidence from text + what you already know.
- Q: "Meera grabbed her umbrella before leaving." What can you infer? A: It was probably raining (or about to).
- Support every inference with one line from the passage.

### Vocabulary in Context
- Replace the hard word with each option; keep the one that keeps the sentence sensible.
- Word-parts help: "un-" (not), "-less" (without), "re-" (again).

## 2. Tenses - Complete Revision

### Simple, Continuous, Perfect
- Simple present: habits - "She walks to school."
- Present continuous: happening now - "She is walking."
- Present perfect: just finished / unfinished time - "She has walked 3 km today."
- Simple past: finished time - "She walked yesterday."
- Past continuous: ongoing past action - "She was walking when it rained."
- Past perfect: the earlier of two past actions - "The train had left before I reached."

### Subject-Verb Agreement
- One subject one verb: "The list of items is on the desk" (subject = list).
- Either/or, neither/nor: verb agrees with the nearer subject - "Neither the boys nor Ravi was ready."

### Common Errors
- "He don't" -> "He doesn't." "One of my friends are" -> "is."
- Since vs for: since + point of time, for + duration.

## 3. Active and Passive Voice

### Rules
- Object becomes subject; verb becomes be + past participle.
- "Ravi writes a letter." -> "A letter is written by Ravi."
- Only transitive verbs (with objects) can be passivised.

### Tense-wise Forms
- Present: is/am/are + written. Past: was/were + written.
- Future: will be + written. Perfect: has/have been + written.

### When to Use Passive
- When the doer is unknown or unimportant: "The window was broken."
- Practice: convert five sentences of your own; then convert them back.

## 4. Reported Speech

### Statements
- Shift tense one step back: present -> past, past -> past perfect.
- "I am tired," he said. -> He said (that) he was tired.
- Pronouns change by sense: I -> he/she; my -> his/her.

### Questions and Commands
- Question: use asked + statement order - "Where do you live?" -> She asked where I lived.
- Command: told/ordered + to-infinitive - "Sit down." -> The teacher told us to sit down.
- Negative: not to - "Don't run." -> She told me not to run.

### Time-word Changes
- now -> then, today -> that day, tomorrow -> the next day, yesterday -> the day before.

## 5. Modals

### Meanings
- can = ability/permission; could = past ability/polite request.
- may = possibility/permission; might = smaller possibility.
- must = obligation/strong certainty; should = advice; ought to = moral duty.

### Perfect Modals
- must have + V3: sure about the past - "He must have missed the bus."
- might have + V3: possible past - "She might have forgotten."
- should have + V3: criticism of the past - "You should have studied."

## 6. Clauses and Sentence Types

### Kinds of Clauses
- Main clause: stands alone - "I was reading."
- Subordinate: cannot stand alone - "when the phone rang."
- Noun clause: "I know that he is honest." Adjective clause: "The boy who won is my friend." Adverb clause: "We left because it was late."

### Simple, Compound, Complex
- Simple: one clause - "Being tired, she slept early."
- Compound: two mains joined by and/but/so - "I called but he didn't answer."
- Complex: main + subordinate - "When the bell rang, we ran out."

### Practice
- Join with relative pronouns: "This is the girl. Her bag was stolen." -> "This is the girl whose bag was stolen."

## 7. Determiners and Prepositions

### Determiners
- much + uncountable (much water), many + countable (many books).
- few/a few, little/a little: 'a' = some positive amount; without 'a' = almost none.
- each (two or more, individually), every (three or more, as a group).

### Preposition Rules
- in (months/years), on (dates/days), at (clock time): in July, on Monday, at 5 pm.
- Prepositional phrases: good at, afraid of, fond of, angry with (person), angry at (thing).

## 8. Vocabulary Building

### Word Roots
- "port" = carry: transport, export, portable.
- "spect" = look: inspect, spectator, respect.
- "dict" = say: predict, dictionary, verdict.

### Idioms in Use
- once in a blue moon = very rarely. spill the beans = reveal a secret.
- in hot water = in trouble. piece of cake = very easy.

### One-Word Substitutes
- One who cannot read or write: illiterate. A place where bees are kept: apiary.
- That which cannot be believed: incredible.

## 9. Writing - Paragraph, Letter, Story

### Paragraph Writing
- One topic idea per paragraph: topic sentence + 3-4 supporting lines + closing line.
- Use linking words: firstly, moreover, however, therefore, finally.

### Informal Letter
- Format: address, date, salutation (Dear Uncle), body (3 paragraphs), closing (Yours affectionately).
- Body: why you write -> details -> request/question.

### Story Writing
- Plan: beginning (setting), middle (problem), end (solution + message).
- Past tense narration; dialogue adds life; keep one clear message (moral).

## 10. Speaking and Listening

### Pronunciation Focus
- Silent letters: k(nife), w(write), b(lamb), g(sign), t(listen).
- Stress shifts meaning: REcord (noun) vs reCORD (verb).

### Listening Tasks
- Listen to a 2-minute news clip; note who/what/where/when.
- Retell it in 5 sentences to a family member.

## 11. Editing and Omission Practice

### Error Correction
- Spot error type first: verb form, agreement, preposition, article.
- "She go to school daily" -> goes. "He is senior than me" -> senior to me.

### Omission
- Missing words usually: articles (a/an/the), prepositions, helpers (is/has).
- Read the sentence aloud - the ear catches the missing beat.

## 12. Mini Revision Test

1. Change to passive: "They clean the room daily." (A: The room is cleaned daily.)
2. Report: "I will help you," she said. (A: She said she would help me.)
3. Fill: He is fond ____ music. (A: of)
4. One word: A person who writes books. (A: author)
5. Spot error: "One of the boys were absent." (A: were -> was)
