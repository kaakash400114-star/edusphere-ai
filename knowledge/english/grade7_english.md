# Grade 7 English – Complete Study Guide

---

## 1. Reading Comprehension

- **Skim** the passage first for the main idea, then read questions, then scan for details.
- Types of questions: factual (answer in the passage), inferential (read between the lines), vocabulary (guess meaning from context).
- **Main idea** is often in the first or last sentence of a paragraph.
- For "why" questions, answer in a complete sentence using **because/so that**.
- Always answer in your own words unless asked to quote.

## 2. Vocabulary Building

- **Synonyms**: happy–joyful, brave–courageous, quick–rapid, big–large.
- **Antonyms**: accept–reject, temporary–permanent, generous–stingy.
- **Word formation**: prefix (un-, dis-, re-, mis-: unkind, disagree, rewrite, misplace) and suffix (-ful, -less, -ness, -ly: careful, careless, kindness, slowly).
- **One-word substitutions**: one who writes books — author; a place where bees are kept — apiary.
- **Homophones**: their/there, peace/piece, write/right — learn in pairs with meanings.
- **Idioms**: once in a blue moon (rarely), a piece of cake (very easy), spill the beans (reveal a secret).

## 3. Grammar

### Tenses
- Present: simple (habit — "She reads daily"), continuous ("is reading"), perfect ("has read").
- Past: simple, continuous, perfect ("had read"), perfect continuous.
- Future: simple ("will read"), continuous, perfect.
- Common errors: "He go" → "He goes"; "yesterday I have seen" → "yesterday I saw".

### Voice (Active and Passive)
- Active: subject does the action — "Riya wrote a poem."
- Passive: object becomes subject — "A poem was written by Riya."
- Form: correct form of **be** + past participle; only transitive verbs have passive.
- Use passive when the doer is unknown or unimportant.

### Reported Speech
- Statements: say/tell + that; pronouns and tense shift back (present → past).
- Questions: if/whether for yes-no; question word kept for wh-; no question mark.
- Commands: told/ordered + to + verb; requests: asked + to.
- Time words change: now → then, today → that day, tomorrow → the next day.

### Modals
- can (ability), could (polite past), may (permission), might (possibility), must (obligation), should (advice), will/shall (future), would (request).
- Modals take the base verb: "must go", never "must goes".

### Clauses
- **Main clause** stands alone; **subordinate clause** cannot.
- Kinds: noun clause ("I know **that he is honest**"), adjective clause ("the boy **who won**"), adverb clause ("**when it rains**, we stay in").

### Determiners
- Articles a/an/the; quantifiers some, any, much, many, few, little; demonstratives this/that/these/those.
- "a" before consonant sounds (a university), "an" before vowel sounds (an hour).

## 4. Writing Skills

### Notice
- Box format: name of school/body, NOTICE, date, heading, body (what-when-where-who), signature with name and designation. Keep within 50 words.

### Email
- To, Subject line (short and clear), salutation, body (purpose in the first line), closing (Regards + name).

### Paragraph Writing
- One topic per paragraph: **topic sentence → supporting details → conclusion**.
- Keep one tense; use linkers (first, then, moreover, finally).

### Story Writing
- Plan: beginning (setting + characters) → problem → climax → resolution.
- Given outlines: expand with dialogue, description, feelings; give a moral if asked.

## 5. Literature Elements

- **Prose**: plot (events), character (traits shown by actions/speech), setting (time-place), theme (message).
- **Poetry**: stanza, rhyme scheme (aabb, abab), rhythm; **poetic devices** — simile (like/as), metaphor (direct), personification, alliteration, repetition.
- Read the poem twice; identify the speaker and mood before answering.

## 6. Punctuation

- Full stop (.), comma (lists, after introductory words), question mark (?), exclamation (!), apostrophe (possession: Riya's book; contractions: don't), quotation marks for direct speech.
- Capital letters: sentence beginnings, proper nouns, "I", titles.

## 7. Speaking and Listening

- Speak in complete sentences; pause at commas and full stops; stress key words.
- Listening: note who/what/when/where/why; don't write every word — write key points.
- polite expressions: Excuse me, May I…, Could you please…, Thank you.
