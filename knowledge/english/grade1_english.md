## 2. Phonics

### CVC Words (Consonant-Vowel-Consonant)

CVC words follow a simple pattern: **one consonant, one short vowel, one consonant**.

**-at family:**
- cat, bat, hat, rat, mat, sat, fat, pat

**-og family:**
- dog, log, fog, jog, hog, bog, cog

**-un family:**
- sun, bun, fun, run, gun, nun

**More CVC examples:**

| Word | C | V | C |
|------|---|---|---|
| pig | p | i | g |
| hop | h | o | p |
| red | r | e | d |
| cup | c | u | p |
| net | n | e | t |
| fin | f | i | n |

### Word Families

A **word family** is a group of words that share the same ending sound. If you know one word in the family, you can read many others!

**-at family:** cat, bat, hat, mat, rat, sat, fat, pat, flat, splat, chat, brat
**-og family:** dog, log, fog, jog, hog, frog, clog, smog, blog
**-un family:** sun, bun, fun, run, gun, nun, spun, stun, bun

## 3. Dolch Sight Words

Sight words are words that appear very often in books. You should be able to read these words quickly without sounding them out.

### Pre-Primer Sight Words (40 words)

**a, and, away, big, blue, can, come, down, find, for, funny, go, help, here, I, in, is, it, jump, little, look, make, me, my, not, one, play, red, run, said, see, the, three, to, two, up, we, where, yellow, you**

### Primer Sight Words (52 words)

**all, am, are, at, ate, be, black, brown, but, came, did, do, eat, four, get, good, have, he, into, like, must, new, no, now, on, our, out, please, pretty, ran, ride, saw, say, she, so, soon, that, there, they, this, too, under, want, was, well, went, what, white, who, will, with, yes**

### How to Practice Sight Words
- **Flash cards:** Write each word on a card and practice daily.
- **Sight word hunt:** Find sight words in books and magazines.
- **Write and say:** Write each word 3 times and say it out loud.
- **Word wall:** Keep a list of words you have learned on the wall.

## 5. Grammar Basics

### Nouns - Person, Place, or Thing

A **noun** names a person, a place, or a thing.

| Type | Examples |
|------|----------|
| Person | teacher, mom, boy, girl, doctor, farmer, Jack, Maria |
| Place | school, park, home, store, beach, library, city |
| Thing | book, ball, desk, apple, car, toy, pencil, dog |

### Verbs - Action Words

A **verb** tells what someone or something does. It is an action word.

| Verb | Example Sentence |
|------|-----------------|
| run | I **run** fast. |
| jump | The frog can **jump**. |
| eat | We **eat** lunch. |
| play | She likes to **play**. |
| read | He will **read** a book. |
| sleep | The baby will **sleep**. |
| sing | They **sing** a song. |
| write | I **write** my name. |

### Adjectives - Describing Words

An **adjective** describes a noun. It tells what kind, which one, or how many.

| Adjective | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| big | a **big** dog |
| red | a **red** apple |
| three | **three** cats |
| happy | a **happy** girl |
| cold | **cold** water |
| soft | a **soft** pillow |
| tall | a **tall** tree |

### Complete Sentences - Subject + Predicate

Every sentence needs two parts:
1. **Subject** - who or what the sentence is about
2. **Predicate** - what the subject does or is

| Subject | Predicate | Complete Sentence |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|
| The cat | sat on the mat. | The cat sat on the mat. |
| My mom | reads to me. | My mom reads to me. |
| The sun | is bright. | The sun is bright. |
| We | play outside. | We play outside. |
| The bird | sings. | The bird sings. |

### Punctuation

Every sentence ends with a punctuation mark:

| Mark | Name | When to Use | Example |
|------|------|-------------|---------|
| **.** | Period | Tells a fact or makes a statement | The dog is big. |
| **?** | Question Mark | Asks a question | What is your name? |
| **!** | Exclamation Point | Shows strong feeling | I love school! |

### Capitalization

Use a **capital letter** (uppercase) for:
1. The **first word** of every sentence
2. The pronoun **I** (always capitalize the letter I by itself)
3. **Names** of people and pets: Sam, Maria, Fluffy
4. **Places**: Florida, Main Street, Lincoln School

**Examples:**
- **Correct:** The dog ran home.
- **Wrong:** the dog ran home.
- **Correct:** I like apples.
- **Wrong:** i like apples.
- **Correct:** My friend Tom is six.
- **Wrong:** My friend tom is six.

## 6. Reading Skills

### Reading Direction
- Always read from **left to right**.
- Move from the **top line to the bottom line**.
- Return to the left side when you finish a line.
- Turn pages from **left to right**.

### Using Picture Clues
When you come to a word you do not know, look at the pictures! Pictures give hints about what is happening in the story.

**Example:** If the picture shows a girl holding an umbrella and you read "She opened her ___," the picture helps you know the word is "umbrella."

### Using Context Clues
Sometimes other words in the sentence help you figure out an unknown word.

**Example:** "The big ___ barked at the mailman." Even if you cannot read the first word, "barked" tells you it is an animal that barks - a dog!

### Story Elements

Every story has important parts:

| Element | Question It Answers | Example |
|---------|-------------------|---------|
| **Characters** | Who is the story about? | A little boy named Jack and his dog Max |
| **Setting** | Where and when does the story happen? | At the park on a sunny day |
| **Problem** | What went wrong? | Jack cannot find Max |
| **Solution** | How was the problem fixed? | Max comes back with a stick in his mouth |

## 7. Writing Skills

### Letter Formation

**Guidelines for writing letters:**
- Letters sit on the **bottom line**.
- Tall letters (b, d, f, h, k, l, t) go up to the **top line**.
- Small letters (a, c, e, i, m, n, o, r, s, u, v, w, x, z) go up to the **middle line**.
- Letters that go below the line (g, j, p, q, y) have a **tail** that hangs down.
- Always start each letter at the **top** (except for a few like e and d which can start in the middle).

### Simple Sentences (3-5 Words)

Practice writing complete sentences. Every sentence needs:
- A **capital letter** at the beginning
- A **subject** and a **verb**
- A **period** at the end

**Examples of good simple sentences:**
- I like to play.
- The cat is big.
- She ran fast.
- We see a dog.
- He has a red hat.
- My mom is nice.
- The sun is hot.
- I can read.

### Labels

A **label** is a word or short phrase that names something. Labels are not complete sentences.

**Examples of labels:**
- dog (next to a picture of a dog)
- kitchen (on a drawing of a kitchen)
- Mom (next to a picture of a person)
- red flower (describing something in a picture)

### Lists

A **list** is a group of items written one after another, usually with numbers or bullets.

**Example - My Favorite Things:**
1. my bike
2. pizza
3. my dog
4. the park
5. reading books

**Tips for writing lists:**
- Put a number or bullet (•) before each item.
- Write each item on a new line.
- You do not need complete sentences in a list.
