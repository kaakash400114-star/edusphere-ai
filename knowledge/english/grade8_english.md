# Grade 8 English – Complete Study Guide

---

## 1. Reading Comprehension

- **Factual passages**: locate exact answers; watch for dates, names, numbers.
- **Discursive passages**: opinion-based; identify the writer's view (for/against/neutral).
- Inference questions: the answer is suggested, not stated — "The writer probably means…"
- Note-making practice: headings, subheadings, abbreviations, logical order.
- Match-the-following: headings to paragraphs — one heading per main idea.

## 2. Vocabulary

- **Word power**: connotation (positive/negative feel of words: slim vs skinny).
- Prefixes: super-, sub-, inter-, trans- (supersonic, submarine, international, transport).
- Suffixes: -tion, -ment, -able, -ive (creation, agreement, comfortable, active).
- **Phrasal verbs**: give up (quit), look after (care for), put off (postpone), run out of (exhaust the supply).
- Idioms: burn the midnight oil (work late), hit the nail on the head (be exactly right).

## 3. Grammar

### Tenses Review
- Perfect tenses with time expressions: since/for with perfect continuous ("has been raining since morning").
- Past perfect for the earlier of two past actions ("The train had left before I reached").
- Future continuous ("will be playing") and future perfect ("will have finished by 5 pm").

### Active and Passive Voice
- All-tense conversion practice: is writing → is being written; wrote → was written; will write → will be written.
- Verbs with two objects give two passives: "She gave me a pen." → "I was given a pen." / "A pen was given to me."

### Reported Speech
- Universal truths don't shift tense ("The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun").
- Exclamations: exclaimed with joy/sorrow + that; wishes: wished/blessed.
- Mixed practice: statement + question + command in one passage.

### Modals
- Perfect modals: must have (deduction), might have (possibility in past), should have (criticism), could have (unfulfilled ability).
- "Need" and "dare" as modal and main verbs.

### Non-Finites
- **Infinitive** (to + verb: to read), **gerund** (verb-ing as noun: swimming is fun), **participle** (adjectival: the crying baby, a broken chair).
- Gerund after prepositions ("good at painting") and certain verbs (enjoy, avoid, mind).

### Clauses
- Noun, adjective, adverb clauses — identify and punctuate.
- **Conditionals**: Type 1 (if + present, will — real possibility), Type 2 (if + past, would — imaginary), Type 3 (if + past perfect, would have — impossible past).

### Prepositions and Conjunctions
- Time (at 5 pm, on Monday, in June), place (at the door, on the table, in the room), movement (into, onto, through).
- Correlative conjunctions: either…or, neither…nor, not only…but also.

## 4. Writing Skills

### Formal Letter
- Format: sender's address, date, receiver's address, subject, salutation (Sir/Madam), body (introduce-purpose-details-request), complimentary close (Yours faithfully), name.
- Types: complaint (to the editor/authority), application (leave, job), enquiry.

### Diary Entry
- Day, date, time; first person; feelings and reflection; informal tone.

### Story Writing
- From outlines or picture prompts; past tense narration; dialogue punctuation; title and moral.

### Notice
- 50-word limit, box, passive voice ("Students are informed…"), no first person.

## 5. Literature Elements

- **Character sketch**: point-support format (trait + incident that shows it).
- **Theme vs plot**: plot = what happens; theme = what it means.
- **Point of view**: first person ("I") vs third person ("he/she"); narrator reliability.
- Poetic devices added: hyperbole (exaggeration), onomatopoeia (sound words), imagery (sense pictures).

## 6. Punctuation

- Semicolon joins related clauses; colon introduces lists/explanations.
- Dash for sudden breaks; brackets for extra information; hyphen in compound words (well-known).
- Direct speech: new line per speaker, commas inside quotes, end marks inside.

## 7. Speaking Skills

- **Debate basics**: claim, reasons, evidence, rebuttal; respectful tone.
- **Presentation**: eye contact, pace, volume, signposting (firstly, in conclusion).
- Telephone etiquette: identify yourself, state purpose, close politely.
