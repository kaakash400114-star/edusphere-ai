# Grade 8 English - Complete Study Guide

## 1. Reading Comprehension - Advanced

### Skimming and Scanning
- Skim (fast read) for the gist in 60 seconds; scan (search read) for names, numbers, dates.
- Exam pattern: read the questions first, then scan the passage for answers.

### Tone and Attitude
- Tone words: humorous, serious, sympathetic, critical, ironic.
- Clue: the writer's choice of adjectives - "the poor animal" shows sympathy.

### Central Idea vs Theme
- Central idea: what the text says. Theme: what it teaches about life.
- A story about a tortoise winning -> theme: slow and steady wins the race.

## 2. Perfect and Progressive Tenses

### All Twelve Tenses in Use
- Present perfect continuous: "It has been raining since morning."
- Past perfect continuous: "He had been waiting for two hours."
- Future continuous: "This time tomorrow we will be flying."
- Future perfect: "By 2030 she will have finished her degree."

### Tense Consistency
- Keep one time frame inside a paragraph; shift only with a signal word.
- "He opened the door and sees..." -> saw (stay in past).

### Conditional Sentences
- Type 1 (real): If it rains, we will stay home.
- Type 2 (unreal): If I were rich, I would travel.
- Type 3 (past unreal): If he had studied, he would have passed.

## 3. Voice - Special Cases

### Interrogative and Imperative
- Question: "Who wrote this?" -> "By whom was this written?"
- Order: "Shut the door." -> "Let the door be shut."

### Voice with Two Objects
- "The teacher gave us homework." -> "We were given homework" OR "Homework was given to us."

### Common Mistakes
- Keep the tense: past stays past - "He wrote a poem" -> "A poem was written."
- Don't passivise intransitive verbs (laugh, sleep, arrive have no object).

## 4. Reported Speech - Advanced

### Exclamations
- exclaim with joy/sorrow - "Hurrah! We won!" -> They exclaimed with joy that they had won.
- Wish/pray: "Good morning!" -> He wished me good morning.

### Mixed Practice
- "Are you coming with us?" -> He asked whether I was coming with them.
- "Please lend me your pen." -> He requested me to lend him my pen.

### Reporting Verbs
- Choose by mood: promised, warned, suggested, apologised, complained.
- "Don't touch the wire!" -> He warned me not to touch the wire.

## 5. Modals - Fine Differences

### Ability and Possibility
- could (general past ability) vs was able to (one successful effort).
- may/might/could for possibility, in rising order of doubt: may > might > could.

### Obligation
- must (speaker's rule), have to (outside rule), should (advice), need not (no necessity).
- "You must wear a helmet" (law) vs "You should drink more water" (advice).

## 6. Non-Finites: Gerunds, Participles, Infinitives

### Gerunds
- Verb + ing used as a noun: "Swimming keeps me fit." "I enjoy reading."
- After prepositions always gerund: "good at painting", "afraid of flying".

### Participles
- Present participle (V-ing): the crying baby; a walking dictionary.
- Past participle (V3): a broken chair; a stolen purse.
- Misplaced: "Walking in the garden, the flowers looked lovely" -> wrong doer; fix to "As we walked..."

### Infinitives
- to + verb: to err is human; I want to learn.
- Gerund vs infinitive pairs: stop to smoke (purpose) vs stop smoking (quit); remember to lock (duty) vs remember locking (memory).

## 7. Sentence Transformation

### Degrees of Comparison
- Positive -> comparative -> superlative: tall, taller, tallest.
- "No other boy is as tall as Ravi." = "Ravi is taller than any other boy." = "Ravi is the tallest boy."

### Too...to and So...that
- "He is too weak to walk." = "He is so weak that he cannot walk."

### As soon as / No sooner
- "As soon as the bell rang, we left." = "No sooner did the bell ring than we left."

### Unless and If
- "Work hard or you will fail." = "Unless you work hard, you will fail."

## 8. Vocabulary - Precision

### Synonym Nuance
- big vs huge vs enormous: growing scale; happy vs delighted vs ecstatic.
- Choose by context: formal letters prefer "assist" over "help".

### Word Formation
- Prefixes: mis- (wrongly), over- (excess), under- (less), multi- (many).
- Suffixes: -tion (action), -able (capable), -ful (full of), -less (without).

### Phrasal Verbs
- give up = quit, look after = care for, put off = postpone, call off = cancel.
- break down (machine) vs break up (relationship) vs break in (enter by force).

## 9. Writing - Formal Letters and Notices

### Formal Letter Format
- Sender's address, date, receiver's address, subject line, salutation (Sir/Madam), 3 body paragraphs, complimentary close (Yours faithfully), signature.
- Body: purpose -> supporting details -> expected action.

### Types You Must Know
- Complaint (defective product), application (leave/job), editor letter (public issue), enquiry (asking information).

### Notice Writing
- Box format: NOTICE heading, date, body in under 50 words, signature with name and designation.
- One clear event: what, when, where, who to contact.

## 10. Literature Companion - Themes

### Prose Themes
- Courage against odds; friendship and loyalty; honesty rewarded; respect for nature.
- For each chapter: write 3-line summary + 1 theme line + favourite character with reason.

### Poetry Appreciation
- Devices: simile (like/as), metaphor (direct), personification, alliteration, rhyme scheme.
- Central idea in 4-5 lines; poet's message in one line.

### Character Sketch Frame
- Point form: age/role -> qualities (2-3 with proof from text) -> effect on story.

## 11. Punctuation Mastery

### Rules That Exams Test
- Apostrophe: possession (boy's book, boys' books); contraction (don't).
- Quotation marks: dialogue with comma inside - "Wait," she said.
- Semicolon joins close clauses; colon introduces a list.

### Capital Letters
- Proper nouns, first word of a sentence, titles with names, "I".

## 12. Mini Revision Test

1. Passive: "Someone has stolen my bag." (A: My bag has been stolen.)
2. If-clause: "He is lazy, so he fails." -> Type 1 conditional. (A: If he is lazy, he will fail.)
3. Gerund: "She is good at ____ (dance)." (A: dancing)
4. No sooner: "As soon as I closed my eyes, sleep came." (A: No sooner had I closed my eyes than sleep came.)
5. Phrasal verb: postpone = ____ (A: put off)
