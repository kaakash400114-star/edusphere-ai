# Grade 11 English (Core) – Complete Study Guide

---

## 1. Reading Comprehension (Unseen Passages)

- Passage 1 (factual/discursive, 550–600 words): MCQ + objective + one-word answers.
- Passage 2 (case-based, with data/notes): inference, evaluation, comparison.
- Skills: skimming (gist), scanning (specifics), prediction from title, tone detection (critical/appreciative/neutral), inference ("the author implies…").
- Note-making (from Passage 1): headings + subheadings, abbreviations (min 4), logical order, no full sentences — then **summary in ~80 words** with a title.

## 2. Vocabulary and Literary Devices

- **Advanced word study**: euphemism (passed away for died), oxymoron (deafening silence), pun (double meaning).
- Register shift: formal ↔ informal equivalents ( ascertain – find out; purchase – buy).
- Idioms and phrasal verbs in context; collocations for writing precision.

## 3. Grammar Review (applied)

- **Error correction**: tense consistency, concord, preposition choice in 15–20 line passages.
- **Editing**: omissions, spelling, punctuation; one word per line format.
- **Sentence transformation**: reported speech at senior level; voice change including modals (must → has to be); degree change; synthesis (simple/compound/complex).
- **Clauses**: noun/adjective/adverb clause identification and conversion; conditionals Type 1–3; inversion after negatives (Never have I seen…).

## 4. Writing Skills

### Notice (50 words)
- Institutional format; headline; what-when-where-whom; passive constructions; boxed.

### Poster
- Slogan, visual layout description, key message, call to action (e.g., Save Water).

### Letter to the Editor (120–150 words)
- Social concern format: reference line, problem with examples/data, consequences, suggestions, signature as "a concerned citizen".

### Article/Speech (120–150 words)
- Title, by-line, introduction (hook), 2–3 body paragraphs (points + examples), conclusion (call to action); speech adds salutation and audience address.

### Report (120–150 words)
- Headline, by-line, dateline, lead paragraph (5Ws + 1H), body (facts in order), conclusion; past tense, third person, objective tone.

### Job Application + CV
- Letter: position source, fit with qualifications, enclosure line; CV: personal details, objective, education, skills, achievements, references — tabular/bulleted.

## 5. Literature — Hornbill (prose themes)

- **The Portrait of a Lady** (Kushwant Singh): three phases of the grandmother; silent love; the sparrow mourning — relationships beyond words.
- **We're Not Afraid to Die… if We Can All Be Together** (Gordon Cook): true courage, teamwork at sea, optimism against a monstrous wave.
- **Discovering Tut: The Saga Continues** (A.R. Williams): science meets history — CT scan of a mummy; curiosity of ages.
- **The Ailing Planet: the Green Movement's Role** (Nani Palkhivala): earth as a dying patient; sustainable development; "forest precede mankind, deserts follow".
- **The Browning Version** (play excerpt): a teacher's harsh exterior vs student's empathy; transformation.

## 6. Literature — Snapshots (supplementary)

- **The Summer of the Beautiful White Horse** (William Saroyan): honour vs temptation; tribal conscience of the Garoghlanians.
- **The Address** (Marga Minco): war's aftermath; the meaninglessness of possessions after loss.
- **Mother's Day** (play): household respect earned, not demanded — comedy with a message.
- **The Ghat of the Only World** (Amitav Ghosh): friendship, mortality, and a promise kept (Agha Shahid Ali).
- **Birth** (A.J. Cronin): a doctor's vigil — first breath as a miracle of persistence.

## 7. Poetry (Hornbill)

- **A Photograph** (Shirley Toulson): time, loss, memory — "its silence silences".
- **The Laburnum Top** (Ted Hughes): the goldfinch and the tree — life-energy exchange; imagery of stillness and movement.
- **The Voice of the Rain** (Walt Whitman): rain's cycle as a song; personification; eternal return.
- **Childhood** (Markus Natten): when did innocence end? — rationality replacing faith.
- **Father to Son** (Elizabeth Jennings): generation gap; a father's grief for estrangement.

## 8. Note-Making and Summarising

- Note format: title, indented headings/subheadings, recognised abbreviations, key at the end.
- Summary: paraphrase, don't copy; third person; no new points; within limit.

## 9. Punctuation and Style

- Reported speech at advanced level; semicolons, colons, dashes for senior prose.
- Avoiding redundancy and cliché; subject-specific register for reports/articles.

## 10. Exam Strategy

- Attempt writing skills with drafts (5-min outline); literature answers: theme + textual evidence + personal response.
- Time plan (3 h): Reading 40 min, Writing/Grammar 45 min, Literature 80 min, revision 15 min.
