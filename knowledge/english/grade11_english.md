# Grade 11 English - Complete Study Guide

## 1. Reading Comprehension - Advanced Passage Work

### Unseen Passage Discipline
- 800-1000 word passages: philosophy, environment, technology, society.
- Two readings: texture first (who is speaking, to whom), then detail.
- MCQ + objective pattern; eliminate two obvious wrongs before choosing.

### Note the Structure
- Intro (thesis) -> body paragraphs (evidence) -> conclusion (stance restated).
- Locate thesis usually in paragraph 1's final line.

### Inference and Author's Craft
- Why did the author give this example? -> to illustrate/contradict the claim.
- Tone options: objective, sceptical, appreciative, cautionary - match with adjective clues.

## 2. Note-Making and Summarising

### Note-Making Format
- Title (short) -> indented points (2-3 levels), each under 6 words, no full sentences.
- Minimum 4 abbreviations with the key at the end (e.g., govt, e.g., i.e., ~ approximately).
- Mark logical relation: sub-points indent, arrows for cause-effect.

### Summary Writing
- Convert notes into 80-word prose in the third person, present tense.
- One paragraph; no examples or repetitions from the passage.

### Quality Checks
- Coverage: all main points, none of the illustrations.
- Language: reported tone ("The author states..."), no first person.

## 3. Grammar - Error Correction and Transformation

### Error Spotting Priorities
- Concord (agreement), tense shift, preposition idiom, article misuse, word form.
- "The committee have decided" -> has (collective noun, one act).
- "He ordered for a coffee" -> ordered a coffee (no 'for').

### Sentence Transformation Bank
- Remove too: "He is too proud to apologise." -> "He is so proud that he will not apologise."
- Negative without change: "Only Radha can do this." -> "None but Radha can do this."
- Comparative to positive: "He is stronger than his brother." -> "His brother is not as strong as he is."

### Clause Combining
- Join with participles: "He saw the snake. He ran." -> "Seeing the snake, he ran."
- Join with absolute construction: "The weather was fine. We went out." -> "The weather being fine, we went out."

## 4. Writing - Article and Speech

### Article (120-150 words)
- Title + by-line; intro hook (question/fact) -> 2 body paragraphs (causes, effects) -> suggestion/conclusion.
- Keep formal register; one idea per paragraph.

### Speech
- Salutation ("Respected Principal, teachers..."), self-intro, topic announcement.
- Persuasive devices: rule of three, rhetorical question, statistic, appeal.
- Close with a call to action.

### Idea Banks
- Environment: plastic ban, water conservation, e-waste.
- Society: digital detox, road safety, dignity of labour.

## 5. Writing - Report and Job Application

### Report Format
- Headline -> by-line -> date/place -> lead paragraph (what, when, where, who) -> details -> remarks/expectations.
- Past tense, third person, no first-person feelings.

### Job Application with Bio-data
- Application: source of vacancy, suitability (2 skills), request for interview.
- Bio-data: name, DOB, education (table), experience, references, declaration + signature.

### Poster and Advertisement Basics
- Poster: bold title, visual words, date/venue/contact, slogan; under 50 words.
- Classified ad: bracket category, terse phrases, contact line.

## 6. Hornbill - Prose Studies

### The Portrait of a Lady
- Theme: three phases of bonding (village, city, university) and quiet companionship in old age.
- Imagery: grandmother "like the winter landscape in the mountains"; sparrows' silent mourning.

### We're Not Afraid to Die... If We Can All Be Together
- Theme: optimism, teamwork, family unity against a near-fatal ocean voyage.
- Character: the narrator's grit; children's bravery ("We're not afraid of dying").

### Discovering Tut: The Saga Continues
- Theme: science (CT scan) meeting history; the boy king's mystery.
- Skill: separating fact from speculation in science writing.

### The Ailing Planet: the Green Movement's Role
- Themes: sustainable development, earth as a patient, "forest precede mankind, deserts follow".
- Key idea: we are borrowers, not owners, of the planet.

### The Address
- Theme: war's aftermath; possessions without the person are worthless; resolution to forget and move on.

### Mother's Day (Play)
- Theme: housewife's dignity; family transforms when Mrs Pearson swaps personalities with Mrs Fitzgerald.
- Dramatic device: personality swap as comedy with a message.

## 7. Hornbill - Poetry Studies

### A Photograph (Shirley Toulson)
- Theme: time, loss, silence of the dead; the sea "which appears to have changed less".

### The Laburnum Top (Ted Hughes)
- Theme: life-energy circulates - the silent tree goldfinch enters and the tree "trembles and thrills".
- Devices: metaphor (engine = family), transformation imagery.

### The Voice of the Rain (Walt Whitman)
- Theme: rain's eternal cycle = song of the poet; "I give back life to my own origin".
- Devices: personification of rain, cyclic parallel between rain and music.

### Childhood (Markus Natten)
- Theme: when did childhood go? - loss of innocence at stages of rationality; "Hell and Heaven" become mind-forged.

### Father to Son (Elizabeth Jennings)
- Theme: father-son estrangement despite living together; the wish for rebuilding ("What he loves I cannot share").

## 8. Snapshots - Supplementary Reader

### The Summer of the Beautiful White Horse (William Saroyan)
- Theme: honour among a poor but honest tribe; the "bigger" theft of pride vs stealing a horse.
- Characters: Aram (romantic), Mourad (the crazy streak), Uncle Khosrove (comic temper).

### The Address (Marga Minco) - see prose section; here: practice value questions
- Q: Why did the narrator want to forget the address? A: It reopened wounds; objects had lost meaning without mother.

### Albert Einstein at School
- Theme: rote schooling vs curious genius; Einstein's honesty about facts vs ideas.

### Ranga's Marriage (Masti Venkatesha Iyengar)
- Theme: gentle satire on arranged marriage and English-mania in a Karnataka village; narrator's matchmaking humour.

### Mother's Day and Birth
- Birth (A.J. Cronin): theme - a doctor's persistence; life saved against stillbirth odds. Value: duty beyond hours.

## 9. Literary Devices Toolkit

### Figures of Speech
- Simile: like/as (grandmother like winter landscape). Metaphor: direct (laburnum engine).
- Personification: rain speaks. Alliteration: repeated initial sounds. Hyperbole: deliberate excess.
- Oxymoron: cruel kindness. Irony: opposite of what is meant.

### Sound and Structure
- Rhyme scheme notation (abab); free verse = no fixed pattern.
- Repetition and refrain: what they emphasise.

### Application Habit
- For each poem: quote one line + name the device + effect in one sentence.

## 10. Vocabulary - Senior Board Level

### Advanced One-Word Substitutes
- Panacea: cure-all. Renegade: one who deserts a cause. Clemency: mercy.
- Egotist: self-centred talker. Omnivorous: eating everything.

### Idioms in Editorials
- the bottom line = the key point; a shot in the arm = a boost; to bell the cat = take a risk for others.
- move heaven and earth = try everything; an axe to grind = selfish motive.

### Register and Collocation
- Collocations: take a decision (formal), make a mistake, pay heed, pose a threat.
- Formal substitutions: kids -> children, get -> receive, ask -> enquire.

## 11. Debate and Group Discussion Skills

### Debate Frame
- Motion statement -> definition of key terms -> 3 arguments (logic + example) -> rebuttal anticipation -> conclusion.
- Language: "The house believes...", "My worthy opponent claims... however...".

### GD Etiquette
- Enter once, add one point, invite others ("To add to her point...").
- Never interrupt mid-sentence; summarise to close.

## 12. Mini Revision Test

1. Note-making: how many abbreviations minimum? (A: 4, with key)
2. Transform: "He is too weak to lift it." (A: He is so weak that he cannot lift it.)
3. Identify device: "The laburnum top is silent, quite still" then it "trembles". (A: contrast/imagery; the tree personified as alive)
4. Theme of The Ailing Planet in one line. (A: Earth must be treated as a patient; development must be sustainable.)
5. One word: a cure for all diseases. (A: panacea)
