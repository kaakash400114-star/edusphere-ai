# Grade 2 English — Complete Curriculum

## 1. Phonics

### Long Vowels — Silent E (Magic E)

When a word has a **silent e** at the end, the vowel before it says its **own name** (long sound).

**Pattern:** consonant–vowel–consonant–**e**

| Short Vowel Word | Add Silent E | Long Vowel Word |
|-----------------|-------------|----------------|
| mat | mat + e | mate |
| bit | bit + e | bite |
| hop | hop + e | hope |
| tub | tub + e | tube |
| cap | cap + e | cape |
| fin | fin + e | fine |
| kit | kit + e | kite |
| not | not + e | note |
| pin | pin + e | pine |
| rob | rob + e | robe |
| cub | cub + e | cube |
| rid | rid + e | ride |
| tap | tap + e | tape |
| hid | hid + e | hide |
| cut | cut + e | cute |

### Vowel Teams

Two vowels next to each other often work together to make one sound. A helpful rule: **"When two vowels go walking, the first one does the talking."**

**ee and ea — long /ē/ sound:**

| ee Words | ea Words |
|----------|----------|
| see, bee, tree, fee, tee | sea, eat, meat, seat, beat |
| feet, meet, deep, keep | read, bean, clean, dream, team |
| feel, peel, seed, weed | each, lead, reach, teach, leap |
| green, sleep, street, sweet | bread, head (exception — short e) |
| free, three, teeth, week | health, heavy (exception — short e) |

**ai and ay — long /ā/ sound:**

| ai (usually in the middle) | ay (usually at the end) |
|----------------------------|------------------------|
| rain, pain, nail, tail | day, play, say, stay |
| wait, train, brain, chain | away, today, away, may |
| paint, claim, plain, rain | pay, lay, ray, clay |
| sail, mail, fail, trail | play, pray, spray, stray |

**oa and ow — long /ō/ sound:**

| oa Words | ow Words |
|----------|----------|
| boat, coat, goat, float | grow, snow, blow, flow |
| road, toad, soap, loaf | show, slow, glow, know |
| moan, roast, toast, boast | bow, throw, yellow (ow as /ō/) |
| coal, goal, foam, roam | row, own, low, mow |

**ow and ou — /ow/ sound (as in cow):**

| ow Words | ou Words |
|----------|----------|
| cow, how, now, brown | out, shout, house, mouse |
| down, town, clown, frown | loud, cloud, round, found |
| bow, wow, pow, vow | count, mound, sound, ground |

### R-Controlled Vowels (Bossy R)

When the letter **r** comes after a vowel, the **r controls the vowel** and changes its sound.

| R-Controlled | Sound | Example Words |
|-------------|-------|--------------|
| **ar** | /ar/ (like a pirate!) | car, star, far, farm, park, dark, art, barn, jar, sharp |
| **er** | /er/ | her, fern, term, verb, perk, herd, serve, alert |
| **ir** | /er/ (same sound as er) | bird, girl, dirt, fir, sir, stir, third, skirt, chirp |
| **or** | /or/ | for, or, corn, born, fork, port, horse, storm, sort, short |
| **ur** | /er/ (same sound as er) | turn, burn, curl, fur, surf, hurt, purse, nurse, turtle |

### Soft C and Soft G

**Soft c** — When c is followed by e, i, or y, it makes the /s/ sound:
- city, cent, face, ice, nice, pace, race, circle, cell, cycle

**Hard c** — When c is followed by a, o, or u, it makes the /k/ sound:
- cat, cup, cot, can, cap, cold, come, call, class, cut

**Soft g** — When g is followed by e, i, or y, it makes the /j/ sound:
- gym, giant, gentle, gem, page, cage, stage, large, age, edge

**Hard g** — When g is followed by a, o, or u, it makes the /g/ sound:
- go, get, gap, gum, good, game, got, glad, grow, green

### Diphthongs

A **diphthong** is two vowel sounds that glide together.

| Diphthong | Sound | Example Words |
|-----------|-------|--------------|
| **oi** | /oy/ | boil, coin, point, soil, join, foil, oil, noise, voice |
| **oy** | /oy/ | boy, toy, joy, coin, royal, enjoy, destroy |
| **ou** | /ow/ (cow sound) | out, round, cloud, house, mouse, shout, mouth |
| **ou** | /oo/ | soup, group, you, touch, cousin, young |
| **ow** | /ow/ (cow sound) | cow, how, now, down, brown, town, clown |
| **aw** | /aw/ | saw, law, draw, paw, jaw, yawn, lawn, straw |
| **au** | /aw/ | author, autumn, haul, sauce, cause, pause, launch |

### Prefixes

A **prefix** is a group of letters added to the **beginning** of a word that changes its meaning.

| Prefix | Meaning | Examples |
|--------|---------|----------|
| **un-** | not, opposite of | unhappy (not happy), unsafe (not safe), unkind (not kind), undo (do again backwards), unseen (not seen) |
| **re-** | again | redo (do again), reread (read again), replay (play again), rethink (think again), rewrite (write again) |

### Suffixes

A **suffix** is a group of letters added to the **end** of a word that changes its meaning.

| Suffix | Meaning | Examples |
|--------|---------|----------|
| **-s** | more than one (plural) | cats, dogs, books, trees |
| **-es** | more than one (words ending in sh/ch/s/x) | boxes, buses, dishes, watches |
| **-ed** | happened in the past | walked, played, jumped, talked |
| **-ing** | happening now | running, playing, eating, jumping |
| **-ly** | how something is done (adverb) | slowly, quickly, happily, quietly, carefully, nicely |

## 3. Grammar

### Sentence Types

| Type | Purpose | Punctuation | Examples |
|------|---------|-------------|----------|
| **Declarative** | Makes a statement | Period (.) | The sky is blue. I like dogs. |
| **Interrogative** | Asks a question | Question mark (?) | What time is it? Where do you live? |
| **Imperative** | Gives a command | Period (.) or Exclamation (!) | Sit down. Please close the door. Stop! |
| **Exclamatory** | Shows strong feeling | Exclamation (!) | What a beautiful day! I won! |

### Adjectives

Adjectives describe nouns. They tell **what kind**, **which one**, or **how many**.

**What kind:** The **tall** boy has a **red** ball. The **soft** blanket feels **warm**.
**Which one:** I want **that** book. She is wearing **this** dress.
**How many:** There are **five** apples. We need **some** water.

### Adverbs

An **adverb** describes a verb. Many adverbs end in **-ly**. Adverbs tell **how**, **when**, or **where** an action happens.

| How (manner) | When (time) | Where (place) |
|-------------|------------|---------------|
| quickly | today | here |
| slowly | yesterday | there |
| happily | now | outside |
| carefully | soon | upstairs |
| loudly | always | everywhere |
| quietly | never | near |
| softly | sometimes | far |
| bravely | tomorrow | inside |

**Examples:**
- The rabbit ran **quickly**. (How did it run?)
- We will go **tomorrow**. (When will we go?)
- She looked **everywhere**. (Where did she look?)

### Articles

There are three **articles**: **a, an, the**. They come before nouns.

| Article | When to Use | Examples |
|---------|------------|----------|
| **a** | Before words that start with a consonant sound | a dog, a cat, a book, a house |
| **an** | Before words that start with a vowel sound | an apple, an egg, an ice cream, an orange |
| **the** | When talking about a specific noun | the sun, the dog that bit me, the last cookie |

### Pronouns

A **pronoun** takes the place of a noun.

| Subject Pronouns | Object Pronouns | Possessive Pronouns |
|-----------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| I | me | my, mine |
| you | you | your, yours |
| he | him | his |
| she | her | her, hers |
| it | it | its |
| we | us | our, ours |
| they | them | their, theirs |

**Example:** Instead of saying "Maria went to Maria's house," say "**She** went to **her** house."

### Conjunctions

**Conjunctions** join words or sentences together. The three most common are **and**, **but**, and **or**.

| Conjunction | Use | Examples |
|------------|-----|----------|
| **and** | adds more information | I like apples **and** bananas. The cat is small **and** fluffy. |
| **but** | shows contrast (opposite) | I am tired **but** happy. She is short **but** fast. |
| **or** | gives a choice | Do you want juice **or** milk? We can read **or** play. |

## 4. Reading Comprehension

### Main Idea

The **main idea** is what the whole passage is mostly about. It is the most important point.

**How to find the main idea:**
- Ask yourself: "What is this mostly about?"
- Look at the title — it often gives a big hint.
- Check the first and last sentences of the paragraph.
- Think: every detail should connect back to the main idea.

**Example:**
> Bees are amazing insects. They live in hives with thousands of other bees. Bees collect nectar from flowers to make honey. They also help flowers grow by carrying pollen from one flower to another. Without bees, we would not have many of the fruits and vegetables we eat.

**Main idea:** Bees are very important insects that make honey and help plants grow.

### Sequence — Order of Events

**Sequence** means the order in which things happen. Look for **signal words**:

| Signal Words | Meaning |
|-------------|---------|
| first, first of all | at the beginning |
| next, then, after that | what comes after |
| later, soon | a little later |
| finally, last, at last | at the end |

**Example sequence for "Making a Peanut Butter Sandwich":**
1. **First**, get two slices of bread.
2. **Next**, spread peanut butter on one slice.
3. **Then**, spread jelly on the other slice.
4. **After that**, put the two slices together.
5. **Finally**, cut the sandwich in half and eat it!

### Cause and Effect

- **Cause** — why something happened
- **Effect** — what happened as a result

| Cause | Effect |
|-------|--------|
| It rained all day. | The ground was wet. |
| She studied hard for the test. | She got an A. |
| He forgot his jacket. | He was cold. |
| The alarm clock did not go off. | She was late for school. |

**Signal words for cause:** because, since, due to, one reason is
**Signal words for effect:** so, as a result, therefore, consequently, that is why

### Compare and Contrast

- **Compare** — find how things are **alike** (similarities)
- **Contrast** — find how things are **different** (differences)

| Signal Words for Compare | Signal Words for Contrast |
|------------------------|--------------------------|
| both, alike, same, similar, too, also, just like | but, different, however, unlike, on the other hand, while |

**Example — Cats and Dogs:**

| Alike (Compare) | Different (Contrast) |
|-----------------|---------------------|
| Both are pets. | Dogs bark; cats meow. |
| Both have fur. | Cats can climb trees; dogs usually cannot. |
| Both need food and water. | Dogs need walks; cats use a litter box. |
| Both have four legs. | Dogs are usually bigger than cats. |

### Making Predictions

A **prediction** is a smart guess about what will happen next in a story.

**How to make good predictions:**
- Use clues from the story (what has already happened)
- Use what you know about real life
- Look at the pictures
- Ask yourself: "Based on what I know, what will probably happen next?"

**Example:**
> The dark clouds rolled in. The wind started to blow. Mom said, "Everyone inside, quickly!"

**Prediction:** A storm is coming. It will probably start raining or thundering soon.

## 5. Writing

### Paragraphs

A **paragraph** is a group of sentences about ONE main idea. A good paragraph has:

1. **Topic sentence** — tells the main idea (usually the first sentence)
2. **Detail sentences** — give more information and examples
3. **Closing sentence** — wraps up the paragraph (often restates the main idea)

**Example paragraph:**
> **My favorite season is fall.** (topic sentence) The leaves on the trees change to beautiful colors like red, orange, and yellow. I love jumping in big piles of leaves in my yard. The weather gets cooler, so I can wear my favorite sweaters. Halloween and Thanksgiving are in the fall too, and I love both holidays. **Fall is truly the best time of the year.** (closing sentence)

### Friendly Letters

A friendly letter has five parts:

| Part | What It Is | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Heading** | Date (top right) | October 15, 2026 |
| **Greeting** | Dear ___, | Dear Emma, |
| **Body** | The message (your news, questions, feelings) | How are you? I am doing great. I started second grade last week. My teacher is really nice. Do you want to come to my house on Saturday? We can play at the park together. |
| **Closing** | Sign-off word | Your friend, / Love, / Sincerely, |
| **Signature** | Your name | Sam |

### Stories with Beginning, Middle, and End

Every story has three parts:

| Part | Purpose | What to Include |
|------|---------|----------------|
| **Beginning** | Introduces the story | Characters, setting, what is happening |
| **Middle** | The main action | What the character does, the problem or adventure |
| **End** | Wraps up the story | How the problem is solved, how the character feels |

**Example story:**
> **(Beginning)** One sunny morning, a little girl named Lily found a tiny turtle on the sidewalk. She picked it up carefully and looked at its green shell.
>
> **(Middle)** Lily wanted to keep the turtle, but her mom told her that wild animals belong in nature. Lily felt sad at first, but then she had an idea. She filled a plastic tub with water and grass and put the turtle inside. She and her brother carried it to the pond in the park.
>
> **(End)** When they set the turtle down by the pond, it swam away quickly. Lily smiled. She knew the turtle was happy to be home.

### Transition Words

**Transition words** help connect your ideas and make your writing flow smoothly.

| Type | Words |
|------|-------|
| Adding information | also, and, too, besides, another, in addition |
| Showing order | first, next, then, after that, finally, last |
| Showing time | in the morning, later, soon, after, before, meanwhile |
| Showing a result | so, therefore, because of that, as a result |
| Showing contrast | but, however, on the other hand, still, although |
| Giving examples | for example, for instance, such as |
| Ending | in conclusion, to sum up, all in all, in the end |
