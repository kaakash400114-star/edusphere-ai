# Grade 12 English (Core) – Complete Study Guide

---

## 1. Reading Comprehension

- Passage 1 (~650 words discursive) + Passage 2 (case-based factual, data/graphics): MCQs + very short answers.
- Skills at board level: main idea vs supporting detail; author's purpose (persuade/inform/entertain); tone and attitude; inference and evaluation.
- Note-making + summary from Passage 1: headings, abbreviations with key, ~80-word précis in own words.

## 2. Vocabulary and Literary Devices

- Precision vocabulary for analysis: juxtaposition, irony, satire, allegory, ambivalence.
- Figures of speech — identify, quote, explain effect: metaphor, simile, personification, alliteration, hyperbole, oxymoron, transferred epithet ("sorrowful sea"), symbolism.
- Advanced collocations and academic phrasing for essays ("a myriad of", "compelling evidence").

## 3. Grammar (applied)

- **Error correction** in passages: concord, tenses, prepositions, articles.
- **Editing** (omission/extra word), **sentence transformation** (voice, reported speech, conditionals).
- **Reading grammatical accuracy**: cloze passages, one-word substitutions, phrasal verbs in context.

## 4. Writing Skills

### Notice (50 words)
- Board format with institutional header; event/appeal/lost-and-found.

### Invitation + Replies (50 words)
- Formal (third person, no salutation) and informal (first person) invitations; written acceptance/decline with reason.

### Letters (120–150 words)
- **Letter to the Editor**: contemporary social issue — data-backed problem, effects, actionable suggestions.
- **Job Application** with bio-data/CV: vacancy source, qualification match, references.
- Formal register; no contractions; complimentary closes done right.

### Article / Report (120–150 words)
- Article: title, by-line, hook, structured argument, conclusion — opinions supported by examples.
- Report: annual day/accident/celebration — headline, byline, lead (5W+1H), body in past tense, impersonal tone.

## 5. Literature — Flamingo (prose)

- **The Last Lesson** (Alphonse Daudet): linguistic chauvinism; language as identity — value what you lose only when lost.
- **Lost Spring** (Anees Jung): child labour cycles — Saheb (ragpicker's dreams) and Mukesh (bangle-maker's fatalism); "born in the caste of karam".
- **Deep Water** (William Douglas): fear and its conquest — swimming as metaphor for life.
- **The Rattrap** (Selma Lagerlöf): essential human goodness beneath surface; the rat-trap theory of the world; compassion reforms.
- **Indigo** (Louis Fischer): Gandhian leadership — small causes, systemic wins; courage of the Champaran peasants.
- **Poets and Pancakes**: Asokamitran's gentle satire of film-world routine.
- **The Interview**: attitudes to interviews; Umberto Eco's "interstices" idea.
- **Going Places** (A.R. Barton): adolescent hero-worship and fantasy vs reality (Danny Casey).

## 6. Literature — Flamingo (poetry)

- **My Mother at Sixty-six** (Kamala Das): ageing mother, childhood fear of separation; smiling refrain.
- **Keeping Quiet** (Pablo Neruda): introspective silence, not inactivity; peace and mutual understanding.
- **A Thing of Beauty** (Keats): beauty's lasting joy; Greek allusions; Romantic optimism.
- **Aunt Jennifer's Tigers** (Adrienne Rich): art outlives oppression; terrified aunt vs fearless tigers — gender themes.
- **On the Face of It**: Mr Lamb and Derry — disability of body vs disability of attitude.

## 7. Literature — Vistas (supplementary)

- **The Third Level** (Jack Finney): escapism, time-travel fantasy, modern anxiety.
- **The Tiger King** (Kashi Amiran): satire on autocracy and vanity; irony of the 100th tiger.
- **Journey to the End of the Earth** (Tishani Doshi): Antarctica as a time machine — climate urgency.
- **The Enemy** (Pearl S. Buck): a surgeon's ethics over war-hatred — human above nation.
- **On the Face of It** (also listed above — play of perceptions).
- **Memories of Childhood**: Zitkala-Sa (hair-cutting humiliation — resistance to cultural erasure) and Bama (caste discrimination glimpsed through innocence).

## 8. Literary Analysis Method

- Character: traits + turning points + quote evidence.
- Theme statements: not a topic ("freedom") but a claim ("true freedom requires freeing others too").
- Long-answer structure: thesis → 3 points with textual proof → conclusion; 120–150 words discipline.
- Poem analysis: paraphrase stanza → devices → tone shift → central idea.

## 9. Punctuation and Register

- Senior-level punctuation: semicolon/colon accuracy, dash effects, quotation integration.
- Formality control across writing tasks; banned habits: SMS spellings, clichés, empty adjectives.

## 10. Exam Strategy

- Section time budget and word limits; extract-based questions: answer only what is asked.
- Literature: quote short exact phrases with quotation marks; avoid retelling plots.
- Last-day revision: formats, device list, theme lines of each chapter.
