# Grade 12 English - Complete Study Guide

## 1. Reading Comprehension - Board Pattern

### Passage 1 and 2 Strategy
- Two unseen passages (literary + factual/discursive), MCQs and one-word answers.
- 8-minute cap per passage: 3 minutes read, 5 minutes answer.
- Underline transition words: however (contrast), therefore (result), moreover (addition).

### Answer Precision
- One-word/one-sentence answers must quote the exact word from the text when asked "Which word means..."
- Justify-with-evidence questions: point + quote in 2 lines.

## 2. Note-Making and Summary (Board Format)

### Notes Rulebook
- Title: not a sentence; capture the passage's spine.
- Points: numbered 1, 2, 3 with (a), (b) sub-points; indented two spaces per level.
- Abbreviations: minimum 4, listed in the key; standard ones free (e.g., i.e., etc.).
- No sentences: drop articles and verbs where sense survives.

### Summary
- 80 words, one paragraph, third person, past-tense reporting of author's claims.
- Structure: "The passage discusses X. It argues Y. It concludes Z."

## 3. Writing - Article and Report

### Article (120-150 words)
- Title + by-line; problem hook -> causes -> effects -> solution -> closing line.
- Evidence: one statistic or example per body paragraph.

### Report (120-150 words)
- Headline, by-line, place-date line, lead paragraph (5W+H), body (sequence/quotations), conclusion.
- Past tense, passive often natural: "The event was inaugurated by..."

### Value Points Bank
- Environment: single-use plastic, carbon footprint, rainwater harvesting.
- Technology: AI in classrooms, screen-time, cyber-safety.

## 4. Writing - Letters and Applications

### Job Application + CV
- Paragraph 1: post + source. Paragraph 2: qualifications matching the ad. Paragraph 3: experience + interview request.
- CV: career objective, education table, skills, achievements, references, declaration.

### Editor and Complaint Letters
- Editor: civic issue -> impact -> 2 suggestions -> publish request.
- Complaint: product/service facts -> defect chronology -> replacement/refund demand.

### Formal Style Marks
- No contractions, no slang; salutations exact: Sir/Madam; closes: Yours faithfully (unknown name), Yours truly (named).

## 5. Grammar - Senior Accuracy

### Concord in Complex Subjects
- "Each of the players was fit." "Bread and butter is my breakfast" (one idea).
- "A number of students were absent" vs "The number of students was small".

### Transformations Recap
- Affirmation to negative without meaning change: "He is always punctual." -> "He is never late."
- Exclamatory to assertive: "What a beautiful scene!" -> "It is a very beautiful scene."
- Interchange of degrees; join into simple/compound/complex on demand.

### Determiners and Modals Precision
- little/a little, fewer/less, much/many in gap-fills.
- Modals of deduction: must (certain), can't (certain negative), might/may (possible).

## 6. Flamingo - Prose

### The Last Lesson (Alphonse Daudet)
- Theme: linguistic identity as freedom; Franz's regret - "Will they make them sing in German, even the pigeons?"
- Setting: Franco-Prussian war; M. Hamel's forty years of service.

### Lost Spring (Saeb Anees Jung)
- Themes: poverty's cycle - Saheb (ragpicker to restaurant boy, lost freedom), Mukesh (bangle-maker who dares to dream).
- Ironies: "garbage to them is gold"; promises "not to be fulfilled".

### Deep Water (William Douglas)
- Theme: fear conquered through deliberate effort; "all we have to fear is fear itself".

### The Rattrap (Selma Lagerlof)
- Theme: kindness redeems - the peddler's "escape" via the ironmaster's daughter's compassion; signature "Captain von Stahle".

### Indigo (Louis Fischer)
- Theme: Gandhiji's Champaran satyagraha - courage, leadership, self-reliance of peasants.
- Values: civil disobedience, truth-force, sharecroppers' liberation from the 15% tinkathia.

### Poets and Pancakes (Asokamitran)
- Theme: film-studio life; gentle satire on make-up, "Pancake", celebrity culture.

### The Interview
- Theme: interviews as both invasion and truth-teller; Umberto Eco's "interstices" idea - using empty spaces of life.

### Going Places (A.R. Barton)
- Theme: adolescent hero-worship and fantasy; Sophie's dreams vs reality; Danny Casey as escapism.

## 7. Flamingo - Poetry

### My Mother at Sixty-six (Kamala Das)
- Theme: ageing, fear of loss, forced smile; similes "like that of a corpse", "waning, pale as a late winter's moon".
- Devices: childhood "throwing away" fear contrast with present fear.

### An Elementary School Classroom in a Slum (Stephen Spender)
- Themes: social injustice, hollow promises of maps and rulers; the plea for "history theirs whose language is the sun".
- Imagery: "rootless weeds", "squirrel's game".

### Keeping Quiet (Pablo Neruda)
- Theme: introspective stillness - not death but peace; counting to twelve, "no truck with death".
- Paradox: silence that speaks; "Now I'll count and you keep quiet".

### A Thing of Beauty (Keats)
- Theme: beauty's permanence lifting the "pall" from our dark spirits; list of beautiful things - "a bower quiet for us".

### A Roadside Stand (Robert Frost)
- Theme: urban-rural divide; fake pity of "polished traffic"; the farmer's betrayed hope.

### Aunt Jennifer's Tigers (Adrienne Rich)
- Theme: patriarchal oppression vs art's freedom; tigers "prance... unafraid", ringed by the "massive weight of Uncle's wedding band".

## 8. Vistas - Supplementary Reader

### The Third Level (Jack Finney)
- Theme: escapism from modern anxiety; Grand Central's imagined third level - Charley, the letter from Galesburg.

### The Tiger King (Kali - Ramaswamy Aiyer Krishnamurthy)
- Theme: satirical autocracy; the Maharaja's 99 tiger kills and the fatal 100th (wooden tiger's splinter).
- Irony: the astrologer's prophecy fulfilled in a toy tiger.

### Journey to the End of the Earth (Tishani Doshi)
- Theme: Antarctica as geological time machine; Students on Ice programme - "warm in the coldest place".

### The Enemy (Pearl S. Buck)
- Theme: medical ethics over nationalism; Dr Sadao shelters an American POW - duty to humanity above country.

### On the Face of It (Susan Hill)
- Theme: disability as society's gaze; Derry's burned face meets Mr Lamb's tin legs - "what the mind sees matters".

### Magh Bihu (temporarily excluded years) and Memories of Childhood
- The Cutting of My Long Hair (Zitkala-Sa): forced assimilation - shingled hair = loss of identity.
- We Too are Human Beings (Bama): caste insult via the vadai scene; elder's advice - education as the weapon.

## 9. Literary Criticism Basics

### Theme vs Message vs Moral
- Theme: central idea (loss of identity). Message: what author urges. Moral: stated lesson (fables).
- Frame: "Through X, the author explores Y, suggesting Z."

### Character Arc Notes
- Track: starting trait -> trigger -> change -> final state. Example: Derry - withdrawn -> Mr Lamb's talk -> open to life.

### Point of View Effects
- First person (intimacy, bias), third limited (focus), omniscient (scope).
- Identify narrator in each chapter and one advantage it brings.

## 10. Vocabulary - Class 12 Boards

### High-Frequency Board Words
- Alacrity (eagerness), capitulate (surrender), ephemeral (short-lived), insidious (stealthily harmful).
- Redundant (unnecessary), veneer (outward show), ubiquitous (present everywhere).

### Phrasal Precision
- stand up for = defend; come across = find by chance; put up with = tolerate; do away with = abolish.

### Idioms for Value Questions
- a wolf in sheep's clothing = hidden evil; add fuel to fire = worsen; in cold blood = without pity.
- take with a grain of salt = doubt partly; move mountains = achieve the impossible.

## 11. Answer-Writing Craft for Boards

### Literature Answers (40-50 / 120-word types)
- Short (2-3 marks): point + one reason/example, 30-40 words.
- Long (5-6 marks): intro line -> 2-3 developed points with textual reference -> closing value line, 120 words.

### Quote Integration
- Quote naturally: "The line '...refuge in beauty' shows Keats' faith in beauty's healing."
- Never quote more than one line; explain in your own words.

### Presentation
- Headings where asked (report/notice), margins neat; attempt every part of the question.

## 12. Mini Board-Pattern Test

1. Why did M. Hamel call French the most beautiful language? (A: Key to identity and freedom for the enslaved.)
2. Theme of Keeping Quiet in one line. (A: Mindful stillness, not inactivity, can heal the violent world.)
3. Identify device: "her face ashen like that of a corpse". (A: Simile showing ageing as death-like.)
4. One word: present everywhere (A: ubiquitous)
5. Value question: What does The Enemy teach about duty? (A: A doctor's duty is to save life first - humanity above nationality.)
