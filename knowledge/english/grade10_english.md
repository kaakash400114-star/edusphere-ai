# Grade 10 English - Complete Study Guide

## 1. Reading - Discursive Passage Mastery

### Strategy
- 400-word argumentative/descriptive passage, 8 MCQs.
- First reading: 90 seconds, note the author's stand. Second reading: question-driven.
- MCQ traps: extreme words (always, never) are usually wrong; qualified words (some, may) are usually right.

### Evidence Discipline
- Every answer sits in a line - locate the line number mentally before choosing.
- Inference questions: the answer is suggested, never stated; pick the middle path option.

## 2. Reading - Case-Based Factual

### Handling Data
- Passage types: survey results, historical notes, product information with figures.
- Learn to read a pie chart in words: "largest share", "one-fourth", "more than half".
- Statements of comparison: twice, thrice, slightly higher, marginally lower.

### Skill Drill
- Write 3 conclusions from any chart: highest, lowest, trend over time.
- Check units and years - most wrong answers ignore them.

## 3. Writing - Analytical Paragraph (100-120 words)

### Skeleton
- Line 1: what the data represents + overall trend ("The bar chart depicts... overall increase").
- Lines 2-3: key figures with comparisons. Line 4: one-line conclusion.
- Global errors: opinion-giving, exceeding 120 words, copying full phrases.

### Trend Bank
- Verbs: soared, dipped, stabilised, doubled, halved, plateaued.
- Adverbs: sharply, gradually, marginally, consistently.
- Sentence patterns: "From X to Y, there was a consistent decline in..."

## 4. Writing - Letters (100-120 words)

### Formal Application
- Job application: reference to advertisement -> age/qualification summary -> experience -> availability -> closing.
- Leave application: reason, duration, work-handover line, gratitude.

### Complaint and Editor Letters
- Complaint: purchase facts -> defect -> relief sought. Attach bill reference.
- Editor: current public issue (stray animals, traffic, water) -> effect on citizens -> two suggestions -> "Yours truly".

### Letter Etiquette
- Subject line in one phrase. No contractions (write "I am", not "I'm"). Word limit discipline.

## 5. Grammar - Determiners, Gap Fills, Error Correction

### Determiners in Boards
- each/every, much/many, few/little (with and without 'a'), either/neither.
- "The each boy" -> each boy (never pair 'the' with 'each/every').

### Gap-Fill Tactics
- Identify what's missing: noun helper (article), verb helper (be/have), connector (and/but/because).
- Tense blank: look at time markers (yesterday, since, by next year).

### Error-Correction Drills
- Subject-verb: "The quality of the apples were poor" -> was.
- Preposition: "She is married with a doctor" -> to.
- Word form: "He gave me some useful informations" -> information.

## 6. Literature - First Flight (Prose)

### A Letter to God
- Theme: unshakeable faith with a comic irony; Lencho's letter - faith meets post-office kindness.
- Irony: the postmaster's charity is repaid with suspicion of theft.

### Nelson Mandela - Long Walk to Freedom
- Themes: courage is not the absence of fear; freedom is indivisible (both oppressor and oppressed need liberation).
- Key lines: "no one is born hating"; twin obligations (family + country).

### Two Stories About Flying
- His First Flight (young seagull's fear and first leap) - theme: necessity defeats fear.
- The Black Aeroplane - mystery pilot; theme: gratitude and unexplained help.

### From the Diary of Anne Frank
- Theme: paper has more patience than people; growing up under fear.
- Character: Anne - talkative, witty, introspective; Kitty (diary) as confidante.

### Glimpses of India
- Three parts: A Baker from Goa (nostalgia for Portuguese legacy), Coorg (coffee, valour), Tea from Assam (tea origins).
- Skill: match sub-part with author and region.

### Mijbil the Otter
- Theme: man-animal affection; Maxwell's playful otter on a flight.
- Humour from Mij's water games and the tin box escape.

## 7. Literature - First Flight (Poetry)

### Dust of Snow (Robert Frost)
- Theme: a small natural moment changes a ruined mood; crow and hemlock - beauty from dark things.

### Fire and Ice (Robert Frost)
- Theme: desire (fire) and hatred (ice) - two ends of the world; compact irony.

### A Tiger in the Zoo
- Theme: captivity vs freedom; caged strength is frustration. Contrast: velvet cage vs forest shadows.

### How to Tell Wild Animals
- Theme: humour through absurd identification rules; satire on hunting guides.

### The Ball Poem (John Berryman)
- Theme: loss as the first lesson of growing up; money cannot buy back what grief teaches.

### For Anne Gregory (Yeats)
- Theme: inner vs outer beauty; only God loves you for what you are, not your hair colour.

## 8. Literature - Footprints Without Feet

### A Triumph of Surgery
- Theme: over-pampering harms; Tricki the dog saved by plain living.

### The Thief's Story
- Theme: trust reforms a thief; Anil's quiet kindness defeats Hari Singh's craft.
- Ending irony: the torn notes - wet with rain and remorse.

### The Midnight Visitor
- Theme: presence of mind beats firepower; Ausable invents a balcony.

### A Question of Trust
- Theme: a thief robbed by a thief; Horace Danby's irony - safe-cracker caught by a smooth talker.

### Footprints Without Feet
- Theme: science misused brings ruin; Griffin the invisible scientist - lawless and homeless.

## 9. Vocabulary - Board Level

### One-Word Substitutes
- Ephemeral: short-lived. Plagiarism: stealing another's work. Autocracy: rule by one.
- Misogamist: one who hates marriage. Omniscient: all-knowing.

### Idioms and Phrases
- take to task = scold; in the doldrums = depressed; a storm in a teacup = much fuss over little.
- leave no stone unturned = try everything; at the eleventh hour = at the last moment.

### Spelling and Confusables
- believe, achieve (i before e); embarrass, occurrence (double letters).
- lose/loose, effect/affect, principal/principle, advise/advice.

## 10. Analytical Skills - Compare and Evaluate

### Character Comparison
- Frame: Both X and Y ... , but while X ..., Y ... . Example: Lencho's faith vs postmaster's kindness.
- Write a 60-word compare for any two characters from your chapters.

### Theme-to-Life Connection
- Boards ask: "What values does the story teach?" Link theme to a present-day example.
- Practice: one value line + one real-life application for each chapter.

## 11. Revision Mechanics

### The 3-Pass Method
- Pass 1: notes and mind maps. Pass 2: writing formats by hand. Pass 3: past-paper questions timed.

### Common Board Pitfalls
- Ignoring the word limit (marks are cut), missing the subject line, quoting wrong chapter names.
- In literature: writing the summary instead of answering the question asked.

## 12. Mini Board-Pattern Test

1. Why is Lencho's faith ironic? (A: He suspects the very people who helped him.)
2. Analytical sentence for a rise. (A: Online sales rose steadily from 22% to 58% between 2019 and 2022.)
3. Spot error: "Neither of the two boys have done it." (A: have -> has)
4. One word: short-lived (A: ephemeral)
5. Theme of The Ball Poem in one line. (A: Loss teaches a child what money cannot buy back.)
