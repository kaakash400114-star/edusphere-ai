# Grade 10 English – Complete Study Guide

---

## 1. Reading Comprehension

- **Discursive passage** (~400 words): argument tracking, writer's attitude, inference MCQs, vocabulary in context, extract-based questions.
- **Case-based factual passage** (with data/graphics): convert numbers into statements; compare and conclude.
- Strategy: read questions first, underline keywords, scan, then answer precisely within limits (MCQs need no sentences; short answers do).

## 2. Vocabulary

- Context clues (definition, example, contrast), word families, **one-word substitutions** (misogamist, xenophobia, omnivorous).
- Common idioms: at the drop of a hat, bite the bullet, once bitten twice shy.
- Formal vs informal register: kids → children, lots of → a great deal of, ask for → request.

## 3. Grammar (applied, not definitions)

### Tenses
- Gap-filling and error-correction in connected passages; narrative consistency; perfect vs simple past distinctions.

### Determiners
- Article precision (the unique: the sun; zero article with abstract/general plurals); quantifier choice; each/every, much/many, few/little subtleties.

### Reported Speech
- Statement, question, command, request, exclamation conversions; keeping meaning while shifting tense/pronouns/time expressions.

### Modals
- Ability/permission/obligation/possibility in context; semi-modals (used to, need, dare, ought to).

### Subject-Verb Agreement and Clauses
- Tricky concord (along with, as well as, one of the + plural noun + singular verb; neither…nor nearest-verb rule).
- Relative clauses (who/whom/whose/which/that; defining vs non-defining with commas).

## 4. Writing Skills

### Formal Letter (100–120 words)
- Letter to the Editor (social issues): attention line, problem → effects → suggestions → call to action.
- Letter of Enquiry: asking for course/product details, terms, admission procedure.
- Letter of Complaint: product/service problems with dates, expected redress, polite but firm tone.

### Analytical Paragraph (100–120 words)
- From maps, charts, graphs, cues: **overview → key trends (highest/lowest/change) → comparison → conclusion**.
- Language of analysis: increases sharply, declines gradually, the majority, twice as many as, whereas.
- No personal opinion — data speaks.

### Story Writing
- From prompts/outlines; strong opening line, climax, satisfying end; dialogue punctuation; 150–200 words.

## 5. Literature — First Flight (prose themes)

- **A Letter to God**: faith and irony — Lencho's blind faith in God, distrust of people.
- **Nelson Mandela: Long Walk to Freedom**: courage, true freedom = freeing both oppressor and oppressed.
- **Two Stories about Flying** (His First Flight / Black Aeroplane): fear → courage; mystery of help in crisis.
- **From the Diary of Anne Frank**: adolescence, isolation, hope amid war.
- **Glimpses of India**: three regions, three flavours — diversity as unity.
- **Mijbil the Otter**: human-animal bonding.
- **Madam Rides the Bus**: curiosity and a child's first encounter with death.
- **The Sermon at Benares**: Kisa Gotami — death is universal; wisdom through acceptance.
- **The Proposal** (play): farce on human vanity in marriage proposals.

## 6. Literature — Poetry

- **Dust of Snow** (Frost): small moments change moods; crow and hemlock as unlikely healers.
- **Fire and Ice** (Frost): desire and hate as twin ends of the world; symbolism.
- **A Tiger in the Zoo** (Leslie Norris): captivity vs freedom — stark contrast.
- **How to Tell Wild Animals**: humour through absurd "tests".
- **The Ball Poem** (Berryman): loss and growing up — "money is external".
- **Amanda!**: constant nagging vs a child's dream world; tone of escape.
- **The Trees** (Adrienne Rich): nature's revolt, symbolism of freedom.
- **Fog** (Sandburg): fog as a cat — metaphor study.
- **The Tale of Custard the Dragon**: bravery redefined; ballad humour.
- **For Anne Gregory** (Yeats): inner vs outer beauty.

## 7. Figures of Speech (exam favourites)

- Identify + quote + effect for: simile, metaphor, personification, alliteration, repetition, rhyme scheme, hyperbole, irony, symbolism.
- Ironic contrast: title vs situation (a letter answered by humans, not God).

## 8. Punctuation and Sentence Craft

- Editing tasks: omissions, spellings, punctuation in passages.
- Sentence transformation: too…to → so…that; as soon as → no sooner…than; unless/if not; despite/in spite of.

## 9. Exam Strategy

- Reading (20) + Writing & Grammar (20) + Literature (20) — attempt reading first if fast, else strongest section.
- Quote line numbers in literature answers; one idea per sentence; revise verb forms at the end.
