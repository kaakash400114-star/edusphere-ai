## 1. Matter in Our Surroundings

### What Is Matter?
- Anything that has mass and occupies volume. Made of tiny particles that are continuously moving and attract each other.

### Three States of Matter
- **Solid**: particles tightly packed, only vibrate; fixed shape and volume; incompressible.
- **Liquid**: particles close but slide; fixed volume, takes container's shape; nearly incompressible.
- **Gas**: particles far apart, move fast and freely; no fixed shape or volume; highly compressible (CNG in cylinders).

### Changes of State (by temperature and pressure)
- Melting (solid→liquid) at the **melting point**; the temperature stays constant while melting because the heat (latent heat of fusion) breaks the attraction between particles.
- Boiling (liquid→gas) at the **boiling point**; **latent heat of vaporisation** is absorbed.
- **Evaporation**: surface phenomenon - happens at all temperatures. Increases with surface area, temperature, wind speed, and lower humidity. Causes cooling (particles with high energy escape - e.g., sweating cools us; earthen pots cool water).
- **Condensation** and **freezing** are the reverse processes.
- **Sublimation**: solid directly to gas (camphor, naphthalene, dry ice).

### Units
- Temperature in kelvin for science: K = °C + 273.

## 2. Is Matter Around Us Pure

### Mixtures and Pure Substances
- A **pure substance** has one kind of particle (element or compound). A **mixture** contains two or more substances mixed physically.
- **Homogeneous mixture**: uniform throughout (salt solution, air, alloys).
- **Heterogeneous mixture**: not uniform (muddy water, oil and water).

### Types of Mixtures and Separation
- **Solution**: solute + solvent; particle size < 1 nm; transparent, stable (sugar in water). Concentration = amount of solute in a given amount of solution (mass %).
- **Suspension**: big particles, visible, settles on standing (muddy water). Can be filtered.
- **Colloid**: particles between solution and suspension; scatter light - **Tyndall effect** (milk, fog, smoke). Particles don't settle; separated by centrifugation.

### Separation Techniques
- Evaporation (salt from sea water), crystallisation (pure copper sulphate from impure solution - better than evaporation because some solids decompose), filtration, sedimentation-decantation, centrifugation (cream from milk), sublimation (camphor + salt), chromatography (separating dyes in ink), distillation (acetone + water - different boiling points), fractional distillation (petroleum, air components), separating funnel (oil + water), magnetic separation.

### Elements, Compounds
- **Element**: one kind of atom (iron, oxygen). Metals (lustrous, malleable, ductile, conduct), non-metals, metalloids.
- **Compound**: two or more elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio (water H2O, methane CH4) - properties differ from constituent elements; can be separated only chemically.

## 3. Atoms and Molecules

### Laws of Chemical Combination
1. **Law of conservation of mass**: mass is neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction (Lavoisier).
2. **Law of constant proportions**: a compound always contains the same elements in the same proportion by mass (water is always H:O = 1:8).

### Dalton's Atomic Theory (key points)
- Matter is made of tiny indivisible atoms; atoms of an element are identical in mass and properties; atoms combine in small whole-number ratios to form compounds; atoms are rearranged in reactions.

### The Mole Concept
- **Atomic mass unit (u)**: mass scale for atoms relative to carbon-12.
- **Molecular mass**: sum of atomic masses in a molecule (H2O = 18 u).
- **Mole**: 6.022 × 10^2^3 particles (Avogadro's number) = the atomic/molecular mass in grams.
- **Formula unit mass** for ionic compounds (NaCl = 58.5 u).

### Writing Chemical Formulae (criss-cross of valencies)
- Water: H (+1) and O (−2) → H2O.
- Aluminium oxide: Al (+3) and O (−2) → Al2O3.
- **Molecular/ion mass calculations** and simple percentage-composition problems follow from these rules.

## 4. Structure of the Atom

### Charged Particles in Matter
- **Electron** (J.J. Thomson): negative, mass ~1/2000 of a hydrogen atom.
- **Proton** (E. Goldstein / Rutherford): positive, in the nucleus.
- **Neutron** (James Chadwick): no charge, in the nucleus, mass ≈ proton.

### Atomic Models
- **Thomson**: atom as a positive sphere with electrons embedded (like a pudding).
- **Rutherford's alpha-scattering experiment**: most alpha particles passed straight through gold foil - atom is mostly empty; some deflected - a tiny, dense, positive **nucleus**.
- Drawback: revolving electrons should spiral into the nucleus (classical physics) - but atoms are stable.
- **Bohr's model**: electrons revolve only in certain allowed **shells** (energy levels K, L, M, N...) without radiating energy.

### Electronic Configuration
- Maximum electrons per shell = 2n^2 (K=2, L=8, M=18).
- Example: sodium (Z=11) → 2, 8, 1.
- **Valency**: electrons an atom must lose/gain to complete its outer shell (Na=1, O=2, Ne=0 - noble gases have full shells).

### Isotopes and Isobars
- **Isotopes**: same element, same atomic number, different mass number (protium, deuterium, tritium; C-12 and C-14). Used in medicine (Co-60 cancer treatment, I-131 goitre), uranium-235 in nuclear reactors.
- **Average atomic mass** of an element = weighted average of its isotopes (chlorine = 35.5).
- **Isobars**: different elements with the same mass number (Ca-40 and Ar-40).

## 5. The Fundamental Unit of Life

### The Cell
- Robert Hooke discovered cells (1665). **Cell theory** (Schleiden, Schwann; Virchow): all living things are made of cells; the cell is the basic unit of life; all cells arise from pre-existing cells.
- Unicellular (amoeba, chlamydomonas) and multicellular organisms; cells specialise into tissues, organs, organisms.

### Plasma Membrane
- Flexible, selectively permeable membrane. Moves substances by:
  - **Diffusion**: movement of a substance from high to low concentration (CO2/O2 exchange).
  - **Osmosis**: movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane from a region of low solute to high solute concentration. Hypotonic (water enters, cell swells), isotonic (no change), hypertonic (water leaves, cell shrinks).

### Cell Wall, Nucleus, Cytoplasm
- **Cell wall** (plant cells): cellulose; allows cells to withstand dilute solutions (turgid cells).
- **Nucleus**: double-membraned; contains chromosomes (DNA + proteins). Genes are DNA segments carrying hereditary information. Nucleoid in prokaryotes (no nuclear membrane).
- **Cytoplasm**: fluid inside the membrane where organelles exist.

### Cell Organelles
- **Endoplasmic reticulum (ER)**: network of membranes; rough ER has ribosomes (protein-making); smooth ER makes fats and detoxifies.
- **Golgi apparatus**: packages and dispatches materials made in the cell; makes lysosomes.
- **Lysosomes**: "suicide bags" - vesicles with digestive enzymes; clean up worn-out organelles.
- **Mitochondria**: "powerhouses" - release energy from glucose (ATP); have their own DNA and ribosomes.
- **Plastids** (plants): chloroplasts (photosynthesis), chromoplasts, leucoplasts.
- **Vacuoles**: storage of water, sap, waste; big in plant cells, small in animal cells.

## 6. Tissues

### What Are Tissues?
- A group of cells similar in structure that work together for a specific function. Plants have **meristematic** and **permanent** tissues; animals have four basic types (epithelial, connective, muscular, nervous).

### Plant Tissues
- **Meristematic tissue**: actively dividing cells at growing points - **apical** (length), **lateral** (girth), **intercalary** (internodes).
- **Permanent tissues** (differentiated, no division):
  - **Simple**: parenchyma (packing, photosynthesis if chlorophyll - chlorenchyma; aerenchyma floats aquatic plants), collenchyma (flexible support), sclerenchyma (dead, thick walls, strength - husk of coconut).
  - **Complex (conducting)**: **xylem** carries water and minerals up; **phloem** carries food both ways (translocation).

### Animal Tissues
- **Epithelial tissue**: covers surfaces and lines cavities - squamous (lungs, mouth), cuboidal (kidney tubules), columnar (intestine, with cilia in airways), glandular (glands).
- **Connective tissue**: binds and supports - blood (plasma + RBC, WBC, platelets), bone (rigid), cartilage (smooth, flexible - nose, ear), ligaments (bone-bone), tendons (bone-muscle), areolar, adipose (fat).
- **Muscular tissue**: movement - striated/voluntary (attached to bones), smooth/involuntary (internal organs), cardiac (heart, involuntary, rhythmic).
- **Nervous tissue**: neurons (cell body, dendrites, axon) carry messages; found in brain, spinal cord, nerves.

## 7. Motion

### Describing Motion
- **Distance**: total path length (scalar). **Displacement**: shortest straight line from start to end with direction (vector).

### Speed, Velocity, Acceleration
- Speed = distance/time (m/s). Velocity = displacement/time (with direction).
- **Average speed** = total distance / total time.
- **Acceleration** = change in velocity / time (m/s^2); negative acceleration = deceleration.
- **Uniform motion**: equal distances in equal times; **non-uniform**: unequal distances.

### Graphical Representation
- Distance-time graph: horizontal line = at rest; straight sloped line = uniform speed; curve = changing speed.
- Velocity-time graph: slope = acceleration; area under the graph = distance travelled.

### Equations of Motion (uniform acceleration)
```
v = u + at
s = ut + (1/2)at^2
v^2 = u^2 + 2as
```
(u = initial velocity, v = final velocity, a = acceleration, t = time, s = distance)

### Uniform Circular Motion
- Constant speed but changing velocity (direction changes) - acceleration towards the centre. v = 2πr/T. Examples: moon around the earth, athlete running on a circular track.

## 8. Force and Laws of Motion

### Force
- A push or pull that changes the state of motion or shape of a body; it is a vector. Net (balanced/unbalanced) force decides motion. Balanced forces keep a body at rest/uniform motion.

### Newton's Laws
1. **First law (law of inertia)**: a body stays at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by an unbalanced force. **Inertia** = resistance to change in state; inertia ∝ mass (heavier objects harder to move/stop). Examples: passengers jerk forward when a bus brakes; dust comes off a carpet when beaten.
2. **Second law**: force = rate of change of momentum → **F = ma**. Momentum p = mv (kg·m/s). Larger force changes momentum faster. Examples: cricketer pulls hands back while catching (increases time, reduces force); karate chop uses high speed.
3. **Third law**: every action has an equal and opposite reaction (on different bodies). Examples: rocket propulsion, walking (pushing the ground backward), gun recoil.

### Conservation of Momentum
- Total momentum before collision = total momentum after (no external force).
- m1u1 + m2u2 = m1v1 + m2v2.

## 9. Gravitation

### Universal Law of Gravitation
- Every object attracts every other object with a force directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.
```
F = G m1 m2 / r^2
```
- **G = 6.67 × 10⁻^1^1 N·m^2/kg^2** (universal gravitational constant).

### Free Fall and g
- Falling under gravity alone. Acceleration due to gravity on earth: **g = 9.8 m/s^2**; g = GM/R^2.
```
v = u + gt,  h = ut + (1/2)gt^2,  v^2 = u^2 + 2gh
```

### Mass and Weight
- **Mass**: amount of matter (kg), constant everywhere.
- **Weight**: force of gravity on a body, W = mg (newton); changes with g (less on the moon, ~1/6 of earth).

### Thrust, Pressure, Buoyancy
- **Pressure = thrust/area** (Pa). Fluids exert pressure in all directions.
- **Buoyant force (upthrust)**: fluids push up on objects immersed in them. Whether an object floats or sinks depends on its density vs the fluid's density.
- **Archimedes' principle**: buoyant force = weight of fluid displaced. Basis of ship design, hydrometers, hot-air balloons, submarines.

### Relative Density
- Density of substance / density of water (no unit; water = 1000 kg/m^3). Used with a hydrometer to test milk purity and battery acid.

## 10. Work and Energy

### Work (physics sense)
```
W = F × s × cos θ
```
- Work is done when a force moves a body through a distance in the direction of the force. Unit: **joule (J)** = 1 N·m.
- No work when force is perpendicular to displacement (θ = 90°) or when there is no displacement (holding a bag still).
- Positive work (force along motion), negative work (force against motion - friction).

### Energy
- Capacity to do work; unit joule; bigger unit kJ.
- **Kinetic energy**: energy of motion, KE = (1/2)mv^2. Doubling speed quadruples KE.
- **Potential energy**: energy of position/configuration, PE = mgh (gravitational). Examples: stretched rubber, water in a dam, wound spring.

### Law of Conservation of Energy
- Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it changes forms. Total energy stays constant.
- Example: falling body - PE decreases as KE increases; total = constant (ignoring air resistance).

### Power and Commercial Unit of Energy
- **Power** = work/time (watt, W = J/s). 1 kW = 1000 W.
- **1 kilowatt-hour (kWh)** = energy used by 1 kW appliance in 1 hour = 3.6 × 10^6 J ("unit" of electricity bills).

## 11. Sound

### Production and Propagation
- Sound is produced by vibrating bodies and travels as longitudinal **mechanical waves** (compressions and rarefactions) through a medium - not through vacuum.
- Speed: fastest in solids, slower in liquids, slowest in gases (~344 m/s in air at 22°C).

### Characteristics of a Sound Wave
- **Frequency (ν)**: vibrations per second (hertz, Hz) - determines pitch.
- **Time period**: T = 1/ν.
- **Wavelength (λ)**: distance between two successive compressions.
- Relation: **v = λν**.
- **Amplitude**: determines loudness (in decibels). Above ~80 dB = noise pollution; over 120 dB painful.
- **Timbre/quality**: distinguishes voices of same pitch and loudness.

### Reflection of Sound - Echo and Reverberation
- Sound reflects like light. **Echo** needs a barrier at least 17.2 m away (to keep a 0.1 s gap for the human ear).
- **Reverberation**: repeated multiple reflections in big halls - reduced by sound-absorbing materials (curtains, fibreboard, seats).
- **Uses**: sonar (ships find sea depth: 2d = v × t), ultrasound cleaning, echo-location by bats and dolphins, medical ultrasound imaging.

### Human Ear
- Pinna collects sound → auditory canal → **eardrum** vibrates → three ear bones amplify → cochlea converts to electrical signals → auditory nerve → brain.

### Range of Hearing
- Humans: 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz. Below = **infrasound** (earthquake warning in animals); above = **ultrasound** (bats, medical uses).

## 12. Improvement in Food Resources

### Crop Variety Improvement
- Breeding better varieties: higher yield, better quality, disease resistance, shorter maturity, wider adaptability (hybridisation, gene modification).

### Crop Production Management
- **Nutrient management**: macronutrients (N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S) and micronutrients (Fe, Mn, B, Zn, Cu, Mo, Cl); manure (compost, green manure, farm waste) and fertilisers (urea, DAP, NPK) - balance and proper timing avoid pollution.
- **Irrigation**: wells, canals, river lift, tanks; modern water-saving methods - drip and sprinkler.
- **Cropping patterns**: mixed cropping, intercropping (different rows - soybean + maize), crop rotation (cereals + legumes keeps soil fertile).

### Crop Protection Management
- Weeds (wild oat, grass), insect pests, diseases - controlled by weedicides, pesticides, fungicides, and preventive methods (clean fields, resistant varieties, timely sowing). Store grain dry and fumigate against rodents and insects.

### Animal Husbandry
- **Cattle**: milk-yielding (dairy) and draught animals; cross-bred exotic + local breeds; feed = roughage + concentrates (fodder, grains); proper shelter, cleaning, vaccination.
- **Poultry**: egg/broiler varieties; cross-breeding for quality; vaccination against diseases.
- **Fish production**: **aquaculture** (freshwater/marine farming), **pisciculture** (fish farming); capture fishing (inland and marine); composite fish culture (5-6 species with different feeding zones).
- **Bee-keeping (apiculture)**: Italian bee commonly used; honey and wax; pasturage determines honey quality.
