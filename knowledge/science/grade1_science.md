## 1. Living vs Non-Living Things

### Key Concept
Living things are different from non-living things. Scientists look for specific signs to tell them apart.

### Characteristics of Living Things

| Characteristic | What It Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Grow** | Living things get bigger over time | A baby grows into a child; a seedling grows into a tree |
| **Move** | Living things can move on their own (even plants move toward light!) | A dog runs; a sunflower turns to face the sun |
| **Reproduce** | Living things can make babies or new plants like themselves | A cat has kittens; a flower makes seeds |
| **Need Food, Water, and Air** | Living things need resources to survive | A fish needs water and food; a plant needs water and sunlight |
| **Respond to Stimuli** | Living things react to things around them | You pull your hand away from something hot; a plant grows toward light |

### Non-Living Things
Non-living things do **not** grow, move on their own, reproduce, need food, or respond to stimuli.

- **Examples of non-living things:** rocks, water, air, a book, a toy car, a pencil, a table, a chair
- **Important note:** Some non-living things can *move* (a rolling ball), but they don't move on their own — something pushes them.
- **Tricky examples:**
  - 🔥 Fire — it "grows," needs oxygen, and moves, but it is **not** alive because it cannot reproduce
  - 🦠 Viruses — they are studied in higher grades, but at this level we consider them non-living because they cannot grow or reproduce on their own

### Quick Check for Students
Ask yourself these 5 questions:
1. Does it **grow**?
2. Does it **move on its own**?
3. Can it **make more of itself**?
4. Does it **need food, water, or air**?
5. Does it **react to things around it**?

If the answer to most of these is **YES**, it is probably a living thing!

### Activity Idea: Living or Non-Living Sort
- **Materials:** Pictures of various objects (dog, rock, tree, car, bird, water, flower, chair, fish, pencil)
- **Directions:** Students sort pictures into two groups — "Living" and "Non-Living." Discuss any tricky ones as a class.

### Common Misconceptions
| Misconception | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Things that move are alive" | Non-living things can move (balls roll, water flows), but living things move on their own |
| "Plants don't move" | Plants move toward light and water even though we can't see it happen quickly |
| "All living things have legs" | Plants, fish, and bacteria are alive but don't have legs |

## 2. Plants

### Parts of a Plant

```
        🌸 Flower
        │
       🌿 Leaf
        │
   ━━━━━━━━━━━━  Stem
        │
   ║║║║ Root ║║║║
   ║║║║║║║║║║║║║
```

| Part | What It Does | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Root** | Anchors the plant and absorbs water and nutrients | Roots grow underground. They hold the plant in place like an anchor and drink up water from the soil. |
| **Stem** | Supports the plant and carries water and food | The stem is like a highway — it carries water up from the roots and food down from the leaves. It holds the plant upright. |
| **Leaf** | Makes food for the plant using sunlight | Leaves are the "kitchen" of the plant. They catch sunlight and use it to make food. |
| **Flower** | Makes seeds for new plants | Flowers are colorful and attractive so insects visit them and help with making seeds. After pollination, seeds form. |

### What Plants Need to Survive

| Need | Why It's Important | What Happens Without It |
|---|---|---|
| ☀️ **Sunlight** | Plants use light to make their food (energy) | The plant will grow tall and weak, become pale, and eventually die |
| 💧 **Water** | Water carries nutrients from the soil through the roots | The plant will wilt, turn brown, and dry up |
| 🌬️ **Air (Carbon Dioxide)** | Plants "breathe in" CO₂ to make food | Without CO₂, plants cannot make food |
| 🪨 **Soil (Nutrients)** | Soil provides minerals that help plants grow healthy | The plant may grow slowly and look unhealthy |

### Plant Life Cycle

```
  🌱 Seed → 🌿 Sprout → 🌳 Plant → 🌸 Flower → 🌱 Seed (back to start!)
```

1. **Seed:** A tiny package that contains a baby plant. Seeds can be many shapes and sizes (an acorn is a seed, a sunflower seed, a bean).
2. **Sprout (Seedling):** When the seed gets water and warmth, it cracks open and a tiny root pushes down and a tiny shoot pushes up toward the light.
3. **Plant:** The sprout grows taller, grows more leaves, and becomes a full plant.
4. **Flower:** The plant produces flowers. Flowers can be pollinated by insects, wind, or birds.
5. **Seed:** After pollination, the flower turns into a fruit or seed pod. The seeds inside can grow into new plants.

### Fun Facts About Plants
- The tallest plant in the world is a coast redwood tree that is over 380 feet (115 meters) tall!
- Some plants, like the Venus flytrap, eat insects.
- Plants can live for a very long time — some trees are over 4,000 years old.
- Bananas are technically berries, and strawberries are not!

### Activity Idea: Grow a Bean Plant
- **Materials:** A clear plastic cup, paper towel, water, a bean seed
- **Directions:** Line the cup with a damp paper towel. Place the bean between the paper towel and the cup. Add a little water. Watch the root and shoot emerge over several days. Students draw and label each stage.

## 3. Animals

### What Animals Need

All animals need the same basic things to survive:

| Need | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 🍎 **Food** | Animals eat different kinds of food to get energy | Lions eat meat; rabbits eat grass; butterflies drink nectar |
| 💧 **Water** | All animals need water to live | Fish live in water; deer drink from streams |
| 🏠 **Shelter** | Animals need a safe place to live | Birds build nests; bears sleep in caves; rabbits live in burrows |
| 🌬️ **Air (Oxygen)** | Animals breathe oxygen to stay alive | Fish breathe oxygen dissolved in water through gills; land animals breathe through lungs |

### Classifying Animals (Basic Groups)

Animals can be put into groups based on what they look like and how they live.

| Group | Key Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 🐾 **Mammals** | Have hair or fur; babies drink milk from their mother; most give birth to live babies | Dogs, cats, elephants, whales, bats, humans |
| 🐦 **Birds** | Have feathers; lay eggs; have a beak (no teeth); most can fly | Eagles, penguins, ostriches, robins, owls |
| 🐟 **Fish** | Live in water; breathe through gills; have fins and scales | Goldfish, sharks, clownfish, salmon |
| 🐛 **Insects** | Have 6 legs; 3 body parts (head, thorax, abdomen); most have wings; have an exoskeleton | Ants, bees, butterflies, ladybugs, grasshoppers |

> **Note for teachers:** At this grade level, students should understand these 4 basic groups. Reptiles and amphibians are introduced in later grades.

### Baby Animals vs Adult Animals

Many baby animals look different from their parents:

| Baby Name | Adult Name | How They're Different |
|---|---|---|
| Calf | Cow | Smaller, different color fur, shorter legs |
| Kitten | Cat | Smaller, shorter fur, playful |
| Chick | Chicken | Covered in fuzz instead of feathers |
| Tadpole | Frog | Has a tail, lives only in water, no legs |
| Caterpillar | Butterfly | Crawls instead of flies, very different shape |
| Puppy | Dog | Smaller, shorter legs, playful |

**Key idea:** Baby animals need their parents to care for them (feeding, protecting, teaching). Some animals, like fish and insects, may not care for their babies.

### Activity Idea: Animal Classification Game
- **Materials:** Cards with pictures of different animals
- **Directions:** Students sort animal cards into mammal, bird, fish, or insect groups. Then they explain why they sorted each animal that way.

## 4. The Human Body

### The Five Senses

Our body has 5 special tools called **senses** that help us understand the world around us.

| Sense | Body Part | What It Detects | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 👁️ **Sight** | Eyes | Light and color | Seeing a rainbow, reading a book, watching a bird |
| 👃 **Smell** | Nose | Odors (smells in the air) | Smelling fresh cookies, smelling rain, smelling a flower |
| 👂 **Hearing** | Ears | Sounds and vibrations | Hearing a dog bark, hearing music, hearing thunder |
| 👅 **Taste** | Tongue | Flavors (sweet, sour, salty, bitter) | Tasting an apple, tasting lemon, tasting salt |
| ✋ **Touch** | Skin | Texture, temperature, pressure | Feeling a fuzzy blanket, touching ice, feeling a rough rock |

**Fun fact:** Your tongue has about 10,000 taste buds!

### Basic Body Parts Students Should Know

| Body Part | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Head | Top of body | Contains the brain, eyes, ears, nose, and mouth |
| Shoulders | Between neck and arms | Help arms move |
| Arms | Sides of the body | Help reach, grab, and carry |
| Hands | End of arms | Help hold, write, and feel things |
| Chest | Front of upper body | Protects the heart and lungs |
| Stomach | Below the chest | Helps digest food |
| Legs | Below the stomach | Help stand, walk, run, and jump |
| Feet | End of legs | Help balance and walk |

### Keeping Your Body Healthy

There are 4 important ways to keep your body healthy:

| Healthy Habit | Why It Matters | How to Do It |
|---|---|---|
| 🏃 **Exercise** | Makes your heart, muscles, and bones strong | Play outside, ride a bike, jump rope, play tag — at least 60 minutes a day |
| 🥗 **Eating Well** | Gives your body energy and nutrients to grow | Eat fruits, vegetables, whole grains, protein (meat, beans, eggs), and drink plenty of water |
| 🧼 **Washing Hands** | Removes germs that can make you sick | Wash with soap and water for 20 seconds — sing "Happy Birthday" twice! Wash before eating and after using the bathroom |
| 😴 **Sleeping** | Lets your body rest, grow, and repair itself | Children ages 5–7 need 9–12 hours of sleep every night |

### Activity Idea: Five Senses Station
- Set up 5 stations around the room:
  1. **Sight:** Look at pictures, use magnifying glasses
  2. **Smell:** Smell containers with vanilla, lemon, cinnamon (blindfolded)
  3. **Hearing:** Listen to sounds and guess what they are (shaking coins, crinkling paper)
  4. **Taste:** Taste small samples (sweet, sour, salty) blindfolded
  5. **Touch:** Reach into a "mystery box" and describe what you feel

## 5. Matter Basics

### What Is Matter?
**Matter** is everything around you that takes up space and has weight (mass). You are made of matter! Your desk, the air, water, rocks — all matter.

### Three States of Matter

| State | What It Looks Like | Properties | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🧊 **Solid** | Keeps its own shape | Hard or firm; holds its shape; does not flow | Ice, rock, a book, a pencil, wood, a coin |
| 💧 **Liquid** | Takes the shape of its container | Flows; spills; pours; wet | Water, juice, milk, oil, honey |
| 💨 **Gas** | Fills all the space available | Invisible (usually); you can't hold it; floats in the air | Air (oxygen, carbon dioxide), steam from hot water, helium in a balloon |

### Key Words for Each State

| Solid Words | Liquid Words | Gas Words |
|---|---|---|
| Hard | Wet | Invisible |
| Firm | Pours | Floating |
| Rigid | Spills | All around us |
| Doesn't change shape | Takes shape of container | Can't hold it |

### Water Can Be All Three!

Water is special because it can change between all three states:

```
  Ice (Solid) → Water (Liquid) → Steam (Gas)
    ←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←←
```

- **Ice:** When water gets very cold (below 0°C / 32°F), it freezes into a solid
- **Water:** At room temperature, water is a liquid
- **Steam:** When water gets very hot (boiling — 100°C / 212°F), it turns into gas (steam/vapor)

### The Water Cycle (Introduction)

Water moves around our Earth in a big circle called the **water cycle**:

1. ☀️ **Sun heats water** in lakes, rivers, and oceans
2. 💨 **Evaporation:** The water turns into invisible gas (water vapor) and rises into the air
3. ☁️ **Condensation:** The water vapor cools high up and forms clouds
4. 🌧️ **Precipitation:** When clouds get heavy, water falls as rain, snow, sleet, or hail
5. 💧 **Collection:** Rain and snow collect in rivers, lakes, and oceans — and the cycle starts again!

> **Fun fact:** The water on Earth today is the same water that the dinosaurs drank!

### Activity Idea: Melting Ice Race
- **Materials:** Ice cubes, different containers (one in sun, one in shade, one in a warm room)
- **Directions:** Predict which ice cube will melt first. Observe and record results. Discuss why heat changes ice into water.

## 6. Weather

### Types of Weather

| Weather | What It Looks Like | What You Wear | What You Do |
|---|---|---|---|
| ☀️ **Sunny** | Bright sky, sun visible | Sunglasses, hat, sunscreen | Play outside, go swimming |
| 🌧️ **Rainy** | Water falling from clouds | Raincoat, rain boots, umbrella | Splash in puddles, read inside |
| ☁️ **Cloudy** | Sky is covered with clouds | Light jacket | Play outside, have a picnic |
| 💨 **Windy** | Air moving fast; leaves and branches blow | Windbreaker, hat that stays on | Fly a kite |
| ❄️ **Snowy** | White flakes falling from sky | Heavy coat, mittens, scarf, boots | Build a snowman, go sledding |
| ⛈️ **Stormy** | Thunder, lightning, heavy rain | Stay inside! | Stay safe indoors |

### The Four Seasons

Seasons happen because the Earth tilts as it goes around the sun. Each season lasts about 3 months.

| Season | Months (Northern Hemisphere) | Weather | What Happens in Nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🌸 **Spring** | March, April, May | Getting warmer; rain; flowers bloom | Plants start growing; baby animals are born; birds return |
| ☀️ **Summer** | June, July, August | Hot; sunny; long days | Trees are green; fruits ripen; families go on vacation |
| 🍂 **Fall (Autumn)** | September, October, November | Getting cooler; leaves change color and fall | Animals prepare for winter; harvest crops; leaves turn red, orange, yellow |
| ❄️ **Winter** | December, January, February | Cold; snow in many places; short days | Some animals hibernate; trees are bare; people wear warm clothes |

### Activity Ideas

**Weather Calendar:**
- Each day, students draw or write the weather. At the end of the month, count how many sunny, rainy, cloudy, and windy days there were.

**Dress the Bear:**
- A paper bear cutout with various clothing options. Students dress the bear correctly for different weather conditions.

## 7. Earth and Space

### Land and Water

Our Earth has two main types of surfaces:

| Feature | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 🏔️ **Land** | Solid ground that is above water | Mountains, valleys, plains, deserts, forests, beaches |
| 🌊 **Water** | Covers about 71% of Earth's surface | Oceans, lakes, rivers, ponds, streams |

> **Fun fact:** There is one big ocean on Earth, but we name different parts: Pacific Ocean, Atlantic Ocean, Indian Ocean, Arctic Ocean, and Southern Ocean.

### The Sun

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| What is it? | A giant ball of very hot gas — a **star** (the closest star to Earth) |
| What does it do? | Gives us **light** so we can see and **warmth** (heat) so we don't freeze |
| Is it alive? | No, it is not a living thing |
| How far away? | About 93 million miles (150 million km) from Earth |
| Size | The Sun is so big that about 1.3 million Earths could fit inside it! |

### Day and Night

- **Daytime:** When the part of Earth where you live faces the Sun, you see light — it's daytime.
- **Nighttime:** When the part of Earth where you live faces away from the Sun, you are in shadow — it's nighttime.
- **Why does it happen?** The Earth **spins (rotates)** like a top. One full spin takes about **24 hours** (one day and one night).

```
    ☀️ Sun
     |
  🌍 Earth spinning →  (one side faces sun = day, other side = night)
```

### Common Misconceptions
| Misconception | Reality |
|---|---|
| "The Sun moves across the sky" | The Sun doesn't move — the Earth rotates, making it look like the Sun moves |
| "The Sun goes behind the Earth" | Earth spins, turning different sides toward and away from the Sun |
| "The Moon makes its own light" | The Moon reflects (bounces back) light from the Sun — it does not make light |

## 8. Forces and Motion

### Push and Pull

A **force** is a push or a pull. Forces make things move, stop, or change direction.

| Type | What It Means | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ➡️ **Push** | Moving something away from you | Pushing a swing, pushing a shopping cart, opening a door |
| ⬅️ **Pull** | Bringing something toward you | Pulling a wagon, pulling up your socks, opening a drawer |

### Fast and Slow

Things can move at different speeds:

| Speed | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 🏎️ **Fast** | Moves quickly; covers distance in short time | A cheetah running (up to 70 mph!), a race car, a shooting star |
| 🚶 **Slow** | Moves slowly; covers distance in long time | A snail, a turtle, a person walking, a sloth |

> **Fun fact:** The slowest animal in the world is the sloth — it moves at only about 6 feet (2 meters) per minute!

### Heavy and Light

| Property | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 🪨 **Heavy** | Hard to lift; has a lot of mass | A rock, an elephant, a car, a refrigerator |
| 🪶 **Light** | Easy to lift; has very little mass | A feather, a leaf, a cotton ball, a piece of paper |

**Key idea:** Heavy things are harder to push or pull than light things. Try pushing an empty box vs. a box full of books!

### Activity Ideas

**Push and Pull Scavenger Hunt:**
- Students find 5 things in the classroom that you push and 5 things you pull. They draw and label each one.

**Heavy or Light Balance:**
- Use a simple balance (ruler on a triangular block) to compare weights of classroom objects.

## 9. Simple Investigations

### What Is a Scientist?

A scientist is someone who **asks questions** about the world and **tries to find answers** by observing, testing, and thinking carefully. **Everyone can be a scientist!**

### Steps for a Simple Investigation

1. 👀 **Observe:** Look carefully at something. What do you see, hear, smell, feel?
2. ❓ **Ask a Question:** What do you wonder about? "What happens if...?" "Why does...?"
3. 🔍 **Describe What You See:** Use your words to explain what you observed

> At this grade level, students are learning to be curious observers. Formal experiments come in later grades.

### Observation Skills

Good scientists observe with all five senses:

| Sense | Observation Questions |
|---|---|
| 👁️ Sight | What color is it? What shape? How big? Is it moving? |
| 👃 Smell | Does it have a smell? What does it remind you of? |
| 👂 Hearing | Does it make a sound? Is it loud or quiet? |
| 👅 Taste | *(Only with teacher permission!)* Is it sweet, sour, salty, or bitter? |
| ✋ Touch | Is it hard or soft? Rough or smooth? Hot or cold? Wet or dry? |

### Activity Ideas

**Nature Walk Investigation:**
- Take students outside. Each student picks one thing to observe carefully. They draw it, describe it with words, and share one thing they noticed that they hadn't seen before.

**Sink or Float:**
- Fill a tub with water. Students predict whether objects (penny, cork, paper clip, leaf, pencil, rock) will sink or float. Test and record results.

## Quick Reference: Vocabulary Words

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Living** | Something that grows, moves, needs food/water/air, reproduces, and responds to its environment |
| **Non-living** | Something that does not grow, move on its own, or need food |
| **Root** | The part of a plant that grows underground, absorbs water, and holds the plant in place |
| **Stem** | The part of a plant that supports it and carries water and nutrients |
| **Leaf** | The part of a plant that makes food using sunlight |
| **Flower** | The part of a plant that makes seeds |
| **Seed** | A tiny package that can grow into a new plant |
| **Mammal** | An animal with hair or fur that feeds its babies milk |
| **Bird** | An animal with feathers that lays eggs |
| **Fish** | An animal that lives in water and breathes through gills |
| **Insect** | An animal with 6 legs and 3 body parts |
| **Solid** | Matter that keeps its own shape (like ice or a rock) |
| **Liquid** | Matter that flows and takes the shape of its container (like water) |
| **Gas** | Matter that fills all available space and is usually invisible (like air) |
| **Evaporation** | When a liquid turns into a gas (like water turning into steam) |
| **Condensation** | When a gas turns back into a liquid (like water vapor forming clouds) |
| **Precipitation** | Water falling from the sky (rain, snow, hail, sleet) |
| **Force** | A push or a pull |
| **Observe** | To look carefully and notice details |
