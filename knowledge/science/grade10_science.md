## 1. Chemical Reactions and Equations

### Chemical Changes
- A chemical reaction forms new substances. Signs: change of state, colour, temperature, gas bubbles, precipitate.

### Writing Equations
- **Word equation** → **chemical equation** with formulae. Balance atoms on both sides (law of conservation of mass). Physical states in brackets: (s), (l), (g), (aq).

### Types of Reactions
1. **Combination**: A + B → AB (CaO + H2O → Ca(OH)2, releases heat — quicklime to slaked lime).
2. **Decomposition**: AB → A + B; by heat (CaCO3 → CaO + CO2), by electricity (electrolysis of water → H2 + O2), by light (photolysis — silver chloride turns grey in sunlight).
3. **Displacement**: more reactive metal pushes out a less reactive one (Fe + CuSO4 → FeSO4 + Cu).
4. **Double displacement**: exchange of ions (Na2SO4 + BaCl2 → BaSO4↓ + 2NaCl — precipitate formed).
5. **Oxidation–Reduction (redox)**: oxidation = gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen/electrons; reduction = the reverse; both happen together (CuO + H2 → Cu + H2O: CuO reduced, H2 oxidised).

### Effects of Oxidation
- **Corrosion**: rusting of iron (needs air + moisture), green patina on copper, black coating on silver. Prevented by painting, oiling, galvanising (zinc coating), alloying.
- **Rancidity**: fats/oils oxidised → bad smell; prevented by airtight containers, refrigeration, antioxidants, nitrogen flushing in chip packets.

## 2. Acids, Bases and Salts

### Properties
- **Acids**: sour; produce H⁺(aq) ions in water (HCl, H2SO4, HNO3). Strong (HCl) vs weak (acetic acid, citric acid).
- **Bases**: bitter, slippery; produce OH⁻ ions (NaOH, KOH, Ca(OH)2). Strong vs weak (ammonium hydroxide).
- Acid + base → **salt + water** (neutralisation): NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H2O.

### Indicators
- Litmus: acid → blue to red; base → red to blue.
- Methyl orange: acid → red; base → yellow.
- Phenolphthalein: colourless in acid, pink in base.
- **Olfactory indicators** (onion, vanilla) lose smell in bases.
- Turmeric stain turns reddish with base.

### Reactions of Acids and Bases
- Acid + metal → salt + H2↑ (Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl2 + H2). Pop test confirms hydrogen.
- Acid + metal carbonate/bicarbonate → salt + water + CO2↑ (limewater turns milky).
- Base + metal → salt + H2↑ (2NaOH + Zn → Na2ZnO2 + H2).
- Neutralisation: acid + base → salt + water (antacid for indigestion).

### pH Scale
- 0–14; pH < 7 acidic, 7 neutral, > 7 basic. pH paper (universal indicator) shows colour range.
- Body pH: blood 7.4; tooth decay begins when mouth pH < 5.5 (brush with basic toothpaste).
- Plants/animals live in narrow pH ranges; excess acid in soil fixed with lime; acid rain (pH < 5.6) harms aquatic life.

### Important Salts
- **Common salt (NaCl)** → raw material: NaOH (chlor-alkali; gives chlorine + hydrogen), baking soda (NaHCO3 — mild antacid, baking), washing soda (Na2CO3·10H2O — cleaning, removes hardness), bleaching powder (Ca(OCl)Cl — disinfectant).
- **Plaster of Paris**: CaSO4·½H2O — from gypsum; sets to hard gypsum (plastering casts, toys, smoothening walls).
- **Water of crystallisation**: fixed water molecules in crystals (CuSO4·5H2O blue; on heating turns white, on adding water back turns blue).

## 3. Metals and Non-metals

### Physical Properties
- **Metals**: lustrous, malleable, ductile, sonorous, good conductors of heat and electricity, high melting points (gallium and caesium melt in hand; mercury is liquid).
- **Non-metals**: dull, brittle, non-ductile, poor conductors (exception: graphite conducts; diamond — hardest natural substance, both carbon).
- **Metalloids**: in-between (boron, silicon).

### Chemical Properties
- Metals + O2 → metal oxide (basic; Al2O3 shows both — amphoteric). 4Na + O2 → 2Na2O.
- Metals + water → metal oxide/hydroxide + H2. Reactivity: Na, K (cold water vigorously), Ca, Mg (hot water), Al, Fe, Zn (steam), Cu, Au, Ag (no reaction).
- Metals + acids → salt + H2 (Mg + 2HCl → MgCl2 + H2). Non-metals mostly do not react with dilute acids.
- Metals + salt solutions → displacement (Fe + CuSO4 → FeSO4 + Cu).

### Reactivity Series (most → least)
K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb, (H), Cu, Hg, Ag, Au.

### Ionic Compounds
- Formed by transfer of electrons (NaCl: Na loses an e⁻ → Na⁺; Cl gains → Cl⁻; held by strong electrostatic force).
- Properties: hard crystalline solids, high melting/boiling points, conduct electricity when molten or dissolved (ions free to move), mostly water-soluble.

### Extraction of Metals
- **Occurrence**: minerals; ores contain metals profitably.
- Less reactive (Hg, Cu): heat the sulphide/carbonate ore directly (roasting/calcination) → metal by reduction.
- Medium reactive (Zn, Fe, Pb): sulphides roasted to oxides, carbonates calcined, then reduced with carbon (coke).
- Highly reactive (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al): electrolytic reduction of molten chlorides/oxides.
- **Thermite reaction**: Fe2O3 + Al → Al2O3 + Fe (molten iron for railway tracks).
- **Refining**: electrolytic refining — impure anode dissolves, pure metal deposits on cathode; impurities (anode mud) settle below.

### Corrosion and Alloys
- Rusting: Fe2O3·xH2O — needs air and moisture. Prevention: paint, oil, galvanising, electroplating, alloying.
- **Alloys**: homogeneous mixtures — steel (Fe + C), stainless steel (Fe + Ni + Cr), brass (Cu + Zn), bronze (Cu + Sn), solder (Pb + Sn), amalgam (with mercury). Alloying increases strength/resistance.

## 4. Carbon and its Compounds

### Bonding in Carbon
- Carbon forms covalent bonds by sharing electrons; tetravalency (4 bonds) + catenation (self-linking) → millions of compounds. Stable compounds: H2, O2, CH4 (satisfying valencies).

### Allotropes of Carbon
- **Diamond**: rigid 3D network, hardest, non-conducting (except thermal).
- **Graphite**: hexagonal layers, slippery, conducts electricity (free electrons) — pencil leads, electrodes.
- **Fullerene (C60)**: football-shaped.

### Hydrocarbons
- **Saturated (alkanes)**: single bonds — CnH2n+2 (methane, ethane).
- **Unsaturated**: **alkenes** (double bond, CnH2n — ethene) and **alkynes** (triple bond, CnH2n−2 — ethyne).
- **Structural isomers**: same formula, different structure (butane and isobutane).
- **Cyclic**: rings (cyclohexane C6H12; benzene C6H6 with alternate double bonds).

### Functional Groups
- Alcohol (–OH), aldehyde (–CHO), ketone (–CO–), carboxylic acid (–COOH), halogen (–Cl, –Br).

### Reactions
- **Combustion**: hydrocarbons + O2 → CO2 + H2O + heat. Saturated → clean blue flame; unsaturated → sooty yellow flame (incomplete combustion).
- **Oxidation**: alcohols → acids with oxidising agents (alkaline KMnO4 — used as unsaturation test: purple colour disappears).
- **Addition**: unsaturated + H2 (with Ni catalyst, hydrogenation — vegetable oil to vanaspati ghee).
- **Substitution**: saturated + Cl2 in sunlight → chlorinated alkane + HCl (CH4 + Cl2 → CH3Cl + HCl).

### Ethanol and Ethanoic Acid
- **Ethanol**: solvent, fuel additive; reacts with Na → sodium ethoxide + H2; with hot conc. H2SO4 → ethene (dehydration).
- **Ethanoic acid (acetic acid)**: weak acid, vinegar 5–8%; reacts with bases, metals, carbonates; melting point 290 K — glacial acetic acid freezes like ice.
- **Esterification**: acid + alcohol with acid catalyst → sweet-smelling **ester** + water; esters hydrolyse back with base (saponification — soap-making).
- **Soaps and detergents**: soap = sodium salt of long-chain fatty acid; hydrophobic tail traps grease, hydrophilic head pulls it into water (micelle). Detergents work in hard water where soaps form scum.

## 5. Periodic Classification of Elements

### Early Attempts
- **Dobereiner's triads**: middle element's atomic mass ≈ average of the other two (Li, Na, K).
- **Newlands' law of octaves**: every eighth element repeats properties (worked only up to calcium).
- **Mendeleev's periodic table**: arranged by atomic mass with gaps predicting undiscovered elements (eka-silicon = germanium). Anomalies: position of hydrogen, isotopes, some mass-order inversions.

### Modern Periodic Table (Moseley)
- Arranged by **atomic number**. 18 groups (vertical), 7 periods (horizontal).
- **Periodicity**: repeating trends due to similar outer-shell electrons.
- Trends across a period: atomic size decreases, metallic character decreases, non-metallic character increases, electronegativity increases.
- Trends down a group: atomic size increases, metallic character increases, reactivity of metals increases.
- Position resolved: isotopes get one place (same Z); hydrogen still anomalous (unique element).

## 6. Life Processes

### What Are Life Processes?
- Maintenance processes: nutrition, respiration, transport, excretion — they keep the "living state" of molecules working even at rest.

### Nutrition
- **Autotrophic**: photosynthesis — CO2 + H2O → (sunlight, chlorophyll) glucose + O2. Steps: absorb light, convert to chemical energy + split water, convert CO2 to carbohydrate. Stomata (guard cells) exchange gases. Desert plants (CAM) take CO2 at night.
- **Heterotrophic**: holozoic (humans), saprophytic (fungi), parasitic (cuscuta, ticks, leeches).
- **Human digestion**: mouth (saliva — amylase digests starch) → oesophagus (peristalsis) → stomach (HCl kills germs, pepsin digests protein, mucus protects wall) → small intestine (bile emulsifies fats, pancreatic juice, intestinal juice; villi absorb nutrients) → large intestine (water absorption) → rectum → anus.

### Respiration
- **Aerobic**: glucose → CO2 + H2O + 38 ATP (mitochondria).
- **Anaerobic**: in yeast → ethanol + CO2 (fermentation); in muscles → lactic acid (cramps).
- Breathing: diaphragm flattens (inhalation), ribs rise; alveoli exchange gases with blood; haemoglobin carries oxygen.
- Human respiratory passage: nostrils → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli.

### Transportation
- **Blood**: plasma + RBC (haemoglobin, O2), WBC (defence), platelets (clotting).
- **Heart**: 4 chambers, double circulation — oxygenated and deoxygenated blood kept separate; valves prevent backflow; right side pumps to lungs, left side to the body.
- **Blood vessels**: arteries (thick-walled, away from heart), veins (valves, toward heart), capillaries (exchange).
- **Lymph**: carries digested fat, drains extra fluid.
- **Plants**: **xylem** moves water up (transpiration pull + root pressure); **phloem** moves food (translocation, using ATP).

### Excretion
- **Human**: kidneys (nephrons filter blood — glomerular filtration, reabsorption), ureters → bladder → urethra. Urine = water + urea + salts.
- **Plants**: gaseous wastes through stomata; resins and gums stored in old xylem; excess water by transpiration.

## 7. Control and Coordination

### Nervous System
- **Neuron**: dendrites (receive) → cell body → axon → nerve endings; synapse passes signals chemically between neurons.
- **Reflex action**: fast, automatic response (knee jerk, withdrawing hand from heat) — spinal cord mediates via **reflex arc** (receptor → sensory neuron → spinal cord → motor neuron → muscle).
- **Human nervous system**: **CNS** (brain + spinal cord) and **PNS** (nerves).
- **Brain**: cerebrum (thinking, memory), cerebellum (balance, posture), medulla (involuntary — heartbeat, breathing), pons.

### Coordination in Plants
- **Tropisms**: phototropism (towards light), geotropism (roots downward), hydrotropism (water), chemotropism (pollen tubes).
- Chemicals: auxin (cell elongation, tip bending), gibberellin (growth), cytokinin (cell division), abscisic acid (wilting — inhibits growth).

### Hormones in Animals
- Endocrine glands → hormones into blood: thyroid (thyroxine — metabolism; iodine), pituitary (growth), pancreas (insulin — blood sugar), adrenal (adrenaline — emergency), testes/ovaries (testosterone/oestrogen — puberty).
- Feedback control: high blood sugar → insulin → sugar stored → sugar falls → insulin secretion falls.

## 8. How Do Organisms Reproduce?

### Importance
- Reproduction creates new individuals of a species — variation helps survival of the species.

### Asexual Reproduction
- **Fission** (amoeba — binary; plasmodium — multiple), **budding** (hydra, yeast), **fragmentation** (spiroid filamentous algae), **regeneration** (planaria), **spore formation** (rhizopus), **vegetative propagation** (potato eyes, money plant stems; advantages: faster, identical, seedless fruits like banana).
- **Tissue culture**: new plants from callus in nutrient medium.

### Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants
- **Flower parts**: sepals, petals, **stamen** (anther + filament — male), **carpel/pistil** (stigma + style + ovary — female).
- Pollination: self or cross (by wind, water, insects) → pollen tube grows through style → male gamete fuses with egg (fertilisation) → zygote; another gamete fuses with two polar nuclei → endosperm (double fertilisation).
- After fertilisation: ovule → seed; ovary → fruit.

### Human Reproductive System
- **Male**: testes (in scrotum — lower temperature), vas deferens, seminal vesicles, prostate, penis. Sperm produced continuously from puberty.
- **Female**: ovaries (eggs + oestrogen/progesterone), fallopian tube (fertilisation site), uterus (implantation — **placenta** nourishes the foetus), vagina (birth canal).
- **Menstrual cycle**: egg matures ~monthly; uterus wall thickens; if not fertilised, wall sheds (menstruation).
- **Reproductive health**: sexually transmitted diseases (bacterial — gonorrhoea, syphilis; viral — HIV/AIDS, warts); contraception — barrier (condoms), hormonal pills, copper-T, surgical (vasectomy/tubectomy).
- Population control and safe practices are social responsibilities.

## 9. Heredity and Evolution

### Heredity
- Transmission of traits from parents to offspring. **Mendel's experiments** with pea plants:
  - **F1 generation**: tall × short → all tall (tallness dominant, Tt).
  - **F2**: 3 tall : 1 short (TT, Tt, Tt, tt) — 3:1 phenotype, 1:2:1 genotype.
  - **Dihybrid cross**: round-yellow × wrinkled-green → 9:3:3:1 — traits are inherited independently.
- Sex determination in humans: XX female, XY male; the father's sperm (X or Y) decides the child's sex.

### Evolution
- **Acquired vs inherited traits**: changes in non-reproductive body cells are not inherited (weight training); only germline changes pass on.
- **Speciation**: variation + natural selection + geographical isolation → new species (cannot interbreed).
- **Evolution by stages**: complex organs (eye) evolved step by step, each useful; wings in birds/bats/insects are **analogous** (different origin, same function); human hand and dog's forelimb are **homologous** (same structure, different function — common ancestry).
- **Fossils**: preserved remains — deeper = older; carbon dating estimates ages.
- **Evolution should not be equated with progress**: simple life still thrives; humans are not the "peak" — just one branch of the tree of life.

## 10. Light — Reflection and Refraction

### Reflection (mirrors)
- Laws: angle of incidence = angle of reflection; incident ray, normal, reflected ray in one plane.
- **Plane mirror**: virtual, erect, laterally inverted image, same size.
- **Spherical mirrors**: concave (converging) and convex (diverging).
  - Terms: pole, centre of curvature, principal axis, focus, focal length (f = R/2).
  - **Concave mirror** image changes with object position (inverted real images; virtual-erect-magnified when very close — used by dentists).
  - **Convex mirror**: always virtual, erect, diminished — wider field of view (vehicles' rear-view).
- **Mirror formula**: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f (sign convention: distances measured from pole; against incident light = negative).
- **Magnification**: m = h'/h = −v/u (negative = inverted/real).

### Refraction (lenses and glass)
- Light bends when it changes medium. Laws: incident ray, refracted ray and normal in one plane; sin i/sin r = constant (**refractive index**, n = c/v — speed of light in the medium).
- **Glass slab**: parallel shift of the emergent ray.
- **Lenses**: convex (converging) and concave (diverging). Terms: optical centre, two foci.
- **Lens formula**: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f; power P = 1/f (dioptre, D); magnification m = v/u.
- Uses: magnifying glass (object within focus of convex lens), camera, projector, eyes.

## 11. The Human Eye and the Colourful World

### The Eye
- Cornea → iris/pupil (light control) → lens (fine focus — ciliary muscles change thickness = **accommodation**) → retina (rods — dim light; cones — colour) → optic nerve → brain.
- **Near point**: 25 cm; far point: infinity. Cataract: cloudy lens — corrected by surgery.

### Defects of Vision
- **Myopia** (nearsightedness): sees near clearly, far blurred — eyeball too long/lens too curved; image in front of retina; corrected with concave lens.
- **Hypermetropia** (farsightedness): near objects blurred; image behind retina; corrected with convex lens.
- **Presbyopia**: aging lens loses flexibility — bifocal lenses (both defects).

### Dispersion and Scattering
- **Dispersion**: prism splits white light into VIBGYOR — different colours bend differently (violet most).
- **Rainbow**: dispersion + total internal reflection in water droplets, always seen with the sun behind.
- **Atmospheric refraction**: stars twinkle; the sun is visible ~2 min before actual sunrise and after sunset.
- **Tyndall effect**: light beams visible in colloidal/foggy air (smoke, mist).
- **Why the sky is blue**: shorter (blue) wavelengths scatter more (Rayleigh scattering); sun looks reddish at sunrise/sunset because blue is scattered away on the long path.
- **Danger signals are red**: red scatters the least — visible from far away.

## 12. Electricity

### Electric Current and Circuit
- Flow of charge; I = Q/t; unit ampere (A); measured by ammeter (in series).
- Convention: current flows opposite to electron motion (from + to −).

### Potential Difference
- Work done per unit charge: V = W/Q; unit volt; measured by voltmeter (parallel).
- Battery maintains potential difference (EMF).

### Ohm's Law
- At constant temperature: **V = IR**. R = resistance (ohm, Ω). 
- **Resistance** ∝ length, ∝ 1/area, depends on material (resistivity ρ, Ω·m). Metals low ρ; alloys higher (nichrome for heaters); insulators very high.

### Series and Parallel Circuits
- **Series**: Rs = R1 + R2 + …; same current everywhere; used in fairy lights (all go out if one breaks).
- **Parallel**: 1/Rp = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + …; same voltage across branches; independent control (household wiring).
- Equivalent resistance of parallel is less than the smallest.

### Heating Effect of Current
```
H = I²Rt  (joule's law of heating)
```
- Applications: electric heaters, kettles, irons, filament bulbs (tungsten, high melting point), fuses (thin wire melts on excess current — safety device).

### Electric Power
```
P = VI = I²R = V²/R
```
- Unit watt; commercial unit kWh (1 unit = 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J). Appliance ratings (e.g., 220 V, 100 W) let us compute current, resistance and energy used.

## 13. Magnetic Effects of Electric Current

### Magnets Basics
- Magnetic field: region around a magnet; represented by **field lines** — leave N, enter S, never cross; closer lines = stronger field.
- Compass needle aligns with the field; earth behaves like a magnet.

### Electric Current and Magnetism (Oersted)
- A current-carrying wire deflects a compass — current produces a magnetic field.

### Fields Around Conductors
- **Straight wire**: concentric circles; direction by **right-hand thumb rule** (thumb = current, curled fingers = field).
- **Circular loop**: field like a disk through the loop; stronger with more turns.
- **Solenoid**: coil producing a uniform field inside like a bar magnet (electromagnet with soft iron core — cranes, electric bells, MRI).

### Force on a Conductor (Motor Effect)
- Fleming's left-hand rule: forefinger = field, middle finger = current, thumb = force.
- **Electric motor**: converts electrical to mechanical energy — coil in a magnetic field rotates due to force; split-ring commutator reverses current each half-turn for continuous rotation. Used in fans, mixers, pumps.

### Electromagnetic Induction (Faraday)
- Changing magnetic field induces current in a closed coil. Fleming's right-hand rule gives induced current direction.
- **Electric generator**: converts mechanical to electrical energy — coil rotated in a field; **AC generator** (alternating current, reverses direction periodically; in India 50 Hz) and **DC generator** (split-ring commutator).
- Domestic circuits: live (red/brown), neutral (black/blue), earth (green — safety); fuse in series with live; overloading and short-circuit protection.
