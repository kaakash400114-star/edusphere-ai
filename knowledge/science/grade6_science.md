## 1. Food: Where Does It Come From? (Components of Food)

### 1.1 Major Components of Food

Our food contains several nutrients that our body needs for energy, growth, and good health.

| Nutrient       | Main Sources                                  | Function                                      |
|----------------|-----------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| **Carbohydrates** | Rice, wheat, bread, potatoes, sugar, cereals | Main source of **energy** for the body         |
| **Proteins**   | Milk, eggs, fish, meat, pulses (dal), soybean | Needed for **growth** and repair of the body   |
| **Fats**       | Oil, butter, ghee, cheese, nuts               | Store energy, **insulate** the body, protect organs |
| **Vitamins**   | Fruits, vegetables, milk, eggs, fish          | Protect the body from **diseases**, keep skin/eyes healthy |
| **Minerals**   | Green leafy vegetables, milk, salt, cereals   | Needed for **strong bones and teeth**, proper functioning of body |
| **Dietary Fibre (Roughage)** | Whole grains, fruits, vegetables, salads | Helps in **digestion** and bowel movement      |
| **Water**      | Drinking water, fruits, juices, vegetables    | Absorbs nutrients, removes waste, **regulates** body temperature |

### 1.2 Vitamins - Types and Deficiency Diseases

| Vitamin | Sources                      | Deficiency Disease             |
|---------|------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| **Vitamin A** | Milk, carrot, mango, papaya, green leafy vegetables | **Night blindness** (difficulty seeing in dim light) |
| **Vitamin B1 (Thiamine)** | Whole grains, milk, eggs, meat | **Beri-beri** (weakness, nerve damage) |
| **Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid)** | Citrus fruits (orange, lemon), amla, tomato | **Scurvy** (bleeding gums, slow wound healing) |
| **Vitamin D** | Sunlight, milk, fish liver oil, eggs | **Rickets** (soft and weak bones) |

### 1.3 Minerals - Deficiency Diseases

| Mineral         | Sources                      | Deficiency Disease               |
|-----------------|------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Iron**        | Spinach, liver, jaggery, meat | **Anaemia** (tiredness, pale skin) |
| **Iodine**      | Iodised salt, seafood        | **Goitre** (swollen neck)         |
| **Calcium**     | Milk, cheese, eggs, ragi     | Weak bones and teeth               |

### 1.4 Balanced Diet

A **balanced diet** is one that contains all the nutrients in the right proportion. It includes:
- Cereals and grains (carbohydrates)
- Pulses, milk, eggs, meat (proteins)
- Fruits and vegetables (vitamins and minerals)
- Fats and oils (in small amounts)
- Plenty of water

> **Common Mistake:** Students often think fats are completely harmful. Actually, fats are essential - only **excess** fat is harmful. Our body needs some fat for insulation and stored energy.

### 1.5 Food and Materials - Sorting

Food items can be sorted based on:
- **Taste:** Sweet (sugar), sour (lemon), salty (salt), bitter (neem), umami
- **State:** Solid (rice), Liquid (milk), Gas (carbonated drinks have CO2)
- **Source:** Plant-based (wheat, rice) vs Animal-based (milk, eggs)
- **Nutrient content:** Rich in proteins vs rich in carbohydrates vs rich in fats

**Teaching Tip:** Ask students to bring a food packet from home and read the nutrition label - they can identify which nutrients are present and in what amounts.

## 2. Sorting Materials Into Groups

### 2.1 What Are Materials?

**Materials** are the substances of which objects are made. For example, a chair is made of wood, a glass is made of glass.

### 2.2 Properties of Materials

Materials can be sorted based on their properties:

| Property             | Meaning                                      | Examples                         |
|----------------------|----------------------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| **Appearance**       | Shiny or dull                                | Gold (shiny), chalk (dull)       |
| **Hardness**         | Can be scratched easily or not              | Diamond (very hard), chalk (soft) |
| **Solubility**       | Dissolves in water or not                   | Salt (soluble), sand (insoluble) |
| **Transparency**     | Allows light to pass through or not         | Glass (transparent), wood (opaque), frosted glass (translucent) |
| **Density**          | Heavy or light for its size                  | Iron (dense), cotton (light)      |
| **Conductivity**     | Conducts heat/electricity or not            | Copper (conductor), plastic (insulator) |
| **Malleability**     | Can be hammered into sheets                | Gold, aluminium (malleable)       |
| **Ductility**        | Can be drawn into wires                     | Copper, aluminium (ductile)       |

### 2.3 Metals and Non-Metals

**Metals:**
- Hard, shiny (lustrous), malleable, ductile
- Good conductors of heat and electricity
- Examples: Iron, copper, gold, silver, aluminium, zinc

**Non-Metals:**
- Generally soft, non-lustrous, brittle
- Poor conductors of heat and electricity
- Examples: Carbon (coal), sulphur, oxygen, nitrogen, phosphorus

> **Common Mistake:** Students sometimes think all shiny things are metals. Diamond is shiny but is a non-metal (it is a form of carbon). Similarly, mercury is a metal but it is a liquid at room temperature!

### 2.4 Sorting Techniques

Materials can be sorted by:
- **Colour** (red, blue, green)
- **Shape** (round, square, flat)
- **Size** (large, small)
- **Texture** (rough, smooth)
- **Material** (wood, plastic, metal)

**Teaching Tip:** Give students a mixed pile of objects (coins, erasers, pebbles, leaves) and ask them to sort using different criteria. This builds observation and classification skills.

## 3. Fibre to Fabric

### 3.1 What Is a Fibre?

A **fibre** is a thin, thread-like strand from which fabrics (cloth) are made.

### 3.2 Types of Fibres

| Classification       | Type             | Source                    | Examples                                    |
|----------------------|------------------|---------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| **Natural fibres**   | Plant fibres     | Plants                    | Cotton, jute, flax, hemp                   |
|                      | Animal fibres    | Animals                   | Wool (from sheep, goat), Silk (from silkworm) |
| **Synthetic fibres** | Man-made         | Chemical processes        | Nylon, polyester, acrylic, rayon            |

### 3.3 Plant Fibres in Detail

**Cotton:**
- Obtained from the **cotton plant** (fruit = cotton bolls)
- Grows well in black, alluvial soil
- Major producing states in India: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu
- Used for: clothes, bedsheets, towels, surgical cotton

**Jute:**
- Obtained from the **stem of the jute plant**
- Grows in warm, humid climate with loamy soil
- Major producing state in India: West Bengal, Assam
- Used for: gunny bags, ropes, carpets, mats

### 3.4 Animal Fibres in Detail

**Wool:**
- Comes from **sheep, goat, yak, camel** (also rabbit - angora wool)
- Sheep rearing for wool is called **sheep farming**
- The process: shearing → scouring (washing) → sorting → spinning → weaving/knitting

**Silk:**
- Comes from **silkworms** (caterpillars of the silk moth, *Bombyx mori*)
- Discovered in **China** around 3500 BC
- Process: silkworms feed on mulberry leaves → spin cocoons → cocoons boiled → silk threads unwound → weaving
- **Sericulture** = rearing of silkworms for silk production

### 3.5 Spinning, Weaving, and Knitting

| Process      | Description                                                         |
|--------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Spinning** | Converting fibres into yarn (done on a charkha or spinning wheel)  |
| **Weaving**  | Making fabric by arranging two sets of yarns (warp & weft) on a loom |
| **Knitting** | Making fabric by interlocking loops of a single yarn (sweaters, socks) |

### 3.6 Natural vs Synthetic - Comparison

| Feature            | Natural Fibres            | Synthetic Fibres              |
|--------------------|---------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Source             | Plants/Animals            | Petrochemicals                |
| Comfort            | More comfortable, breathable | Less breathable              |
| Durability         | Less durable              | Very durable                  |
| Wrinkle resistance | Wrinkle easily            | Wrinkle-resistant             |
| Cost               | Generally more expensive  | Cheaper                       |
| Environment        | Biodegradable             | Non-biodegradable             |
| Water absorption   | Absorb water              | Do not absorb water easily    |

> **Common Mistake:** Rayon is NOT a natural fibre - it is made from wood pulp but through a chemical process, so it is a **semi-synthetic** fibre (classified under synthetic).

**Teaching Tip:** Burn different fabric samples (with supervision). Natural fibres burn with a smell of burning hair/paper; synthetic fibres melt and form hard beads.

## 4. Separation of Mixtures

### 4.1 Why Do We Need Separation?

We need to separate mixtures to:
- Remove harmful or useless substances (e.g., removing pebbles from rice)
- Obtain a useful component (e.g., obtaining butter from curd)
- Remove impurities to make food safe (e.g., filtering water)

### 4.2 Methods of Separation

#### Handpicking
- **Used when:** Components are large, visible, and differ in colour/shape
- **Example:** Removing stones from rice, picking rotten fruits from a basket
- **Principle:** Manual separation by visual inspection

#### Threshing
- **Used when:** Separating grain from the stalk
- **Example:** Separating wheat grains from the stalk after harvesting
- **Methods:** By hand, using cattle, or with a machine (combine harvester)

#### Winnowing
- **Used when:** Separating heavier and lighter components using wind
- **Example:** Separating husk from wheat grains
- **Principle:** Wind blows away the lighter husk; heavier grains fall down

#### Sieving
- **Used when:** Components are of different sizes
- **Example:** Separating fine flour from bran (choker); separating sand from gravel
- **Principle:** Smaller particles pass through the sieve holes, larger ones stay on top

#### Sedimentation and Decantation
- **Sedimentation:** Heavier particles settle at the bottom when water is added (e.g., mud settles in water)
- **Decantation:** Pouring out the clear liquid from the top without disturbing the sediment
- **Example:** Cleaning muddy water at home, separating oil and water

#### Filtration
- **Used when:** Separating an insoluble solid from a liquid using a filter
- **Example:** Separating tea leaves from tea, filtering muddy water using filter paper or cloth
- **Principle:** The filter allows the liquid (filtrate) to pass but traps the solid (residue)

#### Evaporation
- **Used when:** Recovering a soluble solid dissolved in a liquid by evaporating the liquid
- **Example:** Obtaining common salt from seawater
- **Principle:** Water evaporates, leaving the solid behind
- **Note:** Salt obtained this way may contain impurities - this is **not crystallisation** (which gives purer crystals)

#### Condensation
- **Used when:** Converting a gas/vapour back to liquid
- **Example:** Water droplets on the outside of a cold glass; collecting distilled water

### 4.3 Choosing the Right Method

| Situation                                    | Method                        |
|----------------------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Remove stones from pulses                    | Handpicking                   |
| Separate grain from husk                      | Winnowing                     |
| Separate bran from flour                      | Sieving                       |
| Get salt from seawater                        | Evaporation                   |
| Remove tea leaves from liquid tea             | Filtration                    |
| Clean muddy water                             | Sedimentation + Decantation   |
| Separate two immiscible liquids (oil + water) | Decantation                   |

> **Common Mistake:** Students confuse filtration and evaporation. **Filtration** separates an *insoluble* solid from liquid (tea leaves). **Evaporation** separates a *soluble* solid from liquid (salt from water).

**Teaching Tip:** Set up a mini separation station in class with mixtures and let students choose and apply the correct method for each.

## 5. Body Movements (Body Movements and Joints)

### 5.1 The Human Skeleton

The human skeleton is made up of **206 bones** in adults. It provides:
- **Shape** and support to the body
- **Protection** to internal organs (skull protects brain, ribcage protects heart and lungs)
- **Movement** (bones and muscles work together)
- **Attachment** for muscles

### 5.2 Types of Skeletons

| Type          | Description                                     | Examples                           |
|---------------|-------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| **Endoskeleton** | Skeleton inside the body                       | Humans, dogs, fish, birds          |
| **Exoskeleton** | Hard covering outside the body                | Cockroach, crab, snail (shell)     |
| **Hydrostatic skeleton** | Body supported by fluid pressure            | Earthworm, jellyfish               |

### 5.3 Parts of the Human Skeleton

- **Skull** - protects the brain (cranium + facial bones)
- **Ribcage (Ribs)** - protects the heart and lungs (12 pairs)
- **Backbone (Vertebral column / Spine)** - supports the body, protects the spinal cord
- **Pelvis** - supports the lower body
- **Limbs** - arms (upper arm + forearm + hand) and legs (thigh + shin + foot)
- **Shoulder bones** (Pectoral girdle) - collarbone (clavicle) and shoulder blade (scapula)
- **Hip bones** (Pelvic girdle) - supports the legs

### 5.4 Joints - Where Bones Meet

A **joint** is the place where two or more bones meet.

| Type of Joint           | Movement              | Examples                                |
|-------------------------|-----------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| **Ball and socket joint** | Rotational movement in all directions | Shoulder joint, hip joint               |
| **Hinge joint**          | Movement in one direction (like a door hinge) | Elbow, knee, fingers                   |
| **Pivot joint**          | Rotation around a single axis | Neck (skull on backbone), forearm       |
| **Fixed (Immovable) joint** | No movement      | Joints between skull bones, joints in upper jaw |
| **Gliding joint**        | Sliding movement     | Joints between vertebrae, wrist bones   |

### 5.5 How Do Muscles Help in Movement?

- Muscles are attached to bones by **tendons**
- Muscles work in **pairs** - when one contracts (shortens), the other relaxes
- Example: Bending the arm - the bicep contracts, tricep relaxes; straightening - tricep contracts, bicep relaxes
- These pairs are called **antagonistic muscle pairs**

### 5.6 Movement in Other Animals

| Animal          | Type of Movement                    | Body Part Used         |
|-----------------|-------------------------------------|------------------------|
| Earthworm       | Crawling (alternate extension-compression of segments) | Muscles + bristles (setae) |
| Snail           | Crawling using a muscular **foot** and mucus | Muscular foot          |
| Cockroach       | Walking, flying, crawling           | 3 pairs of legs + 2 pairs of wings |
| Fish            | Swimming                            | Fins and tail          |
| Birds           | Flying                              | Wings and feathers     |
| Snake           | Slithering                          | Muscles and scales     |

> **Common Mistake:** Students think joints are where muscles attach. Joints are where **bones** meet. Muscles attach to bones via tendons.

**Teaching Tip:** Have students move their own arms and legs and feel the muscles contracting and relaxing. They can observe ball-and-socket vs hinge joints by rotating their shoulders vs bending elbows.

## 6. The Living Organisms and Their Surroundings (Living vs Non-Living)

### 6.1 Characteristics of Living Things

Living organisms show the following characteristics:

1. **Growth:** All living things grow over time. (Plants grow throughout life; animals stop growing at maturity.)
2. **Movement:** Living things can move. (Plants show movement - leaves closing, roots growing toward water - though they don't move from place to place.)
3. **Reproduction:** Living things produce young ones of their own kind.
4. **Respiration:** Living things breathe - they take in oxygen and give out carbon dioxide. (Plants respire too!)
5. **Response to Stimuli:** Living things respond to changes in their surroundings. (Touch-me-not plant folds leaves when touched.)
6. **Excretion:** Living things remove waste products from their body.
7. **Need for Food/Nutrition:** Living things need food for energy and growth.

### 6.2 Living vs Non-Living

| Feature           | Living                           | Non-Living                        |
|-------------------|----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Growth            | Yes                              | No (except crystals)              |
| Movement          | Yes                              | No (though air/water moves)       |
| Reproduction      | Yes                              | No                                |
| Respiration       | Yes                              | No                                |
| Response          | Yes                              | No                                |
| Excretion         | Yes                              | No                                |
| Life cycle        | Yes (birth → growth → death)    | No                                |
| Cellular structure| Made of cells                    | No cells                          |

### 6.3 Habitat and Adaptation

- **Habitat:** The natural home of a living organism where it lives, grows, and reproduces.
  - **Terrestrial habitat:** Land (forests, deserts, mountains, grasslands)
  - **Aquatic habitat:** Water (freshwater - ponds, lakes, rivers; marine - oceans, seas)
- **Adaptation:** The special features or habits that help an organism survive in its habitat.

**Examples of adaptations:**
- **Desert plants:** Leaves modified into spines (cactus) to reduce water loss; thick stems store water; deep roots
- **Aquatic plants:** Leaves floating or submerged; flexible stems; air spaces in tissues to float
- **Mountain animals:** Thick fur (snow leopard), strong hooves for climbing
- **Camel (desert):** Hump stores fat; can go without water for days; wide hooves for walking on sand

### 6.4 Biotic vs Abiotic Components

- **Biotic:** Living things in a habitat (plants, animals, microorganisms)
- **Abiotic:** Non-living things in a habitat (air, water, soil, sunlight, temperature, humidity)

> **Common Mistake:** Students often think only animals respire. ALL living things respire, including plants. Respiration happens 24 hours a day, unlike photosynthesis which only occurs in sunlight.

**Teaching Tip:** Take students on a nature walk and ask them to list biotic and abiotic components they observe. Discuss how each organism is adapted to its surroundings.

## 7. Getting to Know Plants (Parts of a Plant and Their Functions)

### 7.1 Parts of a Plant

A typical plant has the following parts:

#### 🌱 Root
- Grows **underground** (in most plants)
- **Functions:**
  - Anchors the plant firmly in the soil
  - Absorbs **water and minerals** from the soil
  - Stores food in some plants (carrot, radish, sweet potato)
- **Types:**
  - **Taproot:** One main root with smaller lateral roots (mustard, carrot, gram)
  - **Fibrous root:** Many thin, branching roots of similar size (wheat, rice, maize, grass)
- **Root modifications:** Carrot, radish, beetroot store food; prop roots in banyan tree provide support

#### 🌿 Stem
- Grows **above ground** (in most plants)
- **Functions:**
  - Supports the plant and holds leaves, flowers, fruits
  - Carries **water and minerals** from roots to leaves (through xylem)
  - Carries **food** from leaves to other parts (through phloem)
  - Sometimes stores food (potato is a modified underground stem)
- **Stem modifications:**
  - Potato - underground stem storing food (NOT a root!)
  - Tendrils in passionflower - modified stem for climbing
  - Cactus stem - thick, green, stores water, does photosynthesis

#### 🍃 Leaf
- **Functions:**
  - **Photosynthesis:** Makes food for the plant using sunlight, CO2, and water (produces glucose + oxygen)
  - **Transpiration:** Loss of excess water through tiny pores called **stomata**
  - **Gas exchange:** Stomata take in CO2 and release O2
- **Parts of a leaf:**
  - **Petiole** - stalk that attaches leaf to stem
  - **Lamina (leaf blade)** - the broad, flat, green part
  - **Midrib** - the central vein
  - **Veins** - network of veins for transport
- **Types of leaves:**
  - **Simple leaf:** Single blade (mango, guava)
  - **Compound leaf:** Blade divided into leaflets (neem, rose, tamarind)
- **Venation:**
  - **Parallel venation:** Veins run parallel (grass, wheat, rice, banana) - found in plants with **fibrous roots**
  - **Reticulate venation:** Veins form a network (mango, peepal, rose) - found in plants with **taproots**

#### 🌸 Flower
- **Function:** Reproductive part of the plant - produces seeds and fruits after pollination and fertilisation
- **Parts of a flower:**
  - **Sepals:** Green leaf-like parts, protect the flower bud
  - **Petals:** Colourful parts that attract insects for pollination
  - **Stamens (Male part):** Anther (produces pollen) + Filament (stalk)
  - **Pistil (Female part):** Stigma (receives pollen) + Style (tube) + Ovary (contains ovules → become seeds)
  - **Unisexual flower:** Has only stamens OR only pistil (papaya, watermelon)
  - **Bisexual flower:** Has both stamens and pistil (mustard, hibiscus, lily)

### 7.2 Types of Plants

| Type            | Characteristics                              | Examples                       |
|-----------------|----------------------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| **Herbs**       | Short, soft green stems, no woody parts      | Wheat, rice, mint, grass       |
| **Shrubs**      | Medium-sized, woody stems, branch near ground | Rose, hibiscus, tulsi, cotton  |
| **Trees**       | Tall, thick woody stems, branch high up       | Mango, neem, banyan, peepal     |
| **Creepers**    | Grow along the ground, weak stems             | Pumpkin, watermelon, sweet potato |
| **Climbers**    | Climb up support with tendrils               | Grape vine, bitter gourd, pea   |

### 7.3 Roots, Stems, and Leaves - Quick Matching

| Root Type         | Venation Type     | Plant Examples        |
|--------------------|-------------------|-----------------------|
| Taproot            | Reticulate        | Mustard, carrot, mango|
| Fibrous root       | Parallel          | Wheat, rice, grass    |

> **Common Mistake:** Students often think potato is a root. Potato is a **modified underground stem**. Similarly, sweet potato is a modified root. Onion is a modified stem (bulb), not a root.

**Teaching Tip:** Bring real plants (or parts) to class. Let students observe roots, stems, leaves, and flowers directly. Pluck a leaf and trace its veins with a pen to understand venation patterns.

## 9. Water

### 9.1 Importance of Water

Water is essential for all living organisms:
- Drinking - all animals and plants need water to survive
- Cooking food
- Washing, cleaning, bathing
- Agriculture - irrigation of crops
- Industrial use - factories need water
- Transport - ships and boats travel on water
- Generating electricity - hydroelectric power

### 9.2 Sources of Water

| Source              | Description                                                   |
|---------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Rain**            | Primary source of all fresh water                              |
| **Rivers & Lakes**  | Surface water - used for drinking, irrigation, transport      |
| **Wells & Tube wells** | Groundwater drawn through wells                              |
| **Ponds & Tanks**   | Small bodies of surface water                                  |
| **Glaciers**        | Frozen freshwater - source of many rivers                     |
| **Oceans & Seas**   | Saltwater - NOT directly usable for drinking                  |

### 9.3 The Water Cycle

1. The Sun heats water in oceans, rivers, lakes → water **evaporates**
2. Water vapour rises, cools, and **condenses** to form tiny water droplets → forms **clouds**
3. When droplets become heavy, they fall as **rain** (precipitation)
4. Rainwater flows into rivers, seeps into ground, or flows back to oceans
5. The cycle **repeats continuously**

> **Key Point:** The amount of water on Earth remains roughly the same - water is neither created nor destroyed in the water cycle. This is a natural form of recycling.

### 9.4 Forms of Water

- **Solid:** Ice, snow, glacier
- **Liquid:** Water (river, lake, ocean, tap water)
- **Gas (Water vapour):** Invisible gas in air; steam when boiled

### 9.5 Water Conservation - Why and How

**Why conserve water?**
- Only about **3%** of Earth's water is freshwater, and much of that is frozen in glaciers
- Groundwater levels are dropping in many areas
- Growing population means more water demand
- Wastewater pollutes rivers and lakes

**How to conserve water:**
- Turn off taps while brushing teeth
- Repair leaking taps and pipes immediately
- Use water from washing vegetables/rice for watering plants
- Harvest rainwater (**rainwater harvesting**) - collect rainwater on rooftops, store in tanks or recharge groundwater
- Avoid wasting water during bathing, washing
- Use drip irrigation in agriculture
- Treat and reuse wastewater

> **Common Mistake:** Students think the water cycle means we will never run out of water. While the cycle does recycle water, we CAN run out of **usable, clean freshwater** in specific locations. Pollution and overuse can make water unavailable.

**Teaching Tip:** Conduct a "water audit" - ask students to measure how much water their family uses in a day and identify 3 ways to reduce it.

## 10. Air

### 10.1 Is Air Present Around Us?

Yes! Air is present everywhere around us, even though we cannot see it. Evidence:
- A glass inverted in water - water does not fill it completely because air is trapped
- Blowing on a pinwheel makes it rotate
- Leaves rustle when the wind blows

### 10.2 Composition of Air

| Component       | Percentage (approx.) | Notes                                       |
|-----------------|----------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| **Nitrogen**    | 78%                  | Does not support burning or breathing by itself |
| **Oxygen**      | 21%                  | Essential for respiration and burning       |
| **Carbon Dioxide (CO2)** | 0.03-0.04%     | Used by plants for photosynthesis           |
| **Argon & other gases** | ~0.9%          | Inert gases                                 |
| **Water Vapour** | Variable (0-4%)     | More in humid areas, less in dry areas      |
| **Dust particles** | Variable            | More near factories, construction sites    |

> **Common Mistake:** Students think CO2 is a large part of air. It is actually only about **0.04%** - oxygen (21%) and nitrogen (78%) make up nearly all of the air.

### 10.3 Properties of Air

1. **Air occupies space:** Compress a syringe with its opening closed - the air inside prevents full compression.
2. **Air has weight:** Inflate two balloons, balance them on a stick, then deflate one - the deflated side rises, proving air has weight.
3. **Air exerts pressure:** Air pressure helps us drink through a straw; atmospheric pressure presses on the surface of a liquid.
4. **Air is invisible:** We cannot see air, but we can feel it (wind) and observe its effects.
5. **Air can be compressed:** tyres, balloons, syringes - air fills them and can be squeezed.
6. **Air supports burning:** A candle in a closed jar goes out when it uses up all the oxygen.

### 10.4 Uses of Air

- **Breathing:** All living organisms need oxygen from air for respiration
- **Burning (Combustion):** Oxygen supports burning
- **Photosynthesis:** Plants use CO2 from air to make food
- **Wind energy:** Moving air (wind) turns windmills to generate electricity
- **Transport:** Birds and aircraft fly through air; sailboats use wind
- **Sound:** Sound needs a medium to travel - air carries sound waves
- **Sports:** Filling balls, tyres with air

### 10.5 Oxygen vs Carbon Dioxide - The Balance

- Animals and humans **take in O2 and release CO2** (respiration)
- Plants **take in CO2 and release O2** (photosynthesis, during daytime)
- This maintains the **oxygen-carbon dioxide balance** in the atmosphere
- **Deforestation** and **pollution** disturb this balance

> **Common Mistake:** Students think plants only release oxygen. Plants also **respire** 24 hours a day (taking in O2 and releasing CO2). Photosynthesis (releasing O2) happens only during the day when sunlight is available. The net effect during the day is O2 release.

**Teaching Tip:** Set up an experiment: place a small candle in a glass, cover it, and time how long it burns. Then repeat with a larger glass. Students will see that more air = longer burning, demonstrating that air supports combustion.

## 11. Garbage In, Garbage Out (Waste Management)

### 11.1 What Is Garbage?

**Garbage (waste)** refers to unwanted materials that we discard. It includes:
- Kitchen waste (peels, leftover food)
- Paper waste
- Plastic waste (bags, bottles, packaging)
- Glass waste
- Metal waste
- Garden waste (leaves, twigs)
- Medical waste (in hospitals)

### 11.2 Types of Waste

| Type                  | Description                                     | Examples                              | Disposal                          |
|-----------------------|--------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Biodegradable**     | Can be broken down by microorganisms naturally   | Fruit peels, vegetable scraps, paper, cotton, wood | Composting, landfill             |
| **Non-biodegradable** | Cannot be broken down naturally; persists for years | Plastic bags, bottles, glass, metal, aluminium foil | Recycling, incineration, landfill |

### 11.3 Problems Due to Garbage

1. **Environmental pollution:** Plastic in soil, water bodies
2. **Health hazards:** Rotting garbage attracts flies, mosquitoes → spreads diseases (malaria, dengue, cholera)
3. **Water pollution:** Garbage dumped in rivers and lakes contaminates water
4. **Soil pollution:** Plastics and chemicals in soil make it infertile
5. **Air pollution:** Burning garbage releases harmful gases (CO, CO2, toxic fumes)
6. **Blockage of drains:** Plastic bags and wrappers clog drains, causing flooding

### 11.4 Methods of Garbage Disposal

#### Composting
- Decomposing biodegradable waste into **manure** (compost)
- Microorganisms (bacteria, fungi) break down organic waste
- Very useful for kitchen waste and garden waste
- Compost is rich in nutrients and used as **organic fertiliser**

#### Landfill
- Waste is buried in low-lying areas and covered with soil
- Common method in cities for large-scale waste

#### Recycling
- Converting waste materials into new products
- Paper, glass, metal, and some plastics can be recycled
- Reduces the amount of waste going to landfills
- Example: Waste paper → recycled paper; PET bottles → polyester fibre for clothes

#### Incineration (Burning)
- Waste is burned at high temperatures
- Reduces volume of waste
- **Disadvantage:** Releases harmful gases, causes air pollution
- Should NOT be done at home or in open areas

#### Vermicomposting
- Using **earthworms** to decompose organic waste
- Earthworms eat the waste and their excreta (vermicompost) is excellent fertiliser
- Red wigglers (*Eisenia fetida*) are commonly used

### 11.5 Reduce, Reuse, Recycle (The 3 R's)

| R              | Meaning                              | Examples                                     |
|----------------|--------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| **Reduce**     | Use less, minimise waste             | Avoid extra packaging; use both sides of paper |
| **Reuse**      | Use items again instead of throwing away | Carry cloth bags; repair old items; donate   |
| **Recycle**    | Process waste into new products     | Recycle paper, glass, metal, plastic          |

### 11.6 Things We Should Do

- **Segregate waste** at home: biodegradable in one bin, non-biodegradable in another
- **Avoid single-use plastics** - carry cloth/jute bags
- **Do not burn garbage** - especially plastics (releases toxic fumes)
- **Compost kitchen waste** - even at home
- **Don't litter** - use dustbins and waste bins
- **Participate in recycling drives** at school and community
- **Reduce food waste** - take only as much as you can eat
- **Use paper wisely** - recycle old notebooks, use both sides

### 11.7 Paper and Plastic - Key Facts

|              | Paper                            | Plastic                                    |
|--------------|----------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| Biodegradable | ✅ Yes (decomposes in 2-5 weeks) | ❌ No (takes 100s of years)                 |
| Recyclable   | ✅ Yes                            | ✅ Yes (but not all types)                   |
| Source       | Wood (trees)                     | Petrochemicals (fossil fuels)               |
| Environment  | Trees are cut                    | Persists forever, harms animals             |

> **Common Mistake:** Students think all waste can be burned safely. Burning plastic releases extremely toxic gases including dioxins, which are harmful to health and the environment. Plastic should be **recycled**, not burned.

**Teaching Tip:** Start a composting project in the school garden. Have students collect kitchen waste, layer it with dry leaves, and observe decomposition over weeks. Also conduct a waste audit of the classroom - sort a day's waste into biodegradable and non-biodegradable piles.

## Common Mistakes Across All Chapters

| Mistake                                          | Correction                                                    |
|--------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| Fats are harmful                                 | Fats are essential in small amounts; only excess is harmful   |
| All shiny things are metals                      | Diamond is shiny but non-metal; mercury is metal but liquid   |
| Potato is a root                                 | Potato is a modified underground stem                          |
| Rayon is a natural fibre                          | Rayon is semi-synthetic (chemically processed wood pulp)      |
| Plants don't respire                              | Plants respire 24 hours a day; they photosynthesise in daylight |
| CO2 is a large part of air                       | CO2 is only ~0.04% of air                                    |
| All waste can be burned safely                   | Burning plastic releases toxic dioxins                        |
| Filtration and evaporation are the same          | Filtration = insoluble solid; Evaporation = soluble solid     |
| Roots absorb sunlight                            | Leaves absorb sunlight for photosynthesis; roots absorb water |
| Joints are where muscles attach                  | Joints are where bones meet; muscles attach via tendons        |
