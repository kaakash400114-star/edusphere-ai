# Foundation Mathematics (Pre-KG)


## Counting 1 to 10
Children learn to count one to ten by touching and moving things. The teacher gives small blocks or toys and asks children to count each one out loud. We sing counting songs with claps and jumps to make it fun. Playing games like "how many steps?" helps children count actions. Children see the numbers on cards while they touch objects. The teacher shows them how one block is one and two blocks are two. We count our fingers and toes, and toys, and snacks, again and again. They learn numbers by seeing them, saying them, and feeling them.

## Shapes all around us
Tiny children learn to see shapes everywhere, like circles on a clock and squares on a window. The teacher shows them real things, like a round ball and a block. They play a game by hiding shapes and finding them together. The children clap and stomp their feet in circles. Singing a song about shapes makes it fun to learn. They can also use their hands to make a triangle or a square.

## Big and small, more and less
Little friends learn about big and small things. They also learn about more and less. We use real toys like big cars and small cars. We line them up to see which has more. We sing songs about big and small animals, like a big lion and a small mouse. We play games with blocks, building a big tower and a small tower. Children hold their hands wide for big and pinch fingers for small. We use simple actions to show the differences. They love to point out big and small things all around the room.

## Patterns: what comes next?
Tiny children learn to see patterns and guess what comes next. They use toys like blocks or beads to make patterns, such as red-blue-red-blue. The teacher shows them how to clap patterns like slow-slow-fast-slow-slow-fast. We sing songs with repeating words and actions, like "Head, Shoulders, Knees, and Toes." We play games by lining up animals and asking, "Who comes next?" The children use hands and bodies to copy patterns. This makes learning fun and helps them see order in things. They love to show you their pattern skills!

## Sorting into groups
Tiny children learn to sort by looking at things and putting them into groups. A teacher gives them fun things like colorful buttons or toy animals. The teacher says, "Can you put all the red things together?" and helps the children find them. The teacher can sing a sorting song like, "Sort, sort, put them in a pile, sort them by color or shape, with a smile!" Children can sort by color, shape, or size by just moving the things. A teacher can turn it into a game, like "Can the teddy bears find their friends?" to make it fun and exciting.

## Positions: up, down, inside, outside
Children learn words like up, down, inside, and outside. They learn up means high and down means low. Inside means in something and outside means not in. To teach this, a teacher uses a box and a ball. The teacher puts the ball on the box and says, "The ball is up!" The teacher puts the ball under the box and says, "The ball is down!" The teacher puts the ball in the box and says, "The ball is inside!" The teacher puts the ball next to the box and says, "The ball is outside!" We also sing a song and play games moving our bodies up and down, and going in and out of a circle.

## Full and empty, heavy and light
Children learn about full and empty by looking at boxes and cups. A teacher shows them a box with toys in it and says, "The box is full!" Then she takes all the toys out and says, "Now the box is empty!" They learn about heavy and light by holding different objects. A child holds a big book and says, "It is heavy!" Then they hold a feather and say, "It is light!" The teacher sings a song: "Heavy goes down, light goes up!" The children play a game where they put heavy blocks in one basket and light feathers in another.
