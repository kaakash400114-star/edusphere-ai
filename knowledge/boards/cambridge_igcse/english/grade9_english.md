# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 9 English

## Developing Critical Reading Skills
Students learn to analyse non-fiction and media texts, focusing on language, purpose, and audience. They examine how writers use tone, structure, and rhetorical devices to present arguments and influence readers. Key techniques include identifying bias, evaluating evidence, and distinguishing between fact and opinion. For example, a student might deconstruct a newspaper editorial, pinpointing its use of emotive language and selective data to persuade. They practice comparing and contrasting texts from different sources, such as an official report and a blog post, to assess differing perspectives on a single issue.

## Advanced Writing for Impact
Students master the craft of constructing sophisticated written arguments for different purposes and audiences. They develop skills in crafting a clear thesis, structuring paragraphs with topic sentences and evidence, and using persuasive techniques. The focus is on coherence and cohesion, employing transitional phrases and logical connectors to guide the reader. For instance, a student might write a formal letter to a local council, using objective language and statistical evidence to support a proposal for a new community facility, ensuring the tone is appropriately formal and persuasive.

## In-depth Poetry and Prose Analysis
Students engage in detailed analysis of complex literary texts, including poetry, prose, and drama. They explore themes, characterisation, narrative voice, and the use of symbolism, metaphor, and imagery. Students learn to support interpretations with specific textual evidence and analyse the effect of the writer's choices on the reader. For example, they might analyse how the imagery in a poem by Carol Ann Duffy reflects themes of memory and loss, or how characterisation in a George Orwell short story creates social commentary.

## Structuring and Developing Extended Responses
This section focuses on planning and organising extended written responses, particularly for exam essays. Students learn the P.E.E. (Point, Evidence, Explanation) structure to build well-developed paragraphs. They practice outlining arguments before writing and ensuring a balanced conclusion that synthesises their main points. For example, in an essay comparing two poems, a student will plan their points of comparison, select relevant quotes to support each point, and develop a structured response that addresses the prompt directly and thoroughly.

## Mastery of Grammar and Stylistic Devices
Students refine their command of Standard English to achieve precision and variety in their writing. This includes complex sentence structures, correct punctuation (particularly commas, semicolons, and apostrophes), and the appropriate use of vocabulary. They actively incorporate advanced stylistic devices such as synecdoche, antithesis, and irony to enhance their work. For instance, a student would distinguish between using 'it's' for contraction and 'its' for possession, and could use anaphora in a speech to add emphasis and rhythm to their argument.

## Speaking and Listening for Academic and Professional Contexts
Students develop formal speaking and listening skills, preparing and delivering structured presentations. They focus on clear enunciation, appropriate body language, and managing question-and-answer sessions. Listening skills involve analysing spoken language for purpose, stance, and rhetorical devices. For example, a student might deliver a persuasive presentation on a global issue like climate change, using visual aids and notes to support their argument, and then respond to questions with well-considered and relevant answers.
