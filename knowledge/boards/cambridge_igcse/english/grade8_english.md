# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 8 English

## Unseen Prose Comprehension
Students will learn to read and interpret unseen fiction and non-fiction texts. They will identify explicit information and infer implicit meaning based on contextual clues. Teaching focuses on summarising main ideas and themes, analysing the writer's use of language and structure, and evaluating the text's effectiveness. Students practise extracting relevant details to support answers, distinguishing fact from opinion, and explaining the effect of literary devices such as metaphor, simile, and personification.

## Transactional Writing for a Global Audience
This section covers writing for practical, real-world purposes. Students learn to format letters, emails, and reports with appropriate tone, register, and structure. They will compose clear, concise, and coherent texts to achieve a specific objective, such as making a complaint, requesting information, or applying for a position. Instruction includes using persuasive language and technical vocabulary correctly, ensuring the writing is audience-appropriate, structuring arguments logically, and proofreading for accuracy in spelling, punctuation, and grammar.

## Shakespearean Drama and Character Analysis
Students will study a prescribed play, such as 'Much Ado About Nothing' or 'The Tempest', to understand its plot, themes of power and love, and historical context. They will analyse the motivations, conflicts, and development of key characters like Beatrice, Benedick, or Prospero. The teaching explores the use of soliloquy, dramatic irony, and poetic language. Students learn to support their interpretations with evidence from the text and discuss how the characters and situations reflect universal human experiences.

## Poetry from Different Cultures
This unit involves analysing a range of poems from diverse international traditions. Students will examine techniques such as imagery, symbolism, and structure to explore themes of identity, conflict, and social justice. They learn to compare and contrast how different poets use language to convey meaning and emotion. Students practise annotating texts for figurative language and connotation, identifying the poet's purpose and intended audience, and formulating structured arguments about a poem's deeper social or cultural significance.

## Grammar and Stylistic Devices
Students will learn to identify and use advanced grammatical structures, including complex sentences with subordinate clauses, varied punctuation for effect, and active/passive voice. They will apply a metalanguage of literary devices, such as alliteration, assonance, onomatopoeia, and hyperbole, to analyse and enhance their own writing. Instruction focuses on achieving linguistic precision and stylistic flair, selecting vocabulary for specific impact, and ensuring grammatical correctness to create clear and sophisticated written expression.

## Structured Argument and Discursive Writing
Students will learn to plan and write balanced arguments on a range of global issues, such as technology in education or environmental policy. They will formulate a clear thesis statement, use relevant evidence and examples to support claims, and acknowledge opposing viewpoints respectfully. Teaching includes logical sequencing of ideas using discourse markers, developing reasoned explanations, and concluding with a persuasive summary that reinforces the argument's validity while acknowledging its complexity.
