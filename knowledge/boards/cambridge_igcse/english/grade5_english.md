# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 5 English

## Phonics and Spelling Patterns
Students learn advanced consonant and vowel digraphs to decode and encode complex words. They explore common letter clusters like 'tch', 'dge', and 'tion', identifying them in words like 'catch', 'fudge', and 'station'. Students are taught to use prefixes ('un-', 're-', 'dis-') and suffixes ('-ful', '-less', '-ment') to build new words, applying these rules to correctly spell words like 'unhappy', 'rebuild', and 'enjoyment'. They are expected to recognize these patterns within reading texts and apply them accurately in their independent writing.

## Grammar and Sentence Structure
Students focus on expanding sentences to add detail and interest. They learn to use coordinating conjunctions ('for', 'and', 'nor', 'but', 'or', 'yet', 'so') to join two independent clauses, as in 'I wanted to play outside, but it started to rain'. The curriculum introduces the use of adverbs to describe verbs (e.g., 'The dog ran quickly') and adjectives to describe nouns (e.g., 'The fluffy cat slept'). Students are also taught to form and use the past, present, and future tenses correctly in their writing.

## Reading Comprehension and Vocabulary
Students develop skills to identify the main idea and supporting details in a variety of texts, including fiction and non-fiction. They learn to sequence events in a story logically and to make simple inferences about a character's feelings or motivations. Vocabulary development is a key focus, with students learning to identify and understand the meaning of words with prefixes and suffixes (e.g., 'unhappy', 'preheat'). They are encouraged to use a dictionary to check the meaning of unfamiliar words encountered during reading.

## Writing for Different Purposes
Students learn to write for a range of audiences and purposes. For informative writing, they structure simple paragraphs around a central idea with a topic sentence, using factual details from research. In narrative writing, they plan a story with a clear beginning, middle, and end, including characters and a setting. They also practice basic transactional writing, such as composing a simple email or a short, polite letter to a family friend, ensuring they include the correct layout and greeting.

## Speaking and Listening Skills
Students learn to listen attentively to others and respond appropriately, demonstrating they have understood what has been said. In group discussions, they practice taking turns to speak, sharing their own ideas, and respecting the contributions of others. They are taught to prepare and deliver short, structured oral presentations, such as describing a place or an object, using clear and expressive language. Students learn to speak in complete sentences to convey their thoughts effectively and to adjust their volume and pace for different situations.

## Media Literacy
Students develop an understanding of different forms of media. They learn to identify the main purpose of simple advertisements, distinguishing between information designed to inform and that designed to sell. Students are introduced to the concept of fact versus opinion, examining short news reports or articles to identify statements that can be proven true and those that are personal feelings. They practice creating simple media texts, such as designing a poster with an image and a short slogan to promote a school event.
