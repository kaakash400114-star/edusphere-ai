# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 7 English

## Grammar and Punctuation
Students will master the correct use of complex sentence structures, including subordinate clauses and participial phrases. They will learn to employ a wider range of punctuation, such as semicolons to join independent clauses and colons to introduce lists or explanations. For example, they will correctly punctuate a sentence like: "The expedition was delayed; however, the team remained optimistic about their chances of success." This formal understanding ensures clarity and sophistication in their writing.

## Comprehension and Close Reading
In this unit, students develop skills to analyse and interpret a wide variety of texts, including non-fiction articles and literary extracts. They will identify the main ideas, supporting details, and the author's purpose and perspective. Students learn to infer meaning from contextual clues and evaluate how writers use language and structure to achieve specific effects, such as creating suspense or persuading an audience. This analytical approach forms the bedrock of literary and critical study.

## Composition: Structured Writing
Students will focus on planning and writing well-structured formal essays and reports. They will learn to formulate a clear and arguable thesis statement and develop it with logical paragraphs, each containing a topic sentence, evidence, and analysis. Emphasis is placed on organising arguments coherently, using formal register, and ensuring all writing has a discernible introduction, body, and conclusion to effectively communicate complex ideas.

## Vocabulary Development
This section expands students' lexicon with more sophisticated and subject-specific vocabulary. Students will learn to identify and use precise synonyms and antonyms to avoid repetition and enhance expression. They will also study word roots, prefixes, and suffixes to decode and understand unfamiliar terms, for instance, identifying the prefix 'pre-' in 'premonition' to mean 'before'. This active vocabulary building is essential for eloquent and nuanced communication.

## Speaking and Listening Skills
Students will practise formal oral presentations and discussions on a range of topics. They will learn to structure their spoken arguments clearly, use persuasive language, and respond to questions with appropriate detail and evidence. A key focus is on active listening: understanding and summarising others' points before offering a considered response. These skills are assessed through activities like formal debates and individual presentations on set topics.

## Introduction to Literary Devices
Students will be introduced to a core set of literary devices used in prose, poetry, and drama. They will learn to identify and analyse simile ('as brave as a lion'), metaphor ('the world is a stage), and personification ('the wind whispered secrets'). By examining how authors use these devices to create imagery, mood, and character, students begin to appreciate the artistry of language and its power to evoke emotion and meaning.
