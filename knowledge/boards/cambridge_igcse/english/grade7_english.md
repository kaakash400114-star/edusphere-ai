# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 7 English


## Grammar and Punctuation
Students will master the correct use of complex sentence structures, including subordinate clauses and participial phrases. They will learn to employ a wider range of punctuation, such as semicolons to join independent clauses and colons to introduce lists or explanations. For example, they will correctly punctuate a sentence like: "The expedition was delayed; however, the team remained optimistic about their chances of success." This formal understanding ensures clarity and sophistication in their writing.

## Comprehension and Close Reading
In this unit, students develop skills to analyse and interpret a wide variety of texts, including non-fiction articles and literary extracts. They will identify the main ideas, supporting details, and the author's purpose and perspective. Students learn to infer meaning from contextual clues and evaluate how writers use language and structure to achieve specific effects, such as creating suspense or persuading an audience. This analytical approach forms the bedrock of literary and critical study.

## Composition: Structured Writing
Students will apply the PEEL (Point, Evidence, Explanation, Link) paragraph structure to build a formal five-paragraph essay. A clear, debatable thesis statement must be presented in the introduction to guide the entire argument. Each body paragraph will begin with a topic sentence, followed by relevant textual or statistical evidence, followed by analysis to prove how the evidence supports the point, and finished with a linking sentence to the next argument. Coherence is ensured through the use of precise transitional phrases, and a formal tone must be maintained throughout. This methodology allows students to systematically deconstruct a topic, formulate a convincing argument, and present it with logical clarity.

## Vocabulary Development
Students will develop vocabulary through the systematic analysis of word parts, including prefixes such as 'pre-' (meaning 'before'), roots like 'port' (meaning 'to carry'), and suffixes including '-tion' (which forms nouns), enabling them to decode unfamiliar terms like 'transportation'. The curriculum requires students to identify and use precise synonyms to avoid repetition, for example, substituting 'exclaimed' for 'said' to convey varied emotion. They will also study and employ antonyms to establish clear contrasts, such as using 'chaotic' to describe the opposite of 'orderly'. Furthermore, students will expand their lexicon by learning subject-specific terminology to enhance clarity and comprehension in academic contexts. This instruction in morphology, synonym/antonym usage, and specialized vocabulary forms the core of building a sophisticated and precise written and oral command of English.

## Speaking and Listening Skills
Students will learn to structure formal presentations using a clear framework, such as introduction with a thesis, development with supporting evidence, and a conclusion, while employing persuasive devices like rhetorical questions and repetition for effect. In discussions, they must formulate arguments using a claim, evidence, and warrant structure to demonstrate critical thinking. Active listening requires summarising others' main points before responding with either agreement, disagreement using counter-evidence, or building upon the original idea. These skills are practised through activities like formal debates on current affairs where students must prepare opening statements, rebut opposing claims, and conclude with a summary of their argument's validity.

## Introduction to Literary Devices
Students will be introduced to a core set of literary devices used in prose, poetry, and drama. They will learn to identify and analyse simile ('as brave as a lion'), metaphor ('the world is a stage), and personification ('the wind whispered secrets'). By examining how authors use these devices to create imagery, mood, and character, students begin to appreciate the artistry of language and its power to evoke emotion and meaning.
