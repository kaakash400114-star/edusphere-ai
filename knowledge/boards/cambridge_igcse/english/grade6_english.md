# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 6 English
## Reading Comprehension: Non-Fiction Texts
Students learn to identify the main idea and supporting details in factual texts, such as news articles and biographies. They practise distinguishing between fact and opinion, and learn to infer meaning from a writer’s language and purpose. For example, in a text on recycling, students must highlight the central argument and list the pieces of evidence used. They also explore the impact of specific word choices on the reader’s understanding, recognising how persuasive language is used to present a viewpoint.

## Writing: Descriptive and Narrative Structures
Students focus on crafting vivid and engaging descriptions using the five senses. They learn to build suspense in narrative writing through techniques like foreshadowing and varying sentence length. For example, a student might describe a bustling market, using specific details like the smell of spices and the sound of haggling to create a clear image. In narratives, they will practise structuring a story with a clear beginning, middle, and end, ensuring the plot is logical and the characters are developed through their actions and dialogue.

## Grammar: Parts of Speech and Sentence Construction
Students consolidate their understanding of the eight parts of speech, including identifying nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, prepositions, conjunctions, and interjections in complex sentences. They learn to expand simple sentences by adding subordinate clauses and using a variety of conjunctions. For example, they can transform "The dog barked" into "The dog, which was hiding under the bed, barked loudly when the thunder rumbled," demonstrating a clear understanding of clause structure and descriptive language.

## Vocabulary Development: Synonyms, Antonyms, and Word Families
Students expand their vocabulary by learning to identify and use synonyms and antonyms to improve their writing precision. They explore word families, understanding how root words, prefixes (un-, re-, dis-), and suffixes (-able, -ment, -ful) change a word’s meaning. For example, from the root 'happy', students can generate 'unhappy', 'happiness', and 'happily'. They apply this knowledge by choosing more sophisticated vocabulary for descriptive writing, replacing common words like 'good' or 'bad' with more specific alternatives.

## Speaking and Listening: Formal Presentations and Discussion
Students develop the skills to deliver a clear, well-structured presentation to an audience. They learn to use appropriate body language, eye contact, and vocal variation to engage listeners. For example, a student might prepare a three-minute talk on a chosen interest, such as football or a specific book. In group discussions, they practise active listening, building on others' points and expressing their own opinions clearly and respectfully, demonstrating turn-taking and constructive dialogue skills.

## Spelling and Punctuation: Advanced Rules and Application
Students master the spelling rules for common prefixes and suffixes, as well as exceptions. They learn to use a wider range of punctuation, including semicolons, colons, and dashes, to structure complex sentences and add emphasis. For example, they would use a semicolon to link two closely related independent clauses ("The train was delayed; the passengers waited patiently"). They also focus on correctly spelling words with double consonants (e.g., 'necessary') and silent letters (e.g., 'island').
