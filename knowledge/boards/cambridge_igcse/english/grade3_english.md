# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 3 English

## Phonics and Letter-Sound Relationships
Children learn to match the 42 main sounds (phonemes) to their corresponding letters or letter groups (graphemes). They study the relationship between sounds and spelling, such as the /k/ sound being written as 'c', 'k', or 'ck'. Lessons focus on blending sounds to read simple, decodable words like 'cat', 'ship', and 'moon'. They also learn to segment words into individual sounds to support spelling, for example, breaking 'frog' into /f/-/r/-/o/-/g/. This systematic approach builds a strong foundation for reading fluency and accurate spelling.

## Reading Comprehension
Students develop the ability to understand and extract meaning from simple, age-appropriate texts, including stories, poems, and non-fiction passages. They learn to identify the main character and the setting in a story. Children answer direct 'who', 'what', 'where', and 'when' questions based on the text. They begin to make simple predictions about what might happen next and can retell a familiar story in the correct sequence. This skill is supported by using picture cues and understanding the basic structure of a narrative.

## Sentence Structure and Grammar
Learners explore the fundamental building blocks of a sentence. They understand that a sentence expresses a complete idea and must begin with a capital letter and end with a full stop. Students learn to identify and use nouns (e.g., 'dog', 'house') to name things and verbs (e.g., 'run', 'walk') to describe actions. They are introduced to the use of capital letters for proper nouns, such as the names of people ('Sarah') or places ('London'). Lessons focus on forming simple, compound sentences using 'and'.

## Spelling Patterns and Vocabulary
Children are taught common spelling rules and patterns to improve their accuracy. This includes learning tricky high-frequency words, often called 'sight words', such as 'said', 'where', and 'could'. They study word families that share the same ending, like 'cat', 'hat', and 'bat'. Vocabulary is expanded by learning the names of common objects, animals, and concepts. For example, they might learn the specific terms for baby animals ('puppy', 'kitten') and begin to use descriptive adjectives (e.g., 'big', 'small', 'happy').

## Writing for Different Purposes
Students practise writing for a range of simple, real-world audiences. They learn to write short, clear instructions, such as 'How to make a sandwich'. This includes using numbered or bulleted lists. They also write simple personal recounts, like a diary entry about their weekend, focusing on using the past tense. For imaginative writing, they create short stories with a beginning, middle, and end, often using sentence starters provided by the teacher to structure their ideas.

## Speaking and Listening Skills
Learners develop their ability to communicate effectively through structured activities. They practise speaking clearly in full sentences when answering questions or describing a picture. Listening skills are honed by following simple, two-step instructions ('Please get your book and open it'). Children take turns speaking in small group discussions, listening to their peers without interrupting. They also participate in role-playing scenarios, such as a visit to a shop, practising polite language like 'please' and 'thank you'.
