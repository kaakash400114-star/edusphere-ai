# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 1 English

## Listening and Understanding
Children learn to listen carefully and follow simple, two-step instructions. They focus on distinguishing between different sounds, especially at the beginning and end of words. They will listen to stories from different cultures, like Anansi the Spider from the Caribbean, and answer simple questions about the characters and events. This skill is practiced through games such as 'Simon Says' and by identifying the main sound they hear in a word, such as 's' in 'sun' or 'c' in 'cat'.

## Speaking and Communicating
Students develop their speaking skills by sharing their experiences and ideas in small groups. They learn to use complete sentences to express needs and wants, for example, 'Can I have the red pencil?' and to describe objects or events using adjectives like 'big', 'small', 'happy', or 'sad'. They practice taking turns to talk and listen, presenting a show-and-tell item and explaining what it is and why they chose it.

## Phonics and Word Recognition
Children are taught to link sounds to letters (graphemes) using a systematic approach. They learn the most common sounds, including 's', 'a', 't', 'p', 'i', 'n', and practice blending these sounds to read simple, decodable words like 'sat', 'pin', and 'tap'. They also segment simple words into their individual sounds to aid spelling, identifying the three sounds in 'dog' (/d/ /o/ /g/) to help them write it correctly.

## Reading and Comprehension
Pupils develop early reading skills by reading simple, decodable books and texts with picture support. They use their phonics knowledge to read words aloud and build sight vocabulary for common, tricky words like 'the', 'I', and 'was'. Students begin to understand what they read by answering 'who', 'what', and 'where' questions about a familiar story, such as 'Who went to the market?' in a tale from South Asia.

## Writing and Handwriting
Students learn to form lower-case and upper-case letters correctly, ensuring they are the right size and sit on the line. They practice writing their own name and simple, familiar words. They are encouraged to use their knowledge of letter sounds to attempt spelling in their independent writing. A common exercise involves writing a simple sentence to match a picture, for example, 'The cat is big.', focusing on initial and final consonants.

## Language and Grammar
Children are introduced to the basic building blocks of English. They learn to identify and use nouns for people, places, and things, such as 'teacher', 'school', and 'book'. They also learn to recognize action verbs and understand that a sentence is a group of words that makes sense, starting with a capital letter and ending with a full stop. For example, they can sort words into 'things' and 'doing words' categories.
