# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 1 English


## Listening and Understanding
Children learn to listen carefully and follow simple, two-step instructions. They focus on distinguishing between different sounds, especially at the beginning and end of words. They will listen to stories from different cultures, like Anansi the Spider from the Caribbean, and answer simple questions about the characters and events. This skill is practiced through games such as 'Simon Says' and by identifying the main sound they hear in a word, such as 's' in 'sun' or 'c' in 'cat'.

## Speaking and Communicating
Students develop speaking skills by learning to share experiences and ideas using complete sentences, such as 'Can I have the red pencil?' to express needs and wants. They use descriptive adjectives to characterize objects and events, for instance describing a toy as 'big' or expressing feelings as 'happy'. The curriculum emphasizes fundamental rules for effective communication, including taking turns to speak and actively listen to others. A key method involves presenting a 'show-and-tell' item, where students must explain what the object is and articulate why they chose it. This structured practice ensures students build confidence and competence in verbal expression according to the Cambridge IGCSE standards.

## Phonics and Word Recognition
Children are systematically taught to map phonemes (sounds) to graphemes (letters) using a multi-sensory approach to build foundational reading skills. They begin by learning the most common single-letter graphemes and their corresponding sounds (e.g., 's' as in /s/, 'a' as in /a/). This knowledge is applied to decode (read) simple, decodable words by blending the individual phonemes, such as 'cat' (/k/ /a/ /t/) and 'sun' (/s/ /u/ /n/). Concurrently, students develop encoding (spelling) skills by segmenting spoken words into their constituent sounds to identify graphemes, enabling them to accurately spell words like 'dog' (/d/ /o/ /g/) and 'mop' (/m/ /o/ /p/). The progression includes learning common digraphs, where two letters represent one sound, such as 'sh' as in /sh/ for the word 'ship'. This structured methodology ensures students build essential skills in both word recognition and accurate spelling.

## Reading and Comprehension
Pupils apply systematic synthetic phonics to decode regular consonant-vowel-consonant words like 'cat' and 'sun'. They are taught to read high-frequency irregular words, such as 'the', 'I', and 'was', on sight through dedicated practice. Comprehension skills are developed by learning to identify the main character and the setting in a simple story. For instance, after reading a traditional tale from South Asia, pupils can answer basic questions like 'Who was the main character?' and 'Where did the story happen?' to demonstrate their understanding of the literal text.

## Writing and Handwriting
Students learn to form lower-case and upper-case letters correctly, ensuring they are the right size and sit on the line. They practice writing their own name and simple, familiar words. They are encouraged to use their knowledge of letter sounds to attempt spelling in their independent writing. A common exercise involves writing a simple sentence to match a picture, for example, 'The cat is big.', focusing on initial and final consonants.

## Language and Grammar
Children are introduced to the basic building blocks of English. They learn to identify and use nouns for people, places, and things, such as 'teacher', 'school', and 'book'. They also learn to recognize action verbs and understand that a sentence is a group of words that makes sense, starting with a capital letter and ending with a full stop. For example, they can sort words into 'things' and 'doing words' categories.
