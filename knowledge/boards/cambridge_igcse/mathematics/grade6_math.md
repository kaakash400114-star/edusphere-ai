# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 6 Mathematics

## Number and Place Value
Students extend their understanding of whole numbers and decimals, recognising the value of each digit in numbers up to ten million and three decimal places. They learn to order, compare, and round numbers to a required degree of accuracy, such as rounding 3.4567 to two decimal places (3.46). Pupils explore how to use place value to mentally add and subtract, for example, calculating 45,000 + 2,000 by knowing only the thousands digit changes. They also begin to interpret large numbers in real-world contexts, like understanding population figures or distances between international cities.

## Calculations and Operations
Pupils develop efficient written methods for addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division of whole numbers and decimals. For multiplication, they use the standard algorithm, for instance, calculating 3.4 x 7 by first finding 34 x 7 = 238 and then adjusting for the decimal. In division, they learn to divide numbers with up to four digits by a two-digit number, such as 435 ÷ 15. Students also solve multi-step word problems that require choosing the correct operation, like finding the total cost of several items at different prices. They are introduced to the order of operations (BODMAS) to correctly evaluate expressions like 3 + 4 x 2.

## Fractions, Decimals, and Percentages
Students learn to find equivalent fractions, simplify them to their lowest terms, and compare fractions by finding a common denominator. They perform addition and subtraction with fractions that have different denominators, for example, 1/3 + 1/4 = 7/12. Pupils convert between fractions, decimals, and percentages, understanding that 1/2, 0.5, and 50% represent the same value. They calculate simple percentages of amounts, such as finding 15% of 200 by calculating 10% (20) and 5% (10) and adding them. This understanding is applied to solve problems involving discounts or part quantities of a whole.

## Algebra and Expressions
Pupils are introduced to algebraic notation, using letters to represent unknown numbers or variables. They learn to form expressions from word statements, for example, 'three times a number x' is written as 3x. Students substitute values into expressions and formulas, such as finding the value of 2y + 5 when y = 4. They begin to solve simple one-step equations, like x + 7 = 10, by using inverse operations to find the value of the unknown. This foundational work helps them understand how to represent mathematical relationships and solve for missing values.

## Measurement and Units
Students calculate the perimeter and area of simple shapes, including rectangles, triangles, and compound shapes made from rectangles. They use the formula area = length × width for rectangles and area = (base × height) / 2 for triangles. Pupils convert between different units of measurement, such as changing 1.5 kilometres to 1,500 metres or converting litres to millilitres. They also learn to calculate the volume of cuboids using the formula volume = length × width × height, applying this to real-world problems like finding the capacity of a swimming pool.

## Geometry and Statistics
Pupils identify, describe, and classify 2D shapes, including quadrilaterals and polygons, based on their properties like sides, angles, and symmetry. They learn to draw and measure angles using a protractor, recognising angles of 90°, 180°, and 360°. In statistics, students collect, organise, and interpret data using tally charts, frequency tables, and bar graphs. They find the mean average of a set of numbers, for example, calculating the mean of 4, 7, and 9 by adding them (20) and dividing by the count (3) to get 6.67.
