# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 4 Mathematics

## Number Sense and Place Value
Students understand place value for numbers up to 10,000. They learn that the value of a digit depends on its position, for example, in the number 3,742, the digit '7' represents 700. Pupils can read, write, compare, and order these numbers. They practice partitioning numbers, such as breaking 5,421 into 5,000 + 400 + 20 + 1. Students also learn to round numbers to the nearest 10 and 100, for example, rounding 2,837 to the nearest 10 is 2,840. This forms the foundation for all future arithmetic work.

## Addition and Subtraction
Students extend their addition and subtraction skills to include numbers up to 10,000. They learn to add and subtract using both mental strategies and written methods like column addition and subtraction. For example, to find the sum of 4,567 and 1,234, they would add the thousands, hundreds, tens, and ones separately. Pupils also solve word problems involving real-life situations, such as calculating the total distance of two journeys or finding the difference in temperatures, ensuring they use the correct operation for each problem.

## Multiplication and Division
Students explore multiplication and division facts and learn to calculate with larger numbers. They memorize multiplication tables up to 10x10 and use them to solve problems involving factors and multiples. Pupils are taught to multiply a three-digit number by a one-digit number using a formal written method, for example, 428 x 5. They are also introduced to short division, dividing a two-digit number by a one-digit number, such as 84 ÷ 4. Word problems like sharing 36 sweets equally among 9 children help them apply these concepts practically.

## Measurement and Units
Students learn about standard units of measurement for length, mass, and capacity. They understand that length is measured in millimetres, centimetres, metres, and kilometres; mass in grams and kilograms; and capacity in millilitres and litres. Pupils practice converting between these units, for example, 1,500 grams is equal to 1.5 kilograms. They also measure objects using rulers and scales and solve problems involving measuring and comparing, such as finding the total weight of 3 kg of apples and 500 g of oranges.

## Fractions
Students develop a deeper understanding of fractions. They learn to identify and represent fractions of shapes and sets, shading a quarter of a square or finding three-quarters of 20 marbles. Pupils compare and order simple fractions with the same denominator, such as 1/5 and 4/5. They are also introduced to equivalent fractions, understanding that 1/2 is the same as 2/4 or 3/6, using visual aids like fraction walls to help them see the relationships between different fractions.

## Shapes and Space
Students identify, describe, and classify 2D and 3D shapes. They learn the properties of common polygons, including triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons, and hexagons, counting their sides and angles. For 3D shapes, they explore cubes, cuboids, spheres, and pyramids, identifying faces, edges, and vertices. Pupils also learn about lines of symmetry in 2D shapes, finding that a square has four lines of symmetry. They practice drawing these shapes and sorting them based on their properties.
