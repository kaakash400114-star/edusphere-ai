# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 1 Mathematics

## Number Sense and Counting
Children learn to count, read, and write numbers from 1 to 100. They count forwards and backwards, starting from any number within this range. Pupils count a given number of objects reliably, up to 20, and understand the concept of one-to-one correspondence. They learn to identify and write numbers in both numerals and words. Students begin to compare numbers using terms like 'more', 'less', and 'fewer', and use simple inequality symbols. For example, they can show that 8 pencils are fewer than 10 pencils. This foundational skill supports all future mathematical work.

## Addition and Subtraction
Students explore addition as combining two or more groups of objects and subtraction as taking away from a group. They use concrete materials like counters or blocks to solve simple problems involving sums and differences within 20. Pupils learn the related addition and subtraction facts, understanding that 5 + 3 = 8 is the same as 8 - 3 = 5. They use the language 'plus', 'minus', and 'equals' correctly. For instance, they can solve a problem: 'I have 6 apples and buy 4 more. How many apples do I have now?' The answer is 10.

## Shapes and Spatial Awareness
Children recognize, name, and describe common 2D and 3D shapes, including squares, circles, triangles, rectangles, cubes, and spheres. They identify these shapes in their immediate environment, such as a round clock or a rectangular book. Pupils learn to describe shapes using simple vocabulary like 'sides', 'corners', and 'curved'. They explore how to make new shapes by combining simple 2D shapes, for example, using two triangles to make a square. This develops their ability to see and analyse geometric relationships in the world around them.

## Measurement
Students learn to compare and measure objects using non-standard units. They measure length using informal units like paperclips or hand spans, and capacity using cups or jugs. Pupils compare the weight of objects using balancing scales and describe the results using 'heavier', 'lighter', or 'the same'. They also begin to understand time, recognizing the hour and minute hands on a clock and ordering events using terms like 'before', 'after', and 'first'. For example, they can say that school starts after breakfast.

## Patterns and Data
Children identify, describe, and extend simple patterns using shapes, colours, numbers, or objects. For example, they might complete a pattern like red, blue, red, ___. Pupils sort objects into groups based on one attribute, such as colour, size, or shape, and count the items in each group. This introduces them to the beginnings of data handling. They can create a simple real-world graph, such as a pictograph showing how many children like different fruits, helping them to visually organize and interpret information.

## Practical Problem Solving
Students apply their mathematical knowledge to solve simple, real-life problems. They decide whether to add or subtract to find the answer, using drawings or objects to help them. Pupils learn to explain their thinking and describe how they reached a solution. For example, they might solve: 'There are 7 children on the bus. 3 get off. How many are left?' They use the correct number sentence: 7 - 3 = 4. This skill is crucial for developing logical reasoning and applying mathematics to everyday situations.
