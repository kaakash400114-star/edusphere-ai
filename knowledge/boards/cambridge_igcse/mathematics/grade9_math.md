# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 9 Mathematics
## Algebraic Manipulation and Indices
Students expand and simplify single and double brackets, including the difference of two squares (e.g., (2x + 3)(x - 5) = 2x² - 7x - 15). They factorise quadratic expressions of the form x² + bx + c and ax² + bx + c by finding two numbers with a given sum and product. Indices are applied using laws: a^m × a^n = a^(m+n), (a^m)^n = a^(mn), and a^(-n) = 1/a^n. Students simplify expressions involving negative and fractional powers, such as (16x⁸y²)^(1/4) = 2x²y^(1/2).

## Linear Equations and Inequalities
Learners solve linear equations with unknowns on one or both sides, including those with brackets and fractional coefficients (e.g., 3(2x - 4)/5 = 12). They form and solve equations from word problems involving speed, distance, and time. Linear inequalities are solved and represented on number lines, with solutions written as inequalities (x > 2) or interval notation (2, ∞). Students identify integer or integer value solutions to inequalities, such as finding the smallest integer value of n for which 2n - 7 > 5.

## Geometric Reasoning and Circle Theorems
Students calculate angles using the properties of parallel lines (corresponding, alternate, and co-interior angles). They apply the angle sum and exterior angle properties of triangles and quadrilaterals. Circle theorems are introduced, including that the angle in a semicircle is a right angle, and the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference. Learners use these theorems to solve angle problems within geometric diagrams, proving relationships between angles in complex figures involving multiple intersecting chords or tangents.

## Surface Area and Volume of 3D Solids
Calculations are extended to find the surface area and volume of composite solids, such as a cylinder attached to a hemisphere. Students use formulas: volume of a pyramid (1/3 × base area × height), volume of a cone (1/3 × πr²h), and volume and surface area of a sphere (4/3πr³ and 4πr²). Real-world problems involve converting units (e.g., litres to cubic centimetres) to solve for an unknown dimension, such as finding the height of a water tank with a capacity of 5000 litres and a circular base of radius 1.5 m.

## Coordinate Geometry and Straight-Line Graphs
Learners find the midpoint and gradient of a line segment joining two points, such as A(-2, 5) and B(4, -1). They use the gradient formula (m = (y₂ - y₁)/(x₂ - x₁)) to determine if lines are parallel or perpendicular (m₁ × m₂ = -1). The equation of a straight line is found in the forms y = mx + c and ax + by + c = 0. Students plot and interpret real-world graphs, such as distance-time or cost-volume relationships, determining gradients and intercepts to analyse rates of change.

## Introduction to Probability
Basic probability is formalised using the formula P(Event) = Number of favourable outcomes / Total number outcomes. Students list sample spaces for single and combined events (e.g., rolling two dice) and calculate probabilities of mutually exclusive and independent events. The probability of an event not occurring is 1 - P(Event). Tree diagrams are used to find probabilities for dependent events, such as drawing two balls from a bag without replacement, calculating compound probabilities by multiplying along the branches.
