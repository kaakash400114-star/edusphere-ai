# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 3 Mathematics

## Number and Place Value
Students understand numbers up to 1000. They learn to read, write, and order them. They partition numbers into hundreds, tens, and ones (e.g., 465 = 400 + 60 + 5). Students identify the value of each digit. They count forwards and backwards in steps of 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, and 10. They compare and order numbers using >, <, and = symbols. They round numbers to the nearest 10 or 100. Students use number lines to support their understanding of place value and number sequencing.

## Addition and Subtraction
Students add and subtract numbers up to 1000 using formal written methods. They learn column addition and subtraction, exchanging when necessary. They solve one-step and two-step word problems involving addition and subtraction. They estimate answers by rounding numbers. Students use inverse operations to check calculations. For example, to check 542 - 321 = 221, they add 221 + 321 to see if it equals 542. They also practice mental strategies like bridging through 10 and using known facts.

## Multiplication and Division
Students understand multiplication as repeated addition and division as sharing or grouping. They learn multiplication and division facts for the 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, and 10 times tables. They multiply a one-digit number by a two-digit number using a grid or formal column method. They divide a two-digit number by a one-digit number, including remainders. Students solve problems involving multiplication and division in real-world contexts, such as sharing stickers or calculating costs for a group of people.

## Fractions
Students understand that fractions are parts of a whole. They learn to name and write fractions with denominators of 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, and 10. They find equivalent fractions using diagrams and number lines, for example, recognizing that 1/2 is the same as 2/4. They order and compare unit fractions. They add and subtract fractions with the same denominator (e.g., 3/5 + 1/5 = 4/5). Students identify simple mixed numbers and understand tenths as fractions with a denominator of 10.

## Measurement
Students measure and compare lengths, mass, volume, and capacity using standard units (metres, centimetres, litres, grams). They read time to the nearest minute, including using analogue and digital clocks. They calculate durations of time and solve problems involving time, such as finding the end time of an event. They recognise and use the symbols for pounds (£) and pence (p), adding and subtracting amounts of money. Students find the perimeter of simple 2D shapes by measuring all sides and adding them together.

## Geometry
Students identify, describe, and draw 2D shapes including triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons, hexagons, and octagons. They name and describe 3D shapes including cubes, cuboids, pyramids, and spheres. They recognise and describe right angles, greater than right angles, and less than right angles. They identify lines of symmetry in 2D shapes. Students describe movement and position using terms like 'left', 'right', 'up', 'down', 'above', and 'below'. They use simple coordinates to locate points on a grid.
