# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 12 Mathematics

## Algebra and Graphs
Students manipulate algebraic expressions, including expanding, factorising, and simplifying quadratic and cubic expressions. They solve linear and quadratic equations using factorisation, completing the square, and the quadratic formula, where necessary. Students interpret and sketch graphs of quadratic, cubic, and reciprocal functions, identifying features such as roots, turning points, and asymptotes. Real-world contexts, like calculating projectile motion or profit functions, are modelled using these algebraic techniques and graphical interpretations.

## Functions and Coordinate Geometry
Students explore the concepts of functions, including notation (e.g., f(x)) and the distinction between one-to-one and many-to-one mappings. They find and interpret composite and inverse functions. In coordinate geometry, students find the equation of a straight line given two points or its gradient and a point, and they determine the conditions for parallel and perpendicular lines. They apply these methods to solve problems involving real-world scenarios, such as determining the optimal path for a delivery vehicle based on its speed and direction.

## Calculus
Students understand and apply the principles of differentiation to find the gradient and rate of change of functions, including linear, quadratic, cubic, and trigonometric functions. They use the second derivative to determine the nature of stationary points (maximum, minimum, or point of inflection). In integration, students find the area under a curve and between two curves, and solve problems involving kinematics, such as calculating displacement from velocity. Applications to optimisation problems in business and engineering are frequently examined.

## Trigonometry
Students extend their knowledge of trigonometric ratios to solve problems in two and three dimensions using the sine rule, cosine rule, and area formula (1/2 ab sin C). They solve trigonometric equations within a specified domain, often requiring the application of identities such as sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. Students model periodic phenomena, such as tidal patterns or sound waves, using graphs of sine and cosine functions, and analyse their amplitude, frequency, and period to make predictions about real-world cycles.

## Vectors and Matrices
Students define vectors and perform vector addition, subtraction, and scalar multiplication geometrically and algebraically. They find the magnitude of a vector and its direction, and use the scalar (dot) product to calculate angles between vectors and test for perpendicularity. For matrices, students learn operations like addition, subtraction, and multiplication. They apply matrices to solve systems of linear equations and to perform geometric transformations, such as rotation and reflection, on coordinate points in the plane.

## Probability and Statistics
Students calculate and interpret conditional probabilities using Venn diagrams and formulae, including the laws of total probability and Bayes' theorem. For statistics, they analyse and interpret cumulative frequency curves, box-and-whisker plots, and calculate measures of dispersion such as variance and standard deviation. Students apply the principles of sampling to understand bias and representativeness, and they use statistical methods to compare data sets, for example, assessing the effectiveness of a new teaching strategy against the traditional approach.
