# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 8 Mathematics

## Number and Algebra
Students build upon their understanding of integers and decimals to include rational numbers, recognising that they can be expressed as fractions. They learn to perform exact calculations involving fractions, decimals, and percentages without a calculator, finding one number as a percentage of another and solving percentage increase/decrease problems. They are introduced to standard form (scientific notation) to represent very large and very small numbers, understanding the structure A × 10n where 1 ≤ A < 10 and n is an integer.

## Geometry and Measures
Students learn to calculate the area and perimeter of more complex plane shapes, including compound figures made from rectangles, triangles, and parallelograms. They are formally introduced to the formula for the volume of a prism, V = Ah, where A is the area of the uniform cross-section and h is the height or depth. The concept of surface area is extended to include prisms and cylinders. Students also explore the properties of parallel and perpendicular lines, including the angle rules related to transversals.

## Equations, Formulae, and Identities
This topic focuses on forming and solving linear equations in one variable, including those with variables on both sides. Students learn to rearrange simple formulae to change the subject, applying inverse operations systematically. They are introduced to the difference between an equation, which is only true for specific values, and an identity, such as 3(x + 2) = 3x + 6, which is true for all values of x.

## Ratio, Proportion, and Rates of Change
Students deepen their understanding of ratio and proportion. They solve direct proportion problems, including those involving currency conversion and scaling recipes. Inverse proportion is introduced, with students setting up and solving related equations (e.g., y = k/x). They also calculate rates of change in everyday contexts, such as speed (distance/time) and unit cost (cost/quantity), interpreting their solutions to solve practical problems.

## Probability and Statistics
Students learn to calculate the probability of single events, understanding that probabilities range from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain). They distinguish between theoretical probability and experimental frequency. In statistics, they are taught to calculate the mean, median, mode, and range for both discrete and continuous data, and to interpret these measures in context. They also work with grouped data to estimate the mean and understand the impact of outliers on different averages.

## Graphs and Functions
Students learn to plot and interpret linear graphs from an equation (y = mx + c) and from a set of coordinates. They use the gradient to describe the rate of change and the y-intercept to identify the initial value. They construct conversion graphs and interpret real-world graphs, such as distance-time graphs. Functions are introduced as a mapping relationship, with students evaluating simple functions using function notation, such as finding f(3) for the function f(x) = 2x + 1.
