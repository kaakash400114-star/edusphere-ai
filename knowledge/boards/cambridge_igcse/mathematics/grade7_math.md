# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 7 Mathematics

## Number and Place Value
Students understand and work with integers, fractions, and decimals, extending place value to include millions and thousandths. They learn to interpret and compare negative numbers, such as -5 and -10, and order them on a number line. Pupils are introduced to the concept of ratio and proportion, simplifying ratios like 12:18 to 2:3. They apply these skills to solve problems involving real-world quantities, such as sharing money or dividing ingredients in a recipe, ensuring accurate calculation of values and correct representation of proportional relationships between different quantities.

## Calculations and Accuracy
Students develop fluency in using the four operations: addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division, applying them to positive and negative integers. They use the order of operations (BODMAS/PEDMAS) to correctly evaluate expressions like 3 + 4 x 2. Pupils are taught to round numbers to a specified degree of accuracy, such as rounding 3.876 to two decimal places to get 3.88. These skills are used to estimate answers for multi-step problems and to check the reasonableness of a calculator result, ensuring a high degree of accuracy in all mathematical procedures.

## Algebraic Expressions and Equations
Students begin to work with algebra by understanding and forming simple algebraic expressions. They learn to simplify expressions by combining like terms, for example, simplifying 4x + 2y - x + y to 3x + 3y. Pupils solve one-step and two-step linear equations with unknowns on one or both sides, such as solving 2x + 7 = 13 by first subtracting 7 and then dividing by 2. These methods are applied to represent and solve problems from real-life contexts, like calculating the cost of items with a fixed price and a per-unit cost.

## Geometry and Properties of Shapes
Students classify and name different types of triangles and quadrilaterals based on their properties, such as scalene, isosceles, equilateral, and right-angled. They learn to calculate the perimeter and area of rectangles, triangles, and parallelograms. Pupils identify and draw parallel and perpendicular lines and calculate unknown angles on a straight line, around a point, and within a triangle. These concepts are used to solve practical problems, such as determining the amount of fencing required for a garden plot or the area of a triangular sail.

## Handling Data and Probability
Students learn to collect, organise, and represent data in various ways, including frequency tables, bar charts, and pictograms. They calculate the mean, median, mode, and range for a set of data, understanding what each measure represents. Pupils are introduced to basic probability, using words like 'certain', 'likely', 'unlikely', and 'impossible' to describe the likelihood of simple events like rolling a six on a fair die. They calculate the probability of an event occurring as a fraction, such as the probability of picking a red marble from a bag containing 3 red and 7 blue marbles, which is 3/10.

## Measures and Mensuration
Students convert between different units of measurement, including metric units for length (km, m, cm, mm), mass (kg, g), and capacity (l, ml). They calculate the area of compound shapes by dividing them into simpler rectangles and triangles. Pupils find the volume of cuboids using the formula length × width × height. These skills are applied to solve real-world problems, such as calculating the amount of liquid a container can hold or determining the area of a composite floor plan to estimate the quantity of tiles needed for renovation.
