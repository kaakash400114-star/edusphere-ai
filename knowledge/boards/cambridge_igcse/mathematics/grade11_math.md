# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 11 Mathematics
## Algebraic Methods and Quadratic Functions
Students master advanced algebraic manipulation, including expanding and factorising complex quadratic expressions. They solve quadratic equations by factorisation, completing the square, and using the quadratic formula, x = [-b ± √(b² - 4ac)] / 2a. Learners apply these methods to solve word problems involving area, motion, and growth. They also work with algebraic fractions, simplifying expressions involving quadratic terms and performing addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division with rational expressions. Proficiency in handling discriminant calculations (b² - 4ac) is developed to determine the nature of roots.

## Functions, Graphs, and Coordinate Geometry
Students explore the properties and graphs of polynomial, quadratic, and reciprocal functions. They learn to plot graphs, identify key features such as intercepts, turning points, and asymptotes, and interpret their real-world meanings. Skills in transforming graphs via translation, reflection, and stretching are developed. Learners solve linear and quadratic equations graphically by finding intersection points. Coordinate geometry includes calculating the midpoint and length of line segments, determining gradients, and using the equation of a straight line (y = mx + c) to describe relationships between variables.

## Trigonometry and Its Applications
Students extend their understanding of trigonometry to angles of any size, working with the sine, cosine, and tangent functions in all four quadrants. They use the trigonometric identities sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 and tanθ = sinθ/cosθ. Solving trigonometric equations is covered, including solutions within specified ranges. Applications include finding heights and distances using right-angled triangles and bearings. The sine rule (a/sinA = b/sinB = c/sinC) and cosine rule (c² = a² + b² - 2ab cosC) are used to solve non-right-angled triangles for sides and angles.

## Mensuration and Circle Theorems
Students calculate the perimeter, area, and volume of composite shapes and solids, including cylinders, cones, spheres, and frustums. Formulas for the surface area and volume of these 3D shapes are applied to practical problems, such as calculating material costs or container capacity. Circle theorems are used to prove geometric relationships and calculate angles. Key theorems include the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference, angles in the same segment are equal, and the angle between a tangent and a radius is 90 degrees. These principles are used to solve complex geometric proofs.

## Vectors and Transformations in the Plane
Students understand vector notation, including column vectors, and perform vector addition, subtraction, and multiplication by a scalar. The magnitude of a vector is calculated using Pythagoras' theorem. Learners use vectors to describe translations and represent the position of points and lines. Geometric transformations include reflection, rotation, enlargement, and translation, performed on 2D shapes. The concept of similarity is explored through enlargements with a given scale factor and centre of enlargement. Vector geometry problems involve determining points dividing a line segment in a given ratio.

## Probability and Statistical Measures
Students calculate and interpret probabilities for single and combined events, using the formula P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B). Tree diagrams and sample spaces are used to model compound events. In statistics, learners calculate and interpret measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) and measures of dispersion (range, interquartile range, standard deviation). They construct and interpret cumulative frequency curves and box-and-whisker plots. The concept of correlation is introduced using scatter diagrams and the product-moment correlation coefficient to describe the strength and direction of a linear relationship between two variables.
