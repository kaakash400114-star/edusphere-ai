# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 11 Science

## Atomic Structure and the Periodic Table
Students explore the Rutherford-Bohr model of the atom, defining atomic number as the number of protons and mass number as the sum of protons and neutrons. Isotopes are atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons. Electrons are arranged in energy levels (shells), with the first shell holding a maximum of 2 electrons and subsequent shells holding 8. The periodic table is organized by increasing atomic number; elements in the same group have similar chemical properties due to identical outer electron configurations, for example, all Group 1 alkali metals react vigorously with water.

## Chemical Bonding
Atoms achieve stable electron configurations, usually a full outer shell, by forming bonds. Ionic bonding involves the transfer of electrons from a metal to a non-metal, creating oppositely charged ions that are attracted by strong electrostatic forces, forming a giant lattice structure like sodium chloride (NaCl). Covalent bonding involves the sharing of electron pairs between non-metal atoms to form molecules, such as in water (H₂O) where each oxygen shares one electron with each hydrogen atom. The type of bond determines the substance's properties, like high melting points for ionic compounds.

## Energy, Work, and Power
Energy is the capacity to do work, measured in joules (J). Work is done when a force causes an object to move, calculated as Work = Force × Distance. Power is the rate of doing work or transferring energy, defined as Power = Work / Time, with the unit watt (W). For example, lifting a 50 N box vertically by 2 m requires 100 J of work. If this takes 5 seconds, the power output is 20 W. Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred or transformed, as demonstrated by a pendulum swinging, converting potential energy to kinetic energy and back.

## Waves
Waves transfer energy without transferring matter. They can be mechanical, requiring a medium (like sound waves in air), or electromagnetic, which can travel through a vacuum (like light). Key properties include wavelength (λ), frequency (f), and wave speed (v), related by the equation v = f × λ. Amplitude is the maximum displacement from the rest position. A sound wave travelling at 340 m/s with a frequency of 170 Hz has a wavelength of 2 m. Reflection is when waves bounce off a surface, and refraction is when they change speed and direction as they pass from one medium to another.

## Electricity and Magnetism
An electric current is the flow of charge, measured in amperes (A), and is driven by a potential difference (voltage) measured in volts (V). Ohm’s Law states that V = I × R, where R is the resistance in ohms (Ω). In a series circuit, the total resistance is the sum of all resistances, and the current is the same everywhere. In a parallel circuit, the total resistance is less than any individual resistance, and the current splits at each junction. An electromagnet is created by passing a current through a coil of wire, with its strength increasing with the current or number of coils.

## Movement and Forces
Newton's First Law states that an object will remain at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by an unbalanced force. His Second Law is F = ma, meaning force equals mass times acceleration; a 2 kg object accelerating at 3 m/s² requires a 6 N force. The Third Law states that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. Friction and air resistance are contact forces that oppose motion. A falling object accelerates due to gravity until air resistance equals its weight, at which point it reaches terminal velocity.
