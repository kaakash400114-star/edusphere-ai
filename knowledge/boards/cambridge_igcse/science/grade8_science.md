# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 8 Science  
## The Particle Theory of Matter  
All matter is composed of tiny, constantly moving particles. The theory states particles have energy; their speed increases with temperature. In solids, particles vibrate in a fixed structure. In liquids, they move more freely, allowing flow. Gases have particles with high energy and large spaces between them, spreading to fill any container. This model explains changes of state: adding energy breaks bonds (melting, evaporation), while removing energy allows them to form (freezing, condensation).  

## Introduction to Chemical Reactions  
A chemical reaction involves the formation of new substances with different properties. Reactants are the starting materials, and products are the substances formed. The law of conservation of mass states that mass is neither created nor destroyed; the total mass of reactants equals the total mass of products. For example, when magnesium (Mg) reacts with oxygen (O₂), it forms magnesium oxide (MgO). The reaction is often represented by a word equation or a balanced chemical equation, such as 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO.  

## Forces and Motion  
Forces are pushes or pulls that can change an object’s motion. Newton’s first law states an object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays at constant velocity unless acted upon by an unbalanced force. Force is measured in newtons (N). The equation force = mass × acceleration (F = ma) describes the relationship between force, mass, and acceleration. For instance, a 2 kg object accelerating at 3 m/s² experiences a force of 6 N. Friction opposes motion and can reduce an object’s speed.  

## Energy Transfers  
Energy cannot be created or destroyed; it only changes form. This is the law of conservation of energy. Common transfers include kinetic to thermal energy (e.g., rubbing hands together) and potential to kinetic energy (e.g., a falling apple). Efficiency measures how useful an energy transfer is, calculated as (useful energy output ÷ total energy input) × 100%. For example, a light bulb with 20 J of light output and 100 J electrical input has 20% efficiency. Renewable sources, like solar panels, convert sunlight into electrical energy.  

## Classification of Living Organisms  
Living organisms are classified into hierarchical groups: domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, and species. The five kingdoms are Animalia, Plantae, Fungi, Protista, and Monera. Organisms share characteristics within each group; for example, animals are multicellular and heterotrophic. Binomial nomenclature uses a genus and species name, such as *Homo sapiens* for humans. Dichotomous keys are tools for identification, using paired statements to narrow down species based on observable traits like leaf shape or body symmetry.  

## The Water Cycle and Weathering  
The water cycle describes the continuous movement of water on Earth. Processes include evaporation (liquid to gas), condensation (gas to liquid), precipitation (rain, snow), and collection in oceans, lakes, and rivers. Weathering breaks down rocks into smaller particles through physical (e.g., freeze-thaw cycles) and chemical (e.g., acid rain) means. Erosion transports weathered material by wind, water, or ice. For example, limestone dissolves in slightly acidic water, a chemical weathering process that forms caves.
