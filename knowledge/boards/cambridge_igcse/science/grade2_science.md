# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 2 Science
## Our Living World
Children learn to identify the basic needs of living things, including water, food, air, and shelter. They explore how plants grow from seeds and require sunlight, water, and soil to become strong. Children classify animals, such as birds, fish, and insects, based on observable features like wings or fins. They discover how living things change and grow, observing a plant sprouting or a puppy getting bigger. Through play with toy animals and real plants, they understand that living things need care to survive and stay healthy in their habitats.

## Materials and Their Properties
Children explore different materials like wood, plastic, metal, and fabric. They learn to sort objects based on their properties, such as hard, soft, smooth, rough, heavy, or light. For example, a wooden block is hard and heavy, while a cotton ball is soft and light. They experiment with sinking and floating, predicting which objects will float in water and which will sink. This hands-on activity helps them understand that materials have different characteristics that determine their use, like a metal spoon being good for stirring because it is strong.

## Observing the Weather
Children learn about different types of weather, such as sunny, rainy, windy, and cloudy. They observe and describe daily weather changes, feeling the wind on their faces, seeing raindrops, or feeling the warm sun. They use simple tools, like a wind sock or a rain gauge, to measure and record the weather. By discussing appropriate clothing for different weather, like wearing a coat in the cold or a hat on a sunny day, children understand how weather impacts their daily lives and activities.

## Forces in Action
Children explore the concept of pushes and pulls as simple forces. They learn that a force can make an object start moving, stop moving, change direction, or change shape. For example, pushing a toy car makes it go, and pulling a drawer makes it open. They experiment with ramps and see how a gentle push makes a ball roll slowly and a strong push makes it roll fast. Through building with blocks and playing with magnets, children discover that forces are all around them and are essential for making things happen.

## The Senses and Our Body
Children learn about the five senses: sight, hearing, smell, taste, and touch. They identify the body parts associated with each sense, such as eyes for sight and ears for hearing. They conduct simple activities, like using a magnifying glass to see small details or listening to different sounds in their environment. By tasting sweet and salty foods or smelling flowers, they learn how their senses help them understand and interact with the world. This helps them appreciate their body’s amazing ability to gather information.

## Light and Sound
Children learn about light and sound as forms of energy. They explore how light travels in straight lines and can be blocked to create shadows. Using a flashlight, they make shadows of their hands on a wall. For sound, they discover that it comes from vibrations and can be loud or soft, high or low. They make sounds by tapping a spoon on a table or blowing air through a straw. These simple experiments show how light and sound are part of their everyday experiences, like hearing a bell or seeing the sun.
