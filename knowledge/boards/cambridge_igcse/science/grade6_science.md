# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 6 Science

## Scientific Inquiry
Students develop foundational skills in scientific investigation. They learn to identify a testable question and formulate a simple hypothesis. Experiments are designed to control variables, with an independent variable being changed and a dependent variable being measured. They practice recording data systematically using tables and drawing basic line graphs. Results are evaluated to identify patterns and form conclusions. Safety procedures are a critical component, including the correct use of lab coats and goggles, and following teacher instructions to prevent accidents.

## States of Matter
Matter is classified into solids, liquids, and gases based on particle arrangement and movement. In solids, particles are tightly packed and vibrate in fixed positions. Liquids have particles that are close but can move past one another, taking the shape of their container. Gases consist of fast-moving particles with large spaces between them, expanding to fill any available volume. The water cycle provides a real-world example, with water changing state between liquid (ocean), gas (water vapour), and solid (ice) due to temperature changes.

## Forces and Motion
Forces are pushes or pulls that can change an object's motion or shape. Students learn to identify forces like gravity, friction, and magnetism using force diagrams. Newton's First Law is introduced: an object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion at a constant speed unless acted upon by an unbalanced force. Forces can be contact (a kick to a ball) or non-contact (Earth's gravity). An example is pushing a shopping cart; more force is needed to accelerate it, and friction acts to slow it down.

## Electricity and Magnetism
Electricity is the flow of electric charge, primarily through a circuit. A complete circuit requires a power source, wires, a component (like a bulb), and a closed path to allow current to flow. Students learn to construct simple series circuits where components are connected end-to-end. They identify conductors (materials like copper that allow electricity to flow) and insulators (materials like plastic that prevent flow). Magnets have a north and south pole; like poles repel, and opposite poles attract, a principle used in devices such as simple electric motors.

## The Earth and the Solar System
The Earth is a rocky planet in the Solar System, which orbits the Sun. The Earth has a layered structure, including a thin crust, a thick mantle, and a core. The Earth rotates on its axis every 24 hours, causing day and night, and revolves around the Sun every 365.25 days, causing the year. The Moon orbits the Earth and its phases are caused by the changing positions of the Sun, Earth, and Moon. Planets are classified as terrestrial (like Earth) or gas giants (like Jupiter) based on their composition.

## Basic Biology: Cells and Life Processes
All living organisms are made of cells, the basic building blocks of life. Plant and animal cells share common features like a nucleus, cytoplasm, and a cell membrane. Plant cells additionally have a rigid cell wall and chloroplasts for photosynthesis. Key life processes include nutrition (taking in food for energy), respiration (releasing energy), excretion (removing waste), and reproduction (producing offspring). For instance, green plants use sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide to make their own food through photosynthesis, which is a form of autotrophic nutrition.
