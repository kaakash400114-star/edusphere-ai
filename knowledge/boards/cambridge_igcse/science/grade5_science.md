# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 5 Science
## States of Matter
Matter exists in three states: solid, liquid, and gas. Solids have a fixed shape and volume because their particles are tightly packed in a regular arrangement. Liquids have a fixed volume but take the shape of their container, as their particles can move past one another. Gases have no fixed shape or volume because their particles are far apart and move freely. We can observe changes of state, such as ice (solid) melting into water (liquid) and water boiling into steam (gas).

## Plant Life Cycle
Plants grow from seeds and follow a life cycle. A seed contains a tiny plant embryo and a food supply. When planted in soil with water and sunlight, it germinates, sending a root down and a shoot up. The shoot develops leaves to make food through photosynthesis. The mature plant produces flowers, which are involved in reproduction, often leading to the formation of new seeds. For example, a bean plant germinates in about a week and produces beans (seeds) after several months.

## Basic Electricity
Electricity is the flow of tiny particles called electrons. We use a circuit to make this flow. A simple circuit needs a power source (like a battery), wires, and an object that uses electricity, such as a light bulb or a buzzer. For a circuit to work, it must be complete. If there is a break, the electricity cannot flow, and the device will not work. Switches are used to start and stop the flow of electricity in a circuit.

## Habitats and Adaptations
A habitat is the natural place where a plant or animal lives. Examples include a pond, a desert, or a forest. Living things have special features called adaptations that help them survive in their habitat. For instance, a cactus has thick stems to store water and spines for protection in the desert. A polar bear has thick fur and fat to keep it warm in the cold Arctic. These adaptations allow organisms to find food, shelter, and avoid predators.

## Forces and Motion
A force is a push or a pull that can make an object move faster, slower, or change direction. Gravity is a force that pulls everything towards the Earth. Friction is a force that opposes motion, for example, when you rub your hands together, you feel heat due to friction. If you push a toy car on a flat surface, it will move. If the surface is rough, like carpet, friction will slow the car down much more quickly than on a smooth floor.

## The Sun and Earth
The Sun is a star at the centre of our solar system that provides light and heat. The Earth is a planet that orbits the Sun. It takes the Earth one year to complete one orbit around the Sun. While the Earth orbits the Sun, it also spins on its own axis. This spin is what causes day and night; the side facing the Sun has daylight, and the side facing away has night. The Moon orbits the Earth and is visible because it reflects the Sun's light.
