# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 12 Science

## Thermodynamics and Heat Engines
Students learn the first law of thermodynamics, which states that the change in internal energy of a system equals the heat added to the system minus the work done by the system (ΔU = Q - W). They apply this to heat engines, calculating thermal efficiency as the ratio of work output to heat input (Efficiency = W/Q_h). For example, a Carnot engine operating between a 500 K hot reservoir and a 300 K cold reservoir has a maximum theoretical efficiency of (500 - 300) / 500, or 40%. Students also analyse the irreversible increase of entropy in all real processes.

## Chemical Kinetics and Equilibrium
This topic explores reaction rates and factors affecting them. Students determine the rate of a reaction experimentally by measuring the concentration change of a reactant over time, for instance, monitoring the disappearance of sodium thiosulfate in an acidified solution. They apply the rate equation, Rate = k[A]^m[B]^n, to find the order with respect to each reactant and calculate the rate constant, k, using experimental data. The principles of Le Chatelier are used to predict how changing concentration, pressure, or temperature affects the position of dynamic equilibrium in reactions like the Haber process.

## Advanced Organic Chemistry
Students build upon their knowledge of organic functional groups, focusing on reaction mechanisms. They study nucleophilic substitution (SN1 and SN2) in halogenoalkanes, for example, how bromoethane reacts with aqueous sodium hydroxide to form ethanol. Addition polymerisation of ethene to form polyethylene is examined, alongside condensation polymerisation, such as the formation of nylon from 1,6-diaminohexane and adipoyl chloride. Students also identify isomers, including chain, position, and functional group isomers, and discuss their impact on physical properties like boiling point.

## Electricity and Magnetism
Learners apply Kirchhoff's laws to analyse complex DC circuits. They calculate the current through and potential difference across resistors in series and parallel combinations, using Ohm's Law (V = IR). The concept of magnetic flux is introduced, and Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction is used to calculate the induced electromotive force (EMF) in a coil. For example, if a coil with 100 turns experiences a magnetic field change from 0.2 T to 0.5 T in 0.1 seconds over an area of 0.01 m², the induced EMF is calculated as N × ΔΦ / Δt.

## Nuclear Physics
This module covers the properties of radioactive decay and nuclear reactions. Students identify the three types of radiation—alpha, beta, and gamma—and their characteristics, such as alpha particles being helium nuclei. They apply Einstein's mass-energy equivalence principle, E = mc², to calculate the energy released in nuclear fission or fusion reactions. For instance, calculating the energy released when a uranium-235 nucleus absorbs a neutron and splits, with a mass defect of 0.2 amu, yields an energy release of approximately 1.86 × 10⁻¹¹ joules.

## Genetics and Biotechnology
Students analyse patterns of inheritance, such as autosomal and sex-linked traits, using pedigree charts. They apply Mendel's laws to predict genotype and phenotype ratios in dihybrid crosses, for example, predicting a 9:3:3:1 ratio in a cross between heterozygous parents for two different genes. The basics of genetic engineering are covered, including the use of restriction enzymes to cut DNA and ligase to join it, exemplified by the insertion of the human insulin gene into bacteria to produce insulin for medical use.
