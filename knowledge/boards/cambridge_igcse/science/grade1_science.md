# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 1 Science
## Living Things and Their Needs
Children learn that all living things need food, water, air, and a suitable place to live to grow and stay healthy. They identify basic needs by observing familiar animals, such as pets needing food and water, and plants needing sunlight and water. Students classify objects as living (like a cat) or non-living (like a rock) based on these needs, understanding that living things grow and change over time. For example, a seed needs water and soil to grow into a plant.

## Materials and Their Properties
Children explore the different properties of everyday materials, such as wood, metal, plastic, and water. They learn to describe materials using simple words like hard, soft, smooth, rough, shiny, or dull. Through hands-on activities, students group materials based on these properties, sorting objects like a wooden block (hard, rough) and a metal spoon (hard, smooth). They understand that materials can be changed, like bending plastic or melting ice into water.

## Observing Weather and Seasons
Students observe daily weather conditions and learn to describe them using words like sunny, cloudy, rainy, windy, or cold. They use simple tools like a thermometer to measure temperature and record weather patterns over a week. Children connect seasonal changes to weather, noting that summer is hot and sunny, while winter is cold. They discuss how animals and plants respond to seasons, such as trees losing their leaves in autumn and birds building nests in spring.

## Forces and Movement
Children learn about pushes and pulls as simple forces that make things start or stop moving. They explore how forces change the shape of objects, like squishing play-dough or stretching a rubber band. Students identify forces in daily life, such as pushing a swing or pulling a toy car. They experiment with ramps to understand how the height affects how fast a ball rolls, learning that a steeper ramp creates a stronger force.

## Light and Sound
Students discover that light allows us to see and identify sources like the sun, a torch, or a lamp. They observe how light travels in straight lines and creates shadows when blocked. For sound, they learn it is made by vibrations and can travel through different materials like air, water, and tables. Children produce sounds using objects like drums, bells, or their own voices, distinguishing between loud and quiet sounds and high and low pitches.

## Our Changing Earth
Children explore how the Earth changes slowly, such as water wearing away rocks to make sand or wind shaping sand into dunes. They identify fast changes, like volcanic eruptions creating new land or earthquakes shaking the ground. Through observation, students learn about weathering by examining smooth, rounded rocks in a garden. They understand that these changes can happen over a long time, like a mountain being worn down by rain and wind.
