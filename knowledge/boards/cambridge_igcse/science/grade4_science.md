# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 4 Science
## The World of Living Things
Living things are called organisms and share common characteristics. They grow, reproduce, need food for energy, and respond to their environment. For example, a plant grows taller and its roots spread to find water. An animal, like a cat, moves to find food and runs when it is scared. All living things are made of tiny building blocks called cells. A single-celled organism, like an amoeba, is one complete living thing. Different organisms can be found in various habitats, such as a desert or a rainforest, and they are adapted to live there.

## Materials and Their Properties
Materials have different properties that make them useful. We can group materials by their properties like hardness, transparency, and whether they conduct heat or electricity. For example, glass is transparent, so we use it for windows. Wood is hard and we use it to make furniture. Metals are good conductors of heat, which is why we use a metal pot for cooking on a stove. Some materials, like plastic, can be molded into different shapes. Understanding these properties helps us choose the right material for a specific job, like building a strong bridge or keeping a drink cold.

## Energy and Electricity
Energy is the ability to do work or make things happen. It comes in different forms, like light, heat, sound, and electrical energy. We use electrical energy to power devices in our homes, such as lamps for light and kettles for boiling water. A simple electrical circuit is needed for electricity to flow. It requires a power source like a battery, wires to connect, and a component such as a light bulb to complete the path. If the circuit is broken, for example by a switch being turned off, the light bulb will not work.

## Forces and Motion
A force is a push or a pull that can make an object move, change its direction, or change its shape. Gravity is a force that pulls everything towards the centre of the Earth, which is why a ball falls when you drop it. Friction is a force that opposes motion, such as the feeling that slows down a toy car rolling on a carpet. When you kick a ball, you apply a force that makes it move. If you kick it harder, it will move faster because a bigger force causes a bigger change in motion.

## Earth and Space
The Earth is a planet that orbits the Sun. It has a solid surface made of rock, with large areas of land and water called oceans. The Earth also has an atmosphere, which is a layer of gases surrounding it that protects us from the Sun. The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite; it orbits the Earth and we see its light reflected from the Sun. We experience day and night because the Earth rotates on its axis once every 24 hours. The part facing the Sun has daylight, while the other side has night.

## Investigating Science
Scientists use a method to answer questions and learn about the world. This scientific method often starts with an observation, like noticing a plant in the shade is smaller than one in the sun. A question is asked, such as "Does sunlight help a plant grow?" A test is designed, for example, growing two plants and putting one in the sun and one in a dark cupboard. The results are recorded and compared to see if there is a pattern. This helps us understand the cause-and-effect relationship in the natural world.
