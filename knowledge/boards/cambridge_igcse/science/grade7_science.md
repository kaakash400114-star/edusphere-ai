# Cambridge IGCSE Grade 7 Science

## States of Matter
Students learn that matter exists as solids, liquids, and gases, with distinct properties based on particle arrangement and energy. Solids have a fixed shape and volume because their particles are tightly packed and vibrate. Liquids have a fixed volume but take the shape of their container due to particles that can move past one another. Gases fill any container as their particles are far apart and move freely. Changes of state, like melting or evaporation, are caused by adding or removing thermal energy, altering particle movement without changing the substance itself.

## Cells as Basic Units of Life
The cell is identified as the fundamental building block of all living organisms. Students study the key differences between animal and plant cells. Animal cells lack a rigid cell wall and chloroplasts, while plant cells possess both. The nucleus controls cellular activities, the mitochondrion generates energy through respiration, and the chloroplast enables photosynthesis in plants. Microscopes are essential tools for observing cells, revealing structures too small to be seen with the naked eye, allowing scientists to understand life at its most basic level.

## Forces and Motion
Forces are pushes or pulls that can change an object’s motion. Students explore how forces can start, stop, or change the direction and speed of an object. The concept of balanced and unbalanced forces is introduced; unbalanced forces result in acceleration. Gravity is a force of attraction that pulls objects towards the Earth's centre. Friction is a force that opposes motion between surfaces. For example, pushing a box across the floor requires a force greater than friction to make it move, demonstrating the relationship between forces and motion.

## Energy Forms and Transformations
Energy is the ability to do work and cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred or transformed. Students learn about different forms of energy, including kinetic (energy of movement), potential (stored energy), and chemical energy stored in food or fuels. Common energy transformations are studied, such as electrical energy being converted to light and heat in a light bulb. The principle of conservation of energy is fundamental: the total energy before a transformation is equal to the total energy after.

## Acids and Alkalis
Substances are classified as acidic, alkaline (or basic), or neutral based on their pH. Acids, like lemon juice or vinegar, taste sour and can turn blue litmus paper red. Alkalis, like soap or baking soda, feel slippery and can turn red litmus paper blue. Neutral substances, such as pure water, have a pH of 7 and do not affect litmus paper. The strength of an acid or alkali is measured on the pH scale from 0 to 14, with values below 7 being acidic and above 7 being alkaline.

## Introduction to Ecosystems
An ecosystem is a community of living organisms interacting with their non-living environment. Students learn about key components: producers (like plants), consumers (like animals), and decomposers (like fungi). Energy flows through an ecosystem via food chains, where one organism is eaten by another. Matter is recycled when decomposers break down dead organisms, returning nutrients to the soil. For example, a food chain might show grass being eaten by a rabbit, which is then eaten by a fox, illustrating the flow of energy.
