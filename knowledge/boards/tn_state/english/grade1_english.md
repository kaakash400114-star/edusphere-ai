# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 1 English

## Tamil-English alphabet sounds (phonics)

In Class 1, children learn to connect Tamil and English alphabet sounds with pictures and objects. They practice identifying the first sound of simple words, like ‘a’ for ‘apple’ and ‘அ’ for ‘அரிசி’. Students learn to distinguish between vowel sounds, such as ‘a’ as in ‘cat’ and ‘e’ as in ‘elephant’, and match them with corresponding Tamil letters like ‘அ’ and ‘எ’. They also practice basic blending of sounds to form simple words, such as ‘c-a-t’ becoming ‘cat’. The focus is on building a strong foundation for reading through playful and interactive phonics activities.

## Naming words around us

Children learn to identify and name common objects they see around them, like toys, books, and chairs. These naming words are called nouns, and they name people, animals, places, and things. Students practise by pointing to objects and saying their names correctly. They learn to use singular nouns for one object, such as a ball or a pencil. This helps them build their vocabulary and express themselves clearly when talking about their world.

## One and many

Children learn to distinguish between single items and groups of many items using common objects. For example, a pencil is one, but a bunch of bananas is many. They identify singular items like one mango or one chapati, and plural items like many books or many pencils. Students use simple phrases to describe quantities, such as “one cap” or “many toys”. They understand that the number one represents a single object, while numbers higher than one represent multiple objects. This concept is reinforced through activities like counting one frog or many frogs.

## A and an

In Grade 1 English, we learn to use 'a' and 'an' before singular nouns. We use 'a' before a word that starts with a consonant sound, like 'a cat' or 'a dog'. We use 'an' before a word that starts with a vowel sound, like 'an apple' or 'an egg'. We remember this rule to speak and write correctly. This helps us describe things in a simple and clear way. For example, we say 'a book' and 'an umbrella'.

## This-that, these-those

Children learn to use 'this' and 'that' to talk about one thing near them and one thing far away. They also learn to use 'these' and 'those' to talk about many things near them and many things far away. For example, they say "This is a pencil" for a pencil on their desk and "That is a book" for a book on the shelf. They say "These are pens" for pens in their hand and "Those are crayons" for crayons in a box on the floor. This helps them describe things clearly by saying how many things there are and how far away they are.

## Simple sentences with capital letter and full stop

In Class 1, children learn to write and read simple sentences. A simple sentence has one complete idea. It must start with a capital letter and end with a full stop. We write the first letter of the first word as a big letter, like A or B. The sentence stops at the full stop, like a red dot. For example, "The cat is big." starts with a capital T and ends with a dot. This rule helps us write neatly and read clearly. We practice many sentences like "I see a dog." and "It is a ball." to understand this important writing rule.

## Action words

Children learn about action words, also called verbs, which are words that show what someone or something is doing. They understand that action words describe actions like run, jump, eat, and sing. Children are taught to identify these words in simple sentences, such as "The cat runs" or "I eat food". They learn to use action words correctly to complete sentences and to express their own activities. For example, they can say "I walk to school" or "The bird flies in the sky". By the end of the lesson, children can recognise and use basic action words in their daily speech and writing.

## Rhymes and poems

In Class 1, children learn to enjoy and recite simple rhymes and poems with rhythm and repetition. They listen to and repeat rhyming words to understand the concept of similar sounds, such as "cat" and "mat". Through action songs and finger rhymes, they explore sounds, patterns, and new vocabulary like "rain" and "train". They memorise short poems with clear themes like animals or nature, using expressive voices and actions. This activity helps them develop language skills and a love for words in a fun and engaging way.

## Opposites

Children learn to identify and understand common opposite words. They discover how opposite words describe things that are very different from each other. For example, 'hot' and 'cold' are opposites because one means very warm and the other means very cool. Other pairs include 'big' and 'small', 'up' and 'down', and 'happy' and 'sad'. The children also learn to use these opposite words to make simple sentences. This helps them build a strong vocabulary and understand how words work together.

## Picture reading and speaking

Children learn to observe and describe pictures using simple words. They identify common objects, people, and actions in the pictures and say them aloud. Teachers help them form short, clear sentences like "I see a ball" or "The boy is running". Children also answer simple questions about what they see, such as "What is the colour of the cat?". They practice speaking in complete sentences using words they know from the picture. This activity helps them build vocabulary and improve their speaking skills.
