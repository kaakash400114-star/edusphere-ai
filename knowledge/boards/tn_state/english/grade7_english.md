# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 7 English

## Perfect tenses

In Class 7, we learn about perfect tenses to show actions that are finished or have a connection to the present. The present perfect tense is formed with has/have and the past participle, like "She has eaten her breakfast." The past perfect tense uses had and the past participle, for example, "They had played before the rain came." We also learn the future perfect tense, which will have and the past participle, such as "He will have finished his work by tomorrow." These tenses help us understand when actions happen and if they are complete.

## Modals

Students learn to use modal verbs like can, could, may, might, and must to express different ideas. They understand that modals come before the main verb and help show ability, permission, possibility, or necessity. For example, "I can run fast" shows ability, "You may go now" shows permission, and "We must finish our work" shows a rule or duty. Children also learn to form questions and negatives using modals, such as "Could you help me?" and "She cannot swim." They practice using these words in simple sentences to share what is possible, not possible, or what should be done.

## Voice: active-passive

In Class 7, students learn about active and passive voice in sentences. In active voice, the subject does the action, like "The dog chases the ball". In passive voice, the subject receives the action, and we use the verb "to be" with the past participle, like "The ball is chased by the dog". To change active to passive, move the object to the subject place, add "by" and the doer, and change the verb form. For example, "Ravi reads a book" becomes "A book is read by Ravi". Students also learn that passive voice is used when the doer is unknown or not important.

## Reported speech

In this lesson, you learn about reported speech, which is how we tell someone else what another person has said. When we change direct speech like "I am happy" to reported speech, we move the verb back: "He said that he was happy." We also change pronouns like "I" to "he" or "she," and time words like "now" to "then." For questions, we use "if" or "whether" and make the word order statement-like, like turning "Do you like apples?" into "She asked if I liked apples." These rules help us share what others said clearly and correctly.

## Clauses

In Class 7, we learn about clauses, which are small groups of words with a subject and a verb. A main clause makes complete sense by itself, like "The boy is playing football". An incomplete clause, called a subordinate clause, cannot stand alone and adds extra information, such as "when the bell rang". We understand that subordinate clauses often begin with words like because, if, or when. For example, "I am happy because it is my birthday" shows the main clause and the subordinate clause together. This helps us build better sentences and understand how different parts of a sentence connect.

## Determiners

In Class 7, we learn about determiners which are small words that come before nouns to make them clear. Determiners tell us if we are talking about one thing (the cat) or many things (the cats), a specific thing (that book) or any thing (a book). We also learn about using 'a', 'an' and 'the' correctly, like using 'a' for a consonant sound (a dog) and 'an' for a vowel sound (an apple). Some determiners answer questions like 'whose' (my toy) and 'which' (this book). This helps us speak and write English properly.

## Prepositions fine points

In Grade 7, you will learn how to use prepositions correctly in sentences. Prepositions are words like on, in, under, and between that show the relationship between nouns or pronouns and other words in a sentence. You will understand that a preposition must always be followed by a noun or a pronoun, for example, "The book is on the table" or "She is sitting between her friends". We will also look at prepositional phrases, such as "in the classroom" or "with a blue pen", which add more information to a sentence. Practising these will help you build clear and correct sentences.

## Editing errors

In Class 7, students learn to find and fix common errors in sentences like spelling mistakes, punctuation, and grammar rules. They practice putting commas in lists and after joining words like "and" or "but". Children also learn to check if sentences have a subject and a verb, and fix missing words like articles ("a", "an", "the"). They identify errors in verb tenses, such as changing "play" to "played" for past actions. For example, they correct "The cat sat on the mat" to "The cat sat on the mat" by fixing spelling or adding correct punctuation.

## Poetry-prose comprehension

In Grade 7, students learn to understand the differences between poetry and prose by identifying their unique features, such as rhyme and rhythm in poems and regular sentences in prose. They practice reading a text carefully to find the main idea and supporting details, whether it is a short poem or a prose passage. Students also learn to describe the feelings and images created by the writer’s words, like when a poem uses nature to show happiness. They answer questions based on the text to show that they have understood what they read. For example, they might find lines from a poem that tell us about a child’s love for their country.

## Story-dialogue-descriptive writing

In Class 7, students learn to write stories by creating clear characters and interesting plots using beginning, middle, and end parts. They practice writing dialogues between characters using correct punctuation like commas, quotation marks, and question marks to show who is speaking. Descriptive writing teaches them to use their senses—sight, sound, smell, touch, and taste—to make stories lively by adding details about places, people, and things. For example, instead of saying "a big house," they write "a colourful house with red doors and tall windows." Students also learn to connect dialogue and description smoothly to make their writing engaging and easy to read.
