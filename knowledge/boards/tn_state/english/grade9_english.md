# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 9 English

## Unseen comprehension passages

In Class 9, students learn to read and understand unseen passages by identifying the main idea, supporting details, and writer’s purpose. They practise finding keywords and phrases to answer questions directly from the text, such as who, what, when, where, and why. Students are taught to infer meanings from context, especially for new or difficult words, and to distinguish between facts and opinions. For example, in a passage about festivals, they must spot facts like dates and traditions and separate them from opinions like "the best celebration." They also learn to write short answers in their own words, keeping them clear and to the point.

## Descriptive paragraph and diary writing

In this topic, students learn to write descriptive paragraphs by using vivid details about people, places, or things, such as describing a friend’s smile or a busy market scene. They practice using sensory language like sight, sound, and smell to make their writing more interesting. For diary writing, they learn to record personal experiences and feelings in a simple, informal style, using words like “I felt” or “I saw” to share their day. Students also understand the importance of writing in a logical order, starting with a main idea and adding supporting details. Both types of writing help children express themselves clearly and creatively while following basic grammar rules.

## Story writing and picture composition

In Class 9, students learn to write stories and picture compositions using clear structures like a beginning, middle, and end. They practice creating interesting characters, settings, and a simple problem that gets solved, such as a lost puppy found by kind children. For picture compositions, they describe the scene step by step, using words to show actions and feelings, like "The boy smiled happily as he flew the kite." They also focus on using correct grammar and simple words to make their writing easy to understand, like writing about a festival or a school trip. These activities help them organise their thoughts and express ideas clearly.

## Tenses in depth

In Class 9, students learn to use tenses correctly by understanding the time an action happens—past, present, or future. They study the simple, continuous, perfect, and perfect continuous forms of each tense with specific structures, like "I am playing" for present continuous. They learn to change verbs based on the subject and time, for example, "She sang" (past) or "They will have finished" (future perfect). They practice forming sentences and stories using these tenses to express actions clearly and accurately. This helps them speak and write correctly without mistakes.

## Clauses and sentence transformation

In Class 9, you will learn to identify different kinds of clauses, like independent clauses which can stand alone as sentences and dependent clauses which need help to make complete sense. You will study about subordinate clauses, such as those starting with words like 'because', 'if', 'when', and 'that', and learn to join them to independent clauses correctly to form complex sentences. You will also practice changing simple sentences into complex ones and vice versa by adding or removing clauses, making sure your new sentences are always grammatically correct and meaningful. For example, you can change "The boy was sad" to "The boy who lost his book was sad" by adding a relative clause.

## Reported speech, voice and determiners

Students learn to change statements from direct speech to reported speech by moving the verb back in tense, like turning "I am happy" into "he said he was happy". They understand how to turn questions and commands into reported forms, such as changing "Where do you live?" to "he asked where I lived". Active and passive voice are introduced, showing how the subject and object swap places, like changing "The boy kicks the ball" to "the ball is kicked by the boy". Determiners like 'a', 'an', 'the', 'this', 'these', and 'those' are taught to show whether nouns are specific or general, as in "I ate an apple" versus "I ate the apple". Examples from daily life help children see how these rules work in simple conversations and writing.

## Literature: prose themes and characters

Students learn to identify and explain the main themes like friendship, courage, and justice from prose stories. They study how characters are developed through their words, actions, and thoughts, for example, R.K. Narayan’s Swami and his friends. Children analyse how the plot is built with a clear beginning, middle, and end to show these ideas. They also practice summarising the main events of a story in their own simple words. Finally, students discuss what the author wants to teach us through the characters and their experiences.

## Poetry: central idea and devices

Students learn to find the central idea, or main message, of a poem by reading it carefully and thinking about what the poet wants to tell us. We also study common poetic devices like similes, which compare two things using "like" or "as" (for example, "as brave as a lion"), and metaphors, which say one thing is another thing (for example, "the classroom was a zoo"). Rhyme and rhythm are important because they make the poem sound musical and help us remember the words, like in "Twinkle, twinkle, little star." By understanding these parts, we can better appreciate the beauty and meaning of poems.

## Vocabulary building

Students learn to build their vocabulary by understanding prefixes, suffixes, and root words, which help them figure out the meaning of new words. They practice forming compound words by joining two simple words, like "sun" + "flower" to make "sunflower". Students also expand their word knowledge by learning synonyms, such as "happy" and "joyful", and antonyms, like "hot" and "cold". They are introduced to homophones, words that sound the same but have different meanings and spellings, for example, "pair" and "pear". Additionally, they learn to use context clues from sentences to guess the meaning of unfamiliar words, making their reading and writing stronger.

## Speaking and listening skills

In Class 9, students learn to speak clearly and listen carefully in different situations. They practice expressing their ideas in a group discussion by taking turns and respecting others' opinions. They also learn to listen to a friend's story and ask questions to understand better. When speaking in front of the class, students are taught to stand straight, speak loudly, and make eye contact. For example, they might give a short talk about their favourite book or a festival they celebrate. These skills help them share their thoughts confidently and understand what others are saying.
