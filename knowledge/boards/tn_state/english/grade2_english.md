# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 2 English

## Phonics blends and sight words

Children learn to blend consonant sounds together, like in "bl" from "blue" and "st" from "star". They also recognise common sight words that cannot be easily sounded out, such as "the", "and", and "is". This helps them read simple sentences and stories more smoothly. They practice these blends through word patterns like "bl", "cl", "fl", and "gl" in words like "block", "clap", "flag", and "glow". By mastering these sounds and words, children build a strong foundation for reading and writing.

## Naming words: common and special

Children learn to name things around them using common nouns like ‘chair’, ‘table’, and ‘book’, which are general naming words. They also learn special nouns, or proper nouns, which name specific people, places, or things, like ‘Ravi’, ‘Chennai’, and ‘Diwali’. Common nouns do not start with a capital letter unless they are at the beginning of a sentence, while proper nouns always start with a capital letter. For example, ‘dog’ is a common noun, but ‘Buddy’ is a special noun. This helps children talk about things in their world clearly.

## Describing words

Children learn about words that describe people, animals, things, and places. These describing words tell us more about a noun and are called adjectives. For example, we say a "big ball" instead of just a ball, or a "red rose" instead of just a rose. Describing words make our sentences more interesting and clear. They come before the noun they describe, helping us imagine what something looks, feels, or sounds like like the "soft teddy" or the "loud bell".

## Action words and tenses (is/are, -ed)

In this topic, children learn about action words (verbs) and how to use 'is' and 'are' correctly. We use 'is' for one person or thing, like 'The cat is sleeping'. We use 'are' for two or more people or things, like 'The birds are flying'. They also learn to add '-ed' to show that an action happened in the past, like 'walk' becomes 'walked' or 'play' becomes 'played'. For example, 'I walked to school yesterday' or 'She played with her friend'. These simple rules help children talk about what is happening now and what happened before.

## Am-is-are, has-have

In Class 2, we learn to use 'am', 'is', and 'are' to describe what we are doing or what someone is doing. We say "I am happy" and "She is playing". We also use 'has' and 'have' to talk about things people own. For example, "He has a bag" and "You have a book". We remember that 'I' always uses 'am', 'he', 'she', and 'it' use 'is', and 'you', 'we', and 'they' use 'are'. For 'has' and 'have', 'he', 'she', and 'it' use 'has', while 'I', 'you', 'we', and 'they' use 'have'.

## Position words: in-on-under

In Class 2, children learn to use position words like 'in', 'on', and 'under' to describe where objects are placed. The word 'in' is used for something inside another object, like a toy kept in a box. The word 'on' shows an object is over a surface, such as a book kept on the table. The word 'under' means something is below another thing, like a ball kept under the chair. Students practice these words by identifying and naming the position of different objects in their classroom or at home. These words help them give clear and simple instructions in their daily life.

## Plurals with -s and -es

We learn to make words show more than one person or thing. We add the letter ‘s’ to the end of most words, like ‘cats’ for one cat and two cats. When a word ends with ‘s’, ‘sh’, ‘ch’, ‘x’, or ‘z’, we add ‘es’, such as ‘buses’ for one bus and two buses. A word ending in a consonant and a ‘y’ changes to ‘ies’, like ‘babies’ for one baby and two babies. These special endings help us talk about many things together.

## A-an-the

Children learn to use 'a', 'an', and 'the' before nouns to talk about things. We use 'a' before a word that starts with a consonant sound, like 'a cat'. We use 'an' before a word that starts with a vowel sound, like 'an apple'. We use 'the' when we talk about a special or particular thing, like 'the red car'. We also use 'the' when we talk about something we already know about, like 'the book' on the table.

## Capital letters and stops

Children learn to use capital letters for the first letter of their own name and the names of people like their father or mother. They also use capital letters to begin the first word of a sentence. Full stops are used to mark the end of a sentence, like when they write "I am a boy." or "She is my friend.". They practice writing short sentences with a capital letter at the beginning and a full stop at the end. This helps them write clearly and correctly.

Children learn to start sentences with capital letters. The first letter of a name like ramesh or a place like chennai is also a capital letter. We use full stops to show where one sentence ends and another starts. Every sentence must begin with a capital letter and end with a full stop. For example,ramesh runs. The cat sleeps.

## Reading and picture writing

In this topic, children learn to connect spoken words with written words by reading simple sentences. They identify letters and their sounds to decode short words like “cat” and “dog”. They also practice writing by drawing pictures to tell stories, such as a sun with a smile for a happy day. Children learn to sequence pictures in order, like first a seed, then a plant, to show how things grow. They use their drawings to share ideas, like drawing a family and writing simple words to name them. This helps them understand that pictures and words work together to tell stories.
