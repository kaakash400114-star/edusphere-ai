# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 8 English

## Determiners-quantifiers

In Class 8, we learn about determiners like many, much, few, and little to count or measure nouns. We use 'many' and 'few' with countable nouns, such as "many books" or "few pencils", while 'much' and 'little' go with uncountable nouns, like "much water" or "little sugar". We also learn to choose between 'a few' and 'the few', or 'a little' and 'the little', to talk about some specific quantity. For example, "a few apples" means some apples, but "the few apples" means all the remaining apples. This helps us talk about things more clearly when we give or share items.

## Tense narration

In Class 8, we learn to tell stories using the correct tenses like past, present, and future. We remember that the past tense tells what already happened, such as "She walked to school." For the present tense, we use words for today's actions, like "He reads his book." The future tense talks about what will happen next, for example, "They will play cricket tomorrow." We practice changing sentences between these tenses to keep our stories clear and interesting. This helps us write and speak about events in the right order of time.

## Passive-reported mastery

Students learn to change sentences from active to passive voice by moving the object to the subject's place and using the correct form of the verb ‘be’ with the main verb, such as ‘is’, ‘are’, or ‘was’. They understand that the doer of the action is either not mentioned or added after a preposition like ‘by’. For example, “The teacher writes a letter” becomes “A letter is written by the teacher.” This helps them focus more on the action or the object rather than the person performing it. They practice using the passive voice in different tenses, including the past and future, to make their sentences more varied.

## Clauses: complex sentences

A complex sentence has one main clause, which makes complete sense, and one or more subordinate clauses, which cannot stand alone. A subordinate clause begins with words like because, since, although, when, if, or that. It answers questions like why, when, where, or how about the main clause. For example, "The boy who has a red ball is my friend" has the main clause "The boy is my friend" and the subordinate clause "who has a red ball". Similarly, "We went to the park because it was sunny" has the main clause "We went to the park" and the subordinate clause "because it was sunny".

## Non-finites

In Class 8, we learn about non-finite verbs, which are action words that do not show tense or act as the main verb in a sentence. The three types are infinitives, gerunds, and participles. Infinitives are words formed by adding 'to' before the base verb, like "to run" or "to play". Gerunds are formed by adding '-ing' to a verb and function as nouns, for example, "Swimming is fun". Participles also end in '-ing' or '-ed' and are used like adjectives to describe nouns, such as "the barking dog" or "a broken window". We practise identifying and using these non-finite verbs to make our sentences more interesting and correct.

## Modals deduction

Students learn about modal verbs like must, might, and could for making deductions about present or past events based on evidence. When deducing something is true now, we use ‘must be’ for sure conclusions and ‘might be’ or ‘could be’ for possibilities. For example, if the ground is wet, we say ‘It must have rained’ because we are sure. If something is possible but not certain, we use ‘might have’ or ‘could have’, like ‘She might have gone to the market’ if her bag is missing. These structures help us talk about logical guesses using the clues we see around us.

## Prefixes-suffixes-idioms

In Class 8, you will learn about prefixes, suffixes, and idioms to improve your language skills. A prefix is a small word added to the beginning of a root word to change its meaning, for example, 'un-' in 'unhappy' makes the word mean not happy. A suffix is a small word added to the end of a root word to change its tense or type, for example, '-ing' in 'playing' shows an action happening now. Idioms are phrases that have a special meaning different from their individual words, like 'break a leg' which means good luck. By studying these, you will become better at understanding and using new words in sentences.

## Literary devices

In class 8, students learn to identify common literary devices like simile, metaphor, and personification. A simile compares two things using "like" or "as", for example, "as brave as a lion". A metaphor says something is something else, like "time is money". Personification gives human qualities to non-living things, such as "the wind whispered". They also learn about alliteration, which is the repetition of the same sound at the beginning of words, like "Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers". Understanding these devices helps students appreciate how writers make their stories and poems interesting.

## Formal letter-email-article-speech

In Grade 8 English, students learn to write formal letters, emails, articles, and speeches using correct formats and polite language. They study the structure of a formal letter, including the sender’s address, date, receiver’s address, subject, salutation, body, closing, and signature. For emails, they practice writing clear subject lines and using proper greetings like “Dear Sir/Madam.” In articles, they focus on writing for a school magazine with an introduction, main points, and a conclusion. Students also learn to deliver short speeches on simple topics like “My Favourite Book” using a clear beginning, middle, and end.

## Listening-speaking

In Grade 8, students learn to listen attentively to stories, announcements, and dialogues, identifying the main ideas and important details. They practise speaking clearly in front of the class, using simple and correct sentences to share their own experiences or express their opinions on topics like ‘My Favourite Book’ or ‘Caring for the Environment’. Students are taught to use common polite expressions such as ‘please’, ‘thank you’, and ‘excuse me’ during conversations. They also participate in group discussions, taking turns to speak and listening carefully to what others say. For example, they might describe a picture using adjectives like ‘big’, ‘green’, and ‘happy’.
