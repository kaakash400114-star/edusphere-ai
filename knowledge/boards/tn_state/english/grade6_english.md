# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 6 English

## Nouns-pronouns in use

In this topic, children learn to identify nouns, which are names of people, animals, places, and things, such as "Ravi", "cat", "school", and "book". They also understand pronouns, which are words like "I", "you", "he", "she", and "it" that replace nouns to avoid repetition. Students practice using pronouns correctly in sentences, for example, saying "Ravi has a cat. It is black" instead of "Ravi has a cat. The cat is black". The lessons help them see how nouns and pronouns work together to make sentences clear and short. This builds a strong foundation for better speaking and writing.

## Tenses simple-continuous

Students learn to use simple present and present continuous tenses to talk about everyday actions. Simple present tense is for regular habits, like "I go to school every day." Present continuous tense shows what is happening now, using am, is, or are with the verb ending in -ing, for example, "She is reading a book." They understand the difference between actions that always happen and actions happening at this moment. We also practice simple past and past continuous tenses to describe completed actions and ongoing actions in the past, such as "I played football yesterday" and "He was sleeping at 8 o'clock.

## Adverbs-prepositions

In Class 6, students learn about adverbs and prepositions to make their sentences better and clearer. Adverbs are words that describe verbs, telling us how, when, or where an action happens, like “He runs quickly” or “They play here.” Prepositions are small words that show the relationship between a noun or pronoun and other words in the sentence, such as “The book is on the table” or “She went to the park.” Students also identify prepositions in phrases, like “in the box” or “under the bed,” to understand how they connect ideas. By practicing with simple examples, children learn to use these words correctly to improve their writing and speaking skills.

## Conjunctions

In Class 6, you will learn about conjunctions, which are words that join sentences or parts of sentences. The main conjunctions are ‘and’, ‘but’, ‘or’, ‘so’, and ‘because’. You use ‘and’ to add information, ‘but’ to show contrast, ‘or’ to give choices, ‘so’ to show a result, and ‘because’ to give a reason. For example, you can say, "I like to play cricket and football," or "She was hungry so she ate a banana." You will practice using these words to make your sentences clear and interesting.

## Sentences: phrases-clauses

In Class 6, we learn to find parts of a sentence called phrases and clauses. A phrase is a group of words that makes sense but has no subject and verb, like "on the table" or "in the garden." A clause is a group of words that has a subject and a verb, and it can sometimes make a small sentence by itself, like "The dog barks." There are two kinds of clauses: independent, which can stand alone like "I eat rice," and dependent, which needs an independent clause to make sense, like "when I am hungry." We practice finding these parts in sentences to understand how they work together to tell a story.

## Punctuation mastery

In Class 6, children learn to use punctuation marks like full stops, question marks, and exclamation marks correctly at the end of sentences. They also study commas to separate items in a list and to make the meaning of a sentence clear. For example, in the sentence, "Raju bought apples, bananas, and oranges," the commas help us understand the different things Raju purchased. Students practice using apostrophes to show possession, such as in "Priya's book," which means the book belongs to Priya. Mastering punctuation helps them write correctly and read with better understanding.

## Comprehension

In Class 6, students learn to read and understand short stories, poems, and passages carefully. They practice finding the main idea and details in a text, like identifying the main character and what they do in a story. Students also learn to answer questions about who, what, when, where, why, and how from the information given. They understand simple words and their meanings in the context of the sentence, such as "The sun is hot" where "hot" describes the sun. Finally, they learn to make simple inferences, like guessing how a character feels based on their actions.

## Informal letter-application

In this topic, children learn to write informal letters to friends or family using simple language and friendly tone. They learn to include the sender’s address on the top left corner, followed by the date, and the receiver’s address below the date. The letter must begin with a greeting like "My dear friend" or "Dear mummy" and end with a closing such as "Your loving friend" or "Yours sincerely." Children practice writing short messages about daily activities, like sharing news about school or asking about a relative’s health, ensuring the content is clear and easy to understand.

## Story writing

Children learn to write a simple story by thinking of a main idea and small events that happen before and after. A good story has a beginning, middle, and end, like a small plant with roots, stem, and flowers. They must write short sentences with full stops and use easy words to describe the characters and what happens. For example, a story can be about a lost puppy finding its way home with the help of a kind boy. They practice making their story interesting by adding one or two small surprises.

## Speaking fluency

Children learn to speak fluently by using clear words and simple sentences. They practice speaking in short turns, like when sharing a story or answering a question. They also learn to speak with proper speed and volume, so others can hear them well. For example, they might introduce themselves by saying, "My name is Rahul and I like to play cricket." Teachers help them by giving time to think and not interrupting. The goal is to make talking easy and fun for everyone.
