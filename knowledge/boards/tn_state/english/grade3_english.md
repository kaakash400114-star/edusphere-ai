# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 3 English

## Nouns: common-proper-collective

In this topic, children learn about different types of nouns: common, proper, and collective. Common nouns name general things like "boy," "tree," or "school," while proper nouns name specific people, places, or things, such as "Ravi," "Chennai," or "Mount Road." Collective nouns are words that describe a group of the same kind, like "team," "flock," or "family." They also understand that proper nouns always begin with a capital letter, like "Tamil Nadu" or "Ganges River." By practising with examples, children can tell the difference between naming something general and naming something special.

## Pronouns

Children learn to use words like I, you, he, she, it, we, and they to replace nouns in sentences. They understand that pronouns make speaking and writing simpler by not repeating the same name many times. For example, they learn to say “Ravi likes football. He plays every day” instead of “Ravi likes football. Ravi plays every day”. Children also practice using pronouns for people, animals, and things, such as using “it” for a cat or “they” for friends. This helps them speak and write in short, clear sentences every day.

## Adjectives and comparison

In Class 3, children learn to identify and use adjectives to describe nouns, such as "big ball" or "red kite". They compare nouns using the comparative form, adding "-er" to short words like "taller" or "smaller". For longer words, they use "more" before the adjective, like "more beautiful" or "more interesting". Superlatives are introduced to show the highest degree, using "-est" for short words like "tallest" or "biggest", and "most" for longer ones like "most colourful". Examples like "An elephant is bigger than a dog" help them understand comparisons clearly.

## Verbs: -ing actions

Children learn about action words called verbs, which show what someone or something is doing. We add "-ing" to the verb to show that the action is happening right now, like in "running" or "playing". For example, the verb "run" becomes "running" and "play" becomes "playing". We use these words in sentences to talk about activities, such as "The boy is playing football" or "The cat is running fast". This helps children describe actions that are taking place at the moment of speaking.

## Simple past

Students learn to talk about actions that happened in the past using simple past tense. They add ‘-ed’ to the end of action words like play, jump, and walk to show the action is finished, for example, "I played football" or "She walked to school". They also learn to use ‘was’ and ‘were’ for past sentences like "I was happy" or "They were friends". Students understand that these words help us share stories and things we did before now. They practice by making sentences about their own day, such as "I ate lunch" or "I drew a picture".

## Adverbs of manner

Adverbs of manner tell us how an action is done, like quickly, slowly, or happily. They usually come after the verb, as in "The dog runs fast." Children learn to pick out these words to describe how someone does something. For example, in "She sings loudly," the word "loudly" tells us how she sings. They also practice using these words in their own sentences, like "He walked carefully." These words help make sentences more interesting and clear.

## Prepositions

Prepositions are small words like 'in', 'on', and 'under' that tell us where something is or when it happens. We use 'in' for things inside a place, like "The cat is in the box." We use 'on' for things on a surface, for example, "The book is on the table." We also use 'under' for things below something, such as "The ball is under the chair." These words help us make our sentences clear and show the relationship between objects. Learning prepositions helps us talk about the world around us in a better way.

## Commas and apostrophes

Children learn to use commas to separate words in a list, like "apples, bananas, and grapes". They also use commas to separate days and names, such as "Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday" or "Ravi, Sita, and Anu". Apostrophes are used to show small words missing, like "don't" for "do not". They are also used to show things belonging to someone, like "Priya's book" or "the cat's toy". These small marks help make reading and writing clear and easy to understand.

## Reading a passage

In Class 3, children learn to read short passages with simple sentences and familiar words. They practice reading aloud clearly, focusing on correct pronunciation and stopping at full stops. They also learn to find the main idea of a passage, like what the story is mostly about, and identify key details such as the names of people or places. For example, in a passage about a boy and his dog, they can point out who the boy is and what the dog does. This helps them understand what they read and share the main points with their friends.

## Paragraph writing

Children learn to write small paragraphs with two to three sentences about a simple topic like "my pet" or "my school". A paragraph begins with a capital letter and ends with a full stop. Each sentence must make sense and talk about the same thing, like describing what their pet looks like and what it does. They use connecting words such as "and" and "but" to join ideas together. For example, "I have a dog. It is brown and white. It plays with me every day." This helps them share their ideas clearly on paper.
