# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 4 English

## Countable-uncountable nouns

Children learn to countable nouns are things we can count, like one mango, two pencils, or three books. They also learn uncountable nouns are things we cannot count, like rice, water, or sugar. For countable nouns, we use words such as ‘a’, ‘an’, ‘many’, ‘few’, and numbers, for example, ‘a dog’ or ‘many apples’. For uncountable nouns, we use words like ‘much’, ‘little’, and ‘some’, like ‘some water’ or ‘little rice’. Children practice using these words to talk about things correctly in sentences.

## Reflexive pronouns

In Class 4, we learn about reflexive pronouns like myself, yourself, himself, and ourselves. These pronouns are used when the subject and the object of a sentence are the same person or thing. For example, in the sentence “I made myself a sandwich,” the subject “I” and the object “myself” refer to the same person. Reflexive pronouns are formed by adding self or selves to certain pronouns, such as myself (from I) and themselves (from they). We use them to show that the action of the verb comes back to the subject. For instance, “She hurt herself” means the action of hurting was done by her to herself.

## Adjective degrees

In Class 4, we learn about adjective degrees to compare things. We use the positive degree for one thing, like "a big house". The comparative degree compares two things, like "a bigger house". For more than two things, we use the superlative degree, like "the biggest house". We add "er" for short words and "more" for long words to make comparisons. Some words change their spelling, like "good" becomes "better" and "best". We use these degrees to describe how things are different from each other.

## Present and past continuous

In Class 4, we learn about the present and past continuous tenses to describe actions happening now or were in progress some time ago. We use the present continuous with a form of 'be' and the verb ending in '-ing', like 'He is playing football'. For the past continuous, we use 'was' or 'were' with the '-ing' verb, for example, 'They were eating mangoes'. These tenses help us talk about actions that are not finished yet, whether happening right now or in the past. We practice making sentences to talk about what people are doing at this moment or were doing earlier.

## Will and going to

In Grade 4, children learn to use ‘will’ and ‘going to’ to talk about the future. ‘Will’ is used for instant decisions or promises, like “I will help you with your homework” or “It will rain soon.” ‘Going to’ is used for plans or things we can see are about to happen, like “I am going to play cricket this evening” or “Look at the dark clouds, it is going to rain.” They understand that ‘will’ comes after a subject, for example, “She will come to school,” while ‘going to’ uses ‘am/is/are’ before the subject, like “He is going to write a story.” This helps them share their future ideas clearly.

## Adverb kinds

In Class 4, children learn about different kinds of adverbs that tell us more about actions. Adverbs of time answer the question "when," such as "yesterday," "today," or "soon." Adverbs of place tell us "where," like "here," "there," or "inside." Adverbs of manner explain "how" an action is done, for example, "quickly," "slowly," or "happily." We also learn about adverbs of degree, such as "very" or "too," which tell us about the amount of something. These words help make our sentences more interesting and clear by giving more details.

## Conjunctions: and-but-because

In Class 4, students learn to use the conjunctions 'and', 'but', and 'because' to join two short sentences into one long sentence. The word 'and' is used to add similar ideas, like "I like to play and I like to eat fruits." The word 'but' is used to show a difference or contrast, for example, "The sun is hot but the water is cool." Finally, 'because' is used to give a reason for an action, such as "She went to school because it was her birthday." These small words help make sentences longer and more interesting.

## Question-exclamation punctuation

In Class 4, we learn to use question marks and exclamation marks correctly in sentences. A question mark (?) is placed at the end of a sentence that asks something, like “What is your name?”. An exclamation mark (!) is used for sentences that show strong feelings, such as “Wow! This is a big cake!”. We also practice writing our own questions and exclamations to share our thoughts and feelings. Knowing when to use these helps us read and write better.

## Comprehension skills

In Class 4, children learn to read sentences and short stories carefully to understand the meaning. They learn to find the main idea, which is the important message the writer wants to tell. They also learn to find small details, like names of people and places, from the text. Children practice answering questions by using information directly from the story, like what happened first or what a character did. For example, after reading a story, they can say who the main character is or where the story takes place.

## Letter and notice writing

In Class 4, children learn to write simple letters and notices with proper format and clear language. They understand the structure of a letter, including the sender’s address, date, salutation, body of the letter, closing, and signature. For example, they write a letter to a friend inviting them to a birthday party, using words like “Dear” and “Yours sincerely.” Notices are taught to be short and direct, giving important information like a school event or a holiday notice. Children learn to write a notice for a class picnic, including the date, time, and things to carry. They focus on being polite and using correct spelling and punctuation in all their writing.
