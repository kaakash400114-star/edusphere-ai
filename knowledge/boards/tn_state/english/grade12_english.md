# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 12 English

## Note-making-summaries

In Class 12, students learn to make concise summaries by extracting the main idea and essential supporting details from a passage. They use abbreviations, symbols, and headings to capture information efficiently while ensuring the original meaning is preserved. For example, they might shorten “agriculture” to “agri” or use “→” for “leads to.” A good summary is always written in the student’s own words and is shorter than the original text. The rules include selecting key facts, ignoring examples and minor details, and organising points logically. Students practice by summarising paragraphs, essays, and longer texts to improve comprehension and study skills.

## Notices-invitations

In Class 12, students learn to compose formal notices and invitations with accurate details and proper formats, ensuring clarity for the intended audience. A notice must include a concise title, the date, the issuing authority, a brief message in simple language, and the signature of the person in charge, as seen in school announcements about exams or events. Invitations, whether printed or digital, require specific information like the event name, date, time, venue, and a polite request for attendance, often followed by RSVP details. Students practice structuring these texts formally, using correct spellings and grammar, and tailoring the tone to suit the occasion, such as a formal college function or a community gathering. They also learn to include essential elements like the subject, sender’s name, and contact information to make the communication effective.

## Letters-job applications

Students learn to write formal job applications, which are structured letters to apply for employment opportunities. A job application letter must include a clear subject line, the applicant’s address, the date, the employer’s address, a proper salutation like "Respected Sir/Madam", a body paragraph stating the position applied for, highlighting relevant qualifications and skills, and an expression of willingness to attend an interview. It should end with a formal closing such as "Yours faithfully" or "Yours sincerely", followed by the applicant’s name and signature. Students are taught to use polite and professional language throughout the letter to make a good impression on potential employers.

## Articles-reports

Students learn to write articles and reports for different purposes, following specific formats and structures. For articles, they understand how to write a catchy headline, an introduction that grabs attention, body paragraphs with main points and details, and a conclusion. Reports are formal and factual, teaching students to include a title, headings, subheadings, and simple tables or lists to present information clearly. They learn to use objective language and avoid personal opinions, for example, writing a report on a school event or an article on the importance of trees. The focus is on organising information logically and writing in a clear, simple style suitable for the intended audience.

## Flamingo prose

In the Flamingo prose section for Class 12, students learn to analyse and interpret literary texts by identifying themes, characters, and narrative techniques. They study how authors use language devices like symbolism, irony, and foreshadowing to convey deeper meanings, as seen in stories that explore social issues and human emotions. Students also practise writing critical essays, using textual evidence to support their interpretations and arguments. The curriculum includes classic works where they examine character development and plot structure, such as understanding a protagonist’s journey through various challenges. This develops their ability to think critically and express ideas clearly in both written and verbal forms.

## Flamingo poetry

In the Flamingo poetry unit, students learn to analyse and appreciate various poetic forms such as the sonnet, lyric, and narrative poem, focusing on elements like rhyme scheme, metre, and figurative language including similes, metaphors, and personification. They study specific poems to understand how poets convey emotions and themes like patriotism, nature, and human relationships, for instance, identifying the iambic pentameter in Shakespearean sonnets or the vivid imagery in Robert Frost’s works. Students also develop skills to interpret poetic devices and structure, learning to explain how these elements enhance meaning and beauty in a poem. Through guided analysis and discussion, they explore the cultural and historical contexts of the poetry, deepening their comprehension and appreciation of literary art.

## Vistas

In Class 12, the "Vistas" text explores modern literature through a selection of prose works that develop critical reading and analytical skills. Students learn to identify central themes such as human values, social issues, and moral dilemmas within narratives like "The Tiger King" or "On the Face of It," where authors use irony and symbolism to convey deeper meanings. The curriculum requires students to understand narrative techniques, including characterisation, plot structure, and point of view, as seen in stories with multiple perspectives. They also practice textual analysis by examining figurative language such as metaphors and satire to interpret the writer's intent and message in a given context. Additionally, students engage with vocabulary expansion through context-based learning, identifying and using new words from the prose sections to improve their language proficiency.

## Creative-analytical writing

In creative-analytical writing, students learn to blend imaginative expression with logical reasoning by writing detailed essays, stories, and reviews that combine personal ideas with critical analysis of literature. They explore how to use figurative language like similes and metaphors to make writing vivid while also applying structured arguments to support their viewpoints, such as analysing a poem’s theme or comparing characters from a novel. Students practice crafting clear introductions, well-developed body paragraphs with evidence from texts, and strong conclusions to present their thoughts coherently and persuasively. They also learn to evaluate texts critically by identifying the author’s purpose, tone, and style in various literary works like plays or short stories. This skill helps them express their creativity thoughtfully while strengthening their ability to think deeply about written content.

## Language refinement

Students refine their language skills by learning advanced grammar rules, such as identifying and correctly using complex sentence structures like conditional and compound-complex sentences. They enhance their vocabulary by studying word formation, including prefixes, suffixes, and synonyms, and practice using them in context to avoid repetition. Punctuation rules, particularly the use of colons, semicolons, and apostrophes, are taught to improve clarity in writing. Additionally, students learn to edit and revise their work by spotting common errors in subject-verb agreement, tense consistency, and word order. For example, they learn to change "He go to school" to "He goes to school" for grammatical correctness.

## Exam strategy

Students learn to plan their time wisely by allocating specific minutes to each question based on its marks. They understand the importance of reading all instructions carefully before starting to write answers, like underlining keywords. A key strategy is to attempt all questions, starting with the ones they know best to build confidence, leaving difficult ones for later. For essays, they learn to plan their answer by noting down three main points to ensure their response is clear and well-structured. This helps them present their ideas in a logical order and avoid missing important details.
