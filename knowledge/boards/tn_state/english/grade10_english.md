# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 10 English

## Discursive passage comprehension

In discursive passage comprehension, students learn to identify the main idea and supporting details in texts that present different viewpoints on a topic. They are taught to recognise arguments for and against an issue, such as the advantages and disadvantages of using mobile phones in school. The lesson focuses on understanding how authors structure their opinions using facts, examples, and reasoning. Students practice by summarising both sides of an argument in their own simple words, like explaining why plastic is useful but also why it harms the environment.

## Formal letter writing

Students learn to write formal letters like applications and complaints using a correct structure, including sender’s address, date, receiver’s address, subject, salutation, body, and closing. They must use polite language, avoid slang, and end with "Yours faithfully" when the name is unknown or "Yours sincerely" when the name is known. For example, a letter to the principal requesting a library card includes all these parts and respectful phrases like "I would be grateful if you could kindly issue me a library card." They practice writing within a word limit and ensuring the main points are clearly and concisely presented.

## Analytical paragraph writing

Students learn to write an analytical paragraph by examining a text’s ideas, characters, or themes closely. They start with a clear topic sentence that states a specific point, followed by evidence like a quote or example from the story to support it. Next, they explain how the evidence proves their point, showing their understanding. Finally, they end the paragraph by summarising their thoughts or linking them to the main idea. For example, for a character like Rama, they might write, "Rama shows bravery when he faces Ravana because he fights to rescue Sita, which proves his courage." This helps them think deeply about what they read.

## Grammar: gap filling and error correction

In Class 10, students learn to fill in blanks and correct errors in sentences using grammar rules like subject-verb agreement, tenses, and prepositions. For gap filling, they choose the right word (e.g., "The children *are* playing football" instead of "is") to make sentences correct. For error correction, they spot and fix mistakes like incorrect pronouns ("Me and him went there" → "He and I went there") or articles ("He is an honest man" → "He is a honest man"). They also learn to use conjunctions to join words and sentences properly.

## Reported speech and voice transformations

In reported speech, we change the words of a speaker into a report using reporting verbs like said or told. We shift the tense of the original sentence back one step, like changing present simple to past simple, and adjust pronouns such as "I" to "he" or "she." For questions, we use words like if or whether and turn the question structure into a statement. Commands become requests using "to" or "not to," like asking someone "to close the door." By practicing these rules, students learn to report conversations accurately and clearly.

## Prose: extract-based questions

In this topic, students learn to answer extract-based questions by carefully reading a given prose passage and finding answers within the text. They practice locating specific information, such as characters, events, or descriptions, to respond to factual questions. Students also learn to infer meanings by understanding the context and implied ideas when direct answers are not available. For example, if the passage describes a character as "very brave," students must explain how their actions show bravery. They are taught to write short, complete answers in simple sentences, ensuring they stay close to the passage and do not add extra information.

## Poetry: devices and central idea

Students learn to identify poetic devices such as simile, metaphor, personification, and alliteration to understand the meaning of poems. A simile compares two things using 'like' or 'as', for example, 'as brave as a lion'. A metaphor directly compares things without 'like' or 'as', such as 'time is money'. Personification gives human qualities to non-living things, like 'the wind whispered'. The central idea is the main message or theme of the poem, which students find by analysing these devices and the poem's content. By studying these elements, students learn how poets use language effectively to express their thoughts and feelings.

## Supplementary reader questions

In Class 10, students learn to answer questions about supplementary reader stories. These questions test understanding of the story, characters, and events. Students also learn to find answers directly from the text or use clues to infer meaning. They practice writing short answers in simple sentences using words from the story. For example, they might be asked to describe a character or explain why an action happened. The focus is on careful reading and clear expression of ideas.

## Register and vocabulary

In Class 10, students learn to identify and use different registers, such as formal, informal, and technical, depending on the situation and audience. They understand that vocabulary choices change with the register—for example, using "commence" in formal writing instead of "start" in casual speech. Students also study how context influences word selection, like using "vehicle" in a technical discussion versus "car" in everyday talk. They practice selecting appropriate vocabulary for specific purposes, such as writing a formal letter or participating in a group discussion. This helps them communicate effectively and adjust their language to suit different needs.

## Examination strategy

In this topic, you learn how to prepare for and take examinations effectively. You understand the importance of studying your lessons regularly and revising them well before the exam. You will learn to manage your time during the test by reading all questions carefully first and then answering the easier ones first. The teacher will also teach you about writing neat and clear answers, with correct spellings and proper spacing between words, so that your paper looks easy to read. For example, you will practice writing answers in short, complete sentences and keeping your handwriting clean.
