# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 7 Mathematics

## Integers

In the topic of Integers, children learn about positive and negative numbers, which include whole numbers from zero and their opposites. They study how to add and subtract integers using rules like adding two negative numbers gives a negative sum, while adding a positive and a negative number uses the smaller number from the larger one. For example, (–3) + (–2) = (–5) and (–4) + 6 = 2. Multiplication rules are also covered, such as multiplying a positive and a negative integer gives a negative product, like (–3) × 4 = (–12). Students practice these concepts with number lines and real-life examples like temperature changes or money owed.

## Fractions-decimals

In Grade 7, you learn about fractions and decimals, which are parts of a whole. You will practice converting fractions to decimals by dividing the numerator by the denominator, like changing 3/4 to 0.75. You also learn to convert decimals back to fractions, such as turning 0.5 into 1/2. Another important rule is comparing fractions and decimals to see which is bigger or smaller, for example, knowing that 3/4 is the same as 0.75 and both are greater than 1/2 or 0.5. These skills help you solve real-life problems like sharing sweets or measuring lengths.

## Data handling-mean

In this topic, children learn about the mean, which is a way to find the average of numbers. To find the mean, they add all the numbers together and then divide the total by how many numbers there are. For example, if you have 5, 10, and 15, you add them to get 30 and divide by 3, so the mean is 10. This helps us understand the middle value or a fair share when numbers are grouped. It is a useful tool for comparing different sets of data in a simple way.

## Simple equations

In Class 7, children learn to solve simple equations, which are mathematical statements with an unknown number shown as a letter like ‘x’. They understand that an equation has two sides, a left side and a right side, and they are always equal. To find the unknown number, they must do the same operation on both sides to keep the balance. For example, in x + 5 = 12, we subtract 5 from both sides to get x = 7. They also practice solving equations where the unknown is multiplied or divided, such as 2x = 10 or y ÷ 3 = 4.

## Lines-angles

In Class 7, we learn about different types of lines like straight lines and curved lines, and angles such as right angles, acute angles, and obtuse angles. We measure angles using a protractor and understand that a right angle is 90 degrees, an acute angle is less than 90 degrees, and an obtuse angle is more than 90 degrees. We also learn about pairs of angles like complementary angles (which add up to 90 degrees) and supplementary angles (which add up to 180 degrees). For example, if one angle is 30 degrees, its complementary angle is 60 degrees because 30 + 60 = 90. We practice identifying and drawing these angles in different shapes and real-life objects.

## Triangle properties

In Class 7, children learn about the different parts of a triangle, like sides, vertices, and angles. They discover that the sum of all three angles in a triangle is always 180 degrees. For example, if two angles are 60° and 50°, the third angle must be 70°. They also identify different types of triangles based on their sides: scalene (all sides different), isosceles (two sides equal), and equilateral (all sides equal). Using this, they can classify triangles like a three-sided ruler (scalene) or a traffic sign (isosceles).

## Comparing quantities-percent

In this topic, children learn about percentages and how they help us compare quantities easily. A percentage means a number out of one hundred, written with the symbol '%'. They learn to convert fractions and decimals into percentages and vice versa, for example, converting 1/2 to 50% or 0.75 to 75%. Students also find the percentage of a given number, such as finding 25% of 200, which is 50. They learn to compare quantities using percentages, like finding what percent one number is of another, such as finding what percent 50 is of 200. These skills help them understand real-life situations like discounts, savings, and scores in exams.

## Rational numbers

In Class 7, we learn about rational numbers, which are numbers that can be written as fractions where the top number (numerator) and bottom number (denominator) are whole numbers, and the denominator is not zero. We learn to add, subtract, multiply, and divide rational numbers using simple rules, like finding a common denominator for addition and subtraction. For example, to add ½ and ¼, we find a common denominator, which is 4, and then write ½ as ²⁄₄ before adding to get ¾. We also learn to compare rational numbers and put them in order on a number line, understanding that larger denominators mean smaller parts when the numerators are the same. These rules help us solve everyday problems involving sharing and measuring things accurately.

## Perimeter-area

In class 7, you will learn how to find the perimeter and area of different shapes like squares, rectangles, and triangles. The perimeter is the total length around a shape, and for a square, you calculate it by adding all four sides or using the formula 4 × side. The area tells us how much space a shape covers, and for a rectangle, you find it by multiplying length by breadth. You will also practice finding the area of a triangle by using the formula ½ × base × height. These lessons help you understand the size and boundary of objects around you.

## Expressions

In Class 7, we learn about expressions which are combinations of numbers, variables, and operations like addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. A variable is a letter, such as *x* or *y*, that stands for an unknown number. We practice writing expressions for simple problems, like writing *5 + n* for 5 more than a number *n*. We also learn to evaluate expressions by replacing the variable with a given number; for example, if *x = 2*, then *3x + 1* becomes 3 × 2 + 1, which equals 7. Understanding expressions helps us solve real-life problems by turning them into mathematical sentences.

## Exponents

In Class 7, you learn about exponents, which are small numbers written above and to the right of a big number to show how many times it is multiplied by itself. The big number is called the base, and the small number is the power, like in 4³ (4 to the power 3), which means 4 × 4 × 4 = 64. You also learn rules: when you multiply numbers with the same base, you add the powers, like 2³ × 2⁴ = 2³⁺⁴ = 2⁷. When you divide numbers with the same base, you subtract the powers, such as 5⁵ ÷ 5³ = 5⁵⁻³ = 5². A number with a power of zero is always 1, like 7⁰ = 1.

## Symmetry-solids

In this topic, children learn about symmetry in solids like cubes, pyramids, and cones. They identify planes of symmetry, which are imaginary lines that divide a solid into two equal mirror-image halves. For example, a cube has nine planes of symmetry, while a cone has one vertical plane of symmetry. They also study rotational symmetry, where a solid looks the same after being rotated by certain angles. Children observe and draw lines of symmetry to understand how shapes and solids are balanced and evenly divided.
