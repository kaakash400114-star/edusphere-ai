# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 10 Mathematics

## Real numbers

In Class 10, you learn about real numbers, which include both rational and irrational numbers. You study the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, which states that every composite number can be expressed as a product of primes in a unique way. For example, 12 can be written as 2 × 2 × 3. You also learn how to find the HCF and LCM of two numbers using prime factorisation. Additionally, you explore irrational numbers, like √2, which cannot be written as a fraction, and verify their decimal expansions are non-terminating and non-repeating. Finally, you revise how to represent real numbers on the number line and understand their properties.

## Polynomials

In Class 10, you learn about polynomials which are algebraic expressions with variables and constants, like 2x² + 3x + 5. You study the degree of a polynomial, which is the highest power of the variable, for example, 4x³ + 2x + 1 is a cubic polynomial because the highest power is 3. You also learn to find the zeros of a quadratic polynomial, such as x² - 5x + 6, by solving the equation to get the values of x that make it zero. The relationship between the zeros and coefficients of a quadratic polynomial ax² + bx + c is also important, where the sum of zeros is -b/a and the product is c/a. Finally, you practice dividing polynomials using the division algorithm to find quotients and remainders.

## Pair of linear equations

Students learn to solve pairs of linear equations in two variables, such as 2x + 3y = 7 and x - y = 1, using methods like substitution, elimination, and cross-multiplication. They understand that these equations represent straight lines and find their point of intersection, which gives the unique solution. By comparing coefficients, they also identify cases with no solution (parallel lines) or infinitely many solutions (overlapping lines). Practical problems, like finding the cost of two items or the speed of a boat in still water and a river, are solved using these equations to reinforce real-world application.

## Quadratic equations

In this topic, students learn to solve quadratic equations, which are equations of the form ax² + bx + c = 0, where a, b, and c are numbers and a is not zero. They practice finding the values of x that make the equation true using methods like factorisation, completing the square, and the quadratic formula, x = (-b ± √(b² - 4ac)) / 2a. For example, to solve x² + 5x + 6 = 0, they can factor it into (x + 2)(x + 3) = 0, giving the solutions x = -2 or x = -3. They also learn about the discriminant, D = b² - 4ac, which tells them if the equation has two real solutions, one real solution, or no real solutions.

## Arithmetic progressions

In Arithmetic Progressions (AP), you learn about number sequences where each term increases or decreases by a fixed number called the common difference. For example, in the sequence 3, 7, 11, 15, the common difference is 4. You use the formula for the nth term, which is \( a_n = a + (n-1)d \), where \( a \) is the first term and \( d \) is the common difference. You also find the sum of the first n terms using \( S_n = \frac{n}{2} [2a + (n-1)d] \). These help solve problems like finding missing terms or total amounts in patterns.

## Triangles: similarity

In this topic, you learn how to find if two triangles are similar by checking if their corresponding angles are equal or if their sides are in the same ratio, called the proportion. For example, if one triangle has sides 3 cm, 4 cm, and 5 cm, and another has sides 6 cm, 8 cm, and 10 cm, their sides are in the ratio 1:2, so they are similar. You also study the Basic Proportionality Theorem, which says that if a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle, it divides the other two sides in the same ratio. These rules help you solve problems involving heights and distances in real life.

## Coordinate geometry

In coordinate geometry, you learn to plot points on a graph using numbers called coordinates, like (3, 4), where the first number is the x-coordinate (horizontal) and the second is the y-coordinate (vertical). You find the distance between two points using the formula √[(x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)²], for example, between (1, 2) and (4, 6). The section formula helps divide a line segment in a ratio, like finding the midpoint when the ratio is 1:1. You also learn to write the equation of a straight line using its slope (gradient) and y-intercept, such as y = 2x + 3. These concepts help you understand shapes and positions on a flat surface.

## Introduction to trigonometry

In this topic, you learn about the trigonometric ratios sine, cosine, and tangent for angles in a right-angled triangle, defined as the ratio of sides like opposite/hypotenuse for sine. You explore how these ratios help find unknown sides and angles when given any two pieces of information in a right-angled triangle. For example, if you know an angle and the length of the adjacent side, you can use the cosine ratio to find the hypotenuse. You also study the trigonometric identities, such as sin²θ + cos²θ = 1, which are true for any angle θ. Practical problems involve using these ratios to measure heights and distances, like finding the height of a building or the width of a river.

## Applications of trigonometry

In Class 10, you learn to use trigonometry to solve real-life problems involving heights and distances, like finding the height of a tower or the width of a river. You will use important ideas like the line of sight, the angle of elevation, and the angle of depression to draw clear diagrams. The main formulas you will use are based on right-angled triangles, such as the tangent of an angle being the ratio of the opposite side to the adjacent side. For example, if you know the angle of elevation to the top of a building and your distance from it, you can find the building's height. These methods help us measure things we cannot easily reach with a measuring tape.

## Circles

In the chapter on Circles, you learn about different parts of a circle like the centre, radius, diameter, and circumference. You discover that the diameter is twice the radius, and the circumference is calculated using the formula C = π × d or C = 2 × π × r. You also study chords, arcs, and sectors, understanding that a chord is a line joining two points on the circle, while a sector is a pie-shaped part. For example, if a pizza has a radius of 7 cm, its circumference is 2 × 22/7 × 7 = 44 cm. Additionally, you explore the properties of tangents and learn that a tangent to a circle is always perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.

## Constructions

In this topic, you learn to draw exact figures using only a compass and straightedge without measuring instruments. You will practice constructing the perpendicular bisector of a line segment and dividing it into equal parts, like finding the middle of a stick or sharing a piece of paper equally. You also learn to draw triangles with given sides, such as making a triangle with sides 3 cm, 4 cm, and 5 cm, and to construct tangents to a circle from a point outside it. These constructions help you understand shapes and angles better without using rulers for length.

## Areas related to circles

In this topic, you learn how to find the area of circles and the parts of circles like sectors and segments. You use the formula πr² to calculate the area of a whole circle, where ‘r’ is the radius. For a sector, which looks like a slice of pizza, the area is found using (θ/360) × πr², with ‘θ’ being the angle of the sector. You also study the area of a segment, which is the area between a chord and the arc, calculated by subtracting the triangle's area from the sector's area. These ideas help in real-life situations, like finding the area of a circular park or a design on a plate.

## Surface areas and volumes

Students learn to find the surface areas and volumes of different 3D shapes like cubes, cuboids, cylinders, cones, and spheres. They use formulas such as 6a² for the cube's surface area, 2(lb + bh + lh) for the cuboid, and πr²h for the cylinder's volume. For example, they calculate the paint needed to paint a cube or the amount of water a cylindrical tank can hold. The topic also covers combinations of these solids, like finding the volume of a toy made with a cone on top of a cylinder. They practice applying these formulas to solve real-life problems involving measurements.

## Statistics

In Class 10, you learn to find the mean, median, and mode of grouped data using formulas like the direct method for mean, and locating the median class and mode class. You will understand how to calculate the cumulative frequency and create ogives to find the median graphically. For example, you might find the average height of students using a frequency distribution table. The topic also teaches you about measures of dispersion, like mean deviation and standard deviation, to understand how spread out the data is. You will use formulas like σ = √(∑(xᵢ - x̄)² / N) to calculate standard deviation for ungrouped data.

## Probability

In probability, students learn to find how likely an event is to happen using numbers between 0 and 1. They learn to calculate probability using the formula: Probability = (Number of favourable outcomes) / (Total number of possible outcomes). For example, the probability of picking a red ball from a bag with 3 red and 2 blue balls is 3/5. They also learn about equally likely outcomes, like the chance of getting heads or tails when tossing a fair coin, which is 1/2. Finally, they understand that if an event is certain to happen, its probability is 1, and if it is impossible, its probability is 0.
