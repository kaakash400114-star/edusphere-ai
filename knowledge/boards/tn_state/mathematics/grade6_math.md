# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 6 Mathematics

## Knowing numbers

In this topic, children learn to read and write numbers up to five places using the Indian place value system: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, and ten thousands. They understand the difference between place value and face value, for example, the place value of 5 in 5234 is 5000, while its face value is 5. Students learn to compare and arrange numbers in ascending (smallest to largest) and descending (largest to smallest) order, such as arranging 1245, 987, and 3456 in order. They also learn to form the greatest and smallest numbers using given digits, like making 7530 and 0357 with the digits 7, 5, 3, and 0. Finally, they practice reading and writing number names in both words and numerals, such as 1234 as "one thousand two hundred and thirty-four".

## Whole numbers

In this topic, children learn about whole numbers, which are counting numbers starting from zero, like 0, 1, 2, 3, and so on. They practise writing numerals in words, such as writing 5 as "five," and learn about place values, like knowing that 34 is made of 3 tens and 4 ones. Students also study the basic operations—addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division—using whole numbers, for example, adding 15 and 10 to get 25. They understand simple patterns in numbers, like even and odd numbers, and solve word problems involving real-life situations, such as sharing 20 candies equally among 5 friends.

## Playing with numbers (HCF-LCM)

In this topic, children learn to find the Highest Common Factor (HCF) and Least Common Multiple (LCM) of two numbers using prime factorisation. They break down numbers into their prime factors, like writing 12 as 2 × 2 × 3, and 18 as 2 × 3 × 3. The HCF is found by taking the common prime factors with the smallest power, such as HCF of 12 and 18 is 2 × 3 = 6. The LCM is found by taking all prime factors with the highest power, like LCM of 12 and 18 is 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 = 36. Through simple examples, they practice these methods to solve problems involving sharing things equally (HCF) and grouping them in sets (LCM).

## Basic geometry

In Class 6, we learn about basic geometry by identifying and drawing straight lines, curves, and angles. We study different shapes like squares, rectangles, triangles, and circles, and count their sides and corners. We understand that a line goes on forever in both directions, while a line segment has two endpoints. For example, a square has four equal sides and four right angles. We also learn about rays, which start at one point and go on in one direction, like the sun’s light.

## Elementary shapes

In class six, we learn about simple shapes like triangles, circles, squares, and rectangles. We find out how many sides and corners each shape has, like a triangle has three sides and three corners. We also learn about straight lines, curved lines, and how to draw these shapes neatly. For example, a square has four equal sides and four right-angle corners. We practice making these shapes using a ruler and a compass to understand their properties better.

## Integers

In Class 6, we learn about integers, which are positive and negative numbers including zero on a number line. We understand that numbers to the right of zero are positive (+) and to the left are negative (−). Children learn to compare integers using symbols like > (greater than) and < (less than), for example, −3 is less than +2. We also practice simple addition and subtraction of integers, such as +5 + (−3) = +2 and −4 − (−1) = −3. Finally, we solve real-life problems using integers, like counting temperatures or money gains and losses.

## Fractions

In Class 6, children learn about fractions, which are parts of a whole number. They study proper fractions (like 1/2, 3/4) where the numerator is smaller than the denominator, and improper fractions (like 5/3) where the numerator is bigger. They learn to add and subtract fractions with the same denominator, such as 2/5 + 1/5 = 3/5, and find equivalent fractions by multiplying both numerator and denominator by the same number. Simple division of whole numbers into parts, like sharing 4 apples equally between 2 children (each gets 2/4 or 1/2), is also covered. They understand fractions as numbers between whole numbers, like 1 1/2 lying between 1 and 2.

## Decimals

In Class 6, children learn to read, write, and compare decimal numbers. They understand that the place value of decimals includes tenths, hundredths, and thousandths after the decimal point, just like ones, tens, and hundreds before it. For example, in the number 25.4, the 4 is in the tenths place, meaning it represents four-tenths. They also learn to add and subtract decimals by lining up the decimal points, such as adding 3.25 and 1.15 to get 4.40. This helps them solve simple problems involving money and measurements accurately.

## Data handling

In Class 6, children learn to collect and record data using tally marks and organise it in a table. They represent data using pictographs, where each picture stands for a certain number of items, and bar graphs, which show information using bars of different heights. For example, they can make a bar graph to show the number of fruits in a basket. They also find the arithmetic mean by adding all numbers and dividing by the count, like finding the average marks of a group. These skills help them understand and share information clearly.

## Mensuration

In Grade 6 Mathematics, we learn about mensuration to find the area and perimeter of simple shapes. For a square, the perimeter is 4 times the length of one side, and the area is the length multiplied by itself. For a rectangle, the perimeter is 2 times the sum of its length and breadth, and the area is length multiplied by breadth. We also calculate the area and perimeter of a triangle using its base and height. These measurements help us understand the space around us, like finding the area of a playground or the length of a fence.

## Algebra introduction

In Class 6, we learn about algebra by using letters like a, b, c, or even a smiley face 😊 to stand for numbers. These letters are called variables, and help us write simple rules, such as "adding 2 to a number" as n + 2. We also write simple expressions like 3x + 5, where x is the number, and solve easy problems to find the missing number. For example, if we have the equation y + 3 = 8, we learn to find y by subtracting 3 from both sides. Algebra helps us solve everyday problems using letters instead of big numbers.

## Ratio-proportion

Children learn about ratio as a way to compare two quantities of the same kind, like 2 apples to 3 oranges, written as 2:3. They understand that ratio remains the same even if both numbers are multiplied or divided by the same amount. Proportion is introduced as the equality of two ratios, such as 2:3 = 4:6, meaning the first ratio is equal to the second. They solve simple problems to find missing numbers in proportions, like 2:5 = ?:20, by cross-multiplying. Practical examples, such as mixing lemon juice and water in a recipe, help them apply these concepts in daily life.
