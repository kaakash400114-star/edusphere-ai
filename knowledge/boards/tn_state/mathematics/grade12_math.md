# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 12 Mathematics

## Relations-functions

In Grade 12 Mathematics, you will learn about relations and functions, focusing on how one set of values is connected to another. A relation shows how elements from two sets relate to each other, like pairing a student’s roll number to their marks, while a function is a special type of relation where each input has exactly one output, such as calculating the area of a circle using its radius. You will explore types of functions, including one-one (injective), onto (surjective), and bijective functions, and understand their properties through graphs and mappings. Key concepts include composite functions, where two functions are combined, and inverse functions, which reverse the effect of the original function, like turning temperature from Celsius back to Fahrenheit. These ideas help in solving real-world problems involving patterns and relationships.

## Inverse trigonometry

In inverse trigonometry, you learn about the inverse functions of sine, cosine, and tangent, which are written as sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, and tan⁻¹. These functions give you the angle when you know the value of the trigonometric ratio, like finding the angle whose sine is 1/2, which is 30°. You also study important formulas such as sin⁻¹(x) + cos⁻¹(x) = 90° and tan⁻¹(x) + cot⁻¹(x) = 90°. The principal values of these functions are restricted to specific ranges, like 0° to 180° for cos⁻¹. For example, if cos⁻¹(1/2) is 60°, it is the only answer in the principal range.

## Matrices

In the Matrices chapter, students learn about rectangular arrays of numbers arranged in rows and columns, where each number is called an element. They study the types of matrices like row, column, square, diagonal, and identity matrices, and understand how to perform addition and subtraction of matrices of the same order. Multiplication of matrices is introduced, focusing on the rule that the number of columns in the first matrix must equal the number of rows in the second matrix. For example, multiplying a 2x3 matrix with a 3x2 matrix results in a 2x2 matrix. Students also learn to find the transpose of a matrix by flipping its rows into columns and the inverse of a square matrix, if it exists.

## Determinants

Students learn about determinants, which are special numbers calculated from square matrices using specific rules. For a 2x2 matrix, the determinant is found by multiplying the elements on the main diagonal and subtracting the product of the elements on the other diagonal, like in the example where the determinant of [[a, b], [c, d]] is ad - bc. They also study how to find the determinant of 3x3 matrices using the method of expansion by minors or the Sarrus rule. The key concept of singular and non-singular matrices is introduced, where a determinant of zero means the matrix is singular and does not have an inverse, while a non-zero determinant means it is non-singular and has an inverse.

## Continuity-differentiability

In Class 12 Mathematics, students learn about continuity and differentiability of functions. A function is continuous at a point if its left-hand limit, right-hand limit, and value at that point are all equal. Differentiability means the function has a derivative at that point, which is found using the first principle or standard rules like the product rule and quotient rule. For example, a polynomial function like f(x) = x² + 3x is both continuous and differentiable everywhere, but a piecewise function might not be continuous at a point where its definition changes. Students also study the relationship between continuity and differentiability, understanding that all differentiable functions are continuous, but not all continuous functions are differentiable.

## Derivative applications

In this topic, students learn how derivatives are used to find the rate of change of quantities, such as the speed of a moving object. They also learn to calculate the maximum and minimum values of functions, which helps in solving problems like finding the largest area of a field with a fixed fence length. The rules for finding derivatives, like the product rule and chain rule, are used to solve these problems step by step. For example, if a balloon is being filled with air, the derivative can tell us how fast the radius is growing at any moment. These concepts help in understanding real-life situations where things change over time.

## Integrals

In this topic, you will learn about integrals, which are the opposite of derivatives and help us find the area under curves. You will study basic rules like the power rule, where the integral of \( x^n \) is \( \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} \), and trigonometric integrals, such as the integral of \( \sin x \) being \( -\cos x \). You will also learn about definite integrals, which calculate the exact area between a curve and the x-axis over an interval, like finding the area under \( y = 2x \) from \( x = 0 \) to \( x = 3 \). Additionally, you will explore methods like substitution and integration by parts to solve more complex problems. Understanding integrals is important for real-life applications, such as calculating distances or volumes.

## Integral applications

In Class 12 Mathematics, students learn to apply integration to find areas under curves and between two curves, using definite integrals. They study formulas to calculate the area bounded by a function and the x-axis, such as ∫[a to b] f(x) dx, and between two curves, ∫[a to b] [f(x) - g(x)] dx. Additionally, they explore volumes of solids of revolution, like the volume of a cone or cylinder, using integration formulas such as π∫[a to b] [f(x)]² dx. These concepts help solve real-world problems like determining the area of irregular shapes or the capacity of containers. Students also practice applying the fundamental theorem of calculus to evaluate definite integrals efficiently.

## Differential equations

Students learn to identify and solve first-order differential equations, including variables separable, linear, and homogeneous types. They apply the method of separation of variables to equations like dy/dx = xy, integrating both sides to find the solution. For linear equations of the form dy/dx + Py = Q, they use the integrating factor method to find the general solution. Students also understand the geometric meaning of differential equations, where solutions represent families of curves satisfying given conditions. Examples include solving dy/dx = 2x to find the curve y = x² + c, where c is an arbitrary constant.

## Vectors

In the vectors chapter, you learn about the different ways to represent vectors, like using components or with the i, j, k unit vectors. You will study how to add and subtract vectors, and also multiply them using the scalar (dot) product and vector (cross) product, learning their specific formulas and properties. A key rule is that the cross product of two vectors gives a new vector that is perpendicular to the plane containing the original vectors, like finding the direction of a spinning top. You will also apply these concepts to solve practical problems involving force, velocity, and finding the area of a triangle in space.

## 3D geometry

In 3D geometry, you learn about the coordinate system in three dimensions using the x, y, and z axes. You study the distance formula between two points, like finding how far apart (1, 2, 3) and (4, 5, 6) are. The section formula helps you find the coordinates of a point that divides a line segment internally or externally in a given ratio. You also explore equations of lines in 3D space, such as symmetric form and vector form. For example, a line passing through (1, 0, 2) and parallel to the vector (2, 3, -1) has a specific equation. Finally, you learn about the angle between two lines and the shortest distance between two skew lines using their direction vectors.

## Linear programming

In the topic of linear programming, you learn to solve problems by finding the best possible outcome, such as maximum profit or minimum cost, under certain conditions. These conditions, called constraints, are written as inequalities or equations using variables like x and y. You graph these constraints on a coordinate plane to form a feasible region, which is the area that satisfies all conditions at once. The best solution, called the optimal solution, is always found at one of the corner points of this feasible region. For example, you might solve a problem about making chairs and tables to find the number of each that gives the highest profit without using more materials than available.

## Probability

In Probability, you learn to find the chance of events happening using numbers from 0 to 1. You study important rules like addition, where the probability of two events happening is found by adding their chances if they cannot happen together. You also learn about multiplication, where you multiply the chances of two independent events happening one after the other. For example, the chance of getting a head in a coin toss is ½, and if you toss twice, the chance of two heads is ½ × ½ = ¼. You will solve problems using these rules to understand real-life situations like games and weather.
