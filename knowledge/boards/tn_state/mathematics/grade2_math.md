# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 2 Mathematics

## Numbers to 999

In Class 2, children learn to read, write, and count numbers from 1 to 999. They understand the place value of digits in hundreds, tens, and ones places, for example, in the number 254, 2 is in the hundreds place, 5 is in the tens place, and 4 is in the ones place. Children learn to compare numbers using symbols like greater than (>), less than (<), and equal to (=), such as 150 < 200. They also practice forming the smallest and largest possible numbers using given digits, like making 135 and 531 from 1, 3, and 5. Finally, they learn to expand numbers into their place value form, like 456 = 400 + 50 + 6.

## Addition with carry

In this topic, children learn to add two numbers where the sum of digits in the ones place is 10 or more. They practice writing the carry-over digit in the tens place and adding it to the next column. For example, when adding 8 and 5, the sum is 13, so they write 3 in the ones place and carry over 1 to the tens place. Students solve simple addition problems with carry-over using pictures and number lines to build understanding. They also learn to verify their answers by adding the digits in reverse order.

## Subtraction with borrow

In Class 2, we learn subtraction with borrowing when the top number is smaller than the bottom number in a place. We first borrow one group of ten from the tens place to make the ones place bigger. For example, in 52 minus 8, we cannot subtract 8 from 2, so we borrow 1 ten from the 5, making the ones place 12. Now, we subtract 8 from 12 to get 4, and the remaining 4 tens minus 0 gives us 44. This method helps us solve subtraction problems easily when we need to borrow.

## Multiplication as repeated addition

Children learn that multiplication is a quick way to add the same number again and again. For example, 3 + 3 + 3 is the same as 3 multiplied by 3, which equals 9. They understand that multiplication means counting groups of equal size, like 4 groups of 2 apples each is 4 × 2 = 8 apples. The concept is introduced using simple everyday objects to make it easy to understand. They also learn to read and write multiplication sums, such as 2 × 5 = 10.

## Division as sharing

In Grade 2, children learn division as sharing things equally among groups. They understand that when we share, we divide a whole into smaller, equal parts. For example, if we have 10 apples and we share them equally between 2 friends, each friend gets 5 apples. This is written as 10 ÷ 2 = 5. They also learn that division is the opposite of multiplication, and use simple real-life situations to practice sharing items like sweets, toys, or stickers. The main rule is that the number shared must be divided into equal parts without any leftovers.

## Money: rupees and paise

Children learn about money, identifying coins like 1 rupee, 2 rupees, and 5 rupees, and notes like 10 rupees and 20 rupees. They understand that 100 paise make one rupee, and practice counting money to find the total value. For example, one 5-rupee coin and two 1-rupee coins make 7 rupees. They also learn to compare amounts to see which is more or less, like checking if 10 rupees is more than 5 rupees. Kids use simple addition to add coins and notes, such as adding two 2-rupee coins to get 4 rupees.

## Length, weight, capacity

Children learn to compare the length, weight, and capacity of objects using simple words like longer, shorter, heavier, lighter, more, and less. They measure length using non-standard units like hand spans or paper clips and weight using balance scales with common objects like books or fruits. Capacity is introduced by comparing how much liquid or material containers can hold, using terms like full, half-full, and empty. Practical activities like arranging pencils by size or filling different bottles with water help them understand these concepts. By observing everyday objects, children learn to identify and compare measurements in their surroundings.

## Time: days, months, clock

Children learn about the days of the week and months of the year, understanding that Sunday to Saturday makes one week and January to December makes one year. They are introduced to the clock, learning to identify the hour hand and minute hand, and can read the time to the hour, such as 3 o’clock or 8 o’clock. They also learn about morning, afternoon, and evening, relating time to daily activities like eating lunch or going to school. Using examples like their birthday month or the day they come to school helps them connect time to their lives. This builds a basic understanding of time in a simple and practical way.

## Shapes and patterns

In class 2, children learn to identify and name basic two-dimensional shapes like circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles. They observe and create simple patterns using shapes, colours, or sizes, such as a circle-square-circle-square sequence. Students also understand the difference between straight lines and curves, and they practice drawing these shapes correctly. For example, a square has four equal sides and four corners, while a triangle has three sides and three corners. Through fun activities, they explore repeating patterns and develop their observation skills.

## Data: picture charts

In this topic, children learn about picture charts to understand and display information in a simple way. They count the number of pictures in each category to find out which has more or less. For example, a chart might show different fruits like apples, bananas, and oranges, and children count how many pictures of each fruit are there. This helps them learn to read and compare small groups of objects using pictures. They also practice writing the numbers next to each picture to show the count.
