# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 1 Mathematics

## Numbers 1-100

In Grade 1, children learn to identify, read, and write numbers from 1 to 100. They understand the concept of place value, distinguishing between tens and ones, for example, the number 45 has 4 tens and 5 ones. Students learn to count numbers forward and backward up to 100 and to write numbers in both numerals and words. They also learn to compare numbers using terms like more than, less than, and equal to. Through activities like skip counting by 2s, 5s, and 10s, they develop a strong foundation for number sense and basic arithmetic.

## Addition up to 20

In Grade 1, children learn addition by combining two small groups to find the total number of objects. They use the plus sign (+) to add two numbers and the equal sign (=) to show the result, such as 5 + 2 = 7. Through counting real objects like pencils or toys, they understand that adding means putting groups together. They practice simple sums up to 20, using number bonds to see how numbers combine, for example, 8 + 4 = 12. This helps them build a strong foundation for solving everyday problems involving addition.

## Subtraction up to 20

Children learn to take away smaller numbers from bigger numbers up to 20. They use objects, fingers, or pictures to understand that subtraction means removing some items from a group. For example, if there are 10 apples and we eat 3, we are left with 7 apples (10 - 3 = 7). They also learn that subtraction can be done by counting back on a number line or using a minus sign (-). This helps them find how many things remain when some are taken away.

## Shapes around us

Children learn to identify and name basic shapes like circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles in their surroundings. They observe that circles are round like plates and wheels, squares have four equal sides like chessboards, and triangles have three sides like sandwiches. Rectangles have long sides like doors and books. Students do not learn any formulas or rules for these shapes at this level. They simply recognise shapes by their appearance and see them in everyday objects around their school and home.

## Measurement: long-short, heavy-light

Children learn to compare objects based on their length and weight. They identify which objects are long and which are short, like comparing a pencil with a scale. For weight, they understand which objects are heavy and which are light, such as lifting a book compared to a feather. They use simple words like ‘long’, ‘short’, ‘heavy’, and ‘light’ to describe these differences. These comparisons help them understand basic measurement ideas in everyday life.

## Money: rupees and coins

In Grade 1, children learn to identify and name common Indian coins and currency notes, such as 1 rupee, 2 rupees, 5 rupees, and 10 rupees. They practice matching coins and notes to their corresponding values and learn to count simple amounts using like denominations. Students are introduced to the basic concept of buying and selling, using play money to understand that money is used to purchase items. They also learn to compare the values of different coins and notes to determine which is worth more or less. Through simple classroom activities, children develop the foundational skill of recognising and handling money in everyday situations.

## Time: day-night, o'clock

Children learn to tell time by identifying the hour hand on a clock. They understand that the day is divided into two parts: day and night. For example, they wake up in the morning for school and sleep at night. They practice reading the clock when the hour hand points to a number, such as 8 o’clock for breakfast or 4 o’clock for playtime. This helps them know when different daily activities happen. They also learn to recognize the difference between morning and evening times.

## Sorting and patterns

Children learn to sort objects based on one simple attribute, such as colour, shape, or size. For example, they can group red blocks together and blue blocks together, or separate big balls from small balls. They also identify repeating patterns like red, blue, red, blue or clap, tap, clap, tap. Understanding these patterns helps them predict what comes next, like knowing the next colour in a sequence. Sorting and grouping objects make things easy to find and organise, which is useful in daily life. This activity builds early thinking and observation skills in children.
