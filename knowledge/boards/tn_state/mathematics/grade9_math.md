# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 9 Mathematics

## Real numbers

In this topic, you learn about real numbers, which include both rational and irrational numbers. Rational numbers are fractions like ½ or integers like 4, while irrational numbers cannot be written as fractions, such as √2. You will also practise converting fractions to decimals and decimals to fractions, and understand that repeating decimals are rational numbers. The rules for adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing real numbers are the same as for integers and fractions. For example, you might solve problems like finding the sum of ¾ and 1.2, or multiplying √3 by 2 to understand how real numbers work in different forms.

## Polynomials

In Class 9, you learn about polynomials, which are expressions with variables and numbers combined using addition, subtraction, and multiplication. You identify the degree, coefficient, and constant term of a polynomial, like in the expression \(3x^2 - 5x + 7\), where 2 is the degree, 3 is the coefficient of \(x^2\), and 7 is the constant. You also study different types of polynomials, such as linear (\(2x + 3\)), quadratic (\(x^2 - 4x + 1\)), and cubic (\(x^3 + 2x - 5\)), based on their highest power. The topic includes adding, subtracting, and multiplying polynomials, following simple rules like combining like terms. Finally, you learn about the zeros of a polynomial, which are the values of the variable that make the polynomial equal to zero.

## Coordinate geometry

In coordinate geometry, you learn to plot points on a graph paper using two numbers, called coordinates, which tell you the position. The first number, x-coordinate, tells you how far to move left or right, and the second number, y-coordinate, tells you how far to move up or down from the centre point called the origin. You also learn to find the distance between two points using a simple formula and to see if lines are parallel by checking their slopes, which shows how steep they are. For example, the point (3, 4) is three steps right and four steps up from the origin (0, 0).

## Linear equations in two variables

In Class 9, you learn to solve linear equations in two variables, which are equations like 2x + 3y = 8. You find pairs of numbers (x, y) that make the equation true, such as (1, 2) because 2(1) + 3(2) = 8. You draw graphs of these equations as straight lines and see where two lines cross to find their common solution. For example, the lines from x + y = 5 and 2x - y = 1 meet at the point (2, 3), which is the answer for both equations. This helps you solve real-life problems, like finding the cost of two items together.

## Euclid's geometry

In this topic, you learn about Euclid’s geometry, which is the study of points, lines, and planes. You understand Euclid’s five postulates, like a straight line can be drawn between any two points, and a circle can be drawn with any centre and radius. You also study definitions, such as a line has no breadth and a line segment has two endpoints. For example, you can draw a line segment between two points A and B, and extend it to form a line. These simple ideas form the base of all geometry you will learn in higher classes.

## Lines and angles

In Class 9, we learn about different types of lines and angles, like intersecting lines, parallel lines, and transversals. We understand important rules such as the sum of angles around a point being 360 degrees and the angles on a straight line adding up to 180 degrees. When a transversal crosses parallel lines, we learn about special angle pairs, like corresponding angles being equal and alternate angles being equal. For example, if two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, the angle at the top left of one line will be equal to the angle at the top left of the other line. We also practice finding unknown angles using these rules and solving simple geometry problems.

## Triangles

In the topic on triangles, you learn about different types of triangles based on their sides and angles, like equilateral, isosceles, and scalene triangles, and acute, right, and obtuse triangles. You study the properties of triangles, including the sum of all interior angles being 180 degrees, and the triangle inequality theorem which states that the sum of any two sides is always greater than the third side. You also learn about congruent triangles and the rules to prove them, such as SSS (Side-Side-Side), SAS (Side-Angle-Side), and ASA (Angle-Side-Angle). For example, if two triangles have all three sides equal, they are congruent by the SSS rule. Finally, you explore the concept of similar triangles and how their corresponding sides are proportional and angles are equal.

## Quadrilaterals

In Class 9, you will learn about quadrilaterals, which are four-sided shapes like squares and rectangles. You will study the properties of different quadrilaterals, such as parallelograms, rhombuses, and trapeziums, focusing on rules about their sides and angles. For example, in a parallelogram, opposite sides are equal and parallel, and opposite angles are also equal. You will also learn to find the area of these shapes using formulas, like the area of a rhombus being half the product of its diagonals. Understanding these concepts helps you solve problems involving shapes and their properties.

## Circles

In this topic, children learn about circles, their parts like the centre, radius, diameter, and circumference. They discover that the diameter is twice the radius, and the circumference is found by multiplying the diameter with the value of pi (π), which is approximately 22/7. They also study the area of a circle, calculated as π multiplied by the square of the radius. For example, if a circle has a radius of 7 cm, its area is 22/7 × 7 × 7 = 154 square cm. They learn about chords, arcs, and sectors, understanding how they relate to the circle's overall shape and size.

## Heron's formula

In this topic, students learn Heron’s formula to find the area of a triangle when all three side lengths are known. The formula is Area = √[s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)], where ‘s’ is the semi-perimeter, calculated as (a+b+c)/2. For example, if a triangle has sides 6 cm, 8 cm, and 10 cm, the semi-perimeter s is 12 cm, and the area is √[12(12−6)(12−8)(12−10)] = 24 cm². Students practice applying this formula to solve real-life problems, like finding the area of a triangular plot of land or a playground. This method is useful when the height of the triangle is not given.

## Surface areas and volumes

In this topic, you will learn how to find the surface area and volume of different shapes like cubes, cuboids, cylinders, cones, and spheres. You will use formulas to calculate the total area of all faces of a cube or cuboid, and the area of curved surfaces for cylinders and cones. For example, the volume of a cuboid is found by multiplying its length, breadth, and height. You will also solve problems to find how much paint is needed to cover a box or how much water a tank can hold. These calculations help in understanding the space shapes occupy and the material they need.

## Statistics

In statistics, you learn to collect, organise, and show data using tally marks, frequency tables, and pictographs. You also study bar graphs to compare different groups of information, like the number of students who like different fruits. You will calculate the mean, which is the average of a set of numbers, by adding all the values and dividing by how many there are. For example, to find the mean of 2, 4, and 6, you add them to get 12 and then divide by 3 to get a mean of 4. Understanding these helps you see patterns and make sense of information in your daily life.

## Probability

In the chapter on Probability, you learn to find the likelihood of an event happening using numbers between 0 and 1. The formula is Probability = Number of favourable outcomes ÷ Total number of possible outcomes. For example, if you have a bag with 3 red and 2 blue marbles, the chance of picking a red marble is 3 out of 5. You also learn that an event with probability 0 is impossible, like rolling a 7 on a normal die, while one with probability 1 is certain, like the sun rising. Probability helps us understand and compare chances in games, weather, and daily life.
