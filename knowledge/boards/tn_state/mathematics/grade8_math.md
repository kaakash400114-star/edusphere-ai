# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 8 Mathematics

## Rational numbers

In this topic, children learn about rational numbers which are numbers that can be written as fractions, where both the top number (numerator) and the bottom number (denominator) are whole numbers, and the denominator is not zero. They understand that every integer is a rational number because it can be written as a fraction with 1 as the denominator. For example, the number 5 can be written as 5/1, and -3 can be written as -3/1. Children also learn how to add, subtract, multiply, and divide rational numbers by following the rules of fractions, such as finding a common denominator for addition and subtraction. They practice with examples like adding 1/2 and 1/3 to get 5/6, and multiplying 2/3 by 4/5 to get 8/15.

## Linear equations

In Class 8, you learn to solve linear equations in one variable, which are like puzzles with numbers and a hidden letter. The main rule is to keep both sides of the equation balanced by doing the same operation, like adding or subtracting, on both sides. For example, to solve x + 5 = 12, you subtract 5 from both sides to get x = 7. You also learn to translate word problems into equations, like finding a number if three times it plus 2 equals 20. By practising these steps, you get better at finding the value of the unknown number.

## Quadrilaterals

In Class 8 Mathematics, you learn about quadrilaterals, which are four-sided shapes like squares, rectangles, and parallelograms. You study their special properties: for example, a rectangle has four right angles, while a parallelogram has opposite sides equal and parallel. You also learn about trapeziums, which have only one pair of parallel sides, and rhombuses, where all sides are equal but angles are not always right angles. To find the area of these shapes, you use formulas like length × breadth for rectangles and base × height for parallelograms and trapeziums. Understanding these rules helps you draw and measure quadrilaterals correctly.

## Data handling-probability intro

In Data Handling and Probability, you learn about chance using words like 'impossible', 'certain', and 'maybe' with examples such as 'It is impossible to pull a blue ball from a bag of only red balls'. You understand probability as a number from 0 to 1, where 0 means no chance and 1 means sure to happen, like the sun rising in the morning. You also learn to write probability as fractions, such as the chance of picking a green marble from a bag with 3 green and 2 yellow marbles is 3 out of 5. Real-life examples include predicting the chance of rain or winning a game of dice. These ideas help you make simple predictions and understand everyday events better.

## Squares-roots

In Class 8, you learn about finding squares and square roots of numbers. The square of a number is the result when you multiply the number by itself, like 3 × 3 = 9, so 9 is the square of 3. To find the square root, you look for the number which, when multiplied by itself, gives the original number, such as √9 = 3 because 3 × 3 = 9. You also study perfect squares like 1, 4, 9, 16, and 25, which are squares of whole numbers, and learn to calculate square roots using methods like prime factorisation. For example, √36 = 6 because 6 × 6 = 36.

## Cubes-roots

In this lesson, you will learn to find the cube root of a number, which is the special value that gives the original number when multiplied by itself three times, just as 3 × 3 × 3 = 27, so the cube root of 27 is 3. You will discover perfect cubes like 1, 8, 27, and 64, which are the cubes of counting numbers 1, 2, 3, and 4. The cube root symbol is a small 3, like ∛, so ∛64 means we are looking for the number that makes 64 when multiplied thrice. We can also find cube roots of bigger numbers by breaking them down into smaller, known cubes, making it easier to solve.

## Comparing quantities-interest

In this topic, children learn to find simple interest using the formula Principal × Rate × Time / 100. They understand that principal is the initial amount, rate is the interest per year, and time is the duration in years. For example, if ₹100 is borrowed at 5% interest per year for 2 years, the interest is ₹10. They also compare quantities by calculating simple interest and total amount, which is principal plus interest. This helps them see how money grows over time with a fixed rate of interest.

## Algebraic expressions-identities

In this topic, children learn about algebraic expressions and important identities that help in solving problems easily. They study the standard identities like (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b² and (a – b)² = a² – 2ab + b², which are used to expand and simplify expressions. For example, using the first identity, (x + 3)² can be expanded as x² + 6x + 9. They also learn about the identity (a + b)(a – b) = a² – b², which is useful for finding the difference of squares. These rules make solving big calculations simple and help in understanding how numbers and letters work together in algebra.

## Mensuration

In Class 8, we learn to find the area and perimeter of squares, rectangles, triangles, and circles using specific formulas. We calculate the area of a rectangle by multiplying its length by its breadth, and the perimeter by adding all its sides. For triangles, we use the formula ½ × base × height to find the area, while circles use π × radius². We also learn to find the volume and surface area of cubes and cuboids. For example, the volume of a cuboid is found by multiplying its length, breadth, and height, helping us understand space measurement in everyday objects.

## Exponents-power

In Class 8, you learn about exponents, which are small numbers written above and to the right of a base number to show how many times it is multiplied by itself. For example, in 5³, the base is 5 and the exponent 3 means 5 × 5 × 5 = 125. You practise rules like adding exponents when multiplying the same base, such as 3² × 3³ = 3⁵, and subtracting exponents when dividing, like 7⁵ ÷ 7² = 7³. You also learn that any number raised to the power of zero is 1, so 4⁰ = 1. These rules help you simplify big calculations into smaller, easier steps.

## Direct-inverse proportion

In Class 8, we learn about direct and inverse proportion to understand how two quantities change together. Direct proportion means when one quantity increases, the other also increases in the same ratio, like the number of notebooks and their cost. Inverse proportion means when one quantity increases, the other decreases in the same ratio, like the number of workers and the time they take to finish a job. We use formulas to find the missing values: for direct proportion, \( \frac{x_1}{y_1} = \frac{x_2}{y_2} \), and for inverse proportion, \( x_1 \times y_1 = x_2 \times y_2 \). For example, if 5 workers take 10 days, then 10 workers will take 5 days to finish the same work.

## Factorisation-graphs

Children learn to find factors of numbers and algebraic expressions, like breaking 12 into 3 × 4 or writing ab + ac as a(b + c). They study how to factorise binomials using identities such as a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b), for example, x² − 9 = (x + 3)(x − 3). The topic also covers factorising trinomials like x² + 5x + 6 into (x + 2)(x + 3) by finding two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to 5. These skills help in solving equations and understanding graphs better, as factorisation shows where a line crosses the zero point. Practice with numbers and letters builds strong problem-solving abilities for higher classes.
