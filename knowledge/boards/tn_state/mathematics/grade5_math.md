# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 5 Mathematics

## Very large numbers and estimation

Students learn to read, write, and compare very large numbers up to nine digits using the Indian place value system of ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten thousands, lakhs, and ten lakhs. They practice writing numbers in words and numerals, such as “five lakhs, twenty-three thousand, four hundred and fifty-six” as 5,23,456. The concept of estimation is introduced, where children learn to round off numbers to the nearest ten or hundred to make calculations simpler, like estimating 2,348 to the nearest hundred as 2,300. They solve simple real-life problems involving these large numbers, such as counting items in a big sale or people in a large crowd. These skills help them understand and work with bigger numbers in everyday situations.

## Angles and shapes

Children learn to identify and name angles like right angles, acute angles, and obtuse angles. They use a protractor to measure angles in degrees, like 90° for a right angle. Students also recognise plane shapes like triangles, squares, rectangles, and circles, and count their sides and corners. They learn that a triangle has three sides, a square has four equal sides, and a rectangle has four sides with opposite sides equal. Simple examples include identifying angles in a door hinge or a book corner.

## Area-perimeter

In Class 5, children learn to find the perimeter and area of simple shapes like squares, rectangles, and triangles. The perimeter is the total distance around the shape, and for a rectangle, it is calculated as 2 times the length plus 2 times the breadth. The area measures how much space a shape covers, and for a rectangle, it is found by multiplying the length by the breadth. For example, a rectangle with length 5 cm and breadth 3 cm has a perimeter of 16 cm and an area of 15 square cm. They also learn to use these formulas to solve problems involving real-life objects like books and playgrounds.

## Fractions: unlike operations

In class 5, we learn about unlike fractions, which have different denominators. To add or subtract them, we first find a common denominator by multiplying the denominators together. For example, to add 1/3 and 1/4, we find the common denominator 12. Then we convert each fraction: 1/3 becomes 4/12 and 1/4 becomes 3/12. Now we can add them: 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12. The same rule applies to subtraction. Always simplify the final answer if possible.

## Decimals operations

In Class 5, children learn to add and subtract decimal numbers by writing them one below the other, ensuring the decimal points are in the same line. For example, adding 2.5 and 1.3 is done by placing the numbers neatly and adding the digits in the same place, like the tenths place and the ones place. They also understand that when subtracting, if a digit in the top number is smaller, they can borrow from the next digit, just like in whole numbers. Multiplication of decimals is introduced by first ignoring the decimal point, multiplying the numbers as whole numbers, and then counting the total decimal places in both numbers to place the decimal point in the answer.

## Multiples-factors-HCF-LCM

In this topic, children learn to find multiples and factors of numbers, such as multiples of 4 being 4, 8, 12, and factors of 12 being 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. They understand that a multiple is the result of multiplying a number, while a factor divides the number exactly. Children also learn to find the Highest Common Factor (HCF) of two numbers, like the HCF of 8 and 12 is 4, by listing common factors. Similarly, they find the Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) of two numbers, like the LCM of 3 and 5 is 15, by listing common multiples. These concepts help in solving real-life problems like sharing things equally or grouping items.

## Number patterns

Children learn to recognise and continue number patterns by finding the rule that connects the numbers. They practice counting in steps of 2, 3, 4, and 5 to create growing sequences like 2, 4, 6, 8 or 5, 10, 15, 20. They also identify patterns in place value, such as the ones, tens, and hundreds increasing when counting up. For example, they see how 10, 20, 30, 40 grows by 10 each time. By completing patterns like 12, 14, 16, __, __, they learn to predict the next numbers using the rule.

## Maps and directions

In Grade 5, children learn to read simple maps and understand directions like north, south, east, and west. They identify symbols and colours on maps to find places like schools, hospitals, and parks. Students use a compass to show directions and draw basic routes between two locations, such as their home to the market. They also practice locating nearby places on a simple village or city map, following paths shown with lines. This helps them understand how to move from one place to another using map clues.

## Volume with unit cubes

Children learn about volume by counting small cubes to find the space inside 3D shapes like boxes. They understand that volume is the total number of unit cubes needed to fill the object completely. For example, if a box is 3 cubes long, 2 cubes wide, and 2 cubes tall, the volume is 12 unit cubes (3 × 2 × 2). They practice counting cubes in different arrangements to see that the volume stays the same even if the shape looks different. This helps them build a strong foundation for measuring space in everyday objects.

## Bar graphs

Children learn to draw bar graphs using vertical bars to show information like the number of fruits in a basket. They understand that each bar's height represents a number, and the gaps between bars must be equal for clear comparison. For example, a bar graph can display how many students like different sweets, with each sweet's bar showing its count. Children practice reading graphs by identifying the tallest and shortest bars to find the most and least popular items. They also learn to label the axes—such as "Number of Students" on the vertical side and "Fruits" on the horizontal side—to make the graph easy to understand.
