# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 4 Mathematics

## Large numbers to 10,00,000

In Class 4, children learn to read, write, and count large numbers up to ten lakhs (10,00,000). They understand the place value system with periods: ones, thousands, and lakhs, and know the names of each place, such as ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten thousands, lakhs, and ten lakhs. Students practice writing numbers in both words and figures, like writing "Five lakhs twenty-three thousand four hundred" as 5,23,400. They also learn to compare and arrange numbers in ascending and descending order, such as putting 3,45,600 before 5,12,300. This helps them build a strong foundation for understanding bigger numbers in daily life.

## Big additions-subtractions

In Class 4, we learn to add and subtract big numbers using the place value method. We write numbers in columns like hundreds, tens, and ones to add or subtract easily. For example, to add 234 and 152, we add ones (4+2=6), tens (3+5=8), and hundreds (2+1=3) to get 386. When subtracting, we sometimes need to exchange one ten for ten ones, like subtracting 58 from 124 becomes 114 minus 50 minus 8 to get 66. This helps us solve real-life problems like counting money or finding differences.

## Multiplication 2-digit

In Class 4, we learn to multiply two-digit numbers by one-digit numbers using the standard method. We first multiply the ones place digit of the two-digit number with the one-digit number, then we multiply the tens place digit and write the result in the next place value. For example, to find 24 × 3, we first do 4 × 3 = 12 and write down 2, carrying over 1. Then we do 2 × 3 = 6 and add the carried-over 1 to get 7, making the final answer 72. This helps us understand place value and makes bigger calculations easier.

## Long division

In Class 4, we learn to divide bigger numbers by breaking them into smaller, easier steps, just like sharing sweets equally. We start by dividing the hundreds place, then the tens, and finally the ones place, always bringing down the next number if needed. For example, to divide 96 by 4, we first divide 9 by 4 to get 2, write 2 in the tens place, multiply 2 by 4 to get 8, subtract from 9 to get 1, bring down 6 to make 16, and divide 16 by 4 to get 4, writing 4 in the ones place. We must always check if the answer is correct by multiplying it with the divisor to get back the original number. This method helps us solve big division problems step by step.

## Factors-multiples

In Class 4, we learn about factors and multiples, which help us understand numbers better. Factors are numbers we can multiply together to get another number, like 2 and 3 are factors of 6 because 2 × 3 = 6. Multiples are what we get when we multiply a number by whole numbers, such as the multiples of 4 are 4, 8, 12, 16, and so on. We also practise finding common factors and common multiples, which are numbers shared by two or more numbers. For example, the common factors of 8 and 12 are 1, 2, and 4. This helps us build a strong foundation for future math problems.

## Fractions: equivalent and like

In Class 4, children learn about fractions that are equal, called equivalent fractions. They understand that 1/2 is the same as 2/4 or 3/6 because they show the same part of a whole. They also learn about like fractions, which have the same bottom number, such as 3/8 and 5/8. Children practice adding and subtracting like fractions by keeping the bottom number the same and only adding or subtracting the top numbers. For example, 2/5 + 1/5 = 3/5 because the bottom numbers are the same.

## Decimals: tenths-hundredths

In this topic, children learn about decimals and their place values, understanding tenths and hundredths as parts of a whole. They learn to write decimals like 0.5 (five tenths) and 0.25 (twenty-five hundredths), seeing how the number after the dot shows smaller parts. They practice comparing decimals, such as 0.3 being greater than 0.15, by looking at the tenths place first. Through examples like money (₹5.50 is five rupees and fifty paise) or length (15.5 cm is fifteen centimetres and five millimetres), they connect decimals to real life. Children also learn to add and subtract simple decimals, making sure to write the dots in the same line.

## Measurement conversions

In class 4, children learn to measure lengths using units like centimetres and metres, and understand that 100 centimetres make 1 metre. They also learn about measuring weights using grams and kilograms, knowing that 1000 grams equal 1 kilogram. For measuring liquids, they use millilitres and litres, with 1000 millilitres filling up 1 litre. Students practice converting smaller units to bigger units, like changing 200 centimetres into 2 metres, and bigger units to smaller units, such as 3 kilograms becoming 3000 grams. These help them compare and understand different amounts in everyday life.

## Perimeter and area

In Class 4, children learn to find the perimeter of simple shapes like rectangles and squares by adding the lengths of all their sides. They also understand that area is the space inside a shape, which they can count by covering it with small squares. For rectangles, they multiply the length and breadth to get the area, while for squares, they multiply the side with itself. Using simple examples, like measuring the boundary of a notebook or counting squares on a grid, they practice these concepts. This helps them solve everyday problems, such as finding how much fence is needed for a garden or how much carpet covers a floor.

## Time, money problems

Children learn to read the time on clocks with both hours and minutes, like 3:00 and 4:30. They also add and subtract small amounts of money using rupees and paise, such as ₹5.50 + ₹2.50 = ₹8.00. Problems include finding the total cost of items or the left-over money after buying something. For example, if a pen costs ₹10 and you give ₹20, you get ₹10 back. They practice solving simple word problems about time and money using addition and subtraction.
