# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 3 Mathematics

## Numbers to 10,000

In this topic, children learn to read, write, and count numbers from 1 to 10,000. They understand the place value of digits in ones, tens, hundreds, and thousands places, like in the number 4,352 where 4 is thousands, 3 is hundreds, 5 is tens, and 2 is ones. They learn to compare numbers using greater than (>), less than (<), and equal to (=) signs, for example, 5,420 is greater than 5,021. Children also practice writing numbers in words and expanding them using place values, like 7,865 as seven thousand eight hundred sixty-five. They solve simple addition and subtraction problems with numbers up to four digits using the column method.

## Four operations

Children learn to add, subtract, multiply, and divide numbers using small numbers up to 100. They use simple methods like counting objects, drawing lines, and repeated addition for multiplication. For addition and subtraction, they learn to write sums in columns and carry over or borrow when needed. They practice dividing groups into equal parts, like sharing 8 candies between 2 friends to get 4 each. These operations help them solve simple problems of daily life, such as buying things or sharing toys with friends.

## Fractions: half-quarter-third

In Class 3, children learn about fractions like half (½), quarter (¼), and third (⅓). A fraction shows a part of a whole, like half a chapati or a quarter of a dosa. To find half, we divide something into two equal parts, and for a quarter, we divide it into four equal parts. A third means dividing something into three equal parts, such as sharing a chocolate bar equally among three friends. Children practice identifying and drawing these fractions using everyday objects like fruits or shapes to understand the concept clearly.

## Money: bills and change

In Class 3, we learn about money using Indian rupees (₹) and paise (p). We count and add coins and notes to find the total amount, like adding two 10-rupee notes to make 20 rupees. We also learn to give the correct change by subtracting small amounts, for example, if something costs 15 rupees and you pay with a 20-rupee note, you get 5 rupees back. We practice identifying different coins and notes, such as the ₹5 coin, ₹10 note, and ₹20 note. This helps us understand how to use money in real-life situations like buying small items.

## Measurement with conversions

In Class 3, children learn to measure length, weight, and capacity using standard units like centimetres, metres, grams, kilograms, litres, and millilitres. They practice converting smaller units to bigger units and vice versa, such as 100 centimetres making 1 metre or 1000 grams equal to 1 kilogram. For example, they solve problems like converting 200 centimetres into 2 metres or 1500 grams into 1.5 kilograms. They also learn to add and subtract measurements after converting them to the same unit. These activities help them understand the relationship between different units and solve real-life measurement problems accurately.

## Time and calendar

Children learn to read the time on an analogue clock by identifying the hour and minute hands. They understand that the long hand points to the minutes and the short hand points to the hour, for example, when the short hand is at 3 and the long hand is at 12, it is 3 o’clock. They also learn the days of the week in order, like Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, and the months of the year, such as January, February, March. Children can name the four seasons – summer, winter, rainy, and spring – and match them to the months when they occur. Finally, they learn to read simple dates, like 15th January, by using a calendar.

## Geometry: lines and shapes

In Class 3, children learn to identify and draw straight lines, curved lines, and simple shapes like squares, rectangles, circles, and triangles. They understand that squares and rectangles have four sides and four corners, while circles have no sides or corners. Triangles have three sides and three corners, and they can be of different types like equilateral and isosceles. Children also learn to distinguish between 2D shapes, which are flat, and 3D objects, which have depth, like cubes and spheres. They practise drawing these shapes using pencils and paper to build their spatial understanding.

## Patterns and pictographs

Children learn to observe and create repeating patterns using shapes, colours, and numbers. They understand that patterns follow a rule, like circle-square-circle-square, and can identify missing parts in a sequence. Pictographs are introduced as simple ways to show information using pictures. For example, a picture of one apple might stand for one child, and children count pictures to find out how many children have apples. This helps them collect, organize, and read data using visual pictures in everyday situations.
