# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 11 Science

## Physics: units-vectors-motion-laws-work-gravitation

Students learn about different units of measurement like metre, second, and kilogram to describe physical quantities. They study vectors, which have both magnitude and direction, and use them to understand motion along straight lines and in two dimensions. The laws of motion explain how forces make objects move or stay still, and we use formulas like F = ma to calculate their effects. Work is done when a force moves an object, and we calculate it with the formula W = F × d × cosθ. Finally, students understand the universal law of gravitation, which describes how all objects in the universe attract each other, like how the Earth pulls us down.

## Physics: matter-heat-waves

Students learn about the three states of matter—solid, liquid, and gas—and how heat affects their properties. They study the expansion of solids, liquids, and gases when heated and their contraction when cooled, with examples like a metal ball passing through a ring after heating. The topic introduces temperature measurement using Celsius and Fahrenheit scales, along with the concept of thermal equilibrium. Key formulas include the coefficient of linear expansion and heat transfer equations, such as Q = mcΔT, where Q is heat, m is mass, c is specific heat capacity, and ΔT is temperature change. Students explore conduction, convection, and radiation as modes of heat transfer, with everyday examples like a metal spoon getting hot in soup.

## Chemistry: mole-atom-periodicity

Students learn that atoms are the basic building blocks of matter and combine to form molecules using simple whole number ratios. They understand that a mole is a counting unit similar to a dozen, representing 6.022 x 10²³ particles, and use Avogadro's number to calculate the number of atoms or molecules in a given mass of a substance. The concept of atomic number and mass number helps in writing the notation of an element, such as Carbon-12, where 6 is the number of protons and 6 is the number of neutrons. Students study the periodic table and observe how properties like atomic size and electronegativity change in a regular pattern from left to right and top to bottom in a period and group. They learn to identify elements with similar chemical behaviour, like the alkali metals in Group 1, and write their symbols, such as Sodium (Na).

## Chemistry: bonding-states-equilibrium-redox

Students learn about chemical bonding, including ionic bonding seen in sodium chloride (NaCl) and covalent bonding in water (H₂O), and how electrons are shared or transferred. They study different states of matter like solids, liquids, and gases, and understand how particles behave in each state. Chemical equilibrium is introduced as a reversible reaction where the rate of forward and backward reactions becomes equal, like the formation of ammonia. Students also explore oxidation and reduction processes, learning that oxidation involves loss of electrons and reduction involves gain of electrons, such as in the rusting of iron.

## Chemistry: organic basics-hydrocarbons

In organic chemistry, students learn about hydrocarbons, which are compounds made only of carbon and hydrogen. They study two main types: alkanes, with single bonds like methane (CH₄), and alkenes, with a double bond like ethene (C₂H₄). Rules are taught for naming these compounds using prefixes such as meth-, eth-, and prop- based on the number of carbon atoms. Students also draw their structures to understand their shapes and properties, like how alkenes can undergo addition reactions. This knowledge forms the foundation for understanding more complex carbon compounds.

## Biology: diversity

In this topic, students learn about the incredible variety of life, called biodiversity, which includes all plants, animals, and micro-organisms on Earth. They explore the different ways scientists classify living things into groups, such as kingdoms like Plantae and Animalia, and smaller groups like genus and species, to understand their relationships. Students will identify examples like the neem tree (Azadirachta indica) from the plant kingdom and the Indian tiger (Panthera tigris) from the animal kingdom. They also understand why this diversity is important for the balance of nature and how different habitats support different types of life. The topic explains that every living thing has a unique role and place in the complex web of life.

## Biology: plant structure-physiology

Students learn the structure and functions of flowering plants, starting with the root system for anchorage and water absorption and the shoot system for photosynthesis. They study the internal tissues like xylem for transporting water and minerals, and phloem for carrying food made by leaves. The structure of a leaf, including the epidermis with stomata for gas exchange, and the process of photosynthesis using chlorophyll are explained with the basic reactants carbon dioxide and water. The functions of the flower, including reproduction through pollination and seed formation, are also covered. Finally, students understand how plants transport water through transpiration, a process where water vapour escapes through the stomata of leaves.

## Biology: human physiology

Students learn about the different systems in the human body, starting with the digestive system which breaks down food into small nutrients for energy. They study the respiratory system that helps us breathe in oxygen and breathe out carbon dioxide, and the circulatory system which carries blood and nutrients to all parts of the body. The excretory system removes waste products like urine from the body, and students also learn about the structure and functions of the skin, hair, and nails, which protect our body. The chapter explains how these systems work together to keep the body healthy and help us grow.
