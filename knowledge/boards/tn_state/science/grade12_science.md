# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 12 Science

## Physics: electrostatics-current

In electrostatics, students learn about electric charges and their properties like positive, negative, and neutral, following the rule that like charges repel and unlike charges attract. They study Coulomb's Law to calculate the force between two charges using the formula F = kq1q2/r², where k is a constant, q represents charges, and r is the distance between them. For example, rubbing a balloon on hair makes it charged and able to attract small paper bits. In current electricity, they understand Ohm’s Law, V = IR, which relates voltage (V), current (I), and resistance (R), and learn how a simple circuit with a battery, wires, and a bulb works to make light. They also explore conductors (like metals) and insulators (like plastic) based on how easily charges move through them.

## Physics: magnetism-EMI-AC

Students learn about magnetic fields and how they are produced by moving charges and permanent magnets. They study Faraday's law, which explains that a changing magnetic field induces an electromotive force (EMF) in a conductor, and Lenz's law, which states that the induced current flows in a direction to oppose the change causing it. Ohm’s law is used to calculate the induced current. Alternating current (AC) is introduced, explaining its nature of continuously changing direction, with cycles and frequency being important concepts. The difference between AC and direct current (DC) is highlighted, with AC being used in household wiring and generated in power plants using rotating coils in magnetic fields.

## Physics: optics-modern physics

Students study the wave nature of light through interference and diffraction patterns, learning to calculate fringe widths using the formula β = λD/d, where λ is wavelength, D is screen distance, and d is slit separation. They explore the photoelectric effect, understanding Einstein’s equation K.E.max = hν - φ, where h is Planck’s constant, ν is frequency, and φ is the work function of the metal. The dual nature of matter is introduced via de Broglie’s hypothesis, λ = h/p, linking particle momentum to wavelength, with examples like electron beams in microscopes. Atomic models are covered, including Bohr’s postulates for hydrogen, which quantize angular momentum as mvr = nh/2π. Nuclear physics concepts involve radioactive decay laws and half-life calculations, using N = N₀(1/2)^(t/T), where N₀ is initial atoms, t is time, and T is half-life.

## Chemistry: solutions-electrochemistry

In the electrochemistry chapter, you learn about electrochemical cells where chemical reactions produce electricity, like in batteries, or electricity drives chemical reactions, such as electroplating. You study the rules for writing half-reactions at the cathode and anode, including reduction (gain of electrons) and oxidation (loss of electrons), and use formulas to calculate cell potential using the standard electrode potentials. The Nernst equation helps you understand how concentration affects the voltage of a cell. For example, you learn about Daniel cells with zinc and copper electrodes and how salt bridges complete the electrical circuit. Concepts like conductance, conductivity, and Kohlrausch’s law explain how ions move in solutions and contribute to electrical current.

## Chemistry: kinetics-surface-d-f block

In Class 12, students learn about reaction rates and factors affecting them like temperature, concentration, and catalysts through the rate law equation. They explore surface chemistry including adsorption, catalysis, and colloids, understanding how properties like particle size and charge are crucial. The d- and f-block elements cover their electronic configurations, variable oxidation states, and the unique chemistry of transition metals and lanthanides/actinides, with examples such as rusting of iron and the colour of copper sulphate solutions.

## Chemistry: organic families-biomolecules

Students learn to identify and classify different types of biomolecules like carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids based on their structures and functions. They study the basic building blocks, such as monosaccharides (like glucose) for carbohydrates and amino acids for proteins, and understand how they join to form complex molecules. The topic also covers the chemical tests to distinguish these biomolecules, for example, the Biuret test for proteins and iodine test for starch. Students explore the roles of these molecules in living organisms, such as energy storage and catalysis by enzymes.

## Biology: reproduction

In the study of biology for Class 12, students learn about human reproduction, including the structure and functions of the male and female reproductive organs. They understand the process of fertilisation, where a sperm fuses with an ovum to form a zygote, and the events of pregnancy. The topic also covers the menstrual cycle and its hormonal regulation, explaining the monthly changes in the female reproductive system. Students learn about reproductive health and the importance of family planning methods, including natural and contraceptive techniques, to prevent unwanted pregnancies and sexually transmitted diseases.

## Biology: genetics-evolution

Students learn about Mendel's laws of inheritance, explaining how traits are passed from parents to offspring through genes, with examples like pea plant flower colour. They study the structure of DNA and RNA, understanding how genetic information is stored and translated into proteins. The topic covers the process of evolution, including natural selection and the evidence for it from fossils and comparative anatomy. Students also explore the mechanisms of variation, such as mutations and genetic drift, and how they contribute to biodiversity. Key concepts include chromosomes, alleles, and the Hardy-Weinberg principle, which describes genetic equilibrium in populations.

## Biology: biotechnology-ecology-health

Students learn about biotechnology, its applications in medicine and agriculture, and its ethical implications. They study the basics of genetic engineering, including the use of restriction enzymes and plasmids to produce genetically modified organisms, such as Bt cotton. The topic also covers fundamental ecological concepts like food chains, food webs, and energy flow within an ecosystem. Students explore how human activities impact the environment and health, including pollution and the spread of diseases like tuberculosis and dengue. Finally, they examine the importance of biodiversity and conservation to maintain ecological balance and ensure the well-being of all living organisms.
