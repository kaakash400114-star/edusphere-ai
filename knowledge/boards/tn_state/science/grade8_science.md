# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 8 Science

## Crop production

Students learn about growing crops through important steps like tilling the soil, sowing seeds, watering regularly, and adding manure or fertilizers for better growth. They understand that different plants need different amounts of sunlight, water, and nutrients to grow well, like rice needing more water than wheat. A key concept is crop rotation, where farmers grow different crops in the same field each year to keep the soil healthy and fertile, such as growing pulses after rice. Students also study weeds, which are unwanted plants that compete with crops for food and water, and how to remove them by hand or with tools. Finally, they learn about harvesting, which is gathering the mature crops from the field when they are ready to be used, like picking ripe mangoes or cutting wheat.

## Microorganisms

Microorganisms are tiny living things that cannot be seen with our naked eyes. They are found everywhere, in air, water, soil and inside our bodies. Some microorganisms are useful, like the bacteria that help make curd from milk, while others can cause diseases. Fungi like mushrooms are also microorganisms that grow on things like bread and rotting wood. Viruses are another type of microorganism that cause common illnesses like the flu and measles.

## Metals-non-metals

Metals are solid, shiny materials that can be beaten into thin sheets, like gold and silver, and are good conductors of heat and electricity. Non-metals, such as carbon and sulphur, are mostly dull, not malleable, and poor conductors; they exist in solid, liquid or gas forms. Many metals react with air to form a thin layer of metal oxide, for example, copper turns green when exposed. Metals also react with water to produce metal hydroxides and hydrogen gas; sodium reacts vigorously, whereas iron reacts very slowly. To test if a material is a metal or non-metal, we observe its properties like lustre, malleability and conductivity.

## Coal-petroleum

Coal and petroleum are natural resources found deep inside the earth. They are called fossil fuels because they were formed from the remains of dead plants and animals that lived millions of years ago. Coal is a solid fuel used in thermal power plants to generate electricity for homes and industries. Petroleum is a liquid fuel that is refined to make different products like petrol for vehicles, diesel for trucks, and LPG for cooking gas. We must save these non-renewable resources because once used up, they cannot be made again for a very long time.

## Combustion-flame

Fire, or combustion, needs three things to happen: fuel, oxygen, and heat. This is called the fire triangle. A flame is the visible part of fire, which has three zones: the dark inner zone, the luminous middle zone, and the non-luminous outer zone. The colour of the flame tells us about the temperature: a blue flame is hotter than a yellow flame. We put out fire by removing any one part of the fire triangle, like covering it with sand to block oxygen or pouring water to cool it down. A good example is when we use a matchstick to light a candle, which has wax as fuel, and the flame goes out if we cover it with a glass.

## Conservation of plants-animals

Plants and animals are protected by government rules called laws, which stop people from cutting trees or hunting wild animals. National parks and sanctuaries are safe homes for animals like tigers and elephants where they can live freely. We save animals like the one-horned rhinoceros and plants like the neem tree so that all living things stay on our Earth. Protecting nature means we do not waste water and plant new trees to give homes to birds and squirrels. Every plant and animal has a special job in nature, so we must all help to keep them safe for the future.

## Cell-structure

Cells are the tiniest living parts of all plants, animals, and even small organisms like amoeba. A plant cell has a rigid outer wall, while an animal cell does not. Inside every cell is a control centre called the nucleus, which tells the cell what to do. Small parts called mitochondria provide energy for the cell to work and stay alive, like a tiny power station. Cells work together to form tissues, like the skin tissue on your hand, and tissues make up organs like your stomach.

## Reproduction in animals

Animals make babies to grow their families. Babies can look like their parents through sexual reproduction where a male and female parent are needed. Some animals, like starfish, can make new babies from their own body parts without a partner, called asexual reproduction. Inside the mother’s body, the baby grows in a special place called the uterus until it is born. For example, cats give birth to kittens, and hens lay eggs which hatch into chicks. All animals have different ways to keep their kind living on.

## Adolescence

During adolescence, our bodies change a lot. Boys start to get deeper voices and grow taller, while girls develop breasts and start their monthly periods called menstruation. Hormones are special chemicals inside our body that control all these changes and make us feel different emotions like happiness or anger. It is very important to keep our bodies clean and healthy by taking regular baths and eating nutritious food like fruits, vegetables, and milk. These changes are a normal part of growing up and every person experiences them at some point in their life.

## Force-pressure

Force is a push or pull on an object, like pushing a door or pulling a drawer. Pressure is the amount of force acting on a unit area, and we can calculate it using the formula Pressure = Force / Area. When you stand on soft ground, your weight spreads over a larger area if you lie down, so you feel less pressure. Using a sharp knife helps because the small blade area makes the pressure high, so it cuts easily. We use this knowledge in everyday life, like why tractors have broad tyres to avoid sinking into the soil.

## Friction

Friction is a force that opposes the motion of objects when they move over each other, like a ball rolling slowly and stopping on the floor. It depends on the type of surfaces in contact; a rough surface creates more friction than a smooth one. Friction helps us in many ways, for example, it allows us to walk without slipping and lets the brakes of a cycle or bus stop the wheels. To reduce friction, we use lubricants like oil or grease on machine parts, so they can move smoothly. Sometimes friction is useful, and sometimes it is not, so we try to increase or decrease it based on our needs.

## Sound

Sound is a form of energy produced by vibrations that travel through a medium like air, water, or a solid. We hear sound when these vibrations reach our ears and are sent to the brain. Sound needs a medium to travel and cannot move through empty space, like a vacuum. The loudness of sound depends on the amplitude of the vibration, and its pitch depends on how fast the object vibrates, known as frequency. For example, a drum has a low pitch because it vibrates slowly, while a whistle has a high pitch because it vibrates very quickly.

## Chemical effects of current

When an electric current passes through a liquid, it can cause chemical changes, which we call the chemical effects of current. This process is called electrolysis, and the liquid through which current flows is an electrolyte, like salt water or copper sulphate solution. The two rods dipped in the electrolyte are called electrodes, the positive one is the anode and the negative one is the cathode. In electrolysis, the electrolyte breaks down into new substances at the electrodes, for example, in copper sulphate solution, copper gets deposited on the cathode. This shows that electricity can make liquids change into new materials.

## Light-eyes

The eye is a special sense organ that helps us see the world around us. It has important parts like the lens and the cornea that work together to focus light onto the retina. The retina, which has light-sensitive cells, sends signals to the brain through the optic nerve. The pupil in the middle of the eye can get smaller or bigger to control how much light enters, like a camera. When we see far away things, the lens becomes thinner, and for close things, it becomes fatter to focus the light properly.

## Natural phenomena

In Class 8 Science, you learn about natural phenomena like lightning and thunder, which happen when static electricity builds up in clouds. You also study how earthquakes occur when the Earth’s plates move suddenly, causing the ground to shake. You will understand how rain is formed through the water cycle, where water from oceans and rivers evaporates, forms clouds, and falls as rain. These phenomena help us learn about nature’s powerful forces and how to stay safe during them.
