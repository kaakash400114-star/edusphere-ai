# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 7 Science

## Nutrition in plants

Plants make their own food through a process called photosynthesis using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide. The green pigment called chlorophyll in leaves helps to capture sunlight for this process. Plants absorb water from the soil through their roots and carbon dioxide from the air through tiny pores in their leaves. During photosynthesis, plants convert these raw materials into glucose, a type of sugar that gives them energy to grow. For example, banyan trees and mango trees use photosynthesis to make their own food just like other green plants.

## Nutrition in animals

Animals get their food by eating plants or other animals to grow and stay healthy. Different animals have different ways of eating, like cows chewing grass called cud, and snakes swallowing their food whole. After eating, the food is broken down in the stomach with the help of digestive juices, which turn the food into small pieces that the body can use. For example, we chew food with our teeth, then it goes to the stomach where it is digested, and the nutrients give us energy to run and play. This process is called digestion, and it helps all animals get energy from the food they eat.

## Heat

Heat is a form of energy that makes things feel warm or hot. We measure temperature using a thermometer and the unit is called degree Celsius. Heat always flows from a hotter object to a colder object until both become the same temperature. Materials that let heat pass through them easily, like metal, are called conductors, while materials that do not, like wood, are called insulators. We use good conductors to make cooking pots so that heat can cook our food quickly.

## Acids-bases-salts

Children learn about the sour taste of acids like lemon juice and the soapy feel of bases like soap solution. They discover that acids turn blue litmus paper red, while bases turn red litmus paper blue. Students understand that mixing an acid and a base neutralises each other, forming a salt and water, such as when hydrochloric acid reacts with sodium hydroxide to make common salt. The topic also explains how indicators, like turmeric, help identify acidic or basic substances by changing colour. Finally, children learn about the safe use of acids and bases in daily life, like using baking soda in cooking.

## Physical-chemical changes

In science, we learn that changes can be physical or chemical. Physical changes change the form of a substance, like when water freezes into ice or when we tear paper. Chemical changes make a new substance with different properties, like when milk turns into curd or when wood burns to form ash. In a chemical change, we often see new colours, smells, or gases, and sometimes heat is produced. The main rule is that in a physical change, no new material is made, but in a chemical change, a new substance always forms.

## Respiration

Respiration is the process where our body takes in air to get oxygen and gives out carbon dioxide. We breathe through our nose, and air goes to our lungs through tubes called windpipes. Oxygen from the air helps us get energy from food, which keeps us alive and helps us play and study. Our ribs and a big muscle called diaphragm help us move air in and out of our lungs. When we run or jump, we breathe faster because our body needs more energy.

## Transport in plants-animals

Plants have special tubes called xylem and phloem to transport water and food. Xylem carries water and minerals from the roots to the leaves, like water pipes in a building. Phloem moves food made by the leaves to all other parts of the plant, similar to how a delivery van brings food to shops. Animals have a circulatory system with a heart and blood vessels to transport blood. The heart acts like a pump, pushing blood through arteries and veins to carry oxygen and food to the body's cells. For example, the human heart pumps blood to the legs and the brain to keep them working.

## Reproduction in plants

Plants have different ways to make new plants, like seeds and without seeds. Flowers help plants make seeds through a process called pollination, where pollen from the stamen sticks to the stigma. Some plants, like roses, can grow new plants from their stems or roots, which is called vegetative reproduction. A seed has a baby plant called an embryo and stored food to help it grow. When a seed gets water and air, it germinates and grows into a new plant, like a mango tree from a mango seed.

## Motion-time

Students learn about motion as a change in position of an object with respect to its surroundings. They observe different types of motion, like straight-line movement of a car and circular motion of a ceiling fan. The concept of time is used to measure how fast or slow an object moves, with speed telling us the distance covered in a given time. For example, a bicycle moving 10 metres in 2 seconds has a speed of 5 metres per second. Students also understand that an object at rest or moving with a steady speed is not experiencing any change in motion.

## Electric current effects

Electric current has important effects, like heating the wire through which it flows. When current passes through a thin wire, it becomes very hot and can even glow, like in an electric bulb. This heating effect is used in devices like heaters and irons to warm things up. We can calculate the heat produced using the formula H = I² × R × t, where H is heat, I is current, R is resistance, and t is time. A big current or resistance makes more heat. Current also creates a magnetic effect around it, which helps in making electromagnets for picking up iron nails.

## Light

Light travels in a straight line and reflects when it hits a shiny surface, like a mirror, bouncing back to our eyes. We see things because they reflect light into our eyes; dark objects absorb light and do not reflect it much. A shadow is formed when an object blocks light, and shadows are always on the opposite side of the light source. We use mirrors to reflect light and see things around corners, like in a periscope. Light helps us see the world and makes things visible by travelling in straight lines until it is blocked or reflected.

## Forests-water-wastewater

Forests are big areas with many trees, plants, and animals that give us fresh air and rain. They also act like a big sponge, soaking up rainwater and slowly releasing it into rivers and streams, which is very important for having clean water to drink. When we use water at home, it becomes dirty and is called wastewater, which must be cleaned before going back into rivers. Plants and animals in forests depend on each other for food and shelter, like birds nesting in trees and squirrels eating fruits. We must save our forests and not waste water to help keep nature healthy for everyone.
