# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 6 Science

## Food components

Food is made of different parts called nutrients that help us grow and stay healthy. There are five main nutrients: carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, and minerals. Carbohydrates give us energy, like in rice and chapatis. Proteins help us build strong muscles, found in foods like dal and eggs. Fats give us extra energy, present in nuts and ghee. Vitamins and minerals protect us from diseases, found in fruits like mangoes and vegetables like carrots. We need to eat different foods to get all these nutrients and stay strong.

## Sorting materials

We learn to group things by their properties like colour, size, shape, and how they feel. Materials can be sorted into solids, liquids, or gases based on whether they hold their shape or not. We also sort materials into natural things like wood and stone, and man-made things like plastic and glass. For example, we can sort fruits like apples and oranges by their colour or whether they grow on trees. This helps us understand the world around us better.

## Separation methods

In this topic, students learn about separating mixtures using simple methods. We can separate solid things from liquid by filtering, like using a sieve to get rice from water. To separate bigger solids from smaller ones, we can use hand-picking, like stones from rice. If a solid dissolves in water, we can get it back by heating the water, like getting salt from salt water. We also learn that some things float, so we can separate them by skimming, like oil from water. These methods help us use things in our daily life.

## Plants around us

Plants are living things that grow from seeds and need water, sunlight, and air to live. They make their own food using sunlight in a process called photosynthesis. Plants have different parts like roots, stem, leaves, flowers, and fruits, each with a special job. Roots take water from the soil, the stem carries it to other parts, and leaves help make food. We see plants all around us like neem trees, mango trees, and small rose bushes in our gardens.

## Body movements

Our body moves because of bones and muscles. We have strong bones that make our skeleton, like the backbone and thigh bone. Muscles are soft and pull the bones to help us walk, run, and eat. Joints are places where two bones meet and allow movement, like the knee and elbow. Some joints can move in many directions, like the shoulder, while others move only a little, like the fingers. We also learn about different types of movements such as bending the elbow or turning the head. This helps us understand how our body works every day.

## Living organisms-habitats

Living organisms live in different places called habitats, like forests, ponds, or deserts. A habitat provides everything a living thing needs to survive, such as food, water, air, and shelter. Plants and animals have special features to live in their habitats; for example, cactus plants store water to live in dry deserts. Camels have humps to store fat and help them travel long distances without water in the desert. Different habitats have different types of plants and animals that are suited to live there.

## Motion and measurement

In this topic, children learn about the different ways things move, like walking, running, and rolling. They understand that measuring how long something is helps us describe its size and how far it travels. For example, we use a metre scale to measure the length of a desk and a metre tape for longer distances like a corridor. They also observe that things can move in a straight line, in circles, or back and forth, and we name these different types of motion. Finally, they learn to choose the right measuring tool, like a ruler for small things and a measuring tape for big things, to get the correct answer.

## Light-shadows

Light travels in straight lines, and this is why we see shadows when an object blocks its path. Shadows are formed when light from a source like the sun or a torch cannot pass through an opaque object. The shape of the shadow depends on the shape of the object blocking the light, and its size can change if the object is moved closer or further from the light source. For example, a ball makes a round shadow, and your hand makes a shadow that looks like a bird when you hold it in front of the torch. By moving your hand, you can make the shadow bigger or smaller.

## Electricity-circuits

Electricity flows in a path called a circuit, which must be closed for the current to move and light a bulb. Materials like copper wire allow electricity to pass easily and are called conductors, while plastic stops it and is an insulator. A circuit needs a battery to give the electricity energy, and when you connect wires from the battery’s positive to negative terminal through the bulb, it lights up. If you break the circuit by opening a switch or disconnecting a wire, the light goes off because the flow of electricity stops.

## Magnets

Magnets have two ends called poles—the north pole and the south pole. Like poles (north-north or south-south) push away from each other, while unlike poles (north-south) pull towards each other. Some natural magnets are called lodestones, and we make artificial magnets by rubbing steel bars with a magnet. A magnet can attract objects made of iron, like nails and keys, but not plastic or wood. If you break a magnet, the smaller pieces will still have a north and south pole.

## Water

Water is a very important natural resource that we need to live and keep our environment clean. We learn about the three forms of water: solid as ice, liquid as rain and river water, and gas as water vapour in the air. Plants and animals, like birds and fish, also need water to live and grow. Water is used for many purposes, such as drinking, cooking, bathing, and farming. We must always save water and not waste it, because clean water is not available everywhere and it is very precious for all living things.

## Air

Air is all around us and fills the empty space. It is a mixture of many gases like oxygen, nitrogen, and carbon dioxide. We need oxygen from the air to breathe and live. Moving air is called wind, and we can feel it when it blows the leaves on trees. When air is heated, it becomes light and goes up, making cold air come down in a cycle. This moving air can help us dry clothes and make windmills move. We must keep our air clean by not burning too many things and planting more trees.

## Waste management

We learn about waste and its different types like dry waste and wet waste. Dry waste includes plastic, paper, and glass, while wet waste has food peels and leftover vegetables. We must always put waste in the correct dustbin to keep our environment clean. Garbage bins have colours, like blue for dry waste and green for wet waste, to help us sort them. We should not throw plastic everywhere because it harms animals and makes the land dirty. By throwing waste in the right place, we help keep our school, home, and city neat and healthy.
