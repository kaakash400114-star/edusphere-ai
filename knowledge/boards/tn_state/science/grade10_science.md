# Tamil Nadu State Board Class 10 Science

## Chemical reactions and equations

Students learn about chemical reactions where substances change into new ones with different properties. They study how to write word equations and balance chemical equations by adjusting coefficients to follow the law of conservation of mass. Key concepts include reactants and products, with examples like the reaction of iron with air to form rust or magnesium with oxygen to form magnesium oxide. Students also identify different types of reactions such as combination, decomposition, displacement, and double displacement using real-life examples. The topic helps students understand how to represent changes in matter accurately using symbols and formulas.

## Acids, bases and salts

In this topic, students learn to identify acids, bases, and salts using their taste and effect on litmus paper. They study how acids like hydrochloric acid and acetic acid turn blue litmus red, while bases like sodium hydroxide turn red litmus blue. The concept of neutralisation is introduced, where an acid and a base react to form salt and water, such as hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide producing sodium chloride salt. Students also learn about the pH scale, which measures the acidity or basicity of a substance, with examples like lemon juice being acidic and soap solution being basic. The uses of common salts like sodium chloride (table salt) and baking soda (sodium bicarbonate) in daily life are discussed.

## Metals and non-metals

Metals are shiny, good conductors of heat and electricity, and lose electrons to form positive ions during reactions, like sodium in salt. Non-metals are dull, poor conductors, and gain electrons to form negative ions, like chlorine in salt. Metals react with oxygen to form basic oxides such as magnesium oxide, while non-metals form acidic oxides like carbon dioxide. Metals displace less reactive metals from their salt solutions, for example, iron displaces copper from copper sulphate. The reactivity series orders metals from most reactive, like potassium, to least reactive, like gold, helping predict displacement reactions.

## Carbon and its compounds

In Class 10, you learn about carbon and its amazing ability to form many compounds. Carbon atoms share electrons with other atoms like hydrogen, oxygen, and chlorine to make molecules. You study the special properties of hydrocarbons, which are compounds made of only carbon and hydrogen, such as methane, the main gas in LPG. You also learn about the structures of these molecules, including open-chain and ring forms. Another important concept is the difference between saturated and unsaturated hydrocarbons, with examples like ethane and ethene.

## Periodic classification

Students learn about the periodic classification of elements based on their properties. They study Mendeleev’s periodic table, which arranged elements in order of increasing atomic mass and grouped them with similar properties in columns called groups. The modern periodic table arranges elements by their atomic numbers and shows repeating patterns in their properties, called periodic trends. For example, elements in the same group, like sodium (Na) and potassium (K), have similar chemical behaviour and react with water to produce hydrogen gas. Students also understand how elements are divided into metals, non-metals, and metalloids based on their physical and chemical properties.

## Life processes

Students learn that living things carry out life processes like nutrition, respiration, and transportation to stay alive. Nutrition includes how plants make their own food through photosynthesis using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide, and how animals eat other organisms for food. Respiration is the process where both plants and animals break down food to release energy, taking in oxygen and giving out carbon dioxide. The circulatory system in humans moves substances like food, oxygen, and waste around the body with the help of the heart and blood vessels. These processes are essential for the growth, survival, and functioning of all living organisms.

## Control and coordination

In Class 10, you learn how living organisms control and coordinate their actions using the nervous system and chemical messengers called hormones. The nervous system works with special cells called neurons, which carry messages as electrical impulses from sense organs to the brain and spinal cord. Hormones are produced in glands like the pancreas and released into the blood to regulate processes like growth and metabolism. For example, the hormone insulin controls the level of sugar in your blood, while adrenaline prepares your body for action in stressful situations. The brain, spinal cord, and nerves together form the central nervous system that helps you respond quickly to things like heat or sound.

## How do organisms reproduce

Organisms reproduce to create more of their own kind. Plants like rose and hibiscus make new plants using methods such as stem cuttings, while animals like cats and dogs give birth to live young ones. In flowering plants, male and female parts work together to produce seeds for the next generation. Some simple organisms like yeast and amoeba split into two to make new ones, which is called asexual reproduction. Reproduction is a special power that helps all living things continue their life on Earth.

## Heredity and evolution

In Class 10, students learn that heredity is the passing of traits from parents to children, like getting your mother’s eye colour or your father’s height. The rules of inheritance are explained through Mendel’s work with pea plants, showing how traits are passed down through genes. You will also discover how variations occur due to sexual reproduction and environmental factors, leading to diversity among organisms. Evolution is studied as the slow process by which species change over many generations to adapt better to their surroundings. Finally, you will learn about fossils and classification of organisms to understand how different life forms have developed over time.

## Light: reflection and refraction

Light travels in straight lines and bounces off smooth surfaces like a mirror following the law of reflection, where the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection. When light passes from one transparent medium, like air to water, it bends or refracts; this change in direction is described by Snell’s law, n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r, where n is the refractive index. Lenses, both convex and concave, use refraction to form images: convex lenses can create magnified real images like in a slide projector or virtual images in a magnifying glass. The human eye has a convex lens that focuses light on the retina to form clear images, just like how a camera lens works. These principles help us understand why objects look bent in a glass of water or why we see rainbows after rain.

## The human eye and the colourful world

The human eye has a lens that focuses light on the retina, forming an inverted image. We can see clearly because the lens changes its shape, a process called accommodation, to focus on objects at different distances. The phenomenon of persistence of vision allows us to see movies as continuous images even though they are made of separate frames. When light passes through a prism, it splits into seven colours called the spectrum, which is why we see rainbows in the sky after rain. Sometimes, the eye lens becomes cloudy, causing cataract, and a doctor may have to replace it with an artificial lens to restore vision.

## Electricity

In Class 10 Science, you learn about electricity by understanding electric current, which is the flow of charge through a conductor like a copper wire. Ohm’s Law teaches us that the current in a circuit is equal to the voltage divided by the resistance, written as I = V/R. You study series and parallel circuits, where in a series the current is the same everywhere, and in parallel, the voltage is the same across each branch. Electric power is calculated using the formula P = V × I, and it is measured in watts, like the power rating of a bulb or fan. Safety rules, such as not using damaged switches and using earthing, are also important to prevent accidents from electric shocks.

## Magnetic effects of electric current

Electric currents produce a magnetic field around them, and this effect is strongest when the wire is coiled into a solenoid. The direction of this magnetic field can be found using the Right Hand Thumb Rule, where the thumb points towards the current and the curled fingers show the magnetic field's direction. An electromagnet is created by winding insulated copper wire around a soft iron core and passing an electric current through it. Using this principle, an electric bell works with an electromagnet that pulls a striker to hit a bell every time the circuit is completed. This magnetic effect is also the basic working principle of electric motors.

## Our environment

In Class 10 Science, students learn about the environment and the importance of protecting our natural resources. They understand ecosystems, which are made of living organisms like plants and animals and non-living things like soil and water, all working together. Students study how materials like carbon and nitrogen move between living and non-living parts of nature in cycles. They also learn about pollution, such as air and water pollution, and how human activities like throwing waste or using cars harm the environment. The lesson teaches the 3 R's: Reduce, Reuse, and Recycle, to help keep our surroundings clean and healthy for the future.

## Sustainable management of natural resources

In Class 10 Science, students learn about sustainable management of natural resources, focusing on how to use them wisely without running out. They study the importance of water conservation through methods like rainwater harvesting and drip irrigation to save water for the future. The chapter also explains the three Rs—Reduce, Reuse, and Recycle—to manage waste and protect the environment, such as using old newspapers for packing or turning plastic bottles into planters. Students understand forests as natural resources and learn about their conservation through afforestation and preventing deforestation, like planting trees in empty lands to maintain ecological balance. Finally, they explore the role of dams and reservoirs in storing water for irrigation and electricity while minimizing their impact on ecosystems.
