# Kenyan CBC Grade 5 Mathematics
## Numbers, Operations and Relationships
The learner is guided to read, write, and count numbers up to 99,999. They are taught to identify place values from ten-thousands to ones. The learner is guided to compare and order whole numbers. They are guided to use symbols >, <, and = to compare numbers. The learner is guided to add and subtract five-digit numbers using the column method. For example, adding 12,450 shillings and 7,850 shillings. The learner is guided to solve word problems involving money, like calculating the total cost of ugali and vegetables.

## Measurement
The learner is guided to measure length, mass, and capacity using appropriate units. They are guided to measure length in metres and centimetres. The learner is guided to measure mass in kilograms and grams. They are guided to measure capacity in litres and millilitres. The learner is guided to convert between these units, such as 1,000 millilitres equals 1 litre. The learner is guided to solve problems involving weight, like finding the total mass of a sack of maize.

## Geometry
The learner is guided to identify and describe properties of 2D and 3D shapes. They are guided to name and describe triangles, quadrilaterals, and circles. The learner is guided to name and describe cubes, cuboids, and cylinders. They are guided to relate these shapes to real-world objects in Kenya, such as a boda boda wheel (circle) or a water tank (cuboid). The learner is guided to identify lines of symmetry in shapes.

## Data Handling and Probability
The learner is guided to collect, record, and organise data using tally marks and frequency tables. They are guided to interpret simple bar graphs and pictographs. The learner is guided to answer questions from a graph, such as, "Which animal is most common at Nairobi National Park?" They are guided to find the mode (most frequent item) in a set of data. The learner is guided to conduct a simple survey, like asking classmates about their favourite fruit.

## Algebra
The learner is guided to recognise, create, and extend simple number and shape patterns. They are guided to describe the rule of a pattern, such as "add 3". The learner is guided to use letters to represent unknown numbers in simple equations. For example, in 5 + n = 12, the learner is guided to find that n is 7. They are guided to solve simple balance problems with shapes and numbers to find missing values.

## Financial Literacy
The learner is guided to identify and count Kenyan currency notes and coins. They are guided to find the total value of a group of notes and coins. The learner is guided to calculate correct change during transactions. For example, if an item costs 250 shillings and you pay with a 500-shilling note, the change is 250 shillings. The learner is guided to plan a simple budget for a class trip, considering transport and entry fees.
