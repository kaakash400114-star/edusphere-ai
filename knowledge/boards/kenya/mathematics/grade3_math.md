# Kenyan CBC Grade 3 Mathematics
## Numbers up to 999
The learner is guided to read, write, and order numbers from 1 to 999. They will use hundreds, tens, and ones to understand place value. For example, the number 478 has 4 hundreds, 7 tens, and 8 ones. Learners can compare numbers like 650 and 570 to find which is larger. They practice writing numbers in words, such as 'two hundred and forty-five'. Using Kenyan examples, they can count shillings, like Ksh 450, or count items like maize cobs harvested from a small farm.
## Addition and Subtraction
The learner is guided to add and subtract numbers up to 999. They learn to use the column method for calculations, ensuring to align the hundreds, tens, and ones. For addition, they find the sum of two or more numbers, for example, 345 + 123. For subtraction, they find the difference between numbers, like 600 - 278. Learners solve real-life problems, such as calculating the total cost of school books or finding out how much money is left after buying ugali flour for Ksh 150 from Ksh 500.
## Multiplication and Division
The learner is guided to understand multiplication as repeated addition and division as sharing. They learn multiplication tables up to 5 and use them in simple calculations like 4 x 6. They can solve word problems, such as finding the total cost of 5 boda boda rides at Ksh 50 each. For division, learners practice sharing items equally, like dividing 24 mangoes among 6 friends. They use drawings of objects like fruits or money to represent these groups and find answers to problems.
## Measurement
The learner is guided to measure length, mass, and capacity using standard units. They measure length in metres and centimetres, such as the length of a classroom table. They measure mass in kilograms and grams, like the weight of a bag of sugar. For capacity, they learn litres and millilitres, used for water in a jerrycan. Learners also measure time in hours and minutes and read the time from a clock, connecting this to daily routines like catching a boda boda to school.
## Geometry and Shapes
The learner is guided to identify and name common 2D and 3D shapes. They learn about squares, rectangles, triangles, circles, and ovals. In 3D, they identify cubes, cuboids, cylinders, and spheres, relating these to real objects like a matchbox (cuboid) or a tin of flour (cylinder). Learners can create shapes from paper or clay and describe them by their properties, such as a square having four equal sides and four corners. They observe shapes in their environment, like the round wheels of a matatu or the rectangular shape of a classroom.
## Data and Probability
The learner is guided to collect, organise, and interpret simple data. They can record information using tally marks and present it in pictographs or bar graphs. For example, they can collect data on the favourite wild animals of their classmates, with choices from Nairobi National Park like lions, giraffes, and zebras. Learners will then answer simple questions about their data, such as 'Which animal was chosen the most?'. This helps them understand how data is used to make decisions and find information.
