# Kenyan CBC Grade 10 Mathematics

## Number Theory and Indices
The learner is guided to understand prime and composite numbers up to 100, including unique prime factorization. They learn to apply the laws of indices, such as multiplication and division of like bases, and raising a power to another power. The learner is guided to simplify expressions involving negative and fractional indices, understanding that a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ and a¹/ᵐ = ⁿ√a. They apply this to calculate compound interest, determining final amounts using the formula A = P(1 + r)ⁿ, where principal P, rate r, and number of years n are real numbers from Kenyan financial contexts.

## Algebraic Expressions and Equations
The learner is guided to manipulate algebraic expressions by expanding products of binomials using the distributive property and factorizing quadratic expressions of the form ax² + bx + c. They are guided to solve linear equations in one variable and simple quadratic equations by factorization or using the formula x = [-b ± √(b² - 4ac)] / 2a. The learner is guided to form and solve equations from real-life problems, such as calculating the cost of materials for a project or determining break-even points for a small business in Nairobi.

## Geometry and Measurement
The learner is guided to calculate the area and circumference of circles, using π ≈ 22/7 or 3.14. They are guided to find the surface area and volume of prisms, pyramids, and cones. The learner is guided to apply the concept of similar triangles to solve problems involving indirect measurement, such as determining the height of a tree or a building at the Nairobi National Park by measuring the shadow of a known object. They are also guided to convert between units of area and volume in the metric system.

## Trigonometry
The learner is guided to define sine, cosine, and tangent of an acute angle in a right-angled triangle as the ratio of specific sides (SOH CAH TOA). They are guided to use trigonometric ratios to find unknown angles and sides in right-angled triangles. The learner is guided to apply this knowledge to solve practical problems like calculating the height of a hill, the distance across a river, or the length of a guy wire supporting a telecommunications mast, all using measurements in metres and shillings for surveying costs.

## Statistics and Probability
The learner is guided to collect, organize, and present statistical data using frequency tables and histograms. They are guided to calculate and interpret the mean, median, and mode for both grouped and ungrouped data. The learner is guided to understand probability as a measure of chance between 0 and 1, calculating it for single events. They apply this to analyze simple real-world events, such as predicting the probability of a boda boda operator finding a client in a specific location within an hour or the likelihood of different weather conditions affecting a school sports event.

## Coordinate Geometry and Graphs
The learner is guided to plot points and draw straight-line graphs from equations of the form y = mx + c, interpreting the gradient m as the rate of change and c as the y-intercept. They are guided to find the equation of a line given two points or the gradient and a point. The learner is guided to solve simultaneous equations graphically by finding the point of intersection. They apply this to model linear relationships, such as the cost of transporting ugali in sacks from a wholesale market in Thika to various retail points in Nairobi, determining the most economical route.
