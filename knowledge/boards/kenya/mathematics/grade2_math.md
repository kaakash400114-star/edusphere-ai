# Kenyan CBC Grade 2 Mathematics
## Number Concept and Operations
The learner is guided to count, read, and write whole numbers up to 100 using concrete objects like buttons and sticks. They learn to identify and name numbers forwards and backwards. Learners are guided to add numbers whose sum is not more than 100 using a number line and objects. They also learn to subtract numbers with a difference not more than 100. Through practical examples like sharing 20 shillings among five friends, learners understand the concepts of addition and subtraction in their daily lives.

## Measurement
The learner is guided to measure length using non-standard units such as hand spans and strides. They compare objects to identify shorter, longer, or equal lengths. Learners are guided to measure capacity using containers like jugs and cups. They compare quantities to identify more or less. By measuring flour to make ugali or estimating the amount of water in a drum, learners connect measurement to familiar household activities and develop practical estimation skills.

## Geometry and Shapes
The learner is guided to identify and name common two-dimensional shapes like circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles found in objects like wheels (circles) and books (rectangles). They are guided to identify and name common three-dimensional shapes such as cones, cylinders, and spheres, seeing them in items like traffic cones and balls. Learners describe shapes using simple terms like round, flat, and straight, relating them to objects seen at the Nairobi National Park or in their community.

## Money
The learner is guided to identify and sort Kenyan coins (1 shilling, 5 shillings, 10 shillings, 20 shillings) and banknotes (50 shillings, 100 shillings, 200 shillings, 500 shillings). They are guided to count money to make exact amounts for simple purchases, such as buying mandazi for 40 shillings. Learners use money in play scenarios, like buying snacks at a mock shop, and practice giving change, understanding the value of different denominations in real-world transactions.

## Data and Graphs
The learner is guided to collect and organise data by sorting objects or pictures into groups. They are guided to represent simple data using real objects, tally marks, or pictographs. For example, they can tally the number of boda bodas passing a point in the morning and afternoon. Learners interpret simple pictographs to answer questions like ‘Which group has the most?’, developing early skills in reading and understanding information presented visually.

## Time
The learner is guided to sequence events using words like first, next, and last, such as telling the story of preparing for school. They are guided to identify times of the day and days of the week, linking them to daily routines like eating lunch or attending school. Learners read the time to the hour using an analogue clock. They associate activities with specific times, like leaving school at 4 o'clock, connecting time to the structure of their day.
