# Kenyan CBC Grade 8 Mathematics

## Linear Equations and Inequalities
The learner is guided to form and solve linear equations in one and two unknowns using algebraic methods. They are taught to identify constants, variables, and coefficients in an equation. For example, solving 3x + 5 = 20 involves isolating the variable by first subtracting 5 from both sides to get 3x = 15, then dividing by 3 to find x = 5. Learners are guided to verify solutions by substitution. They also solve linear inequalities like 2y - 4 > 8, representing solutions on a number line to show all possible values of y that make the statement true.

## Ratio, Proportion, and Rates
The learner is guided to express relationships as ratios and simplify them to their lowest terms. They are taught to apply proportions in solving problems involving direct and inverse variation. For instance, if 5 litres of petrol cost Ksh 500, the learner is guided to calculate the cost of 12 litres using the proportion (5/500) = (12/x). Learners also calculate rates, such as finding the average speed of a boda boda travelling 60 km in 1.5 hours. Real-life examples like scaling a recipe for ugali for a larger family are used to solidify understanding.

## Surface Area and Volume
The learner is guided to calculate the surface area and volume of common prisms and cylinders. They are taught to apply formulas for the area of rectangles and circles to find the lateral and total surface area. For example, to find the volume of a cylindrical water tank with a radius of 2m and height of 5m, the learner uses the formula V = πr²h. Learners solve problems, such as calculating the amount of paint needed to cover a tank or the volume of concrete required to build a foundation for a structure in Nairobi.

## Probability and Statistics
The learner is guided to distinguish between theoretical and experimental probability. They are taught to calculate the probability of single events, such as picking a yellow maize seed from a bag containing 10 yellow and 20 white seeds, which is 10/30 or 1/3. Learners organize data into frequency tables and construct bar charts and histograms. For instance, they can collect data on the number of visitors to Nairobi National Park over a week and present it graphically to identify patterns and make simple predictions.

## Financial Mathematics
The learner is guided to perform calculations involving percentages, simple interest, and hire purchase. They are taught to calculate profit, loss, and discount on goods. For example, finding the discount on a school bag marked at Ksh 1200 with a 15% discount means calculating 15% of 1200, which is Ksh 180, making the final price Ksh 1020. Learners also solve problems on simple interest, such as determining the total amount after one year on a savings account depositing Ksh 50,000 at an annual interest rate of 5%.

## Geometric Constructions and Symmetry
The learner is guided to use geometric instruments to accurately construct angles, triangles, and bisectors. They are taught to identify and draw lines of symmetry in 2D shapes, such as an isosceles triangle having one line of symmetry. Using a ruler and compass, learners construct perpendicular bisectors of line segments and angle bisectors. This skill is applied in real-world contexts, such as determining the centre of a circular playing field or designing symmetrical patterns for Kenyan cultural fabrics.
