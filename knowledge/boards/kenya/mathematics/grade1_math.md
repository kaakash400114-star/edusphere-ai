# Kenyan CBC Grade 1 Mathematics
## Numbers 1 to 30
The learner is guided to count, read, and write numerals from 1 to 30 using objects and pictures. They are guided to count in ones and in tens to 30. Learners are guided to identify and name number symbols and number names. For example, they can count 20 ten-shilling coins or count 10 children playing. They are guided to match number symbols with their corresponding number names, such as matching 25 with 'twenty-five'.

## Addition and Subtraction within 10
The learner is guided to add and subtract numbers up to 10 using real-life objects and fingers. They are guided to use the language 'add', 'plus', 'more than', 'subtract', 'take away', and 'less than'. For example, a learner can solve: 'If Mama has 5 chapatis and adds 3 more, how many chapatis are there?' or 'If you have 10 sweets and give away 3, how many do you have left?' They are guided to record these sums using drawings and symbols like + and -.

## Measurement
The learner is guided to compare objects using descriptive words like long/short, heavy/light, and full/empty. They are guided to measure length using non-standard units such as hand spans. For example, a learner is guided to measure the length of a classroom using their hand spans. They are guided to measure the capacity of containers like a jiko or a sufuria using cups. Learners are guided to identify and use the Kenyan shilling (KSh) to identify and compare coins and notes, like KSh 10 and KSh 20.

## Shapes, Space, and Position
The learner is guided to identify and name common shapes, such as circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles. They are guided to identify these shapes in their environment, like a round mandazi, a square ngano (slate), or a triangular mountain. They are guided to use words describing position, such as 'on top of', 'next to', 'under', 'inside', and 'outside'. For example, a learner can describe the position of a boda boda 'next to' a shop or a bird 'on top of' a tree.

## Data and Graphs
The learner is guided to collect, sort, and organize data using tally marks and real objects. They are guided to sort objects based on one attribute, such as colour or size. For example, learners are guided to sort a collection of fruits by type (mangoes, oranges, and passion fruits) or sort toy animals by habitat (those found at Nairobi National Park and those found on a farm). They are guided to present their sorted data in simple picture graphs.

## Time and Money
The learner is guided to sequence events in their daily life using words like 'before', 'after', and 'next'. They are guided to tell time to the hour using analogue clocks. They are guided to identify the days of the week and the months of the year in order. They are guided to identify and use the Kenyan currency (shillings and cents) in simple buying and selling activities. For example, a learner is guided to pay KSh 50 for an item that costs KSh 30 and get KSh 20 in change.
