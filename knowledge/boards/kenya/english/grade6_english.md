# Kenyan CBC Grade 6 English
## Grammar: Subject-Verb Agreement and Tenses
The learner is guided to understand and apply the correct matching between subjects and their verbs in number and person. They learn that singular subjects take singular verbs (e.g., *The pupil reads a book*) while plural subjects take plural verbs (e.g., *Pupils read books*). They are guided to identify subjects that may appear after the verb and use the correct tense for past, present, and future actions. For example, *The teacher taught yesterday* (past) or *The boda boda driver is delivering the package* (present continuous). They learn to construct sentences about events at the Kenya National Theatre or a visit to the Nairobi National Park using the correct tense to show when the action happens.

## Composition: Writing Descriptive Paragraphs
The learner is guided to write clear, descriptive paragraphs that create vivid pictures for the reader. They are guided to use strong adjectives and sensory details to describe a place, an object, or an experience. For example, they can describe the delicious aroma of fresh *ugali* and sukuma wiki, the colourful patterns of a *kanga*, or the bustling energy of a local market in Nairobi. They learn to structure their paragraph with a main idea sentence followed by supporting sentences that provide specific details. The learner is guided to ensure their sentences are grammatically correct and connected using appropriate joining words.

## Literature: Appreciation of Folktales and Proverbs
The learner is guided to listen to, read, and appreciate Kenyan folktales and proverbs. They are guided to identify the main characters, setting, and moral lesson in stories like *The Hare and the Lion*. They learn that proverbs are short, wise sayings that offer advice or teach a truth, for example, *Mwenda pole, hapatuki* (He who walks slowly, does not stumble). The learner is guided to discuss how the values in these stories, such as patience or cleverness, relate to their own lives and modern society in Kenya. They are guided to connect the moral lessons to making good decisions with their family or friends.

## Oral Skills: Formal Presentations and Debates
The learner is guided to plan and deliver short, formal presentations on familiar topics such as *My Favourite Kenyan Dish* or *Why Protecting Our Wildlife is Important*. They are guided to speak clearly, audibly, and with confidence while making eye contact with the audience. They learn to structure their presentation with an introduction, main points, and a conclusion. The learner is also guided to participate in simple classroom debates, taking turns to express their opinion politely on topics like whether boda bodas should be allowed in city centres. They are guided to listen carefully to the other speakers and respond respectfully.

## Reading Comprehension: Identifying Main Ideas and Supporting Details
The learner is guided to read informational texts and stories to find the main idea, which is the most important message. They are guided to distinguish the main idea from the smaller, supporting details that explain it. For example, when reading a short passage about visiting Nairobi National Park, the learner is guided to identify that the main idea might be 'The park is a great place to see animals', while the details are seeing lions, zebras, and giraffes. They are guided to use strategies like skimming and scanning to locate specific information and answer questions about the text.

## Vocabulary: Word Families and Context Clues
The learner is guided to expand their vocabulary by understanding word families. They are guided to identify a base word and add prefixes or suffixes to create new words with related meanings, such as adding *-ed* to *walk* to get *walked*, or adding *un-* to *happy* to get *unhappy*. They are also guided to use context clues—the words and sentences around an unknown word—to figure out its meaning. For example, in the sentence, 'A kilogram of tomatoes costs one hundred Kenyan shillings at the market', the learner is guided to understand that *Kenyan shillings* is the unit of money used in Kenya.
