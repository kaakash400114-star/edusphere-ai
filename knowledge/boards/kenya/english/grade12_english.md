# Kenyan CBC Grade 12 English
## Literary Appreciation of Prose
The learner is guided to analyse character development in Kenyan novels. They use textual evidence to trace how a protagonist, like Wangari from a story set near Nairobi National Park, evolves due to conflict. Learners distinguish between static and dynamic characters, explaining their motivations using direct quotes. They also analyse the reliability of a narrator in a first-person narrative, identifying bias and perspective. This involves evaluating how the narrator's background influences their account of events, such as a local dispute over land ownership.
## Narrative Writing Techniques
The learner is guided to craft complex narratives using advanced techniques. They create a multi-plot story, weaving together a main conflict with a subplot. For example, a character's struggle to save money for university (main plot) can be intertwined with helping a neighbour solve a family issue (subplot). Learners employ varied sentence structures and descriptive language to build vivid settings, like the bustling atmosphere of a Kisumu fish market or a quiet evening in a rural village.
## Functional and Transactional Writing
The learner is guided to produce polished documents for professional contexts. They learn to draft formal business letters requesting sponsorship for a school project, ensuring clarity, conciseness, and a professional tone. The learner is also guided to write persuasive proposals that clearly state an objective, outline the necessary budget in Kenyan Shillings, and detail the expected benefits. For instance, a proposal for a community library would address potential stakeholders, like a local business association or the Constituency Development Fund.
## Grammatical Accuracy and Stylistic Devices
The learner is guided to master complex grammatical structures. They study advanced sentence patterns, including conditional clauses (e.g., 'Had the community invested in better roads, the boda boda business would not face so many accidents') and various forms of inversion for emphasis. The learner is guided to identify and use stylistic devices like litotes (understatement, 'it was no small matter to raise fifty thousand shillings') and zeugma ('She opened her book and her heart to the new student'), analysing their effects on the reader.
## Media and Information Literacy
The learner is guided to critically deconstruct media messages. They analyse advertisements, evaluating how language and imagery are used to persuade consumers. For example, they can deconstruct an ad for a popular brand of ugali, identifying persuasive words and visuals that connect the product to tradition and family. Learners also learn to distinguish between factual reporting and opinion in news articles, checking for bias in the selection of sources and language used, especially on local online platforms.
## Oral Communication and Argumentation
The learner is guided to construct and deliver persuasive arguments. They research a topic, such as the benefits of banning single-use plastic bags, and formulate a clear thesis statement. The learner is guided to use reputable sources for evidence, including statistics from the National Environment Management Authority (NEMA). They learn to anticipate counter-arguments and prepare rebuttals, delivering their speech with confidence, clear articulation, and appropriate body language to a formal audience.
