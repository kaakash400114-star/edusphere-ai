# Kenyan CBC Grade 10 English


## Literary Appreciation of Prose
Learners will analyse characterisation by identifying static and dynamic characters, using textual evidence such as dialogue and actions to justify their classifications. They will explore character motivation by examining the internal and external factors driving a character's decisions within the text. The development of central themes, including conflict and resolution, will be traced by evaluating how character actions advance the plot. This analysis must be situated within a Kenyan societal context to demonstrate how themes like cultural values and social change are reflected. Learners will also evaluate the significance of setting and point of view in shaping characters and themes.

## Appreciation and Interpretation of Poetry
Learners will systematically identify and classify poetic devices such as alliteration in "The Snake" by Ogden Nash, simile in "If" by Rudyard Kipling, and metaphors in classical sonnets. They will analyse structure by differentiating stanzas and calculating rhyme schemes (e.g., ABAB) to understand the poet's blueprint. Through close reading, they will infer the speaker's tone from diction and imagery, then determine the poem's overall mood by examining collective sensory details. Finally, they will establish thematic connections by extracting the poet's message and linking it to specific Kenyan social contexts, such as the challenges faced in agricultural communities or the spirit of national unity.

## Reading Skills for Advanced Comprehension
Learners apply reading strategies to process complex texts by identifying the central argument, a core concept like conservation in an article on deforestation, and distinguishing it from supporting evidence such as statistical data. They use contextual clues to infer unstated meanings, determining the author's tone and potential bias through word choice. To evaluate credibility, students assess an author's qualifications, an organisation's reputation, and the date of publication before accepting a claim. By synthesising information from disparate paragraphs, they form a coherent understanding, connecting historical events in a Kenyan history passage to their broader societal impact.

## Writing Skills: Formal and Functional Communication
Learners master formal communication by composing well-structured letters of complaint and inquiry using standard formats for salutations, body paragraphs, and closings. For functional writing, they practice drafting clear and concise emails, reports, and notices, ensuring the tone and style are appropriate for the audience and purpose. In creative writing, they develop narratives by applying techniques for plot construction, character development, and descriptive setting, often using local contexts such as a bustling Nairobi market or a journey through Nairobi National Park for inspiration. The acquisition of these skills is essential for effective communication in diverse real-life and academic scenarios.

## Communication Skills and Oral Literature
Learners will analyse and apply the structural components of a persuasive speech, ensuring a strong, context-specific hook followed by logical arguments supported by relevant evidence. They will consciously integrate non-verbal communication techniques, such as purposeful gestures and vocal variation, to enhance persuasive impact and audience engagement. The study of Kenyan oral narratives will focus on identifying and analysing stylistic devices like personification, repetition, and proverbs to understand their role in conveying moral lessons and cultural values. Learners will practice reciting selected excerpts, paying close attention to clarity, pacing, and intonation to convey the narrative's emotional and cultural significance effectively.

## Grammar, Usage, and Mechanics
The learner is guided to master grammatical structures for effective expression. They learn to use complex sentences correctly, including subordinate clauses, and apply punctuation like semicolons and colons. Subject-verb agreement in complex sentences is reinforced. Vocabulary expansion focuses on precise word choice using synonyms and antonyms, including Kenyan-specific terms like 'boda boda' or 'matatu', and learners learn to use the comma correctly to separate introductory phrases and items in a series.
