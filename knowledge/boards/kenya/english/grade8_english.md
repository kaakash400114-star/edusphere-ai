# Kenyan CBC Grade 8 English
## Comprehension of Diverse Texts
The learner is guided to identify main ideas and supporting details in a variety of texts, including expository articles on Kenyan wildlife and narratives set in rural Kenya. They learn to distinguish between fact and opinion using texts like a news report on the Nairobi Securities Exchange or a cultural description of a traditional wedding. Learners practice summarizing information, such as the steps to prepare ugali, by extracting key points from a given passage. They also analyze the author's purpose and perspective in texts about issues like conservation or national heritage.

## Writing Expository and Argumentative Texts
The learner is guided to structure well-organized expository texts with clear introductions, body paragraphs, and conclusions, explaining processes like the water cycle or the history of the Kenya National Archives. They are guided to write argumentative essays on topics relevant to Kenya, such as the benefits of using digital payments over cash for paying school fees. They learn to develop a thesis statement and provide logical reasoning and evidence, like statistics on traffic congestion in Nairobi, to support their claims while maintaining a formal tone.

## Grammar and Language Structures
The learner is guided to identify and use complex sentence structures, including conditional clauses (e.g., 'If the government invests in more buses, fewer people will use boda bodas'). They study and apply the rules of subject-verb agreement with collective nouns like 'government' and 'team'. Learners are guided to use active and passive voice appropriately in different contexts and to correctly punctuate direct speech, particularly in narratives that use Kenyan shillings as dialogue examples.

## Creative and Imaginative Writing
The learner is guided to write imaginative short stories set in familiar Kenyan contexts, such as a market in Kisumu or a trip to the Nairobi National Park. They learn to develop characters, settings, and plotlines that are culturally resonant. The learner is guided to use vivid descriptive language to create atmosphere, for example, describing the sights and sounds of a bustling city scene or the tranquility of a lakeside village. They practice crafting dialogue that reflects local expressions and idioms.

## Listening and Speaking Skills
The learner is guided to listen actively for specific information in presentations, debates, and audio texts on topics like Kenyan geography and entrepreneurship. They learn to summarize the main points of a spoken argument, for instance, one discussing the pros and cons of a proposed new railway. The learner is guided to deliver formal oral presentations on researched topics, such as the importance of a specific national park, using appropriate volume, clarity, and eye contact.

## Media and Information Literacy
The learner is guided to critically analyze information from various media sources, including Kenyan news websites and social media platforms. They learn to identify potential biases and differentiate between credible and unreliable sources when researching topics like national elections or public health campaigns. The learner is guided to interpret simple data presented in charts and graphs, such as those showing tourist arrivals to Kenya, and draw logical conclusions based on the visual information.
