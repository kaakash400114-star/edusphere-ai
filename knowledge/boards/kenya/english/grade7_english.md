# Kenyan CBC Grade 7 English

## Narrative Writing Techniques
The learner is guided to craft compelling narratives by applying the plot structure of exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, and resolution. They master character development through dialogue and actions that reveal personality, such as a character saving money for a new boda boda. Learners are shown how to build a rich Kenyan setting, describing scenes like the bustling city of Nairobi or the quiet plains near Nairobi National Park. They will use vivid, sensory language to immerse the reader, describing the smell of ugali and the sound of a matatu hooting.

## Grammatical Structures: The Perfect Tenses
The learner is guided to understand and correctly use the present perfect, past perfect, and future perfect tenses. They are shown that the present perfect (e.g., "I have visited the coast") connects past actions to the present. The past perfect (e.g., "The boda boda rider had already left before we arrived") describes an action completed before another past event. The future perfect (e.g., "They will have saved 10,000 shillings by December") indicates an action that will be completed at a specific future time. Learners practice forming these tenses using regular and irregular verbs.

## Comprehension of Factual Texts
The learner is guided to read and understand informational texts, such as articles on Kenyan agriculture or profiles of national heroes. They identify the main idea and supporting details, distinguishing between primary information and examples. Learners are taught to distinguish between fact (e.g., "The savanna biome covers much of Kenya") and opinion (e.g., "Ugali is the best meal"). They practice summarizing multi-paragraph texts, extracting the essential information to create a concise summary of a passage on the history of the Kenya shilling.

## Descriptive Writing with Figurative Language
The learner is guided to enhance their descriptive writing by using similes, metaphors, and personification. A simile makes a comparison using 'like' or 'as', for example, "The clouds were as fluffy as cotton wool." A metaphor makes a direct comparison, such as "Mzee Ojwang' was a well of stories." Personification gives human qualities to non-human things, like "The morning sun smiled over the hills." Learners apply these techniques to describe Kenyan landscapes, wildlife, and cultural events, creating vivid imagery for the reader.

## Structuring Formal Letters and Emails
The learner is guided to write formal letters and emails with correct structure and appropriate language. They learn to include a clear subject line, a formal salutation (e.g., "Dear Sir/Madam"), a well-organized body with separate paragraphs for each point, and a polite closing (e.g., "Yours faithfully"). They practice writing formal requests, such as a letter to a school principal asking for permission to visit a national museum or an email inquiring about course fees at a local college, using professional and respectful language throughout.

## Research Skills and Citing Sources
The learner is guided to conduct short research projects on a topic of interest, such as traditional Kenyan sports or prominent scientists. They are shown how to use dictionaries and encyclopedias to find relevant information and to identify the most important facts. Learners are taught the importance of integrity by learning to cite their sources to avoid plagiarism. They practice creating a simple bibliography, listing books and websites consulted, ensuring they give credit to the original authors of the information used in their report on the history of the Kenyan flag.
