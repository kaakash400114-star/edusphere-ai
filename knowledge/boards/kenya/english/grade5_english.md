# Kenyan CBC Grade 5 English

## Building Vocabulary with Context
The learner is guided to expand their vocabulary by using context clues to understand the meaning of new words while reading. When encountering a word like 'safari', they look at the surrounding sentences and pictures to infer its meaning. They also learn to group words into categories, for example, words related to transport like 'boda boda', 'matatu', and 'car'. By identifying prefixes and suffixes in words such as 'unhappy' or 'quickly', they can break down unfamiliar terms and build a richer, more descriptive vocabulary for both spoken and written English.

## Understanding Sentence Structures
The learner is guided to identify and construct different types of sentences, including simple, compound, and complex sentences. They learn that a simple sentence has one subject and one predicate, for example, 'The lion sleeps'. They are taught to join two simple sentences with a conjunction like 'and' to form a compound sentence, such as 'The lion sleeps and the zebra grazes'. This skill helps them build longer, more detailed sentences to express their ideas clearly and effectively in their writing.

## Reading Comprehension Strategies
The learner is guided to use specific strategies to understand what they read. They are taught to ask and answer questions about a text before, during, and after reading. For example, while reading a story set at the Nairobi National Park, they might ask, 'What animals are in the park?' They also learn to identify the main idea and supporting details in a paragraph. Finally, they practice summarizing a short passage in their own words to demonstrate their understanding of key events and information.

## Writing Descriptive Paragraphs
The learner is guided to write descriptive paragraphs using sensory details. They are taught to organize their writing with a topic sentence, supporting sentences, and a concluding sentence. For example, a paragraph about 'ugali' could start, 'Ugali is a staple food in Kenya,' followed by sentences describing its taste, texture, and how it is eaten with sukuma wiki. They learn to use vivid adjectives like 'soft', 'warm', and 'delicious' to make their writing more engaging for the reader.

## Listening and Speaking Skills
The learner is guided to practice active listening and clear speaking. They are taught to listen carefully to a story or a set of instructions, such as how to buy vegetables at the market using Kenyan Shillings, and be able to retell the information accurately. In group discussions, they learn to take turns speaking, share their opinions respectfully, and ask questions to clarify points. For example, they might discuss their favourite animal at the park, making sure everyone has a chance to speak.

## Understanding Punctuation and Capitalization
The learner is guided to use correct punctuation and capitalization in their writing. They are taught to end declarative sentences with a period (.), interrogative sentences with a question mark (?), and exclamatory sentences with an exclamation mark (!). They also learn to capitalize the first word of a sentence, the pronoun 'I', and proper nouns such as specific places like 'Kenya' or 'Mount Kenya' and people's names. This ensures their writing is clear, organized, and follows standard English rules.
