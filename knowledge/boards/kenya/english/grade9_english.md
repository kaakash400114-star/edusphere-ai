# Kenyan CBC Grade 9 English

## Narrative Writing Structures
The learner is guided to plan and write engaging narratives using well-structured plots. They learn to use the five stages of plot development: exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, and resolution. Characterisation techniques are explored to create believable characters using dialogue and description. The learner is guided to write a story with a Kenyan setting, such as a day at Nairobi National Park, and to include sensory details. They are also guided to maintain a consistent point of view and develop a clear theme.

## Shakespearean Language Devices
The learner is guided to identify and analyse key language devices used in Shakespearean plays. They are introduced to devices like soliloquy, aside, iambic pentameter, and dramatic irony. The learner is guided to examine how these devices contribute to character development and plot advancement. They are guided to read selected excerpts and explain the effect of a specific device. For example, they can explain how an aside in *Macbeth* reveals a character's true thoughts.

## Functional Grammar for Formal Communication
Learners master functional grammar by mastering subordinating conjunctions like 'although' and 'because' to form complex sentences, such as "Although the service was delayed, the manager apologised." They must apply strict subject-verb agreement rules, ensuring a collective noun like 'the committee' takes a singular verb: 'The committee has made its decision.' Formal communication requires a formal register, eliminating slang and contractions by writing "we will not" instead of "we won't." Students are taught to write a formal letter of complaint, correctly structuring it with a clear recipient address, formal salutations like "Dear Sir/Madam," a detailed body, and a proper closing such as "Yours faithfully." The emphasis is on applying these grammatical conventions consistently to achieve clear, professional, and effective written communication.

## Oral Presentation Techniques
The learner is guided to prepare and deliver effective oral presentations. They are guided to structure a presentation with a clear introduction, body, and conclusion. The learner is guided to use visual aids like posters or slides to enhance understanding. Non-verbal communication skills, such as eye contact and appropriate gestures, are emphasised. The learner is guided to present on a Kenyan topic like the importance of 'ugali' in our culture, ensuring a confident and clear delivery.

## Poetry: Form and Theme
The learner is guided to analyse poetry by examining its form and theme. They are guided to identify different poetic forms like the sonnet and ballad and their structural features. The learner is guided to unpack the central message, or theme, of a poem. They are guided to read poems about Kenyan life and identify universal themes such as resilience or identity. The learner is guided to explain how poetic devices like metaphor and imagery contribute to the overall impact of the poem.

## Argumentative Writing Frameworks
The learner is guided to construct well-reasoned arguments using a clear framework. They are guided to formulate a strong, debatable thesis statement. The learner is guided to support this claim with logical reasons and relevant evidence. They are guided to anticipate and counter opposing viewpoints. For example, the learner is guided to write an essay arguing the benefits and drawbacks of using 'boda bodas' in urban areas like Nairobi, providing evidence in the form of examples.
