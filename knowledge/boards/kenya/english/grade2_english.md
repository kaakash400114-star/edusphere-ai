# Kenyan CBC Grade 2 English

## Reading and Comprehension of Simple Texts
The learner is guided to read aloud simple sentences and short texts using the basic skills of phonics. They learn to blend letter sounds to form words like *cat*, *mat*, and *sun*, and to identify common sight words. The learner is guided to use knowledge of letters and sounds to read and understand level-appropriate texts. They read simple stories and answer basic questions about the main character or an event. For example, they can read "Mama bought ugali" and tell you what Mama bought.

## Writing and Spelling of Common Words
The learner is guided to write letters of the alphabet in both uppercase and lowercase forms, ensuring correct formation. They are guided to write their own name and other simple, high-frequency words correctly. Using phonics skills, the learner is guided to listen to the sounds in words and write them down, like *pen*, *dog*, and *kitten*. For example, they can write the sentence "The dog is big" by sounding out each word and matching it to the correct letters.

## Listening and Speaking Skills
Learners are taught to listen actively for specific details and the main idea from short stories, poems, and oral instructions. They practise identifying key information, such as the sequence of events in a story or the steps in a two-part command. To demonstrate comprehension, they follow multi-step instructions, for example, "Stand up, push in your chair, and line up at the door." For speaking, the focus is on clear articulation of personal ideas in complete sentences using a subject-verb-object structure. They also participate in simple conversations by asking and answering questions on familiar topics, such as "What is your favourite food?" and "Why do you like it?

## Vocabulary Development
The learner builds vocabulary by identifying and naming concrete nouns, specifically common objects found at home and school such as cup, fork, and desk. The curriculum guides the learner to understand and apply descriptive adjectives to provide more detail about these objects and people, for example describing a fork as long or a person as tall. Learners are also introduced to simple action words like eat, run, and write to complete basic sentences. A key focus is understanding common denominations of Kenyan currency, such as identifying and naming a one-shilling coin and a fifty-shilling note. This practical vocabulary is applied in structured activities where learners can say, "The fifty-shilling note is blue" or "The pencil is yellow.

## Basic Grammar and Sentence Structure
Learners are taught to construct simple sentences that express a single, complete idea. A fundamental rule is that every new sentence must begin with a capital letter and end with a full stop. They are introduced to and must correctly use basic prepositions of place, such as 'on', 'in', 'under', and 'next to', to describe the location of objects. For instance, a learner can identify and express the position of an item by writing a simple sentence like, "The book is on the table." This concept is reinforced by having learners describe their own classroom environment using correct sentence structure and positional language.

## Appreciation of Stories and Rhymes
Learners are taught to actively engage with oral narratives and rhymes by identifying key elements such as the main characters, the story setting, and the sequence of simple events. They develop the ability to distinguish between characters and the environment in which the story takes place. For appreciation, learners are guided to express understanding by retelling the main events using their own words and asking relevant questions about the story. This is demonstrated after a story like "The Clever Hare," where a learner can retell the hare's trick and ask, "Why did the hare outsmart the elephant?" By doing so, they confirm comprehension and show thoughtful engagement with the text.
