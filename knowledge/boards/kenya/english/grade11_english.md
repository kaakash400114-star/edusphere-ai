# Kenyan CBC Grade 11 English
## Analyzing Formal Argumentative Structures
The learner is guided to deconstruct formal arguments in texts like editorials or academic papers. They identify the core claim, distinguish supporting evidence from opinions, and evaluate the logical validity of the reasoning. Students examine the use of rhetorical devices such as ethos, pathos, and logos to persuade an audience. They apply this analysis to a Kenyan news article on national policy, tracing the author's argument and assessing the strength of their evidence, which includes statistical data and expert testimony.
## Crafting Persuasive Discourse
The learner is guided to write a structured persuasive essay on a contemporary Kenyan issue. They formulate a clear, debatable thesis statement and develop a logical argument with well-chosen points, relevant examples, and compelling evidence. They learn to employ transitional words to ensure coherence and strengthen their position by anticipating and addressing counter-arguments using formal register and objective language, making the case for a position on issues like boda boda regulations in Nairobi.
## Interpreting Literary Elements in Drama
The learner is guided to analyze how playwrights use dramatic elements like character, dialogue, and setting to convey themes. They explore a specific text to identify subtext, symbolism, and dramatic irony, explaining how these devices enhance meaning and provoke thought. Students examine the setting of a Kenyan play, analyzing how the physical environment and social context shape the characters' motivations and the central conflict, revealing underlying messages about culture and society.
## Integrating Academic and Formal Language
The learner is guided to apply academic vocabulary and formal grammar in expository writing. They learn to avoid slang and contractions, and to construct complex sentences using subordinate clauses for precision. This skill is practiced in writing reports or proposals that require a formal tone. Students are guided to express a complex idea, such as the economic impact of tourism on Nairobi National Park, using precise, unambiguous language suitable for an academic or professional audience.
## Evaluating Media Texts and Bias
The learner is guided to critically analyze diverse media formats, including news articles, advertisements, and documentaries. They identify the creator's purpose and target audience, and examine the use of language, imagery, and selective information to convey a specific message. Students are guided to compare how two different local news outlets report on the same event, such as a policy on shilling exchange rates, to detect potential bias and evaluate the reliability of their sources.
## Creating Cohesive Narrative Descriptions
The learner is guided to write vivid and evocative descriptions that create a strong sense of place and atmosphere. They use precise sensory details (sight, sound, smell, touch, taste) and figurative language like simile and metaphor. This skill is applied by composing a detailed description of a familiar Kenyan setting, such as a busy market in Mombasa or a rural landscape at dusk, guiding the reader to experience the scene by focusing on specific details like the scent of spices or the sound of a distant boda boda.
