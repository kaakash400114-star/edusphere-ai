# Kenyan CBC Grade 10 Science
## Acids, Bases, and Salts
The learner is guided to understand that acids are proton donors with a pH below 7, while bases are proton acceptors with a pH above 7. They are taught to use indicators like litmus paper to identify them. Common acids include hydrochloric acid (stomach acid) and sulphuric acid (car batteries), while bases include sodium hydroxide (lye soap). Learners prepare a salt, such as sodium chloride, by neutralizing hydrochloric acid with sodium hydroxide. They learn that neutralization reactions produce water and a salt, a key principle in titration and industrial processes like manufacturing baking soda for Kenyan cuisine.
## Motion and Newton's Laws
The learner is guided to analyse motion by defining velocity and acceleration using the formula v = d/t and a = Δv/Δt. They apply Newton’s three laws: an object remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by a force (like a boda boda stopping suddenly); force equals mass times acceleration (F=ma); and for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction (a rocket launching). Using examples like the launch of a Kenyan satellite or a matatu accelerating from a stop, learners calculate force and predict motion outcomes in real-world contexts.
## Electric Circuits and Domestic Wiring
The learner is guided to identify circuit components like cells, resistors, and switches. They construct series and parallel circuits and calculate total resistance, current, and voltage using Ohm’s Law (V = IR). Learners connect this to domestic wiring, understanding that parallel circuits allow independent operation of devices like bulbs and radios in a Kenyan home. They are guided to safety practices, such as using the correct amperage fuses for a water heater and avoiding overloaded sockets that could cause fires, crucial knowledge for any homeowner in Nairobi.
## Energy Forms and Conservation
The learner is guided to define energy as the capacity to do work and to identify its forms, including kinetic, potential, chemical, and thermal. They use the formula for gravitational potential energy (PE = mgh) to calculate the energy stored at the top of the Ngong Hills. The law of conservation of energy is taught: energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed. They trace how a boda boda's chemical energy (petrol) becomes kinetic energy to move, with some lost as heat and sound. This principle is essential for understanding Kenya's geothermal and hydroelectric power generation.
## Genetic Inheritance and Variation
The learner is guided to define genetics as the study of heredity and distinguish between genotype and phenotype. They use Punnett squares to predict the probability of traits in offspring, such as seed colour in maize, a key Kenyan crop. Learners learn that Gregor Mendel’s laws of segregation and independent assortment explain how traits are passed down. They explore variation, distinguishing between continuous (e.g., human height) and discontinuous (e.g., blood group) variation, and understand how natural selection and selective breeding influence species, like the development of drought-resistant wheat varieties.
## Climate and Weather Patterns
The learner is guided to differentiate between weather (short-term atmospheric conditions) and climate (long-term patterns). They analyse the influence of Kenya’s location near the Equator on its generally warm climate. Learners examine the roles of the Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ) and the Indian Ocean monsoon in creating the two main rainy seasons (long and short rains), which are vital for agriculture and growing crops like maize and tea for ugali. They interpret data to link weather events to climate change impacts, such as altered rainfall patterns affecting farming communities in rural Kenya.
