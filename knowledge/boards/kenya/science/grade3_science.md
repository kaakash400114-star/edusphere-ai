# Kenyan CBC Grade 3 Science

## The Structure of Plants
The learner is guided to identify and name the basic parts of a common plant, such as a bean seedling or a maize stalk. They learn that a plant has roots, a stem, leaves, and sometimes flowers or fruits. Roots anchor the plant in the soil and absorb water. The stem supports the plant and transports water and food. Leaves make food for the plant using sunlight, water, and air from the atmosphere. For example, a sukuma wiki plant has all these parts working together to help it grow in a Kenyan garden.

## Water Cycle and Weather
The learner is guided to understand the simple water cycle. They learn that the sun heats water in lakes like Naivasha, turning it into an invisible gas called water vapour. This vapour rises, cools, and forms clouds. When the clouds become heavy, the water falls back to the earth as rain. This rain can fill rivers, be used to grow crops like maize, and provide the water we drink. The weather can change, bringing sunny days or heavy rains in different parts of Kenya.

## Caring for Our Environment
The learner is guided to identify ways to keep the environment clean and healthy. They learn that throwing rubbish like plastic bottles and sweet wrappers on the ground makes our surroundings dirty and can harm animals. We should use dustbins and encourage others to do the same. They learn that planting trees, such as jacaranda or acacia, is important because they provide fresh air, prevent soil erosion, and give us shade, making our schools and homes pleasant places to be.

## Force and Movement
The learner is guided to understand that a push or a pull is a force that can make objects start moving, stop moving, or change direction. They learn to identify examples of force in their daily lives. Pushing a heavy box to clean the floor or pulling a chair to sit down uses force. A boda boda rider uses force to pedal and move the motorcycle. When kicking a football to score a goal, you are using a force to make the ball fly towards the net.

## Living and Non-Living Things
The learner is guided to group things into living and non-living. Living things, like the animals at Nairobi National Park or a cat in the neighbourhood, grow, eat, move, and reproduce. Non-living things, like a classroom desk, a textbook, or a rock, do not grow, eat, or reproduce. They learn that a bicycle is non-living because it cannot grow on its own, even though a person uses force to make it move. The learner is guided to use these characteristics to tell the difference.

## Measurement of Mass
The learner is guided to understand that mass is the amount of matter in an object. They learn to use a simple balance to compare the mass of two objects. They learn that a large object like a pineapple is not always heavier than a small one like a mango. They learn that one Kenyan shilling coin has a certain mass and can be used as a reference point. They can use a balance to see that ten 1-shilling coins have more mass than one 50-shilling coin.
