# Kenyan CBC Grade 2 Science
## The Senses and Their Uses
The learner is guided to identify the five main senses: sight, hearing, smell, taste, and touch. They learn that each sense has a specific body part. The eyes see, the ears hear, the nose smells, the tongue tastes, and the skin feels. Learners use their senses to describe objects around them. For example, they can see the colour of a boda boda (red), hear the sound of a matatu (hooting), smell the aroma of cooking ugali, taste sukuma wiki (bitter or sweet), and feel the rough texture of a mango's skin.

## Living and Non-Living Things
The learner is guided to classify objects as either living or non-living. Living things grow, eat, breathe, reproduce, and move. Non-living things do not do these things. Examples of living things include animals at Nairobi National Park like zebras and lions, and plants like trees. Examples of non-living things include tables, chairs, rocks, and coins. Learners can explain that a shilling coin is non-living because it cannot grow or make more of itself, but a goat living on a farm is a living thing because it can eat, grow, and have babies.

## Changes in Weather and Seasons
The learner is guided to observe and describe changes in weather. They learn about different types of weather, such as sunny, rainy, cloudy, and windy. They can link weather to seasons like the long rains and short rains in Kenya. Learners can dress appropriately for the weather. For example, on a sunny day, they wear a hat and sunglasses to protect from the sun. On a rainy day, they wear a raincoat and gumboots to stay dry. They can describe that during the rainy season, rivers fill with water and plants grow well.

## Materials Around Us
The learner is guided to name and describe common materials. They learn about materials like wood, metal, plastic, glass, and cloth. They can identify objects made from these materials found in Kenya. For example, a chair can be made of wood, a sufuria (pot) is made of metal, a jerrycan is plastic, a window can be made of glass, and a kikoi (traditional wrap) is made of cloth. Learners can group objects based on the material they are made from.

## Day and Night
The learner is guided to understand the difference between day and night. They learn that the sun rises in the morning, bringing daylight, and sets in the evening, bringing night. During the day, we are awake and can see because of the sun's light. At night, it is dark and we sleep. Learners can name activities done during the day, like going to school, and activities done at night, like sleeping. They observe that when it is day in Kenya, it is night in other countries far away.

## Caring for Our Environment
The learner is guided to identify ways to keep the environment clean and healthy. They learn that a clean environment helps prevent diseases. They can practice good habits such as using a dustbin instead of littering. Learners understand that dirty water can make people sick. They can describe ways to save water, like turning off the tap after washing hands. They learn that plants and animals need a clean place to live, just like people.
