# Kenyan CBC Grade 9 Science

## Energy Transformation and Conservation
The learner is guided to understand that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another. The law of conservation of energy states that the total energy in an isolated system remains constant. In a hydroelectric power dam, the potential energy of water held at a high altitude is transformed into kinetic energy as it falls, which then spins turbines to generate electrical energy. A boda boda converts chemical energy from petrol into kinetic energy for movement, while also producing heat and sound energy, demonstrating multiple simultaneous transformations.

## Electrical Circuits and Ohm's Law
The learner is guided to analyse simple electrical circuits using Ohm’s Law, which states that the current (I) in a circuit is directly proportional to the voltage (V) and inversely proportional to the resistance (R), expressed as I = V/R. In a Kenyan home, a 240-volt mains supply connected to a 60-watt bulb with a resistance of 960 ohms will draw a current of 0.25 amperes. Circuits can be connected in series, where components share the same current, or in parallel, where components share the same voltage, affecting the overall brightness of bulbs.

## Chemical Bonding and Reactions
The learner is guided to differentiate between ionic and covalent bonding. Ionic bonding involves the transfer of electrons from a metal to a non-metal, creating charged ions (e.g., table salt, NaCl, formed from sodium and chlorine). Covalent bonding involves the sharing of electrons between non-metals (e.g., water, H₂O, formed from hydrogen and oxygen). Chemical reactions are written as balanced equations, such as the formation of ugali starch from maize flour (C₆H₁₀O₅)ₙ and water, where the number of atoms of each element is conserved on both sides.

## Human Nutrition and Digestion
The learner is guided to trace the path of food and the chemical processes of digestion. In the mouth, amylase in saliva begins the breakdown of starch in ugali into maltose. In the stomach, pepsin begins protein digestion. In the small intestine, pancreatic amylase continues carbohydrate digestion, while proteases and lipases break down proteins and fats, respectively. Absorption of nutrients like glucose, amino acids, and fatty acids occurs mainly in the ileum through structures called villi, which are adapted for maximum surface area.

## Force, Motion, and Newton's Laws
The learner is guided to apply Newton’s three laws of motion. An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion at a constant speed unless acted upon by an unbalanced force (First Law). The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass (Second Law; F=ma). For example, a 1000 kg safari vehicle accelerating at 2 m/s² requires a net force of 2000 newtons. When a boda boda stops suddenly, passengers continue moving forward (Third Law).

## Biodiversity and Conservation in Kenya
The learner is guided to identify levels of biodiversity and threats to ecosystems in Kenya. Genetic diversity is the variety of genes within a single species, while species diversity is the variety of different species in an area, such as the wide array of animals in Nairobi National Park. Ecosystem diversity includes different habitats like savannas, forests, and wetlands. Major threats include habitat loss from deforestation, poaching for ivory and bushmeat, and pollution, with conservation efforts involving national park management and community-based initiatives to protect natural resources.
