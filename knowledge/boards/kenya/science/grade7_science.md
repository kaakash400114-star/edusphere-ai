# Kenyan CBC Grade 7 Science

## The Solar System
The learner is guided to understand the solar system is the Sun and all objects that orbit it. The Sun is the star at the center, providing light and heat. Planets are large, spherical bodies that orbit the Sun in nearly circular paths. The eight planets, starting from the Sun, are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. Earth is the only known planet with life, containing vast bodies of water like Lake Victoria and an atmosphere that supports organisms. A moon is a natural satellite that orbits a planet. Earth has one moon, while Jupiter and Saturn have many.

## States of Matter and Their Changes
The learner is guided to identify the three common states of matter: solid, liquid, and gas. A solid has a definite shape and volume; a liquid has a definite volume but takes the shape of its container, such as water in a glass; a gas has neither a definite shape nor volume and fills any space. Matter can change states through physical processes: melting (solid to liquid, like ice into water at 0°C), freezing (liquid to solid), evaporation (liquid to gas, like water boiling at 100°C), and condensation (gas to liquid, like steam forming water droplets on a cold bottle).

## Forces and Motion
The learner is guided to understand that a force is a push or a pull on an object that can change its motion. A force can cause an object to start moving, stop moving, or change direction. The unit of force is the newton (N). An unbalanced force causes a change in motion, while balanced forces do not. For example, when you push a heavy box in your house with a force of 50 N and friction pushes back with 50 N, the box does not move. Gravity is a force that pulls objects towards the center of the Earth, making objects fall downwards when dropped.

## Sources of Energy
The learner is guided to identify different sources of energy in Kenya and their uses. The sun is the primary source of light and heat energy. Other sources include wood burned for cooking in many rural homes, geothermal energy used to generate electricity in Olkaria near Naivasha, and hydroelectric power from dams like the one at Turkwell River. Energy can also be stored in batteries used in flashlights and toys. Fossil fuels like coal and petroleum are also sources of energy, but they are not renewable and can cause pollution.

## Weather and Climate
The learner is guided to distinguish between weather and climate. Weather refers to the atmospheric conditions over a short period, such as today's temperature in Nairobi or whether it will rain in Mombasa tomorrow. Climate, on the other hand, describes the average weather patterns in an area over a long period, typically 30 years or more. For instance, the coastal region of Kenya has a hot and humid climate. Learners should understand that weather is measured using instruments like thermometers (for temperature), rain gauges (for rainfall), and barometers (for air pressure).

## Nutrition and Health
The learner is guided to understand that nutrition is the process of obtaining and using food for growth and energy. A balanced diet is essential for good health and includes carbohydrates (like ugali from maize), proteins (such as beans and meat), fats, vitamins, and minerals. Lack of specific nutrients in the diet can lead to deficiency diseases. For example, not enough Vitamin A can cause night blindness, and a lack of iron can lead to anemia. Regular exercise, like playing football, and proper hygiene, such as washing hands before eating, are also important for maintaining good health.
