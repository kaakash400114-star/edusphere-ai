# Kenyan CBC Grade 4 Science
## Living Things and Their Habitats
Living things are plants and animals that grow, eat, and reproduce. Plants make their own food through a process called photosynthesis, using sunlight, water, and air. Animals get their food by eating plants or other animals. All living things need a place to live called a habitat, which provides food, water, and shelter. A habitat can be a forest, grassland, or even a small pond. The Nairobi National Park is a large habitat for many wild animals like lions, zebras, and giraffes, where they find everything they need to survive.
## Our Body and Health
Our body is made up of different parts like the head, arms, legs, and a chest. Each part has a special job to help us live. The legs help us walk and run, while the arms help us carry things. We must keep our bodies healthy by eating balanced meals like ugali, sukuma wiki, and beans, which give us energy. Drinking clean water and washing our hands with soap before eating prevents diseases. Getting enough sleep and playing games also helps our bodies grow strong and stay healthy.
## Materials and Their Uses
Materials are substances that things are made from. We use different materials for different jobs. For example, we make houses from strong materials like bricks and cement because they last long and protect us from rain and sun. We use plastic to make water bottles because it does not rust. We use metal to make cooking pots because it gets hot quickly. In our markets, we buy school materials like books made of paper and pens made of plastic, all chosen for their specific uses to help us learn and work.
## Forces and Their Effects
A force is a push or a pull. When we push a door, it opens; when we pull a chair, it moves closer. Some forces, like the wind blowing a kite, make things move. Other forces, like our hands holding a heavy book, stop things from moving. Gravity is a force that pulls everything down towards the ground, making a ball fall when you drop it. We use force every day, like when we pedal a boda boda to move, or when we push a heavy box across the floor.
## The Earth and the Sky
The Earth is our planet, and it is round. We live on the Earth's surface. The sky is the space we see above the Earth. During the day, we see the Sun, which is a big, hot star that gives us light and heat. At night, we see the Moon and many stars. The Moon is not a star; it is a large rock that reflects the Sun's light. We can also see clouds, which are made of tiny drops of water or ice floating in the sky. The weather changes, sometimes it is sunny, other times it is rainy or cloudy.
## Measurement and Money
We use measurement to find out the size, amount, or length of things. We measure length in metres (m) and centimetres (cm). For example, a teacher's table might be 1 metre long. We measure weight in kilograms (kg). For example, a bag of maize flour might weigh 2 kilograms. We measure volume in litres (L). For example, a jerrycan of water holds 20 litres. We use money called Kenyan Shillings (KSh) to buy things. If you buy a sweet for KSh 10 and a banana for KSh 5, you have spent a total of KSh 15.
