# Kenyan CBC Grade 6 Science

## Properties of Matter
The learner is guided to understand the three states of matter: solid, liquid, and gas. Solids have a fixed shape and volume, like a stone. Liquids have a fixed volume but take the shape of their container, such as water in a glass. Gases have neither a fixed shape nor volume and fill any space, like the air inside a bicycle tyre. The learner is guided to identify changes of state, such as ice melting into water or boiling water turning into steam.

## Introduction to Electricity
The learner is guided to understand basic electric circuits. A complete circuit needs a power source like a battery, connecting wires, and a device that uses electricity, such as a bulb. The learner is guided to create a simple circuit using a 1.5-volt battery, wires, and a small torch bulb. They learn to identify conductors (materials that allow electricity to pass, like metals) and insulators (materials that block electricity, like plastic or dry wood), using a copper wire and a wooden stick as examples.

## Weather and Climate
The learner is guided to differentiate between weather and climate. Weather is the state of the atmosphere at a specific time and place, like sunny or rainy in Nairobi today. Climate is the average weather pattern of a region over many years. The learner is guided to identify instruments used to measure weather, such as a thermometer (measuring temperature in degrees Celsius), a rain gauge (measuring rainfall in millimetres), and an anemometer (measuring wind speed).

## Plant and Animal Adaptations
The learner is guided to identify specific adaptations of living things for survival. A camel has a hump for storing fat to go long periods without water and wide feet to prevent sinking in desert sand. In a Kenyan context, the learner is guided to observe how the acacia tree has long, sharp thorns to protect its leaves from being eaten by herbivores like giraffes in Nairobi National Park.

## Human Body Systems
The learner is guided to identify the main functions of the skeletal and muscular systems. The skeleton provides support and protection for internal organs, like the rib cage protecting the heart and lungs. Muscles are attached to bones and allow movement. The learner is guided to understand that muscles pull on bones to create actions, such as the biceps muscle pulling the lower arm to lift a 1-kilogram packet of flour.

## Simple Machines
The learner is guided to identify and understand the function of simple machines: levers, pulleys, and inclined planes. A lever is a rigid bar that pivots on a fulcrum to lift a load; a seesaw is a common example. A pulley is a wheel with a rope used to lift heavy objects, like raising a flag. An inclined plane is a sloped surface that reduces effort, such as pushing a loaded wheelbarrow up a ramp instead of lifting it.
