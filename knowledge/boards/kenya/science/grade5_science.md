# Kenyan CBC Grade 5 Science

## Matter and Its Properties
The learner is guided to identify the three states of matter: solid, liquid, and gas. A solid has a definite shape and volume, like a wooden desk or a school building. A liquid has a definite volume but takes the shape of its container, like water in a bottle or milk in a glass. A gas has no definite shape or volume, filling any space it is in, like the air we breathe inside a classroom or the steam from boiling ugali. Matter can change from one state to another through heating or cooling.

## Force and Motion
The learner is guided to understand that a force is a push or a pull that makes an object move, stop, or change direction. We use force every day when we push a classroom door open or pull a drawer closed. Gravity is a force that pulls everything towards the ground, making a ball fall when you drop it. Friction is a force that opposes motion, making it difficult to slide a heavy box across the floor. For example, a boda boda uses force from the engine to move and brakes to stop.

## The Human Body
The learner is guided to identify the major external parts of the human body and their functions. The head holds important sense organs like the eyes for seeing, ears for hearing, and a nose for smelling. The arms and hands are used for holding, writing, and lifting objects. The legs and feet are for walking, running, and standing. For instance, you use your hands to hold your pen in class and your legs to walk to the nearby shamba to see different plants.

## Weather and Climate
The learner is guided to describe different types of weather conditions. Weather is the state of the atmosphere at a specific time and includes conditions like sunny, rainy, cloudy, or windy. We measure temperature in degrees Celsius using a thermometer. In Kenya, we experience two main rainy seasons: the long rains from March to May and the short rains from October to December, which are important for growing crops like maize.

## Living Things and Their Habitats
The learner is guided to group living things according to where they live. A habitat is a natural home for a living thing. Terrestrial habitats are on land, like the savannah in Nairobi National Park where lions and giraffes live. Aquatic habitats are in water, like rivers and lakes where fish and frogs live. Plants and animals in a habitat depend on each other for food and shelter to survive.

## The Solar System
The learner is guided to identify the main parts of the solar system. The solar system is made up of the sun and all the objects that move around it, such as planets, moons, and asteroids. The Earth is the third planet from the sun and is the only known planet to have life on it. It takes the Earth 365 days, or one year, to go all the way around the sun. We can see some planets like Venus and Mars with a powerful telescope on a clear night.
