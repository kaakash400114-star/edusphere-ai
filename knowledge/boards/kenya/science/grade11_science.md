# Kenyan CBC Grade 11 Science
## Organic Chemistry: Hydrocarbons and Derivatives
The learner is guided to classify hydrocarbons as alkanes, alkenes, and alkynes based on their carbon-carbon bonds. They learn to name compounds using IUPAC nomenclature, applying prefixes like meth-, eth-, and prop-. For example, they distinguish between ethane (C₂H₆) and ethene (C₂H₄). The learner is also guided to recognize functional groups like alcohols (-OH), halides (-X), and carboxylic acids (-COOH), understanding how these groups alter a molecule's reactivity and properties, essential for producing products like local soaps or industrial solvents in Kenya.
## Chemical Bonding and Properties of Matter
The learner is guided to differentiate between ionic, covalent, and metallic bonding. They are taught to explain the giant structures formed in substances like sodium chloride (table salt) and diamond. They learn how the type of bonding and structure determine properties such as melting point, electrical conductivity, and solubility; for example, ionic compounds conduct electricity when molten, while covalent networks like graphite are good conductors of heat. The learner is guided to apply this knowledge to understand the properties of Kenyan minerals like trona or salt.
## Genetics and Biotechnology Applications
The learner is guided to describe the central dogma of molecular biology: DNA → RNA → Protein. They learn about the process of DNA replication, transcription, and translation, including the role of the genetic code. They are taught to explain how mutations, like a sickle cell mutation, can alter protein function. The learner is also guided to explore applications of biotechnology, such as genetic modification of crops to improve drought resistance, a critical technology for enhancing food security in regions like Kenya's arid and semi-arid lands (ASALs).
## Ecological Systems and Conservation
The learner is guided to define an ecosystem and explain the relationship between biotic and abiotic components. They learn about energy flow through trophic levels and nutrient cycling. They are guided to describe biodiversity, its importance, and threats like deforestation and climate change. Using a local example, the learner is guided to analyze the ecological importance of Nairobi National Park, the only wildlife capital park in the world, and the conservation challenges it faces, such as human-wildlife conflict and habitat fragmentation.
## Energy, Work, and Power
The learner is guided to define energy as the capacity to do work and is taught to calculate work done using the formula Work = Force × Distance. They learn about different forms of energy, including kinetic and potential energy, and the principle of energy conservation. The learner is guided to relate this to power (Power = Work/Time), explaining its practical applications in devices like water pumps used in irrigation or the calculation of electricity costs for a business in Kenya, measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh) and billed in Kenyan Shillings.
## Motion and Forces
The learner is guided to describe motion using distance, displacement, speed, velocity, and acceleration. They learn to calculate these quantities using formulas like v = d/t and a = Δv/Δt. They are guided to explain Newton's laws of motion; for example, a boda boda accelerates because a net force acts on it, opposing frictional forces. The learner is also taught to distinguish between mass and weight, and to explain the concept of moments of force, relating this to practical situations like using a crowbar to lift a heavy object.
