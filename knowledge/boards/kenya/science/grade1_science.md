# Kenyan CBC Grade 1 Science
## My Senses
The learner is guided to identify the five senses: sight, hearing, touch, smell, and taste. They learn that the eyes see, the ears hear, the skin touches, the nose smells, and the tongue tastes. Through play-based activities, they explore their senses by observing a colourful ball (sight), listening to a school bell (hearing), feeling the rough texture of a mango tree bark (touch), smelling a rose flower, and tasting sweet mangoes. They understand that our senses help us learn about and interact with the world around us.

## The Living World
The learner is guided to recognise the difference between living things and non-living things. Living things, like plants, animals, and people, grow, eat, and reproduce. Non-living things, like tables, stones, and books, do not grow or eat. They classify objects in their environment, such as a dog as living and a stone as non-living. They observe that a flower in their school garden needs soil and water to grow, connecting the basic needs of living things to their concrete experiences.

## Materials Around Us
The learner is guided to group common materials, such as wood, metal, plastic, and paper, based on their properties. They learn that metal is hard and shiny, like a kitchen pot, while plastic can be smooth and light, like a toy. They use their sense of touch to explore and describe the texture of objects, such as the smooth surface of a plastic cup and the rough feel of a wooden desk. They understand that materials are chosen for objects based on their characteristics.

## The Sun and Our Weather
The learner is guided to observe that the sun is a source of heat and light that we feel during the day. They learn that the sun makes us feel warm and helps plants grow by providing light. They identify different types of weather, such as sunny, cloudy, and rainy days. They connect weather to their daily activities, understanding that we carry umbrellas when it rains and wear hats to protect ourselves from the hot sun in Nairobi.

## Caring for My Environment
The learner is guided to identify simple ways to keep their environment clean and healthy. They learn that littering makes the environment dirty, so they use dustbins to dispose of sweet wrappers and plastic bottles. They understand the importance of planting trees and flowers to make the air fresh and beautiful, like in the Uhuru Gardens Memorial Park. They are guided to practice conserving water by turning off the tap tightly after use, a habit that helps protect this vital resource.

## Safety in My Surroundings
The learner is guided to identify common safety signs and hazards at home and school. They learn that the red 'stop' sign means they must wait and look before crossing the road near a busy boda boda junction. They understand that fire and hot objects, like a cooking pot making ugali, are dangerous and should not be touched without an adult. They are guided to know the importance of washing their hands with soap and clean water to remove germs and stay healthy.
