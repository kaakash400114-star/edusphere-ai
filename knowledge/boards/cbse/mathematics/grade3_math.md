# CBSE Class 3 Mathematics - NCERT (Math-Magic 3)

## Place value up to 4-digit numbers and rounding
In Class 3, children learn about place value up to 4-digit numbers like 1234, where each digit has a different place: ones, tens, hundreds, and thousands. They understand that the digit '1' in 1234 stands for one thousand, '2' for two hundred, '3' for thirty, and '4' for four ones. Children also learn to round numbers to the nearest ten, such as rounding 47 to 50 because 47 is closer to 50 than 40. They use simple rules like looking at the ones digit—if it is 5 or more, they round up; if less, they round down. For example, 42 rounds to 40 because 2 is less than 5. This helps them estimate numbers easily in real life.

Thousands, hundreds, tens, ones: 5,238. Compare, order, expand; round to the nearest ten and hundred (NCERT "Give and Take").

## Addition and subtraction up to 4 digits
In Class 3, children learn to add and subtract numbers with up to four digits using the place value system. They start by writing numbers in columns (ones, tens, hundreds, thousands) and adding or subtracting from the rightmost place. If adding a column gives a two-digit number, they carry the tens digit to the next left column. For subtraction, if a digit in the top number is smaller, they borrow from the next left column, making the top digit ten bigger. For example, in 3256 + 4328, they add 6+8=14, write 4 and carry 1; then 5+2+1=8; and so on.

Column addition with carry over, subtraction with borrowing; mixed word problems (money, distance, collections).

## Multiplication by 1-digit and tables to 10
In this topic, children learn to multiply one-digit numbers by other one-digit numbers using repeated addition. They memorise multiplication tables from 1 to 10 to solve problems quickly, like knowing 3 times 4 is 12. They understand that multiplication is the same as adding the same number multiple times, for example, 2 × 3 means 2 + 2 + 2. Children also solve simple word problems involving multiplication, such as finding the total number of items in equal groups. They practice writing and solving multiplication sums using tables and repeated addition methods.

Multiplying 2- and 3-digit numbers by 1 digit; commutative idea; tables 2-10 by pattern (NCERT "Fun with Multiply").

## Division without and with simple remainder
In Class 3, children learn to divide numbers into equal groups using the division symbol (÷). They understand division means sharing or grouping equally, like sharing 15 pencils among 3 children, giving each child 5 pencils. When a number cannot be divided equally, they learn about remainders, such as 16 ÷ 3 = 5 with 1 left over. They practice dividing using multiplication facts, like knowing 3 × 4 = 12 to solve 12 ÷ 3 = 4. Children use simple division tables and solve real-life problems, like dividing 20 stickers among 4 friends to get 5 stickers each.

Share and group: 84 ÷ 7; relate multiplication facts to division facts; simple remainders in sharing stories.

## Fractions: half, quarter, third
In Grade 3, children learn about fractions like half, quarter, and third as equal parts of a whole. A half is one of two equal parts, for example, cutting a chapati into two equal pieces gives two halves. A quarter is one of four equal parts, like dividing a chocolate bar into four equal squares. A third is one of three equal parts, such as sharing a sandwich equally among three friends. Children understand that fractions show how many parts are taken from a whole when divided into equal shares. They use simple examples like fruits, shapes, and everyday objects to practice identifying and writing these fractions.

One out of equal parts: 1/2, 1/3, 1/4 of a whole and of a collection (NCERT "Fun with Fractions"); compare simple unit fractions.

## Money: adding bills and change
Children learn to add money by counting notes and coins to find the total cost. They start by adding notes of the same value, like ₹50 and ₹50, to get ₹100. Then they add different coins and notes, for example, ₹20, ₹10, and a ₹5 coin to make ₹35. They also learn to add the cost of two things, like a pencil for ₹15 and an eraser for ₹10, to find the total ₹25. Finally, they practice adding the change received after buying something, such as getting ₹3 back when paying ₹20 for a ₹17 item.

Make a bill, add amounts, give change from ₹100/₹500 (NCERT "Rupees and Paise").

## Time: reading the calendar, minutes past
Children learn to read a calendar to find the day, date, and month. They understand that a week has 7 days and a month has 4 weeks. They also learn about time in minutes, like 15 minutes past 1 o'clock or 30 minutes past 2 o'clock. They practise telling the time using clock faces to see the long hand pointing to the minutes. For example, when the long hand points to 3, it is 15 minutes past the hour. This helps them plan their daily activities and know the time for school or play.

Read a clock to 5 minutes; a.m./p.m.; days in each month; simple duration ("the train took 3 hours").

## Length (m/cm/km) and weight (g/kg)
In Class 3, we learn to measure length in centimetres (cm), metres (m), and kilometres (km). We know that 100 cm make 1 m, and 1000 m make 1 km. For example, a pencil might be 15 cm long, while a school ground may be 200 m long. We also learn about weight in grams (g) and kilograms (kg), understanding that 1000 g equal 1 kg. A small apple could weigh about 100 g, while a big watermelon might weigh 2 kg. These measurements help us compare and understand the size and weight of things around us.

Choose the right unit; convert m→cm, kg→g; measure-and-compare activities.

## Geometry: lines, angles as turns, 2D-3D shapes
In Grade 3, children learn to identify and draw straight lines, curved lines, and standing lines. They understand angles as turns, like a half turn or quarter turn, while moving or playing. Students recognise and name basic 2D shapes such as circle, square, rectangle, and triangle, and identify their corners and sides. They also observe 3D shapes like cube, cuboid, cylinder, and sphere, noting how these shapes look in everyday objects. Through simple activities, children compare shapes by counting their corners and sides to see how they are alike or different.

Straight/curved lines; open/closed figures; corner "angle" idea; faces and edges of solids (boxes, cylinders).

## Data handling: pictograph and bar basics
In Class 3, children learn about pictographs and bar graphs to understand and represent simple data. A pictograph uses pictures or symbols to show information, like one picture standing for two children, making it easy to count. They also learn to draw and read basic bar graphs, where bars of different heights show quantities, such as the number of fruits in a basket. By organising data in these ways, children practice counting, comparing, and answering questions like "Which has more?" or "How many in all?" using visual tools. This helps them develop early skills in reading and interpreting information presented in charts.

Tally marks, pictographs with a key, simple bar reading (favourite fruit of the class).
