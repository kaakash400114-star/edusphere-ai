# CBSE Class 4 Mathematics - NCERT (Math-Magic 4)

## Large numbers: 5- and 6-digit, Indian place value
In Grade 4, children learn about big numbers called 5-digit and 6-digit numbers. These numbers have places like ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten-thousands, and lakhs, each showing its value. For example, the number 12,345 has 1 in the ten-thousands place, 2 in the thousands place, 3 in the hundreds place, 4 in the tens place, and 5 in the ones place. Similarly, 1,00,000 is the smallest 6-digit number, read as one lakh. Children practice reading, writing, and understanding how to count and compare these large numbers using the Indian place value system. They also learn to add and subtract these numbers, making sure to carry over or borrow correctly between places.

Lakh and ten-thousand places; read/write numerals and number names; compare, order, round to nearest 1000; introduction to the international system (thousand, million).

## Addition and subtraction of large numbers
In Class 4, we learn to add and subtract big numbers like 4325 and 5678 using the column method. We write the numbers one below the other, lining up the digits correctly – units under units, tens under tens, and so on. For addition, we start adding from the rightmost digit (units place) and carry over any extra number to the next column. For subtraction, we also start from the right, and if the top number is smaller, we borrow from the next left column. For example, when adding 3568 and 2425, we get 5993, and when subtracting 2425 from 5993, we get 3568.

Column work to 6 digits; estimation by rounding first; multi-step word problems (population, money, distances between cities).

## Multiplication: 2-digit × 2-digit/3-digit
In this topic, children learn to multiply 2-digit numbers by 2-digit and 3-digit numbers using the standard column method. They first multiply the ones digit of the second number with the entire first number, writing the result below. Then they multiply the tens digit, shifting the one place to the left, and add both products to get the final answer. For example, to multiply 24 by 15, they find 24 × 5 = 120 and then 24 × 10 = 240, adding them to get 360. This method helps them understand place value and build a strong foundation for larger calculations.

Lattice/array idea then algorithm; multiply by 10, 100, 1000 by shifting; word problems (tickets, buses, collections).

## Division: long division by 1- and 2-digit numbers
In Class 4, children learn to divide big numbers by small numbers using the long division method. They divide numbers by 1-digit (like 4, 6, 8) and 2-digit (like 12, 15, 20) numbers step by step. They find how many times the divisor fits into the dividend, write the quotient above, and subtract to bring down the next number. For example, to divide 84 by 4, they see 4 goes into 8 two times, write 2, then 4 goes into 4 one time, write 1, to get the answer 21. This helps them share things equally and solve simple problems in real life.

Dividend, divisor, quotient, remainder; check by multiply-back; problems with sharing and grouping.

## Factors and multiples, prime numbers
In Class 4, we learn about factors and multiples. Factors are numbers that divide another number exactly, like 1, 2, 3, and 6 are factors of 6. Multiples are numbers you get when you multiply a number by whole numbers, such as 5, 10, 15, and 20 are multiples of 5. We also learn about prime numbers, which are numbers greater than 1 that have only two factors: 1 and the number itself, like 2, 3, 5, and 7. For example, 4 is not a prime number because it has three factors: 1, 2, and 4. We practice finding factors and multiples using fun activities and number charts.

Factors come in pairs; common factors; multiples and common multiples; primes to 100 (2, 3, 5, 7, 11...); divisibility by 2, 5, 10.

## Fractions: equivalent, comparing, simple addition
In Class 4, children learn about equivalent fractions, which are different fractions that show the same part of a whole, like ½ and 2/4. They compare fractions by looking at the numerators and denominators, understanding that a smaller numerator means a smaller part when the denominator is the same. They also learn to add simple fractions with the same denominator, such as ¼ + ²/₄ = ³/₄, by adding the numerators while keeping the denominator unchanged. Using visual aids like fraction strips or circles, they practice identifying, comparing, and adding fractions to build a strong foundation. These concepts help them understand how fractions work in everyday life, like sharing or dividing things equally.

Equivalent fractions by multiplying top and bottom; compare with same numerator/denominator; add like fractions; fraction of a collection (1/4 of 20).

## Decimals: tenths and hundredths
In Class 4, we learn about decimals like tenths and hundredths, which are parts of a whole number. A tenth is written after a dot, like 0.1, and it means one out of ten equal parts. A hundredth is written as 0.01, meaning one out of one hundred equal parts. For example, 0.3 means three tenths, and 0.07 means seven hundredths. We also learn to add these decimals, like 0.2 + 0.4 = 0.6, and compare them to see which is bigger or smaller.

Money and length as decimals (₹12.50, 3.6 m); place value of tenths/hundredths; compare and add simple decimals.

## Measurement conversions and word problems
In this topic, children learn to convert measurements between units like centimetres and metres, grams and kilograms, and litres and millilitres. They understand that 1 metre is equal to 100 centimetres, 1 kilogram is 1000 grams, and 1 litre is 1000 millilitres, using these rules to solve simple word problems. For example, they might find the total length of two ribbons, one 50 cm long and the other 1 metre long, by converting metres to centimetres first. They also solve problems involving weights, like finding how many 500 g packets make 2 kilograms, and volumes, such as calculating how many 250 ml glasses fill a 1 litre jug. These activities help them apply measurement concepts in everyday situations.

km↔m, m↔cm, kg↔g, l↔ml; add/subtract measures with conversion; perimeter by counting/walking the boundary.

## Perimeter and area (counting squares)
In Class 4, children learn to find the perimeter by adding the lengths of all sides of a shape, like a square or rectangle, measured in centimetres. They also learn to find the area by counting the number of small squares that fit inside a shape, with each small square equal to one square centimetre. For example, a rectangle with 4 squares in a row and 3 squares in a column has an area of 12 square centimetres. They understand that perimeter is the total distance around the shape, while area is the space it covers. These concepts help them solve simple problems involving everyday objects like tables or playgrounds.

Perimeter as the walk around; area as cover with unit squares of rectangles/squares; compare perimeters of same-area shapes.

## Time, speed as distance in unit time, money problems
In Class 4 Mathematics, children learn to tell time using the 12-hour and 24-hour clocks and calculate durations by subtracting start and end times. They understand speed as the distance covered in one unit of time, such as kilometres per hour, and solve simple problems involving distance, speed, and time. For money, they practice adding and subtracting amounts in rupees and paise, and making change using Indian currency notes and coins. Examples include calculating how long a journey takes or finding the total cost of items bought from a shop. These concepts help children apply maths to real-life situations like planning trips or managing pocket money.

Read timetables; duration across hours; simple "how far in one hour" beginnings; profit-free buying-selling stories with bills.
