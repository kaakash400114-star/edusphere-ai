# CBSE Class 12 Mathematics - NCERT (Part I & II essentials)

## Relations and functions
In Class 12 Mathematics, you learn about relations and functions, which describe how sets of numbers are connected. A relation is a collection of ordered pairs, like a list of students and their roll numbers, while a function is a special relation where every input gives exactly one output, such as a rule that turns a roll number into a student’s name. You also study types of functions, like one-one (injective), onto (surjective), and bijective functions, which help understand if outputs are unique or cover the entire set. You practice verifying these properties and work with composite functions, where two functions are applied in a sequence, like adding marks and then calculating a percentage.

Types of relations (reflexive, symmetric, transitive, equivalence); one-one, onto functions; composition; inverse of a function.

## Inverse trigonometric functions
Students learn about inverse trigonometric functions, which are the opposite of regular trigonometric functions like sine, cosine, and tangent. They study the principal value branches and the restricted domains that make these functions one-to-one, ensuring each input has only one output. For example, the inverse sine function, written as sin⁻¹x, gives the angle whose sine is x. They memorise important formulas like the derivative of sin⁻¹x and its integral, and learn how to simplify expressions involving these functions, such as sin⁻¹x + cos⁻¹x = π/2. These concepts help solve problems in calculus and geometry.

Domain-range; principal value branch; graphs; properties: sin⁻^1 + cos⁻^1 = π/2 family; simplifications.

## Matrices
In Class 12, you learn about matrices as rectangular arrangements of numbers in rows and columns, where a matrix with m rows and n columns is called an m × n matrix. You study operations like addition, subtraction, and multiplication, remembering that multiplication is only possible when the number of columns in the first matrix equals the number of rows in the second, for example, multiplying a 2×3 matrix by a 3×2 matrix. Key concepts include finding the transpose of a matrix, which swaps its rows with columns, and the inverse of a square matrix, which when multiplied by the original matrix gives the identity matrix. You also learn to solve systems of linear equations using matrix methods, like finding the inverse of a coefficient matrix to determine the values of variables.

Types; equality, addition, scalar and matrix multiplication (non-commutativity); transpose; symmetric-skew symmetric; elementary operations idea.

## Determinants
In the topic of Determinants, students learn to calculate the value of a 2x2 and 3x3 square matrix using the rule of diagonals (Sarrus's rule) and expansion by minors, also known as cofactor expansion. They understand key concepts like the minor, cofactor, and adjoint of a matrix, which are essential for finding the inverse of a matrix. For example, the determinant of a 2x2 matrix [[a, b], [c, d]] is calculated as (ad - bc). Students also apply properties of determinants, such as the effect of row or column operations, to simplify complex calculations. Finally, they use determinants to solve systems of linear equations and find the area of triangles using coordinate geometry.

Determinant of order up to 3; properties; area of triangle; adjoint-inverse; solving systems by matrix method (consistent, inconsistent, unique).

## Continuity and differentiability
In Class 12 Mathematics, you learn about continuity and differentiability of functions, focusing on whether a function has breaks and if its graph has a smooth tangent at every point. You study the rules for differentiating composite functions, like the chain rule, and trigonometric, exponential, and logarithmic functions, such as differentiating \( \sin(2x) \) or \( \log x \). The topic also includes applying differentiation to find equations of tangent and normal lines to curves, like finding the tangent to \( y = x^2 \) at a given point. Finally, you explore how differentiability implies continuity but continuity does not guarantee differentiability, with examples like the absolute value function \( |x| \) at \( x = 0 \).

Continuity checks; differentiability; chain rule; implicit functions; inverse trig derivatives; exponential-logarithmic derivatives; log-differentiation and parametric idea.

## Application of derivatives
In the topic 'Application of derivatives', students learn to use derivatives to find the rate of change of quantities and determine if a function is increasing or decreasing. They apply the first derivative test to find the maximum and minimum values of functions, which helps in solving problems like finding the dimensions of a rectangle with the largest area for a fixed perimeter. The second derivative test is used to confirm whether a critical point is a maximum or minimum, and derivatives also help in finding the tangent and normal equations to a curve at a given point. For example, if the distance of a car is given by a function, its derivative gives the speed of the car at any time.

Rate of change; increasing-decreasing; maxima-minima (first and second derivative tests); tangents-normals; approximation; word problems (optimisation).

## Integrals
In Class 12, students learn about Integrals, which are the reverse of derivatives, used to find areas and accumulate quantities. They study basic integration rules like integrating functions like xⁿ, eˣ, sin x, and cos x, using formulas such as ∫xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1) + C. They also learn methods like substitution to simplify complex integrals and integrate functions using partial fractions for rational expressions. For example, finding the area under a curve y = x² from x=0 to x=2 involves evaluating ∫₀² x² dx, which equals [x³/3]₀² = 8/3. The topic helps in solving practical problems in physics and economics involving rates of change and accumulation.

Indefinite integration: standard results, substitution, partial fractions, by parts (ILATE); definite integrals and properties.

## Applications of the integrals
In this topic, students learn to find the area enclosed by curves using definite integrals, such as the area between a parabola and the x-axis. They also calculate the area between two curves, like a line and a circle, by subtracting their integrals. Additionally, students apply integrals to find the volume of solids of revolution, such as the volume generated by rotating a curve around the x-axis or y-axis. These problems help them understand how integrals solve real-world measurement problems. The key formulas include the area under a curve ∫y dx and the volume π ∫y² dx, applied to functions like y = x² or y = √x.

Area under curves: lines, parabolas, circles (simple bounded regions).

## Differential equations
In Class 12, you learn to find the solution of a differential equation, which relates a function with its derivatives. You will study methods to solve first-order equations, like variable separable and linear equations, such as dy/dx = 2x. You also learn about second-order equations with constant coefficients and how to find their particular and general solutions, for example, d²y/dx² + 3 dy/dx + 2y = 0. Additionally, you explore the applications of differential equations in solving problems related to population growth and Newton's law of cooling.

Order-degree; variable separable; homogeneous type (idea); linear equations with integrating factor; modelling statements (growth-decay).

## Vector algebra
In Vector Algebra, students learn about vectors as quantities having both magnitude and direction. They study vector operations like addition, subtraction, and multiplication by a scalar, following the triangle and parallelogram laws. The dot product (or scalar product) and cross product (or vector product) are introduced, with formulas like **a · b = |a||b|cosθ** and **a × b = |a||b|sinθ n̂**. Applications include finding the angle between two vectors or calculating the area of a parallelogram formed by them. Students also practice resolving vectors into components along the x, y, and z axes for easier computation.

Vectors and scalars; direction cosines; addition, scalar-vector multiplication; dot-cross products; projections; area of parallelogram-triangle via cross product.

## Three-dimensional geometry
In this topic, students learn about three-dimensional geometry, focusing on the direction cosines and direction ratios of a line connecting two points. They study the equations of lines in space, including the Cartesian and vector forms, and how to find the angle between two lines. The topic also covers the equations of planes in different forms, such as general, normal, and intercept form, and teaches how to find the distance of a point from a plane. For example, students learn to calculate the angle between two lines given their direction ratios or the equation of a plane passing through three non-collinear points. Finally, they explore the concept of the shortest distance between two skew lines using vector methods.

Direction cosines-ratios; line equations (cartesian-vector); angle between lines; shortest distance (skew lines idea); plane equations; angle line-plane.

## Linear programming
In linear programming, students learn to solve real-life problems by finding the best possible outcome, like maximum profit or minimum cost, under certain conditions. They form linear inequalities based on given constraints and draw their graphs to identify the feasible region, which is the common area satisfying all conditions. The optimal solution is found at one of the corner points of this feasible region by testing the objective function. For example, a factory may use this method to decide how many toys and dolls to make to earn the highest profit while staying within limits of materials and time.

Constraints, objective function, feasible region, corner point method; diet-manufacturing problems; unbounded-infeasible recognition.

## Probability
In Class 12 Probability, you learn about independent and dependent events, where independent events do not affect each other's outcomes, like tossing two coins. You study the addition rule for mutually exclusive events, which cannot happen together, such as rolling a die to get either a 2 or a 5. Conditional probability is introduced, where the chance of an event changes given another event has occurred, like drawing a second card from a deck after the first one is not replaced. Bayes' Theorem helps you find the reverse probability of an event based on new information, like testing for a disease. You also revise the multiplication rule and total probability to solve complex real-life problems involving chance.

Conditional probability; multiplication theorem; independence; Bayes' theorem; random variables and probability distributions; mean-variance of distributions; binomial distribution (statements).
