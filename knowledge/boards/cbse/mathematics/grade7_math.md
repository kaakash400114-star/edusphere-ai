# CBSE Class 7 Mathematics - NCERT

## Integers
In Class 7, you learn about integers, which are all whole numbers including positive numbers, negative numbers, and zero. You discover how to add and subtract integers using rules like adding two negative numbers gives a negative answer, while subtracting a negative number is like adding a positive one. For example, (+5) + (-3) equals +2, and (+4) – (-2) equals +6. You also multiply and divide integers, remembering that multiplying a positive and a negative gives a negative result, like (+3) × (-4) = -12. These rules help you solve problems involving numbers above and below zero.

Properties of addition/subtraction/multiplication of integers; division; word problems; number-line reasoning; properties (closure, commutative, distributive over addition).

## Fractions and decimals
In Grade 7, you learn to add and subtract fractions with different denominators by finding a common denominator and then adding or subtracting the numerators. You also multiply a fraction by a whole number by multiplying the numerator and keeping the denominator the same. For decimals, you will learn to add, subtract, and multiply them, making sure to line up the decimal points correctly. You will also practice converting between fractions and decimals, like writing ½ as 0.5. For example, to add ½ and ¼, you find a common denominator of 4 and get ¾.

Multiply fractions (fraction × fraction); divide fractions; multiply/divide decimals; applications in money, measurement.

## Data handling
In data handling, you learn to collect information, organise it in tables, and represent it using pictographs and bar graphs. A pictograph uses pictures to show data, where each picture stands for a certain number of items, like one apple picture for 10 apples. A bar graph uses vertical or horizontal bars to display data clearly, with one bar showing the count for each category, such as the number of students who like different fruits. You also find the mode, which is the number or item that appears most often in the data, like mango being the most liked fruit. Finally, you learn to interpret these graphs to answer simple questions about the information.

Collect, organise, representative values: mean, median, mode; double bar graphs; chance and probability as likelihood (fair die, coin).

## Simple equations
In Simple Equations, children learn to find the value of an unknown number, usually shown as a letter like 'x' or 'y'. They understand that both sides of an equation must be kept equal, whatever they do to one side, they must do to the other, like adding, subtracting, multiplying or dividing. For example, in x + 5 = 12, we subtract 5 from both sides to find x = 7. They also learn to translate simple word problems into equations, such as if Arun has 'a' pencils and his mother gives him 3 more, making it 15 pencils, the equation is a + 3 = 15. The main goal is to solve these equations to find the hidden number.

Setting up equations from statements; solving with balance (two operations); checking solutions; transfer (transposing) method.

## Lines and angles
In this topic, you learn about different types of angles like acute, obtuse, right, and straight angles. You also study pairs of angles such as complementary angles (which add up to 90°) and supplementary angles (which add up to 180°). Another important concept is adjacent angles, which share a common vertex and arm but do not overlap. You will also learn about vertically opposite angles, which are always equal when two lines cross each other. Finally, you explore how to find unknown angles using these angle properties and simple calculations.

Related angles: complementary, supplementary, adjacent, linear pair, vertically opposite; parallel lines and transversal angle pairs.

## The triangle and its properties
Students learn about different types of triangles based on sides and angles, like equilateral triangles with three equal sides and scalene triangles with all sides different. They understand important properties, such as the sum of all angles in any triangle being 180 degrees, and use this to find missing angles in problems. The topic also introduces the Pythagorean theorem for right-angled triangles, showing how the square of the longest side (hypotenuse) equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides. For example, in a right-angled triangle with sides 3 cm, 4 cm, and 5 cm, students verify that 3² + 4² = 5². Additionally, they explore the concept of medians and altitudes, which are special lines drawn from a vertex to the opposite side.

Angle sum (180°); exterior angle; types by sides/angles; Pythagoras property (statement and simple use); median and altitude.

## Comparing quantities
In comparing quantities, children learn to find the ratio between two numbers, like comparing the number of apples to oranges in a basket. They understand that ratios can be written in different forms, such as 2:3 or 2/3, to show how many times one quantity is of another. Students also learn about percentages, which means parts per hundred, and practice converting fractions and ratios into percentages to compare them easily, for example, turning 3/4 into 75%. They solve simple problems to find the percentage of a given number, like calculating 10% of 200 rupees. Finally, they explore profit and loss by understanding if selling an item for more or less money results in a gain or loss compared to the cost price.

Percentage idea; convert fraction-decimal-percent; profit/loss (simple); simple interest: P, R, T formula and use.

## Rational numbers
In Grade 7, you learn about rational numbers, which are numbers that can be written as fractions like p/q where q is not zero. You compare them using the number line and learn to add and subtract them by finding a common denominator. Multiplication and division are done by multiplying numerators with numerators and denominators with denominators, like how 1/2 multiplied by 1/3 gives 1/6. You also convert between fractions and decimals, such as turning 1/4 into 0.25. These rules help you solve problems with parts of whole things.

Meaning; on number line; equivalent; compare; add/subtract/multiply/divide rational numbers; properties.

## Perimeter and area
In this topic, children learn how to measure the boundary of simple shapes like squares and rectangles, which is called the perimeter. They find the perimeter by adding the lengths of all the sides, or using formulas like 4 × side for a square and 2 × (length + breadth) for a rectangle. For area, they understand it is the space inside a shape and use formulas like side × side for a square and length × breadth for a rectangle to calculate it. They practice with real-life examples, such as finding the perimeter of a book cover or the area of a classroom floor. These help them solve everyday problems involving space and distance.

Squares, rectangles, triangles; parallelogram (base × height); circles: circumference and area with π; conversion of units; applications.

## Algebraic expressions
In Class 7, we learn to write and solve algebraic expressions using numbers, letters, and mathematical signs. We understand that letters stand for unknown numbers and follow rules like adding and subtracting terms with the same letters, such as 3x + 2x = 5x. We also learn to expand expressions using the distributive property, for example, 2(x + 3) becomes 2x + 6. Simple equations are solved by finding the value of the unknown letter, like solving x + 5 = 12 to get x = 7. These ideas help us solve everyday problems in a fun and easy way.

Terms, factors, coefficients; like/unlike; add/subtract expressions; find value by substitution; use patterns.

## Exponents and powers
In class 7, we learn about exponents and powers to write big numbers in a simple way. A number like 5 multiplied by itself 4 times is written as 5 raised to the power 4, which is written as 5⁴. We learn rules like adding exponents when multiplying the same base, such as 3² × 3³ = 3²⁺³ = 3⁵. We also learn to write numbers in standard form, like writing 7000 as 7 × 10³. These rules help us solve problems quickly and understand larger numbers easily.

Base-index; laws: product, quotient, power of power, zero exponent; large numbers in standard (scientific) form.

## Symmetry and visualising solid shapes
In Class 7 Mathematics, children learn about symmetry by identifying lines of symmetry in shapes like squares and circles, which divide them into matching halves. They explore visualising solid shapes by understanding their faces, edges, and vertices, such as counting these parts in a cube or cuboid. The topic also covers nets of 3D shapes, which are 2D patterns that can be folded to form solids like pyramids and prisms. Children practice drawing symmetrical figures and recognising rotational symmetry, where shapes look the same after a turn, like a fan or a windmill. By studying these concepts, they develop skills in observation and spatial reasoning to understand the world around them.

Line and rotational symmetry; nets of solids; drawing solids on isometric paper; faces-edges-vertices relationships.
