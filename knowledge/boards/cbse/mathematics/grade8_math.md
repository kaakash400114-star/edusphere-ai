# CBSE Class 8 Mathematics - NCERT

## Rational numbers
In Class 8, you will learn about rational numbers, which are numbers that can be written as a fraction where both the numerator and denominator are integers, and the denominator is not zero. You will understand how to add, subtract, multiply, and divide rational numbers by following specific rules, such as finding a common denominator for addition and subtraction. Multiplying rational numbers is done by multiplying the numerators and denominators, while dividing involves multiplying by the reciprocal of the divisor. For example, to add 1/2 and 1/3, you find a common denominator like 6, making it 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6. You will also explore how to represent rational numbers on a number line and compare them to determine which is larger or smaller.

Properties of operations on rationals; role of 0 and 1; negative of a number; reciprocal; distributive law; representation between any two rationals.

## Linear equations in one variable
In this topic, children learn to solve linear equations in one variable, which are equations like 2x + 3 = 7 with only one unknown number, x. They follow rules to keep the equation balanced, doing the same thing to both sides, like adding or subtracting numbers to isolate the variable. For example, to solve 3x – 5 = 10, they first add 5 to both sides to get 3x = 15, then divide by 3 to find x = 5. They practice writing equations from word problems, such as finding a number if adding 12 to it gives 20, and then solve it step by step. The focus is on understanding the variable as a missing number and using inverse operations to find it.

Solve with variables on both sides; fractional equations; reduce to linear form; word problems: ages, numbers, perimeter, money.

## Understanding quadrilaterals
In Class 8, you learn to identify and draw different types of quadrilaterals like squares, rectangles, rhombuses, parallelograms, and trapeziums. You understand that quadrilaterals have four sides and four angles, and you discover special properties like opposite sides being equal and parallel in parallelograms. The topic also teaches you to find the sum of the interior angles of any quadrilateral, which is always 360 degrees. For example, a square has four equal sides and four right angles, each of 90 degrees. Finally, you learn to distinguish between quadrilaterals based on their unique properties and side lengths.

Polygon angle-sum; exterior angles; kinds of quadrilaterals; parallelogram properties (diagonals); special parallelograms: rhombus, rectangle, square.

## Data handling
In Data Handling, you learn to collect, read, and organise information using different methods like tally marks and frequency tables. You will understand how to group data into class intervals and represent it using bar graphs, where the height of each bar shows the quantity. For example, you can make a bar graph of favourite fruits in your class, with different bars for apples, bananas, and oranges. You also learn about probability, which is the chance of an event happening, like the chance of picking a red marble from a bag. These skills help you understand and share information clearly.

Organise raw data; grouped frequency; histogram; pie chart central angles; probability of simple events (cards, dice, coins).

## Squares and square roots
In Class 8, we learn about perfect squares, which are numbers made by multiplying a number with itself, like 16 is 4 times 4. We also discover how to find the square root of a perfect square, which is the number that was multiplied to get it, such as the square root of 25 is 5. To calculate squares easily, we use methods like multiplying the number by itself or using identities like (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b², for example, 12² is (10 + 2)² = 100 + 40 + 4 = 144. We explore patterns in squares and learn to estimate square roots of non-perfect squares between two known squares, like understanding that the square root of 30 is between 5 and 6. Finally, we solve simple word problems involving squares and square roots, such as finding the area of a square garden if the side length is given.

Properties of square numbers; patterns; find square root by repeated subtraction, prime factorisation, long division; decimals in square roots.

## Cubes and cube roots
In this topic, we learn about numbers that can be formed by multiplying a number by itself three times, called perfect cubes. For example, 2 × 2 × 2 = 8, so 8 is the cube of 2. We also find the cube root, which is the number that gives the original number when multiplied three times, such as the cube root of 8 is 2. We use patterns like 1³ = 1, 2³ = 8, and 3³ = 27 to identify cubes. To find cube roots, we try numbers until we get the correct cube, like finding that the cube root of 64 is 4 because 4 × 4 × 4 = 64.

Cube numbers and patterns; cube root by prime factorisation; units digits tricks.

## Comparing quantities
In class 8, you will learn to compare quantities using percentages, ratios, and proportions to understand changes in values like prices or scores. We practice finding the percentage increase or decrease of items, such as a school fee rise from ₹500 to ₹600, which is a 20% increase. You also study simple and compound interest with formulas like Simple Interest = (P × R × T)/100 to calculate money earned in a savings account. We compare ratios to determine relationships between quantities, for example, finding that 2 kilograms of flour mixed with 1 kilogram of sugar gives a 2:1 ratio. Lastly, we learn to apply these concepts to solve real-life problems involving discounts, taxes, and profit calculations.

Percentage increase-decrease; discount, marked price; profit-loss percent; VAT/GST idea; compound interest by yearly and formula method; growth and decay.

## Algebraic expressions and identities
In Class 8, students learn about algebraic expressions and how to work with them. They study the different parts of an expression, like terms, coefficients, and variables, and practice adding and subtracting them by combining like terms, such as adding 3x and 2x to get 5x. They also explore important identities, which are special formulas that always remain true, like (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b², and use them to solve problems quickly. Students multiply algebraic expressions using the distributive property and expand them by applying these identities. By the end of the topic, they can simplify expressions and use identities to find answers without doing long calculations.

Multiply monomials-binomials; identities: (a+b)^2, (a−b)^2, a^2−b^2, (x+a)(x+b); apply identities to multiply and simplify.

## Mensuration
In Mensuration, you learn to find the area and perimeter of 2D shapes like squares, rectangles, triangles, and circles. The formulas help you calculate these measures, for example, the area of a rectangle is length times width. You also study the surface area and volume of 3D objects such as cubes, cuboids, and cylinders. For a cube, the volume is found by multiplying the side by itself three times. These formulas help solve problems about the space inside objects and the covering on their outside.

Area of trapezium, general quadrilateral, polygon; surface area of cube-cuboid-cylinder; volume of cube-cuboid-cylinder; capacity vs volume.

## Exponents and powers
In Class 8, we learn how to write very big or very small numbers using exponents, which are small numbers written above and to the right of another number called the base. We study important rules like the product rule, which says when you multiply numbers with the same base, you add their exponents (for example, 2³ × 2⁴ = 2³⁺⁴ = 2⁷). We also learn the quotient rule, where dividing numbers with the same base means subtracting their exponents (like 5⁶ ÷ 5² = 5⁶⁻² = 5⁴). Additionally, we explore how to write numbers in standard form using powers of ten, making calculations with large numbers much easier to understand and manage.

Negative exponents; laws extended to negative powers; very small/large numbers in standard form.

## Direct and inverse proportions
In this topic, we learn about direct and inverse proportions. When two quantities are in direct proportion, they increase or decrease together, like the number of bananas and their cost. If one quantity doubles, the other also doubles. In inverse proportion, when one quantity increases, the other decreases, such as the number of workers and the time taken to finish a job. If more workers join, the time to finish the work reduces. We use formulas to find missing values in both cases. For example, if 5 workers take 10 days, 10 workers will take half the time.

Recognise types; constant of proportionality; applications: speed-time, workers-days, food for days.

## Factorisation
In Class 8, you learn factorisation, which is writing a number or algebra as a multiplication of smaller parts. For numbers, you find their factors, like 12 can be written as 4 × 3 or 2 × 2 × 3. In algebra, you learn to factorise expressions by taking out common factors, such as 2x + 4 becoming 2(x + 2). You also study factorisation using identities, like a² – b² = (a + b)(a – b), to simplify expressions. This helps you solve equations and make maths problems easier to understand.

Common factors; grouping; using identities; split the middle term; division of a polynomial by a monomial and binomial.

## Introduction to graphs
Students learn to read and draw simple line graphs and bar graphs to show information clearly. They understand how to identify the x-axis and y-axis, label them with correct numbers, and plot points accurately using given data. For example, they can show the growth of a plant over months by marking height on the y-axis and time on the x-axis. They also learn to interpret the graphs and answer questions about the data shown, such as finding the highest or lowest value. These skills help them organise and understand numbers in a visual way.

Linear graphs; coordinates; reading distance-time and simple conversion graphs; plotting from equations (y = x, y = 2x + 1).

## Playing with numbers
In this topic, children learn about the special qualities of numbers like even, odd, prime, and composite numbers. They explore how numbers can be divided evenly by other numbers, such as understanding that 12 can be divided by 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 12. They also study divisibility rules, like a number being divisible by 2 if it ends with an even digit, or by 3 if the sum of its digits is divisible by 3. For example, 45 is divisible by 3 because 4 + 5 = 9, which is divisible by 3. Additionally, they learn to break down numbers into smaller parts using factors and express numbers as products of their prime factors.

Number puzzles; digits and place value generalisation (2-digit = 10x + y); divisibility reasons (why 3's rule works).
