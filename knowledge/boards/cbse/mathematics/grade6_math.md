# CBSE Class 6 Mathematics - NCERT (Ganita Prakash)

## Knowing our numbers
In Class 6, we learn to read and write very big numbers in words and figures, like 35,46,200. We also study the place value system, which tells us the value of each digit in a number, such as the 3 in 35,46,200 standing for 3 crores. Comparing numbers is important, and we use symbols like > (greater than) and < (less than) to arrange numbers from smallest to biggest or biggest to smallest. To make counting large numbers easy, we place commas after every three digits starting from the right, like 1,00,00,000 for one crore. We also learn to round off numbers to the nearest tens, hundreds, or thousands, for example, 834 becomes 830 when rounded to the nearest ten.

Indian vs international place value; compare large numbers; estimation and rounding; Roman numerals; brackets and the order of operations (BODMAS).

## Whole numbers
In Class 6, children learn about whole numbers, which start from 0 and go on forever: 0, 1, 2, 3, and so on. They practice counting whole numbers in order and finding the number that comes before or after a given number, like knowing 15 comes after 14. Students also learn to compare whole numbers using symbols like > (greater than), < (less than), and = (equal to), for example, 20 > 15. They explore the place value system to understand the value of each digit in a number, such as 345 having 3 hundreds, 4 tens, and 5 ones. Lastly, they learn to arrange whole numbers in increasing or decreasing order, like arranging 45, 12, and 78 as 12, 45, 78.

Number line; successor/predecessor; properties of addition and multiplication (closure, commutative, associative, identity); patterns.

## Playing with numbers: factors, multiples, HCF-LCM
In this topic, children learn about factors and multiples of numbers. A factor is a number that divides another number exactly, like 2 and 3 are factors of 6. A multiple is the result of multiplying a number by whole numbers, such as 10, 15, and 20 are multiples of 5. They also find the Highest Common Factor (HCF), which is the biggest number that divides two or more numbers without leaving a remainder. For example, the HCF of 12 and 18 is 6. Similarly, they learn the Least Common Multiple (LCM), the smallest number that is a multiple of two or more numbers, like the LCM of 4 and 6 is 12. These concepts help in solving problems with numbers easily.

Tests of divisibility (2,3,4,5,6,8,9,10,11); primes, composites, prime factorisation; HCF and LCM by prime factorisation; word problems (bells, tiles, capacities).

## Basic geometrical ideas
In this topic, children learn about points, lines, line segments, and rays. They understand that a point has no size and is shown with a dot, while a line goes on forever in both directions. They also learn about angles, which are made by two rays meeting at a point, and shapes like triangles and quadrilaterals, which have a fixed number of sides and corners. For example, a triangle has three sides and three angles, and a square has four equal sides and four right angles. They practise drawing these shapes and naming their parts clearly.

Point, line, ray, segment; intersecting vs parallel lines; angles and types; triangles classified by sides and angles; quadrilaterals; circles: centre, radius, diameter, chord, arc.

## Understanding elementary shapes
In this topic, children learn to identify and describe different shapes like circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles. They understand how to count the number of sides and corners (vertices) in each shape, for example, a square has four sides and four corners. Children also learn about straight and curved lines, and how to draw shapes using rulers and compasses. They explore basic ideas of angles, such as right angles, by comparing corners to the shape of an 'L'. Finally, they learn to sort shapes based on their properties like size, colour, or number of sides.

Measuring and comparing line segments; angles with protractor; perpendiculars; classification of triangles and quadrilaterals; 3D shapes: faces, edges, vertices; polyhedra vs non-polyhedra.

## Integers
In Class 6, we learn about integers, which are positive and negative whole numbers including zero. Positive numbers are greater than zero, like +5 or 5, and negative numbers are less than zero, like -3. We use a number line to compare and order integers, where numbers to the right are bigger and numbers to the left are smaller. When adding integers, we move right on the number line for positive numbers and left for negative numbers, so 4 + (-2) = 2. Subtracting integers is like adding the opposite, so 5 - 3 = 2 is the same as 5 + (-3) = 2.

Negative numbers on the number line; ordering; add and subtract integers with movement-model; connect to temperature and lifts.

## Fractions
In Class 6, you learn about fractions which are parts of a whole. A fraction has a numerator (top number) and a denominator (bottom number), like in ¾ where 3 is the numerator and 4 is the denominator. You learn to add and subtract fractions with the same denominator, for example, ¼ + ¼ = 2/4, which can also be written as ½. You also learn to find equivalent fractions by multiplying both the numerator and denominator by the same number, like turning ½ into 4/8 by multiplying both by 4. Finally, you learn about proper fractions (where numerator is smaller than denominator) and improper fractions (where numerator is bigger than denominator).

Proper, improper, mixed; equivalent; compare; add/subtract like and unlike; multiply a fraction by a whole number; fraction division (by whole number, intro).

## Decimals
In Grade 6, children learn about decimals as another way to write numbers, just like rupees and paise. They understand that a decimal point separates the whole number part from the fractional part, such as ₹5.75 meaning five rupees and seventy-five paise. Students practise reading, writing, and comparing decimals, using place value charts to see which number is bigger or smaller. They learn to add and subtract decimals carefully, lining up the decimal points just like adding whole numbers. For example, 4.25 + 2.10 is done by adding the whole numbers and then the decimal parts separately.

Tenths, hundredths, thousandths; compare, order; add/subtract; multiply by 10, 100; decimals in money, length, weight.

## Data handling
In Class 6, students learn to collect data through simple methods like surveys and tally marks. They organise this data using pictographs and bar graphs, where each picture or bar stands for a fixed number of items. Children learn to read and interpret these graphs to answer questions about the information, such as finding out which item has the most votes. They also practice recording data in a frequency table to count how many times each item appears. This helps them understand how information is collected, sorted, and shared using pictures and numbers.

Tally, pictograph, bar graph; organise data; sectors of a circle graph (reading); mean as "equal share" (intro).

## Mensuration
In Class 6 Mensuration, children learn to find the area and perimeter of simple shapes like squares, rectangles, and triangles. They use formulas such as perimeter = 2 × (length + breadth) for rectangles and area = length × breadth. For squares, they calculate perimeter = 4 × side and area = side × side. They also learn to measure the area of triangles using the formula ½ × base × height. Real-life examples include finding the boundary length of a playground (perimeter) or the space inside a book cover (area). These concepts help them solve everyday problems using measurement and calculation.

Perimeter of rectilinear figures; area of rectangles, squares; area by counting squares; simple composite rectilinear areas.

## Algebra: the language of unknowns
In Class 6, we learn to use letters like a, b, c to stand for unknown numbers in Maths, which is called Algebra. We write simple expressions such as "3 more than a number" as `x + 3` or "5 times a number" as `5y`. We also learn to find the missing number in simple equations like `x + 4 = 10` by guessing and checking. This helps us solve problems where some information is not known. The rules of addition and subtraction stay the same, even when numbers are replaced by letters.

Variable idea with matchstick patterns; expressions; terms, coefficients; add/subtract like terms; simple equations by trial and balance (one operation).

## Ratio and proportion
In this topic, children learn about ratios, which compare two quantities using the same units, like saying "for every 2 apples, there are 3 oranges." They understand ratios can be written as 2:3 or 2/3, showing the relation between parts. Proportion is introduced as the equality of two ratios, such as 2:3 = 4:6, meaning the relation between quantities stays the same. Children solve simple proportion problems to find missing values, like determining how many pens cost ₹40 if 5 pens cost ₹100. They also learn to identify and use the unitary method to find the value of one unit before solving bigger problems.

Ratio as comparison (3:2); simplify; equivalent ratios; proportion as equal ratios; unitary method with direct examples.
