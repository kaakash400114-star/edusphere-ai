# CBSE Class 1 Mathematics - NCERT (Math-Magic 1)

## Numbers 1 to 9 and 0
Children learn to write numerals from 1 to 9 and recognise the number zero, which stands for "nothing" or "no objects". They count objects like pencils, erasers, and marbles up to nine and match them with the correct numerals. The concept of "more" and "less" is introduced using groups of objects, such as having three sweets versus five sweets. They also learn the number names one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, and zero. Simple addition of numbers within nine is taught using picture aids, like adding two apples and one apple to get three apples.

Children count objects one by one (0-9), match number names to numerals, and compare "more or less". NCERT method: count real things - pencils, stones, buttons - before written numerals. Zero means "nothing left". Example: 5 candles on a cake, blow one out, 4 remain.

## Addition up to 20
In Class 1, children learn to add numbers up to 20 using simple counting and grouping methods. They understand that addition means putting things together, like 5 apples and 3 apples make 8 apples in total. They use number lines and finger counting to solve sums such as 7 + 4 = 11 or 9 + 6 = 15. Children also practice writing addition sums in the vertical format, starting with the ones place. Through activities like counting objects or using number grids, they build a strong foundation for basic arithmetic.

Adding means putting groups together. NCERT method: count on from the bigger number. Example: 7 + 3 → start at 7, count 8, 9, 10. Story: 4 birds on a branch, 2 more come → 6 birds.

## Subtraction up to 20
In Grade 1 Mathematics, children learn to subtract numbers up to 20 using objects, fingers, or pictures. They understand that subtraction means 'taking away' or 'finding the difference' between two quantities. For example, if you have 8 apples and eat 3, you are left with 5, which is written as 8 - 3 = 5. They practice writing subtraction sums in the format 'minuend - subtrahend = difference', such as 10 - 4 = 6. Children also learn to solve simple word problems involving everyday situations like sharing or losing items.

Taking away, or "how many more". Example: 9 mangoes, eat 4 → 5 left. NCERT method: cross out objects in a picture, then count what remains.

## Numbers from 10 to 20
Children learn to identify and write numbers from 10 to 20, understanding that each number is made of tens and ones, like 10 is one ten and 0 ones. They practice counting forward and backward from 10 to 20, using objects like marbles or pencils to show quantities. Children compare numbers to find which is bigger or smaller, such as 15 is more than 12, and learn to arrange them in order. They also learn the number names in words, matching them with the numerals, like writing “thirteen” for 13. Through simple activities, they understand that numbers can be grouped into tens and ones for easy counting.

A ten and some ones: 13 = 10 + 3. Teen-number place value begins here. Example: a bundle of 10 sticks plus 6 loose sticks = 16.

## Shapes around us (plane shapes)
In this topic, children learn to identify and name basic plane shapes like circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles. They observe these shapes in their surroundings, such as a round clock or a square book. Students understand that shapes differ in their number of sides and corners, like a triangle having three sides and three corners. They also learn to distinguish between straight and curved lines, identifying edges of shapes accordingly. Through examples like a triangular traffic sign or a rectangular door, children recognize shapes in everyday objects and their simple properties.

Circle, triangle, square, rectangle - found in balls, rotis, windows, kites. Sides and corners: a triangle has 3 sides, a square has 4 equal sides.

## Sorting, patterns and data
In Class 1, children learn to sort objects based on one simple attribute, such as colour, size or shape, for example grouping all red crayons together. They also identify and extend simple patterns using shapes, colours or objects, like continuing a sequence of circle, square, circle, square. Data handling involves organising objects into groups and counting how many are in each group, such as counting the number of boys and girls in the class. These activities help children develop early observation and logical thinking skills by recognising similarities and differences. The focus is on practical, hands-on experiences to build a strong foundation for understanding mathematical concepts.

Group objects by colour, size, shape; continue simple patterns (red-blue-red-blue → next is red). This is the NCERT foundation for data handling.

## Measurement: long-short, heavy-light
In Grade 1 Mathematics, children learn to compare the lengths of objects using words like ‘long’ and ‘short’, such as identifying a pencil as long and an eraser as short. They also compare weights using ‘heavy’ and ‘light’, for example, a school bag is heavy while a feather is light. Children are taught to make these comparisons by direct observation, without using any measuring tools or numbers. The focus is on understanding the basic concepts of size and weight through everyday objects like books, water bottles, and toys. These activities help them develop the ability to describe and categorise objects based on their physical attributes in simple terms.

Direct comparison before units: the pencil is longer than the crayon; the elephant is heavier than the dog. Vocabulary: longest/shortest, heaviest/lightest, thick-thin, near-far.

## Money: coins and notes (rupees)
In Class 1, children learn about Indian currency by identifying common coins and notes of rupees, such as 1 rupee, 2 rupees, 5 rupees, 10 rupees, and 20 rupees. They understand that money is used to buy things and practise counting small amounts of coins and notes to find the total value. For example, they can add two 5-rupee coins to make 10 rupees or count one 10-rupee note and one 2-rupee coin to make 12 rupees. The lesson helps them recognise the different shapes and colours of money and understand that each coin or note has a specific value. Simple word problems are introduced to apply counting skills in real-life situations, like buying a pencil for 5 rupees.

Recognise ₹1, ₹2, ₹5, ₹10 coins/notes; a ₹10 note buys more than a ₹2 coin. Simple shop-play: one toy costs ₹5, give ₹10, how much back?

## Time: day, night, earlier-later
In Grade 1, children learn to identify parts of the day, such as morning, afternoon, evening, night, and day. They understand that the day is for being active and the night is for sleeping. Students learn to compare events using words like earlier and later, such as identifying which activity happens before or after another. For example, brushing teeth happens earlier than eating breakfast, and going to school happens later than waking up. They also understand the sequence of daily activities, like waking up, eating, playing, and sleeping.

Daily-routine ordering: waking, school, play, sleep. Days of the week in order. Reading the hour hand only (o'clock times).

## Numbers 21 to 99 and counting in tens
Children learn to recognise, read, and write numbers from 21 to 99, understanding their place value as tens and ones. For example, the number 25 is made of 2 tens and 5 ones, and 30 is made of 3 tens. They count forward and backward in tens from 10 to 100, like 10, 20, 30, up to 100, and also count groups of objects in tens. The topic teaches how to form two-digit numbers by combining tens and ones, such as 4 tens and 3 ones making 43. Children also compare numbers using the words 'more than', 'less than', and 'equal to', like 45 is more than 40.

Two tens and three ones = 23; skip counting in tens (10, 20, 30 ... 90). NCERT uses bundles of ten sticks as the picture of tens.
