# CBSE Class 1 Mathematics - NCERT (Math-Magic 1)

## Numbers 1 to 9 and 0
Children count objects one by one (0-9), match number names to numerals, and compare "more or less". NCERT method: count real things - pencils, stones, buttons - before written numerals. Zero means "nothing left". Example: 5 candles on a cake, blow one out, 4 remain.

## Addition up to 20
Adding means putting groups together. NCERT method: count on from the bigger number. Example: 7 + 3 → start at 7, count 8, 9, 10. Story: 4 birds on a branch, 2 more come → 6 birds.

## Subtraction up to 20
Taking away, or "how many more". Example: 9 mangoes, eat 4 → 5 left. NCERT method: cross out objects in a picture, then count what remains.

## Numbers from 10 to 20
A ten and some ones: 13 = 10 + 3. Teen-number place value begins here. Example: a bundle of 10 sticks plus 6 loose sticks = 16.

## Shapes around us (plane shapes)
Circle, triangle, square, rectangle - found in balls, rotis, windows, kites. Sides and corners: a triangle has 3 sides, a square has 4 equal sides.

## Sorting, patterns and data
Group objects by colour, size, shape; continue simple patterns (red-blue-red-blue → next is red). This is the NCERT foundation for data handling.

## Measurement: long-short, heavy-light
Direct comparison before units: the pencil is longer than the crayon; the elephant is heavier than the dog. Vocabulary: longest/shortest, heaviest/lightest, thick-thin, near-far.

## Money: coins and notes (rupees)
Recognise ₹1, ₹2, ₹5, ₹10 coins/notes; a ₹10 note buys more than a ₹2 coin. Simple shop-play: one toy costs ₹5, give ₹10, how much back?

## Time: day, night, earlier-later
Daily-routine ordering: waking, school, play, sleep. Days of the week in order. Reading the hour hand only (o'clock times).

## Numbers 21 to 99 and counting in tens
Two tens and three ones = 23; skip counting in tens (10, 20, 30 ... 90). NCERT uses bundles of ten sticks as the picture of tens.
