# CBSE Class 9 English - Language & Literature approach

## Reading: unseen passages (factual, literary, discursive)
Students learn to read and understand unseen passages, which can be factual, literary, or discursive. For factual passages, they identify the main idea and supporting details, such as finding the key fact about a festival in a news report. Literary passages help them understand characters, settings, and simple themes, like describing a character’s feelings in a short story. Discursive passages teach them to identify the writer’s opinion and arguments, for example, spotting whether a passage is about saving trees for or against. They also learn to find meanings of new words from the context, like guessing "brave" from a sentence where someone helps others.

Skim-scan strategies; MCQ + short answers; vocabulary in context; 20-mark practice format.

## Writing: descriptive paragraph, diary, story
Students learn to write descriptive paragraphs using sensory details like sights, sounds, and smells to make a scene come alive. They write diary entries by keeping a record of personal experiences, using first-person voice and including the date. For story writing, they learn to create short narratives with a clear beginning, middle, and end, and include characters, a setting, and a simple problem that gets solved. Students practice using vivid words and arranging their ideas in a logical order to make their writing interesting and easy to understand.

Person-place description with sensory detail; diary tone honesty; story with given beginnings/endings, 150-200 words.

## Grammar: tenses in depth
In this topic, students learn to use different tenses correctly by understanding the structure of each tense. The simple present tense is formed with the subject and base verb (e.g., "She reads books"), while the present continuous tense uses the subject, is/am/are, and the verb with -ing (e.g., "They are playing cricket"). The simple past tense is made by adding -ed to regular verbs (e.g., "I walked to school") or using the third form for irregular verbs (e.g., "She ate an apple"). Students also study the past continuous tense, which combines the subject, was/were, and the verb with -ing (e.g., "The rain was falling heavily"). Mastery of these tenses helps students describe actions accurately in time.

All 12 tenses with signal words; errors in tense consistency across a passage; editing tasks.

## Grammar: clauses and sentence transformation
In Class 9, you will learn to identify and use different kinds of clauses in sentences. A main clause can stand alone as a full sentence, while a subordinate clause depends on the main clause for meaning. You will study three types of subordinate clauses: noun clauses (which act like nouns, e.g., *I know what you did*), adverb clauses (which tell us how, when, or why, e.g., *He left because he was tired*), and adjective clauses (which describe nouns, e.g., *The book that I read was interesting*). You will also practise changing sentence structures, like turning two simple sentences into one complex sentence using a conjunction or a subordinate clause to make your writing more varied and clear.

Noun-adjective-adverb clauses; transform simple↔complex↔compound; do-as-directed items.

## Grammar: reported speech, voice, determiners, modals
In this topic, children learn to change direct speech into reported speech by adjusting tense, pronouns, and removing quotation marks, for example, "He said, 'I am happy'" becomes "He said that he was happy". They understand active voice where the subject does the action ("Ravi writes a letter") and passive voice where the subject receives the action ("A letter is written by Ravi"). Students also study determiners like 'a', 'an', 'the', and 'this' that introduce nouns. Finally, they explore modals like 'can', 'should', and 'must' to show possibility, advice, or necessity.

Board-exam style transformations; gap-filling format; error-correction format.

## Literature: theme-character-plot devices
Students learn to find the main idea, or theme, of a story, like honesty in a tale about a truthful child. They identify the people, or characters, in the story and understand how their actions and feelings move the story forward. The plot is the sequence of events, like a problem and how it is solved. Students also see how writers use special tools, such as dialogue and suspense, to make the story interesting and clear. For example, a sudden storm can create excitement and lead to the story's important moment.

Beehive/Moments texts: infer traits with evidence; irony, humour, twist; title justification answers.

## Poetry comprehension
In this topic, children learn to understand the meaning and feelings in poems by reading them carefully. They identify different types of poems, like rhyming verses, free verse, and haikus, and see how the poet uses words to create pictures or sounds. Children also find poetic devices such as similes (comparing things using 'like' or 'as') and metaphors (direct comparisons), for example, "as brave as a lion" or "time is a thief." They practice reading aloud to notice rhythm and mood, and share their thoughts about what the poem makes them see or feel.

Central idea, imagery, rhyme scheme, poetic devices (simile, metaphor, personification, repetition); write a short appreciation.

## Vocabulary building
In Class 9, students learn to identify and understand new words from their reading texts by using context clues and word roots, prefixes, and suffixes. They explore how affixes like ‘un-’, ‘re-’, and ‘-tion’ change a word’s meaning, such as turning ‘happy’ into ‘unhappy’ or ‘act’ into ‘action’. Learners also study synonyms and antonyms to expand their expression, understanding that ‘large’ is similar to ‘big’ but opposite to ‘small’. They are taught to use a dictionary to find word meanings and pronunciations, and to keep a personal word bank to note down and revise new vocabulary regularly. This helps them build a strong vocabulary for better reading comprehension and writing skills.

Synonyms-antonyms in context; one-word substitutions; collocations; phrasal verbs for exams.

## Speaking-listening skills
Students learn to listen carefully to others and speak clearly in group discussions. They follow rules like raising their hand before speaking and listening without interrupting. Children practice introducing themselves and sharing their ideas using simple, complete sentences. They also learn to understand stories and instructions spoken aloud by their teacher and classmates. For example, they can retell a short story in their own words after listening to it once.

Pronunciation: word stress, intonation in questions; formal vs informal register; 1-minute talk structure.

## Exam writing formats
In Class 9, students learn about different formats for writing exams like essays, letters, and reports. For essays, they understand the introduction-body-conclusion structure to share their ideas clearly. Letter writing rules include knowing the formal parts like the sender's address, date, and receiver's address, and using proper salutations like "Dear Sir/Madam" or "Yours sincerely". Reports follow a specific format with headings and subheadings to present information in a neat, organised way. Students also practise writing paragraphs with a main idea and supporting details to make their answers strong and easy to read.

Letter to the editor; analytical paragraph (data interpretation) preview; notice and email formats as per CBSE pattern.
