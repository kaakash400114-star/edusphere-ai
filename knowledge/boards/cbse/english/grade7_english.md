# CBSE Class 7 English - NCERT (Honeycomb) approach

## Tense mastery: perfect and perfect continuous
In Class 7, we learn to use perfect tenses to show actions that are complete or have an effect now. The present perfect tense is formed with have/has + past participle, like "I have finished my homework" (work is done). The past perfect tense uses had + past participle, such as "She had eaten lunch before the bell rang" (eating was finished before another past action). We also study the perfect continuous tenses to describe actions that started in the past and continue up to the present or another time. The present perfect continuous tense is made with have/has + been + present participle, for example, "They have been playing cricket since morning" (playing started in the past and continues now). The past perfect continuous tense uses had + been + present participle, like "The train had been waiting for an hour before it arrived" (waiting was ongoing before another past event).

Present/past perfect with since-for-already-yet; continuous perfects; timeline reading of tenses.

## Modals: shall-should, will-would, can-could, may-might, must
In Class 7, students learn about modals like shall, should, will, would, can, could, may, might, and must. These special helping words tell us about possibility, permission, obligation, or future actions. For example, "must" shows something is very necessary, like "You must finish your homework," while "can" shows ability, like "I can swim." We also learn that "could" is a softer way of saying "can," as in "Could you help me?" and "might" shows less certainty than "may," like "It might rain today." Students practice using these words correctly in sentences to express different ideas clearly.

Obligation, advice, possibility, permission; polite forms; short answers.

## Voice: active-passive across tenses
In Class 7, we learn about active and passive voice in sentences. Active voice means the subject does the action, like "Rani reads a book." Passive voice means the subject receives the action, like "A book is read by Rani." We change active sentences to passive by making the object the subject, using the correct tense of 'be' + past participle, and adding 'by' + the doer. For example, "The boy kicks the ball" becomes "The ball is kicked by the boy." We practice this for present, past, and future tenses to see how the voice changes the focus of a sentence.

Simple present/past/future and continuous passives; agent with by; when passive is better.

## Reported speech: statements, questions, commands
In Class 7, students learn to change direct speech into reported speech by adjusting verbs and pronouns. Statements like "I am happy" become "He said that he was happy". Questions change to statements with if or whether; "Are you coming?" becomes "He asked if I was coming". Commands turn into requests or orders using "to" or "not to"; "Close the door" becomes "He told me to close the door". Tenses shift back one step, and time expressions like "now" change to "then". This helps in sharing what others said accurately.

Say-tell-ask; tense, pronoun, place-time shifts; reporting questions (if/whether; wh-word) and orders (to + verb).

## Clauses: noun, adjective, adverb
In Class 7, we learn about different kinds of clauses. A clause has a subject and a verb. A noun clause acts like a noun, for example, "I know where you live". An adjective clause describes a noun, like "The book that I read was interesting". An adverb clause tells us more about a verb, such as "He arrived when the bell rang". We learn to spot these clauses in sentences and understand how they make our language clear.

"I believe (that) he is right." "The book (which) you gave..." "She ran (because she was late)." Identify and use.

## Determiners and quantifiers
In Grade 7 English, you learn about determiners and quantifiers, which are small words that come before a noun to tell us more about it. Determiners like 'the', 'a', and 'an' specify if we are talking about one specific thing or any one thing. Quantifiers, such as 'some', 'many', and 'few', tell us the quantity of a noun, like if we have a little or a lot. For example, 'the dog' is a specific animal, while 'many dogs' means there are several of them. You also learn to use 'this', 'that', 'these', and 'those' to point out nouns that are near or far from you.

a-an-the rules; some-any, much-many, few-little, each-every, either-neither; articles with special nouns (the sun, an hour).

## Prepositions: fine distinctions
In Class 7, we learn about prepositions like on, in, and to, and notice how their use changes with different objects. The preposition "in" is used for spaces inside something, like "in the box," while "on" is for surfaces, like "on the table." We also learn about prepositions of time, such as "at" for exact times ("at 5 o'clock"), "on" for days ("on Monday"), and "in" for months ("in July"). To choose the right preposition, we must look at the word that comes after it. For example, we say "listen to music" because "music" comes after "to.

in-into, on-onto, at-in for places, since-for, between-among; preposition + gerund (good at swimming).

## Editing and error correction
In Class 7, students learn to edit sentences and paragraphs by spotting and correcting errors in grammar, punctuation, and spelling. They practice identifying and fixing common mistakes like subject-verb agreement (e.g., "The dogs barks" becomes "The dogs bark"), capitalisation errors (e.g., "delhi" becomes "Delhi"), and incorrect word usage (e.g., "their" instead of "there"). Children are also taught to use full stops, commas, and question marks correctly to make their writing clear and easy to understand. For example, they learn to correct "the cat is sleeping on the mat" by adding a full stop to make it two sentences. Through these exercises, students improve their writing skills and attention to detail.

Subject-verb agreement, tense slips, spelling traps; punctuate a passage; omission exercises.

## Comprehension: poetry and prose
In Class 7, students learn to understand and appreciate both poetry and prose by identifying key elements like rhyme, rhythm, and mood in poems, and characters, settings, and main ideas in stories. They practise answering questions based on the text, finding information directly stated and making simple inferences. For example, they can point out the rhyming words in a poem or describe the main character's feelings from a prose passage. Students also learn to use clues from the text to support their answers, such as explaining why a character might be happy using details from the story. This helps them become better readers who can understand what they read clearly.

Theme, tone, imagery in poems; character sketch in prose; infer motive; justify with lines.

## Writing: story, dialogue, descriptive, informal letter
Students learn to write short stories with a clear beginning, middle, and end, using characters and a simple plot, like a story about a lost puppy finding its way home. They practice writing dialogues using quotation marks to show what characters say, such as “Where have you been?” asked mother. For descriptive writing, children use their senses to paint a picture with words, for example, describing a hot summer day by writing, “The sun was shining brightly and the birds were chirping loudly.” Informal letters are written to family or friends with a friendly tone, including a greeting, main message, and closing, like writing to a cousin about a birthday party they enjoyed.

Plot mountain (beginning-conflict-climax-resolution); dialogue punctuation; describe a place with senses; letter to a cousin.
