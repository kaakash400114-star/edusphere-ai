# CBSE Class 1 English - NCERT (Marigold 1) approach

## Alphabet and phonics A-Z
In Grade 1, children learn to recognise and write all uppercase (A-Z) and lowercase (a-z) letters of the English alphabet correctly. They learn to match each letter with its corresponding sound, including both short vowel sounds (as in 'a' for apple) and consonant sounds (as in 'b' for ball). Students begin to blend these sounds to read simple two and three-letter words, such as 'cat', 'sun', and 'dog'. They also practise identifying the starting sound in spoken words, like finding the 'm' sound in 'milk'. This foundational skill helps them build a strong base for reading and writing.

Letter names and sounds (a as in apple, z as in zebra). Capital and small letters. Vowels a, e, i, o, u; the rest are consonants. Rhyming songs build sound awareness.

## Naming words (naming myself, things)
In this topic, children learn to identify and use naming words, which are called nouns. They understand that nouns name people, like their own name and their family members, such as mother, father, and sister. Children also learn to name things they see around them, like chair, table, ball, and book. The teacher helps them recognise nouns in simple sentences, such as "Rita has a cat" where Rita and cat are naming words. By the end, children can point out nouns in their classroom and use them to talk about themselves and the objects they know.

Naming words = nouns in child language: Amma, school, ball, mango. Point-and-name practice around home and class.

## One and many (singular-plural)
Students learn to change singular words, which name one thing, to plural words, which name many things. They add 's' to most words, like turning 'cat' into 'cats' or 'ball' into 'balls'. For words ending with 's', 'sh', 'ch', 'x', or 'z', they add 'es', making words like 'bus' into 'buses' and 'box' into 'boxes'. Some special words change their spelling, like 'foot' becoming 'feet' and 'tooth' becoming 'teeth', while others stay the same, like 'sheep' and 'deer'. This helps children talk about one object or many objects correctly.

One cat, two cats; one mango, many mangoes. Add -s for most plurals. Irregular pairs appear later.

## A and an
We learn to use 'a' and 'an' before a noun in a sentence. We use 'a' before words that begin with a consonant sound, like a cat, a dog, a book, and a pen. We use 'an' before words that begin with a vowel sound, like an apple, an egg, an umbrella, and an orange. The 'an' helps us say the next word more easily when it starts with a vowel sound. We always use 'a' or 'an' with singular nouns and before a noun that is not specific.

'a' before a sounding consonant: a ball, a cat. 'an' before a vowel sound: an apple, an elephant, an umbrella.

## These are / those are
Children learn to use "these are" for things near them and "those are" for things far away. They understand that "these" is plural and points to objects close by, like "These are pencils." They also learn that "those" is plural and points to objects far away, such as "Those are balloons." The lesson helps them distinguish between near and far objects using simple sentences. They practice with everyday items to see the difference. This helps them speak and write clearly about things around them.

This/these for near, that/those for far. "This is my book." "Those are birds."

## Simple sentences and capital letter, full stop
In Grade 1, we learn to write simple sentences. A simple sentence has one idea, like "The cat is big." We start a sentence with a capital letter. We also use a full stop at the end of a sentence. The capital letter comes only at the beginning. The full stop tells us when the sentence ends. We do not use a capital letter in the middle of a sentence. We write each word carefully. We make sure every sentence has a capital letter and a full stop. This helps us write clearly and correctly.

A sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with a full stop. "I am Ria. I am six years old."

## Action words (verbs in use)
In Grade 1, we learn about action words, which are also called verbs. Action words tell us what a person, animal, or thing is doing. For example, in the sentence "Ravi runs fast," the word "runs" is an action word because it tells us what Ravi is doing. Other action words we learn are jump, sing, eat, and read. We can find action words in sentences to understand what is happening. We also learn to make sentences using our own action words like, "The bird flies" or "The dog barks". Action words make our sentences more interesting.

Run, jump, eat, sing, sleep, read. Use in sentences: "Birds fly." "Fish swim."

## Rhymes and poems
In Class 1, children learn to recognise and enjoy rhymes and simple poems through repetition and rhythm. They identify rhyming words at the end of lines, such as 'cat' and 'mat' in a poem like 'The Fat Cat'. Students listen to recitations and clap along to understand beats and patterns in verses. They also memorise short, fun rhymes and recite them with expression, building confidence in speaking. The focus is on playful exposure to language sounds and structures, fostering an early love for poetry.

Recite with rhythm; identify rhyming pairs (cat-hat, sun-fun). Builds listening and pronunciation.

## Opposites (basic)
Children learn to identify and understand the meaning of basic opposite words like 'big' and 'small', 'tall' and 'short', 'happy' and 'sad'. They learn to use these words to describe things they see around them, for example, a big ball and a small ball, or a tall tree and a short bush. The concept is to understand that opposite words show differences in size, height, feelings, or other simple qualities. Children practice matching words to their correct opposites and using them in simple sentences. This helps them build a strong foundation for describing the world accurately.

big-small, hot-cold, day-night, up-down, happy-sad, in-out. Match-and-say practice.

## Picture reading and describing
Children learn to identify and name objects, people, and animals in pictures. They describe what they see using simple words like "big," "small," "happy," or "sad." They also point out the colours and count the number of things in the picture. For example, they can say, "The cat is big and black." They learn to tell what is happening in the picture, like "The boy is running." Teachers help them use short sentences to describe the picture clearly.

Say three sentences about a picture: who, what, where. Speak in full sentences; listen to others.
