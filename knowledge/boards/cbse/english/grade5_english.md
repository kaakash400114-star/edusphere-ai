# CBSE Class 5 English - NCERT (Marigold 5) approach

## Nouns: abstract and material
In Class 5, children learn to identify two types of nouns: abstract and material. Abstract nouns are names of ideas, feelings, or qualities that cannot be seen or touched, like happiness, courage, or honesty. Material nouns are names of things that can be seen or touched, as they are made of a material, such as gold, wood, cotton, or water. For example, "love" is an abstract noun, while "gold" is a material noun. Children practice distinguishing between these nouns in sentences to understand the different ways we name things in our world.

Honesty, childhood (abstract); gold, water (material); countable vs uncountable revision; collective nouns in context.

## Pronouns: relative and interrogative
In Class 5, we learn about relative pronouns like who, which, and that, which join a sentence to give more details about a person or thing, as in "The boy who won the race is my friend." We also study interrogative pronouns such as who, what, where, and when, which are used to ask questions, like "What is your favourite game?" These pronouns help us make our sentences clearer and more interesting. Relative pronouns connect two sentences, while interrogative pronouns help us get information. We practice using them in sentences to improve our speaking and writing skills.

who, which, that joining clauses: "The boy who won is my friend."; what, who, whose in questions.

## Adjectives and adverbs: position and order
In Class 5, we learn how to place adjectives and adverbs correctly in sentences to describe words better. Adjectives come before nouns to tell us about a person, place, or thing, like a "red dress" or a "big book". Adverbs usually come after verbs to describe how an action is done, such as "run fast" or "sing loudly". We also learn about the order of adjectives when we have more than one, like using "a beautiful, new toy" where "beautiful" comes before "new". These rules help us make our sentences clear and interesting.

Adjective order (size-age-colour); adverbs modifying adjectives ("very tall"); enough placement.

## Verbs: perfect tenses (introduction)
In Grade 5, we learn about perfect tenses to talk about actions that are finished or have a connection to the present. We form the present perfect tense by using the verb 'have' or 'has' with the third form of the verb (past participle). For example, "She has finished her homework" means the action is done, but the time is not specified. Similarly, the past perfect tense uses 'had' with the third form of the verb to show that an action was completed before another past action. For instance, "He had eaten his lunch before the bell rang" tells us the eating happened first. These tenses help us sequence events clearly in our sentences.

has/have + done: completed action with present relevance; signal words (just, already, yet, since, for).

## Modals: can, could, may, might, must
In Class 5, we learn about special helper words called modals: can, could, may, might, and must. We use 'can' to show we are able to do something, like "I can swim." 'Could' is for polite requests or past ability, for example, "Could I borrow your book?" 'May' is used to ask for permission, "May I go to the washroom?" 'Might' shows a small chance of something happening, "It might rain today." Lastly, 'must' is for something very important or necessary, such as "You must always look at the road.

Ability, permission, possibility, obligation in polite speech and writing.

## Voice: active to passive (introduction)
In Class 5, we learn about changing sentences from active voice to passive voice. The active voice shows who does the action, like “Ravi kicks the ball,” while the passive voice shows what happens to the object, like “The ball is kicked by Ravi.” To make passive sentences, we use the helping verb ‘is’, ‘am’, or ‘are’ with the past form of the verb, like “The cat drinks milk” becomes “Milk is drunk by the cat.” This helps us focus on the thing being acted upon. We practice making simple sentences using this rule.

"Ravi eats a mango" → "A mango is eaten by Ravi."; recognise, transform simple sentences.

## Reported speech (statements)
Reported speech tells us what someone else said without using their exact words. We change the verb of the reporting speech to the past tense, like said or told. If the reporting verb is in the past tense, the pronouns and time expressions also change; for example, 'I' becomes 'he' or 'she', and 'now' becomes 'then'. The word 'that' is often used to introduce the reported statement, like 'Ravi said that he was happy'. We do not use quotation marks in reported speech.

said to → told; tense shifts one step back; pronoun and time-word changes (now→then).

## Punctuation and paragraphing
In Class 5, you learn to use full stops, question marks, and commas to make your sentences clear and easy to read. You will also put capital letters at the start of sentences and for names of people, places, and days. Paragraphs help you group your ideas; start a new paragraph when you write about a new thing or change who you are talking about. For example, after writing about what you eat for breakfast, you can start a new paragraph to write about what you do in school. This makes your writing organised and neat for others to read.

Commas for clauses; colon for lists; paragraph unity: one idea per paragraph with linking words (first, then, finally).

## Comprehension: fact vs opinion, inference
In Class 5, you will learn to tell the difference between facts and opinions. A fact is something true that you can check, like “Mumbai has 20 million people,” while an opinion is what someone thinks, like “Mumbai is the best city.” You will also practice making inferences, which are smart guesses using clues from the story. For example, if someone shivers in a story, you can infer they are feeling cold. These skills help you read stories more carefully and understand what the author wants to say.

Find facts, spot opinions, infer feelings from actions; vocabulary in context.

## Composition: letter (formal), diary entry, story completion
In Class 5, you learn to write a formal letter using a proper salutation like "Dear Sir/Ma'am" and a polite closing such as "Yours sincerely". For a diary entry, you write about your personal day using simple past tense sentences and date the page, like "Monday, 5 August 2024". When completing a story, you use your imagination to add a good ending that matches the beginning, such as helping a lost puppy find its way home. These activities teach you how to share information, feelings, and ideas in different formats.

Formal letter format (sender, date, salutation, subject); diary tone; complete a story with a twist and moral.
