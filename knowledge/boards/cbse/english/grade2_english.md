# CBSE Class 2 English - NCERT (Marigold 2) approach

## Naming words: common and special (proper) nouns
A naming word is a noun that names a person, place, animal, or thing. We learn that there are two kinds of nouns: common nouns and proper nouns. A common noun is a general name for something, like boy, school, cat, or book. A proper noun is the special name of a particular person, place, animal, or thing, and it always begins with a capital letter. For example, Ravi is a proper noun but boy is a common noun, and Mumbai is a proper noun but city is a common noun.

Common: boy, city, river. Special names start with capital letters: Ravi, Chennai, Ganga.

## Describing words (adjectives)
In Class 2, we learn about describing words called adjectives. Adjectives are words that tell us more about nouns, such as size, colour, shape, and how things look. For example, we say "big ball" instead of just "ball" and "red car" instead of just "car". We also learn to use words like "tall", "short", "happy", and "sad" to describe people and things. Adjectives make our sentences more interesting and clear. We practice using them in simple sentences to describe objects around us.

Big elephant, small ant, red rose, sweet mango. Use two describing words in one sentence.

## Action words and simple tenses
Children learn to identify action words, or verbs, which tell us what someone or something is doing. They understand that these words can show actions happening right now, like 'run', 'jump', or 'play', using the simple present tense. For actions that have already finished, they learn to use 'ed' words such as 'walked', 'climbed', and 'painted'. They also discover that 'will' is used to talk about actions that will happen in the future, like 'will eat' or 'will sleep'. Through these simple rules, they begin to build sentences that describe actions clearly.

Today I play; yesterday I played; tomorrow I will play. -ed ending and will.

## Am, is, are; has, have
In Class 2, children learn to use the verb forms "am", "is", and "are" to show who is doing an action. We use "am" with the word "I", like "I am happy". We use "is" with words like "he", "she", "it", and singular names, for example, "He is my friend". "Are" is used with "we", "you", "they", and plural names, such as "We are playing". They also learn about "has" and "have" to show who owns something. "Has" is used with "he", "she", "it", and singular names, like "She has a doll". "Have" is used with "I", "we", "you", and "they", such as "They have books".

He is, they are; she has, we have. Agreement practice in speech and writing.

## Prepositions: in, on, under, near, between
Children learn to use prepositions like 'in', 'on', 'under', 'near', and 'between' to describe the position of objects. 'In' shows something is inside another thing, like a ball in a box. 'On' means an object is on top of something else, such as a book on a table. 'Under' describes when something is below another thing, for example, a cat under a chair. 'Near' tells us when an object is close to another, like a pencil near the notebook. 'Between' shows an object is in the middle of two things, like a toy between two friends.

The cat is under the table; the ball is between two boxes. Position words in pictures.

## Plurals with -es and irregular pairs
In Class 2, children learn to change words from singular to plural by adding "-es" to words that end with sounds like s, x, z, ch, and sh. For example, "bus" becomes "buses" and "box" becomes "boxes". They also study special pairs of words that do not follow this rule, such as "child" and "children" or "foot" and "feet". These words change in different ways when we talk about more than one. By practising these rules, children can correctly name many objects in groups.

Boxes, mangoes, buses; man-men, child-children, tooth-teeth.

## Articles a, an, the
In Class 2, we learn to use small words like a, an, and the before nouns. The word a is used before words that start with consonant sounds, such as a book or a cat. The word an is used before words that start with vowel sounds, such as an apple or an umbrella. The word the is used for specific things we already know, like the ball or the sun. These small words help us make our sentences clear and correct.

A dog barks; an owl hoots; the sun rises (special nouns take the).

## Punctuation: capital letters, full stop, question mark
We learn to use a capital letter at the start of a sentence. For example, the first letter of 'The' and 'Mohan' are written in capital letters. We also learn to use a full stop at the end of a sentence, like in 'Ritu is playing.' A question mark is used at the end of a question, such as 'Where are you going?' These marks help us to write and read sentences correctly. We also use capital letters for the names of people and places. Mastering these punctuation marks helps in writing clear and correct sentences.

Names, sentence starts; ask questions with ? - "Where is your school?"

## Reading comprehension (short passage)
Children learn to read short passages and understand what they have read. They will answer simple questions about the main idea and details from the story. They also identify the beginning, middle, and end of a story. For example, after reading about a boy helping his mother, they can say what the boy did and how the story ended. They practice finding answers directly in the text using pictures and words. This helps them become better readers and understand stories clearly.

Read a 6-8 line story; answer who, what, where questions in full sentences.

## Simple composition: my family, my pet, my school
In Class 2, students learn to write simple compositions about their family, pet, or school using short sentences. They write three to four sentences to describe one topic at a time, like naming family members or their favourite pet. They must begin each sentence with a capital letter and end with a full stop. They use simple words and phrases such as "My mother is a doctor" or "My dog plays with a ball." The focus is on clear, factual descriptions without joining sentences with 'and' or 'but'.

5-6 sentences with describing words and full stops; read aloud with clear pronunciation.
