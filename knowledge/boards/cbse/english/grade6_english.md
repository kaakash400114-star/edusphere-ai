# CBSE Class 6 English - NCERT (Honeysuckle) approach

## Nouns, number and gender review; case
In Class 6, we revise nouns and learn to classify them into singular and plural forms, like adding 's' or 'es' to make words plural such as 'cat' to 'cats'. We also identify the gender of nouns, like 'lion' (masculine) and 'lioness' (feminine), and learn about neuter gender for things like 'table'. Further, we study the three cases of nouns: nominative (subject), accusative (object), and genitive (possession), for example, 'Ravi (nom.) plays cricket (acc.) and the ball's (gen.) colour is red'. This helps us use nouns correctly in sentences.

Subject/object case; possessive with 's and of; countable-uncountable with much/many/few/little.

## Pronouns: personal, possessive, emphatic
In Class 6, children learn about pronouns which are words that take the place of nouns to make sentences better. Personal pronouns like I, we, you, he, she, it, and they are used in place of names or things. Possessive pronouns like my, your, his, her, its, our, and their show ownership or belonging to someone. Emphatic pronouns like myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, and yourselves add force to a sentence by showing the doer of the action clearly. For example, "I myself solved the sum" uses the emphatic pronoun 'myself' to stress that 'I' did the work. These pronouns help in making the language simple and clear to understand and use.

I/me/myself; avoid ambiguity: pronoun must clearly point to its noun.

## Adjectives: kinds and degrees
Students learn to use adjectives to describe nouns and understand their different kinds like descriptive and numeral. They also study the three degrees of comparison: positive, comparative, and superlative. For example, "tall" is positive, "taller" is comparative, and "tallest" is superlative. Rules include adding "er" for short adjectives and "more" for longer ones to form comparatives, and "est" or "most" for superlatives. Students practice using these to compare things, like "This book is thicker than that book" or "She is the tallest girl in the class".

Quality, quantity, number; demonstrative, interrogative; comparative-superlative formation and double-comparison errors.

## Verbs: all simple tenses + continuous
In class six, we learn about verbs in simple tenses like present, past, and future, and also about continuous tenses. We add words like 'will' before the verb for future tense and 'ed' at the end for past tense. For continuous tenses, we use the present form of the verb with 'am', 'is', or 'are' and add '-ing', like 'I am playing'. We also learn to use 'was' or 'were' with the verb and '-ing' for past continuous tense, like 'He was running'. This helps us talk about actions happening now, before, or later, and actions that are still going on.

Present/past/future simple and continuous; subject-verb agreement rules (each, everyone, either-or).

## Adverbs: formation and comparison
We learn about adverbs, which are words that describe verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs. We form adverbs from adjectives usually by adding 'ly', like 'quick' becomes 'quickly' or 'silent' becomes 'silently'. Some adverbs do not change their form, like 'today', 'very', and 'well'. We also learn to compare adverbs to show how much something is done using the words 'more' and 'most', for example, 'more quickly' and 'most quickly'. This helps us speak and write better sentences.

-ly rules (happy→happily); comparative adverbs (faster, more slowly); position and meaning.

## Prepositions and phrasal verbs (intro)
Students in Class 6 learn that prepositions are small words like ‘in’, ‘on’, and ‘under’ that show the relationship between a noun and other words in a sentence. They understand that a preposition usually comes before a noun or pronoun to indicate location, time, or direction, such as ‘The book is on the table’ or ‘We play in the park’. The topic also introduces phrasal verbs, which are a verb combined with a preposition or adverb to create a new meaning, like ‘look after’ meaning to take care of something or ‘give up’ meaning to stop trying. Through simple examples, children learn to use these words correctly to make their sentences clear and interesting. They practice identifying and using both prepositions and phrasal verbs in everyday contexts.

Place, time, movement prepositions; common phrasals: get up, look for, put off.

## Conjunctions: coordinating and subordinating
In Class 6, children learn about conjunctions, which are words that join sentences or words together. They learn about two main types: coordinating conjunctions (like *and*, *but*, *or*, *so*) join equal parts of sentences, such as "Rahul likes to play cricket and football." Subordinating conjunctions (like *because*, *if*, *when*, *since*) join a main part of a sentence to a smaller part that depends on it, for example, "We will go to the park if the weather is nice." Children also learn that using the right conjunction helps make sentences clear and interesting.

and-but-or-nor; because-although-unless-if-when; punctuation with clauses.

## Sentences, phrases and clauses (introduction)
In Class 6, students learn to recognise sentences, phrases, and clauses. A sentence is a group of words that makes complete sense and has a subject and a predicate, for example, "Ravi is playing football." A phrase is a group of words that does not have a subject or a predicate and makes incomplete sense, like "under the tree." A clause is a group of words that has a subject and a predicate but may not make complete sense on its own, such as "when the bell rings." They also learn about different types of clauses, like main and subordinate clauses.

Subject-predicate; main vs subordinate clause; simple, compound sentences.

## Punctuation: apostrophe, quotation, hyphen
In Class 6, we learn to use the apostrophe to show possession, like “Ria’s book” or “the children’s toys.” We also use quotation marks to show exactly what someone says, for example, “Please help me,” said the boy. A hyphen joins words together to form compound adjectives, like a “well-known” story or a “five-year-old” child. These marks help us write clearly and correctly. By using punctuation properly, our sentences become easy to read and understand.

Possession rules; dialogue punctuation; compound words (well-known).

## Comprehension and précis (summary)
In Class 6, students learn to read a passage carefully and find its main idea and important details. They practise writing short summaries, also called précis, by using their own words to keep only the most necessary information. A good summary must be short, clear, and complete, without adding any new ideas or missing key points. For example, if a passage is about a boy helping his grandmother, the précis will tell us he helped her, how he helped, and why it was nice. Students also learn to remove less important words and sentences while keeping the story's meaning safe.

Main idea, supporting details, summary in 4-5 lines; dictionary skills: syllables, stress, parts of speech labels.

## Writing: informal letter, application, story
In Class 6, children learn to write informal letters to friends and family, using simple words and a friendly tone, and they include the sender’s address, date, and a loving closing. They also practice writing applications to school authorities, like the principal, to request things, using a polite structure with a subject line and a formal closing. For writing stories, they learn to create short tales with a beginning, middle, and end, making sure to include interesting characters and a simple problem that gets solved. They use descriptive words to make their stories exciting and fun to read. These activities help children express their ideas clearly and follow proper writing rules.

Informal letter to a friend; formal application to the headmaster; picture story with dialogue; notice writing basics.
