# CBSE Class 4 English - NCERT (Marigold 4) approach

## Nouns: countable-uncountable, gender, number
In Class 4, we learn about nouns, which are naming words for people, animals, places, and things. Nouns can be countable, like "books" or "chairs" because we can count them, or uncountable, like "water" or "sugar" because we cannot count them separately. We also learn about gender nouns, where a male is called a "boy" and a female is a "girl", and a male animal is a "bull" while the female is a "cow". Finally, we study number, where nouns can be singular (one) like "pencil" or plural (many) like "pencils". Some plurals change their spelling, like "child" to "children".

Much/many with uncountables (water, chairs); masculine-feminine-neuter pairs; plural spellings (-ies, -ves: leaf→leaves).

## Pronouns: reflexive and possessive
In Class 4, students learn about reflexive pronouns like myself, yourself, and ourselves, which show that the subject and object of a sentence are the same person, as in "I hurt myself." They also study possessive pronouns such as mine, yours, and ours, which replace a noun to show ownership, like "This book is mine." The lesson teaches children to use these pronouns correctly to make their sentences clear and interesting. For example, they learn to say "The cat licked its paw" instead of repeating "The cat licked the cat’s paw." This helps them speak and write in a simple and correct way.

myself, yourself, himself, themselves; mine, yours, hers, theirs; possessive adjectives vs pronouns.

## Adjectives: order and comparison
In Class 4, we learn to place adjectives in the right order before a noun, like describing a "big, red ball" instead of "red, big ball". We also learn to compare things using adjectives, such as big, bigger, and biggest to show increasing size. Another rule is using good, better, and best to describe qualities in different degrees. For example, a "small ant" becomes "smaller than a bee". We practice these rules to make our sentences clear and interesting when we talk about people, animals, or things around us.

Comparatives/superlatives; longer words use more/most (beautiful-more beautiful); than with comparatives.

## Verbs: simple present habit vs present continuous action
In class 4, we learn about two types of verbs to talk about the present time. The simple present tense is used for habits and actions that happen always, like "I play football every day." We use the verb with 's', 'es', or 'ies' for he, she, it, such as "She goes to school." The present continuous tense is for actions happening right now, using "am", "is", or "are" with the verb ending in "-ing", like "He is eating lunch." We understand that the simple present shows repeated actions, while the present continuous shows actions happening at this moment. For example, "They read books" is a habit, but "They are reading books" means they are reading now.

Habits with do/does negatives-questions; -ing actions now; signal words (usually, now, today).

## Simple past vs past continuous
In Class 4, we learn about the simple past and past continuous tenses to talk about actions that happened before now. The simple past tense is used for actions that started and finished completely, like "She played football yesterday". We use the past continuous tense for actions that were happening for some time in the past, like "The children were eating mangoes at 4 o'clock". We make the past continuous tense with "was" or "were" and the "-ing" form of the verb, such as "They were walking to school". Sometimes we use both tenses together to show two actions happening at the same time, for example, "While it was raining, the boy was reading a book".

Was/were + -ing for background, simple past for events; when/while sentences.

## Future forms: will and going to (intro)
In Class 4, children learn to use “will” and “going to” to talk about the future. They use “will” for quick decisions and promises, like “I will help my mother” or “It will rain soon.” They use “going to” for plans that are already made, like “We are going to the park.” “Going to” also shows we see something happening, such as “The sky is dark, it is going to rain.” They understand that both words tell us about things that have not yet happened, but “going to” is for things we think about and “will” is for new ideas.

Predictions with will; plans with going to.

## Adverbs: time, place, manner, frequency
Children learn to recognise and use adverbs that tell us when, where, how, and how often something happens. Adverbs of time tell us about the time of an action, like "yesterday," "today," or "soon." Adverbs of place show where an action takes place, such as "here," "there," or "outside." Adverbs of manner describe how an action is done, for example, "quickly," "slowly," or "happily." Adverbs of frequency tell us how often an action occurs, like "always," "sometimes," or "never." They understand that these words help make sentences more clear and interesting by giving more details about the action.

Yesterday, here, bravely, always - position in sentence; frequency scale (always→never).

## Conjunctions: and, but, or, because, so
In Class 4, children learn to use conjunctions like 'and', 'but', 'or', 'because', and 'so' to join two short sentences into one longer sentence. The word 'and' is used to add similar ideas, for example, "She likes to play and sing." The word 'but' shows a contrast, like "He is tired but still studies." The word 'or' gives a choice, such as "Would you like tea or coffee?" The word 'because' tells the reason, for example, "She is happy because she got a gift." The word 'so' shows the result, like "It is raining, so we will stay at home.

Join words, sentences; show reason and result; punctuation with but/because.

## Punctuation: question mark, exclamation, quotation marks
In Class 4, we learn to use punctuation marks like question marks, exclamation marks, and quotation marks correctly. A question mark (?) is used at the end of a sentence that asks something, for example, "What is your name?". An exclamation mark (!) shows strong feeling or surprise, like "Wow! This is a big surprise!". Quotation marks (" ") are used around the exact words someone says, for instance, Ravi said, "I love playing cricket.". We practice using these marks in our writing to make our sentences clear and interesting.

Direct speech with speaker tags; commas in direct address; exclamation for strong feeling.

## Comprehension, dictionary use and formal writing
Children learn to read short stories and passages carefully to answer questions about the main idea and details. They use a dictionary to find the meaning of new words, focusing on the first letter and simple definitions. For formal writing, they learn to write a friendly letter with a clear date, salutation like ‘Dear’, and a closing like ‘From’. They also write short paragraphs about a topic using a beginning, middle, and end, and use full stops and capital letters correctly.

Skim a passage, scan for facts; alphabetical order and guide words; write a short letter and notice with format.
