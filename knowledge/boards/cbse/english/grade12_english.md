# CBSE Class 12 English - Core approach

## Reading comprehension: note-making and summarising
In Grade 12, students learn to make structured notes by identifying key points from passages, using headings and sub-headings to organise information logically. They master summarising techniques by condensing lengthy texts into shorter versions, ensuring they retain the main ideas while omitting minor details. For example, a long news report on environmental policies can be summarised into a paragraph highlighting the main issues and proposed solutions. Students focus on using their own words and maintaining the original meaning without adding personal opinions. These skills help them effectively study for exams and manage complex information in higher education and professional life.

Two unseen passages with note format (title, keys, abbreviations), 80-word summary; MCQ pattern practice.

## Writing: notice, invitation and replies
In Class 12, you learn to write formal notices for school events like the annual day or parent-teacher meetings, following a structured format with a heading, date, time, venue, and signature. You also practice creating invitations for occasions such as cultural competitions or farewell parties, ensuring they include all necessary details like the occasion, date, time, venue, and an RSVP contact. When writing replies, you learn to accept or decline invitations politely, using clear and appropriate language to confirm attendance or express regret. For example, a notice for a science exhibition would have the title "NOTICE" followed by the date of the event and details of participating. These skills help you communicate effectively and formally in real-life situations.

School notice; formal invitation card + reply; wording constraints.

## Writing: letters to editor and job application
In Class 12, students learn to write formal letters such as letters to the editor and job applications, focusing on proper structure, salutations, and closings. For letters to the editor, they practice expressing opinions on current issues like school safety or environmental concerns, ensuring clarity and brevity. Job applications require careful attention to format, including a well-structured resume, a cover letter highlighting skills and experience, and a subject line that matches the job advertisement. Students are taught to maintain a polite and professional tone, using correct grammar and formal language throughout the letter. Examples include drafting a letter to a local newspaper about road safety or applying for a position as a school prefect, demonstrating practical application of these skills.

Argument letter structure; application with CV; formal register discipline.

## Writing: article and report
In Class 12, students learn to write formal articles and reports using structured formats and standard language conventions. For articles, they practice writing engaging introductions, developing logical arguments with supporting evidence in the body, and writing concise conclusions on topics like social issues or school events. Reports must follow a specific format with headings like Aim, Procedure, Observations, and Conclusion, presenting factual information objectively on subjects such as science experiments or surveys. Students focus on using formal vocabulary, clear transitions between ideas, and accurate grammar to maintain a professional tone. They also learn to cite sources correctly to avoid plagiarism and ensure the information is credible and reliable.

Article 150-200 words with heading; newspaper report: headline-byline-lead-body.

## Literature: Flamingo prose
In the Flamingo prose section, students learn to analyse literary texts by identifying themes, characterisation, and narrative techniques. They study the structure of different genres like short stories, essays, and biographies, and understand how authors use language devices such as symbolism and irony. For example, in "The Last Lesson," they examine how the setting conveys patriotism and loss. Students are also taught to interpret the writer’s perspective and evaluate the socio-cultural context of the texts. This develops their critical thinking and textual appreciation skills.

Extract-based MCQs; character-theme analysis with evidence; author's craft questions.

## Literature: Flamingo poetry
Students in Class 12 study a selection of poems from the prescribed Flamingo textbook to understand themes, figures of speech, and poetic devices. They learn to analyse the mood, tone, and central message of each poem, such as the metaphorical journey in 'Aunt Jennifer's Tigers'. The curriculum requires them to identify and explain elements like alliteration, simile, and symbolism, using textual evidence to support their interpretations. Students are expected to appreciate the cultural and social contexts of the poetry, connecting them to broader human experiences. This section develops critical thinking and analytical skills required for answering comprehension and essay-type questions.

Devices at depth: irony, paradox, symbolism; central idea 120-word answers; comparison tasks.

## Literature: Vistas
In the Vistas reader for Class 12, students study diverse prose texts that explore themes of science fiction, social issues, and moral dilemmas. They learn to identify central ideas, character development, and narrative techniques, such as foreshadowing and irony, used by authors. For instance, they analyse the futuristic world in ‘The Adventure’ or the societal satire in ‘The Tiger King’. Students develop critical thinking by interpreting symbolism and understanding the writers' perspectives on complex subjects. They also practise writing analytical responses, including summaries and interpretations, to demonstrate comprehension of the prescribed texts.

Supplementary reader themes; alternate endings; inferential answers.

## Creative and analytical writing
In Class 12, students learn to write both creative and analytical pieces with a clear focus on structure and technique. For creative writing, they explore formats like the short story and one-act play, understanding how to build characters, create dialogue, and develop a plot with a beginning, middle, and end. In analytical writing, they learn to structure arguments logically, beginning with a clear thesis statement, supporting it with evidence from texts, and concluding with a summary of their main points. They practice writing formal analytical essays, newspaper columns, and business letters, ensuring each piece has a formal tone and appropriate organisation. Students also learn to distinguish between factual reporting and creative expression in their writing.

Story completion; review writing (book-film); analytical paragraphs on quotes.

## Language refinement
Students learn to refine their language by understanding advanced grammatical structures such as conditional sentences, reported speech, and complex conjunctions. They focus on improving sentence construction through exercises on combining simple sentences into complex ones and using appropriate punctuation marks like the semicolon and colon. Vocabulary enhancement is a key component, where students learn to use synonyms, antonyms, and context-specific words accurately in writing. Emphasis is placed on developing coherence and cohesion through techniques like sequencing ideas logically and using transition words. For example, students learn to change direct speech ("He said, ‘I am tired’") into indirect speech ("He said that he was tired") correctly.

Common errors at board level; phrasal-collocational precision; punctuation for clarity.

## Examination strategy
Students learn effective strategies for approaching examinations, including time management techniques to allocate time for each question based on marks. They understand the importance of reading question papers thoroughly to understand instructions and identify easy and difficult questions before attempting them. Pupils are taught how to structure answers logically by introducing the main idea, providing supporting details with examples, and concluding appropriately to score better marks. They also learn to revise their answers neatly, ensuring correct grammar, spelling, and punctuation to avoid losing marks on presentation. These methods help students feel more confident and organised during examinations.

Time allocation across sections; presentation standards; attempt sequencing; revision planner.
