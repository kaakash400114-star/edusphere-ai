# CBSE Class 3 English - NCERT (Marigold 3) approach

## Nouns: common, proper, collective
In Grade 3, we learn about nouns. Nouns are words that name people, places, animals, or things. We learn about three kinds of nouns: common nouns, proper nouns, and collective nouns. Common nouns name general things like 'chair' or 'city'. Proper nouns name special things like 'Riya' or 'Mumbai' and always begin with a capital letter. Collective nouns name groups of people or things like 'team' or 'flock'. We practise using these nouns to make our sentences better.

Common (girl), proper (Meena), collective (a bunch of grapes, a herd of elephants, a flock of birds).

## Pronouns: he, she, it, they, we
In this lesson, we learn about pronouns like he, she, it, we, and they. A pronoun is a small word that takes the place of a noun, such as a person, animal, or thing, to make sentences shorter and clearer. We use 'he' for a boy or a male, and 'she' for a girl or a female. For example, "Ravi is playing; he is very fast." We use 'it' for animals, objects, or things that are not people, like "The cat is sleeping; it is on the mat." We use 'we' when talking about ourselves, like "We are students," and 'they' for two or more people, animals, or things, such as "The children are happy; they are friends.

Replace naming words to avoid repeats; use I always with a capital; his/her/its/their.

## Adjectives and degrees of comparison (intro)
Children learn that adjectives are describing words that tell us more about nouns, like "big" for a house or "red" for a flower. They understand how to compare things using three degrees of comparison: positive (tall), comparative (taller), and superlative (tallest). The rules change for short words (add -er/-est) and long words (use more/most), like "fast" to "faster" and "beautiful" to "more beautiful". Simple examples help them see how adjectives make sentences more interesting, such as "This ball is small, that ball is smaller, and the blue ball is the smallest". They practice using these words to compare objects in their daily life.

Describing size, colour, feeling; big-bigger-biggest, tall-taller-tallest with than.

## Verbs: is/are/am with -ing (present continuous)
In Class 3, children learn to use the helping verbs 'is', 'are', and 'am' with the '-ing' form of a verb to show an action happening right now. This is called the present continuous tense. For example, we say "The cat is sleeping" because the action of sleeping is happening at the moment. We use 'is' with 'he', 'she', 'it', or a singular name, like "He is eating". We use 'are' with 'we', 'you', or 'they', like "They are playing". We use 'am' with 'I', for example, "I am reading". This helps us talk about actions that are not yet finished.

She is reading; they are playing; spell -ing forms (run→running).

## Simple past of regular and common irregular verbs
In Class 3, we learn about the simple past tense to talk about actions that happened before now. Regular verbs make their past form by adding -ed to the base verb, like "walked" or "played". Common irregular verbs do not follow this rule, so we must remember their special past forms, such as "went" for "go" and "ate" for "eat". We use these past forms in sentences to say what someone did earlier, for example, "Ravi walked to school" or "Sita ate a mango". This helps us describe events that have already happened in the past.

walk→walked, play→played; go→went, eat→ate, see→saw in short stories.

## Adverbs of manner (how)
Children learn about adverbs of manner, which tell us how an action is done. These words often end with ‘-ly’ and come after the verb in a sentence. For example, ‘She walks slowly’ or ‘He runs quickly’. They help describe actions like singing loudly, eating neatly, or writing carefully. The teacher shows how to change adjectives into adverbs, like turning ‘slow’ into ‘slowly’. By using these words, children can make their sentences more interesting and clear. This helps them speak and write better English.

quickly, slowly, neatly, loudly - most add -ly; place them near the verb.

## Prepositions and prepositional phrases
In Grade 3, children learn about prepositions, which are words that show the relationship between a noun and another word in a sentence. They understand that prepositions often tell us about place, time, or how things move. For example, words like 'in', 'on', 'under', 'beside', and 'between' are common prepositions used to describe locations. A prepositional phrase is a small group of words starting with a preposition and ending with a noun or pronoun, such as 'in the box' or 'under the table'. Children learn to identify these phrases to better understand how things are connected in sentences.

in, on, under, behind, between, across; longer phrases: "across the bridge", "behind the school gate".

## Punctuation: comma in lists, apostrophe for belonging
In Class 3, children learn to use commas to separate three or more items in a list, such as buying apples, bananas, and oranges from the market. They also understand the apostrophe is used to show belonging, like in 'Ravi's bag' or 'the cat's paw'. This helps make writing clear and easy to read. Punctuation marks like commas and apostrophes are small but important for correct sentences. Children practice using them in simple sentences to improve their writing skills.

Ravi, Anu and Sam bought apples, bananas and grapes. This is Ravi's bag.

## Comprehension and unseen passage
In Class 3, children learn to read and understand short unseen passages without any prior preparation. They practice finding the main idea of a paragraph and identifying important details like who, what, where, and when. For example, after reading a story, they can answer simple questions such as "Who was the main character?" or "Where did the story happen?". They also learn to find specific words or sentences that tell us the answers. These skills help them become better readers and understand new stories on their own.

Read a 10-line passage; find the main idea; answer in complete sentences; guess word meaning from the passage.

## Guided composition: paragraph writing
In Grade 3, children learn to write a short paragraph by organising their thoughts into a topic sentence and a few supporting sentences. They understand that a good paragraph has a main idea and gives small details about that idea, just like a story has a hero and small events. Children practice writing on simple topics like "My favourite game" or "The rain," using one sentence to tell the main idea and two sentences to add little details. They learn to write in order from one idea to the next, using words like "and" or "because" to connect their sentences.

Topic sentence + 3-4 detail sentences + ending; describe a person, place or festival; check capitals and full stops.
