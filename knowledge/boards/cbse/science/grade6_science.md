# CBSE Class 6 Science - NCERT

## Components of food
Food is made of different things our bodies need. Some food gives us energy, like rice and chapatis, to run and play. Other food helps us grow big and strong, such as milk, eggs, and dal. We also need food to stay healthy and fight sickness, which we get from fruits like oranges and mangoes, and vegetables like carrots and spinach. Different foods have different jobs, so we must eat many kinds every day to stay fit.

Nutrients: carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, roughage, water; deficiency diseases (rickets, scurvy, goitre, anaemia); balanced diet; testing starch and protein.

## Sorting materials into groups
We group materials based on their properties like colour, shape, hardness, and transparency. For example, we sort a steel spoon and an iron nail under the group of hard and opaque metals, while a glass tumbler and a plastic bottle go into the transparent group. We also separate materials into natural and man-made; wood is natural, but a plastic chair is man-made. Some materials dissolve in water to form a solution, like salt or sugar, while others like sand do not. We learn to make groups using different characteristics for better organisation and understanding of things around us.

Objects by material (metal, plastic, glass); properties: lustre, hardness, transparency, solubility, float/sink; grouping helps us study and use materials.

## Separation of substances
Students learn about separating mixtures using simple methods like handpicking, winnowing, sieving, and dissolving. Handpicking is used to remove big impurities like stones from rice, while winnowing separates husk from grains using wind. Sieving helps filter out impurities by pouring the mixture through a net with small holes, like separating flour from pebbles. When things dissolve in water, we can separate them by filtration or evaporation, like getting salt back from salt water by boiling it dry. These methods help us get pure substances from mixtures in our daily life.

Handpicking, winnowing, sieving, magnetic separation, sedimentation-decantation, filtration, evaporation; why we separate (grain from stalk, salt from sea water); saturated solutions idea.

## Getting to know plants
Plants are living things that need air, water, sunlight, and food to grow. They make their own food using a process called photosynthesis, where they take in sunlight and change carbon dioxide and water into food. The parts of a plant include the roots, which hold the plant in the ground and take in water; the stem, which carries water to the leaves; and the leaves, where food is made. Flowers make seeds after pollination by bees or butterflies, and these seeds grow into new plants. Some plants, like mango trees and banyan trees, are big, while others, like spinach and tulsi, are small and grow close to the ground.

Herbs, shrubs, trees; climbers, creepers; root systems (tap, fibrous); leaf venation; flower parts; transpiration experiment.

## Body movements
Our body moves with the help of bones and joints. Bones form our skeleton, and joints allow them to bend and rotate, like our knees and elbows. Muscles pull on bones to help us walk, run, and pick things up. Some joints are movable, like our shoulder, while others are fixed, like those in our skull. We learn about different types of joints and how they help us in daily activities.

Human skeleton joints: ball-and-socket (shoulder), hinge (elbow/knee), pivot (neck); bones (femur longest); gait of snail, cockroach, birds, fish, snake.

## The living organisms and their surroundings
Living organisms need air, water, food, and shelter to live and grow. Plants make their own food using sunlight, water, and air through a process called photosynthesis. Animals depend on plants or other animals for their food. All living things change as they grow, like a seed growing into a plant or a baby growing into a child. Organisms live in different places called habitats, such as forests, ponds, or deserts, where they get everything they need to survive.

Habitat and adaptation: terrestrial (desert cactus/camel, mountain pine/bear), aquatic (fish, water hyacinth); biotic-abiotic components; stimulus and response examples.

## Motion and measurement of distances
In this topic, you learn about motion and how to measure distances. You will understand that an object is in motion when it changes its position over time, like a car moving on the road or a bird flying in the sky. You will study two types of motion: rectilinear motion, where objects move in a straight line like a train on a track, and periodic motion, where objects repeat their movement in a fixed time, like a swinging pendulum. To measure the length of objects, you use standard units like metre (m) and centimetre (cm), and you learn to use a metre scale correctly. For example, the length of your school desk can be measured in centimetres using this scale.

Standard units (SI: metre); measuring tape/scale rules; rest and motion; types: straight, circular, rotational, periodic (pendulum); speed as distance in unit time (gently).

## Light, shadows and reflections
Light travels in a straight line and when an object blocks it, a shadow is formed. The size and shape of a shadow change depending on how close the object is to the light source. We use a torch and a ball to see how a shadow becomes bigger when the ball is far from the wall and smaller when it is near. Opaque objects make dark shadows, while translucent objects make lighter shadows. A mirror reflects light to form an image that looks the same as the object, like seeing your face in a shiny steel spoon.

Light travels in straight lines; luminous vs non-luminous; transparent, translucent, opaque; shadows; pinhole camera idea; mirrors and reflection basics.

## Electricity and circuits
In Class 6, we learn about electricity and how it flows in circuits to make things work, like a bulb glowing or a fan moving. We discover that a circuit needs a complete path for electricity to flow, which is made by connecting wires from a power source, like a battery, to a device, like a bulb. We also learn that for a bulb to glow, the two of its terminals must be connected to the two terminals of the battery without any break. If the circuit is broken by opening a switch or disconnecting a wire, the electricity stops, and the bulb goes off. We understand that materials allow electricity to pass through them and are called conductors, while those that do not are called insulators.

Cell, bulb, wires, switch; complete circuit; conductors vs insulators; torch diagram; safety with mains electricity.

## Fun with magnets
Magnets have two ends called north and south poles, and they always attract each other if the opposite poles face one another. A magnet can make certain materials like iron, steel, and nickel stick to it, but it will not affect things like wood or plastic. When you hang a magnet freely, it always points towards the north and south directions, just like a compass. If you break a magnet into two pieces, each new piece will also have its own north and south pole. We use magnets in many things, like fridge doors to hold notes and in toy cars to make them move.

Magnetic and non-magnetic materials; poles attract-opposite; compass; making a magnet; magnets in daily life (stickers, pin holders).

## Water
Water is a precious natural resource that we must save. We learn about the three states of water: solid like ice, liquid like water we drink, and gas like water vapour in air. Rain is the main source of fresh water on Earth, and it collects in rivers, lakes, and underground wells to form sources of water. Water is very important for all living things, like plants and animals, and we should not waste it. Dirty water must be cleaned before we drink it to stay healthy and not fall sick.

Sources; water cycle: evaporation-condensation-precipitation; forms of water; floods-droughts; conservation; rainwater harvesting.

## Air around us
Air is all around us, but we cannot see it. Air is a mixture of gases like nitrogen, oxygen, and carbon dioxide. We need oxygen from the air to stay alive, just like plants take in carbon dioxide to make food. Air makes sound travel, which is why we can hear a bell ringing far away. The wind is moving air that helps dry clothes and cools our body when we sweat. Sometimes, air is polluted by smoke and dust from factories and vehicles.

Composition: nitrogen ~78%, oxygen ~21%, CO2 and others; oxygen supports burning; air has water vapour, dust; wind, breathing and air.

## Garbage in, garbage out
We learn about different kinds of waste at home and school, like leftover food, paper, and plastic. Some waste breaks down naturally, like banana peels, and helps make the soil rich. Other waste, like plastic bags and toys, takes many years to disappear and harms the environment. We sort our waste into two groups: wet waste for plants and dry waste like plastic for recycling. Always throwing garbage in a dustbin keeps our surroundings clean and stops animals from getting sick.

Biodegradable vs non-biodegradable; vermicomposting; recycling paper and plastic; reduce-reuse-recycle; village panchayat/city corporation waste care.
