# CBSE Class 3 Science - NCERT-aligned EVS (Looking Around 3)

## Our food: from farm to table
Food comes from farms where farmers grow crops like rice and wheat, and raise animals like cows and chickens for milk and eggs. Fruits and vegetables such as mangoes, tomatoes, and carrots grow on plants in soil and need water and sunlight to grow big and healthy. After harvesting, food is cleaned, cooked, and packed to keep it safe and fresh, and then brought to markets where our parents buy it for our home. At home, food is stored properly, like keeping fruits in the fridge, and cooked to make it tasty and easy to eat, giving us energy to play and study. Different foods give us different nutrients, like milk making our bones strong, and keeping a balance of all helps us stay healthy and grow well.

Where rice, wheat, dal, milk come from; parts of plants we eat: root (carrot), leaf (spinach), stem (sugarcane), flower (cauliflower), fruit, seed.

## Parts of a plant and their jobs
Plants have different parts, each with a special job. The roots hold the plant in the soil and take in water, like a straw drinking from a cup. The stem carries water and food from the roots to the rest of the plant, acting like a strong pipe. The leaves make food for the plant using sunlight in a process called photosynthesis. Flowers help the plant make seeds, which grow into new plants. Seeds need water, air, and soil to sprout and become a little seedling.

Root holds and absorbs; stem carries water and holds leaves; leaf makes food (a tiny kitchen); flower makes fruit and seed; seed grows into a new plant.

## Animals: feeding habits and body coverings
Animals have different body coverings like feathers, fur, scales, and skin to protect them. Their coverings help them stay warm, keep cool, and hide from other animals. Animals eat different kinds of food such as plants, other animals, or both. We group animals by what they eat: herbivores eat only plants, carnivores eat only meat, and omnivores eat both plants and meat. For example, a cow is a herbivore, a lion is a carnivore, and a bear is an omnivore.

Herbivore (cow, deer), carnivore (lion), omnivore (crow, human); fur, feathers, scales, shells; how bodies help animals live where they live.

## Birds: beaks, claws, nests
Birds have different beaks to eat their food, like a parrot’s strong beak to crack nuts or a sparrow’s small beak to eat seeds. Their claws help them hold branches, catch prey, or walk on the ground, like a crow’s curved claws to grip trees or a duck’s webbed feet to swim. Birds build nests using twigs, grass, and mud to lay their eggs and keep the baby birds safe. Some nests are like bowls on branches, while others are hidden in holes to protect them from danger. Each bird’s beak, claws, and nest is special and helps it live in its own way.

Different beaks for different food (duck, eagle, sparrow); claws for perching/holding; nest materials and care of eggs and chicks.

## The human skeleton and muscles
The human body is supported by a strong frame called the skeleton, which is made up of many bones. We have more than 200 bones in our body, and they join together at places called joints. Muscles are soft parts that help our bones to move, like when we walk or wave our hands. Our skeleton protects important soft parts inside our body, such as the heart and lungs. Bones also help us to sit, stand, and play because they give our body shape and support.

Bones give shape and protect organs; skull protects the brain, ribs the heart and lungs; joints and movement; muscles pull bones.

## Sense organs and how the brain uses them
We have five sense organs: eyes, ears, nose, tongue, and skin. Eyes help us see things like a red ball or a blue book. Ears let us hear sounds such as a bell ringing or a bird singing. The nose helps us smell good things like flowers and bad things like a rotten banana. The tongue helps us taste sweet jaggery and salty chips. Our skin helps us feel soft pillows, rough sand, and hot sun. The brain is the main control centre. It gets information from all our sense organs and helps us understand the world around us.

Eyes, ears, nose, tongue, skin send messages; care of each sense organ; safety with sharp/loud/hot.

## Water: forms and the water cycle in nature
Water exists in different forms like liquid (in rivers and taps), solid (ice in glaciers and snow), and gas (water vapour in air). The water cycle shows how water moves from the ground to the sky and back again. The sun heats water in oceans and lakes, making it evaporate into vapour. This vapour cools and condenses to form clouds, which bring rain. Rainwater flows into rivers and soaks into the ground, completing the cycle. We use water for drinking, washing, and growing plants, so we must not waste it.

Ice, water, steam; evaporation and condensation in daily life (wet clothes dry, dew, steam from hot food); rain formation in simple words.

## Air and weather around us
Air is all around us, and we need it to live. We cannot see air, but we can feel it when it moves; this moving air is called wind. The weather tells us if the day is hot, cold, sunny, or rainy, and we use words like 'windy' or 'cloudy' to describe it. Rain comes from clouds, and wind helps dry clothes on a rope. Wind can push things like paper boats and make trees dance. We wear different clothes, like woollens in winter and light clothes in summer, to stay comfortable in changing weather.

Cool air, warm air, wind; air has weight and fills space; keeping air clean; weather and seasons change what living things do.

## Our earth: land, water and sky
Our Earth has three main parts: the land with soil and rocks, the water in rivers, seas and oceans, and the sky above us which has air and clouds. Land has different shapes like mountains, plains and valleys, while water covers most of the Earth and helps plants and animals live. The sky is a big space where we see the Sun during the day and the Moon and stars at night. We learn that all these parts—land, water and sky—are important for our lives and we must keep them clean. Rain from the sky helps the land grow plants and fills our rivers and ponds with water.

Globe: more water than land; mountains, plains, rivers; day and night with the sun; directions (N, S, E, W) with shadow.

## Caring for our environment
We learn that keeping our environment clean and healthy is very important. We must not throw trash like plastic bags, paper, or food waste on the ground; instead, we should always use a dustbin. We should also save water by turning off the tap when it is not being used and save electricity by switching off lights and fans when we leave a room. We can help plants grow by watering them and making sure they get sunlight, because plants give us fresh air to breathe. By taking these small steps every day, we can take good care of our Earth and make it a beautiful place for everyone.

Reduce-reuse-recycle; composting leaves; not wasting paper, water, electricity; planting and caring for trees.
