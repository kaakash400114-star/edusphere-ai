# CBSE Class 11 Science - NCERT (Physics, Chemistry, Biology essentials)

## Physics: Units, measurement and motion
Students learn about fundamental quantities like length, mass, and time, and their standard units in the International System of Units (SI). They understand the difference between scalar and vector quantities, learning to represent vectors using arrows and add them using the head-to-tail rule. The topic covers motion in a straight line, teaching key terms like displacement, velocity, and acceleration, along with their formulas to calculate speed and distance. They also study uniformly accelerated motion, using equations of motion to solve problems involving objects moving with constant acceleration. For example, they calculate the time it takes for a stone dropped from a tower to reach the ground.

Dimensional analysis; errors; vectors and scalars, addition-resolution; kinematics graphs; projectile motion; Newton's laws applied; friction; work-energy-theorem; centre of mass idea; rotational basics (torque, angular momentum statements); gravitation: Kepler's laws, g variation.

## Physics: Properties of matter, thermodynamics, waves
In the Properties of Matter, students learn about Hooke's Law for elastic deformation and the concept of pressure in fluids, using Pascal's principle. Thermodynamics introduces the laws of thermodynamics, specifically the first law concerning energy conservation and heat transfer processes like conduction. Waves cover the principles of wave motion, including the relationship between frequency, wavelength, and speed, using sound waves as a common example. Key formulas include the elastic potential energy ½kx² and the wave speed equation v = fλ. The study also explores properties like viscosity and surface tension in everyday substances such as honey and water.

Elasticity basics; pressure, Pascal-Archimedes-Bernoulli; viscosity, surface tension; thermal expansion; calorimetry, heat transfer; kinetic theory preview; oscillations: SHM equation-energy; waves: superposition, beats, Doppler (ideas).

## Chemistry: Basic concepts and structure of atom
In this topic, students learn about the basic building blocks of matter and how atoms are structured. They study Dalton’s atomic theory, which explains that atoms are indivisible particles of an element, and discover that atoms are made up of even smaller sub-atomic particles like protons, neutrons, and electrons. The rules for filling electrons in an atom’s shells, known as the Bohr model, are taught, explaining how electrons are arranged in energy levels around the nucleus, for example, in a sodium atom which has two electrons in the first shell and eight in the second. Students also learn about atomic number, mass number, and how to write atomic symbols, such as for carbon-12, which has 6 protons and 6 neutrons. Concepts of isotopes, like hydrogen having three forms (protium, deuterium, and tritium), are also covered.

Mole concept and stoichiometry; empirical-molecular formula; electron configurations (Aufbau, Hund, Pauli); quantum numbers; periodic trends reasoning.

## Chemistry: Bonding and states
Students learn about the different ways atoms join together to form molecules through chemical bonds. They study the formation of ionic bonds, where metals lose electrons to non-metals, creating ions like in sodium chloride (NaCl), and covalent bonds, where atoms share electrons to form molecules like water (H₂O). The topic also explains how different bond types affect the physical states of matter, like why gases are generally made of covalent molecules while many solids are ionic. Furthermore, they understand how the arrangement of bonds and molecules leads to properties like conductivity and melting points.

VSEPR shapes; hybridisation (sp, sp2, sp3); MO diagram basics (O2 paramagnetic); gas laws, ideal gas equation, kinetic theory; liquids: vapour pressure, surface tension.

## Chemistry: Thermodynamics, equilibrium, redox
In thermodynamics, you learn about different systems, like an open cup of tea losing heat to the surroundings, and the laws governing energy transfer. Equilibrium studies the state of balance in reversible reactions, such as the dissociation of dinitrogen tetroxide (N₂O₄) into nitrogen dioxide (NO₂). Redox reactions involve the transfer of electrons, like the rusting of iron or the reaction between zinc and copper sulphate, where one substance is oxidised and another is reduced. Key formulas include the equilibrium constant (Kc) and the relationship between Gibbs free energy (ΔG) and cell potential (E°).

Enthalpy-Hess law; entropy-spontaneity (qualitative); Kc-Kp, Le Chatelier; ionic equilibrium: pH, buffers; redox titrations; hydrogen and s-block highlights; p-block group 13-14 highlights; basic organic: nomenclature, isomerism, reaction mechanisms (fission, electrophile-nucleophile); hydrocarbons reactions.

## Biology: Diversity of the living world
Students learn about the wide variety of life on Earth, known as biodiversity, and how scientists classify organisms into groups called kingdoms and species. They discover the rules of biological nomenclature, where each plant and animal gets a unique two-part Latin name, like Homo sapiens for humans or Mangifera indica for the mango. The classification system groups living things based on shared characteristics, such as whether they are prokaryotic or eukaryotic, autotrophic or heterotrophic, and their basic cellular structures. For example, all animals are multicellular and heterotrophic, while bacteria are mostly unicellular and prokaryotic. This organised system helps us understand the relationships between different forms of life.

Taxonomic categories; five-kingdom overview; plant and animal kingdom salient phyla/classes with examples.

## Biology: Structural organisation and plant life
In this topic, students learn about the structural organisation of flowering plants, studying the basic tissues like meristematic and permanent tissues. They explore the anatomy of roots, stems, and leaves to understand how different tissues like xylem and phloem function in transport. The chapter also covers the morphology and anatomy of flowers, fruits, and seeds, focusing on their role in reproduction and dispersal. Students examine various plant parts, such as the root hairs for absorption and stomata for gas exchange, to understand their specific functions.

Morphology of root-stem-leaf-flower; anatomy: monocot vs dicot; cell structure, organelles; cell cycle: mitosis-meiosis; transport: water ascent, transpiration; photosynthesis (C3-C4 idea); respiration; plant growth hormones.

## Biology: Human physiology essentials
Students learn about the basic life processes in humans, including nutrition where food is digested in the stomach and small intestine, respiration where oxygen is taken in and carbon dioxide is released, and circulation where blood is pumped by the heart to all parts of the body. They study the structure and functions of important organs like the kidneys that filter blood to form urine and the lungs where exchange of gases occurs. The nervous system, including the brain and spinal cord, and the endocrine system with glands like the pancreas that release hormones, are also covered to understand how the body is controlled.

Digestion-absorption; breathing-gas transport; body fluids-circulation; excretory system-nephron; locomotion-muscle contraction idea; neural coordination-neuron, synapse; endocrine glands and hormones; reproduction basics; immunity innate vs acquired outline.
