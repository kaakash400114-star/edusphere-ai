# CBSE Class 12 Science - NCERT (Physics, Chemistry, Biology essentials)

## Physics: Electrostatics and current electricity
In Class 12 Electrostatics, you learn about electric charges, Coulomb's law to find force between charges, and the electric field due to charges. You study how charges move in conductors, capacitance of capacitors, and the energy stored in them. In Current Electricity, you understand Ohm's law, resistance, and how resistances combine in series and parallel circuits. You also learn about Kirchhoff’s laws to solve complex circuits and the heating effect of current, which is used in devices like geysers and heaters.

Coulomb's law, field-dipole; Gauss law idea; potential-capacitance-dielectrics; drift velocity, Ohm's law, resistivity; Kirchhoff rules, Wheatstone, potentiometer-metre bridge ideas.

## Physics: Magnetism, EMI and AC
Students learn about magnetic fields around current-carrying conductors and the right-hand thumb rule to determine their direction. They study electromagnetic induction, Faraday’s law which says a changing magnetic field induces an EMF, and Lenz’s law to find the direction of induced current. The topic covers alternating current (AC), including its generation using a rotating coil in a magnetic field, and the difference between AC and direct current (DC). Students calculate RMS values for AC voltage and current and understand the role of transformers in stepping up or stepping down AC voltage for practical use.

Moving charge force, Biot-Savart, Ampere law; solenoid-toroid; loop-dipole; EMI: Faraday-Lenz; self-mutual inductance; AC circuits with phasors, LCR resonance; transformer; power lines.

## Physics: Optics, dual nature, atoms-nuclei
In this topic, students study the wave nature of light and understand how interference and diffraction patterns are formed when light passes through small openings or slits. They also learn about the photoelectric effect, which demonstrates that light behaves as particles called photons, and Einstein's photoelectric equation explains this phenomenon. The atomic structure is covered through Bohr's model of the hydrogen atom, where electrons orbit the nucleus in fixed energy levels. Additionally, students explore the nuclear composition of atoms, learning about isotopes and radioactive decay processes like alpha, beta, and gamma radiation.

Ray optics: mirrors-lenses-instruments; wave optics: Huygens, YDSE fringe idea, diffraction broad view; photoelectric effect; de Broglie; Bohr model; nuclei: mass defect, fission-fusion; semiconductors: diode, LED, photodiode basics; communication preview.

## Chemistry: Solutions and electrochemistry
Students learn about the concentration of solutions using units like molarity and molality, and they study Raoult’s Law for calculating the vapour pressure of ideal solutions. In electrochemistry, they identify different types of electrochemical cells, including galvanic cells like the Daniell cell, and calculate electrode potentials using the Nernst equation. They understand the relationship between Gibbs free energy and cell potential, and learn about Faraday’s laws of electrolysis for determining the amount of substance deposited during electrolysis. Practical applications include rust prevention and electroplating.

Concentration terms; Raoult-Henry; colligative properties; Nernst, conductance, Kohlrausch; batteries-fuel cells; corrosion.

## Chemistry: Kinetics, surface chemistry, d-f block
In kinetics, students learn about reaction rates and the factors affecting them, like temperature and concentration, using the rate law equation. For surface chemistry, they study adsorption, where molecules stick to a surface, and catalysis, where substances speed up reactions without being used up. In the d-block and f-block sections, children explore the electronic configurations of elements like iron and copper, and learn about their coloured compounds and varied oxidation states. They also discover how these elements form complexes, like the blue colour of [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺, due to their unique electron arrangements. These concepts help understand why reactions happen at different speeds and how transition metals are used in everyday life.

Rate law, order-molecularity, Arrhenius, half-life; adsorption, catalysis, colloids; lanthanoid contraction, coordination compounds: nomenclature, VBT-CFT ideas, isomerism.

## Chemistry: Haloalkanes, alcohols, carbonyls, amines, biomolecules
Students learn about different groups of carbon compounds, starting with haloalkanes where hydrogen atoms are replaced by halogens like chlorine in compounds such as chloromethane. They then study alcohols, which have an -OH group, like ethanol found in beverages, and carbonyls, containing a carbon-oxygen double bond as in acetone used in nail polish. Amines, derived from ammonia, are explored with examples like methylamine, while biomolecules include essential sugars like glucose and proteins made of amino acids linked by peptide bonds. These topics cover naming rules, structural formulas, and key reactions to understand how these compounds behave and are used in daily life.

SN1-SN2 outline; alcohol-phenol acidity; aldehyde-ketone nucleophilic addition; carboxyl acidity; amine basicity; diazonium uses; carbohydrates-proteins-vitamins-nucleic acids structure classes; polymers classes; chemistry in everyday life highlights.

## Biology: Reproduction
In Biology, students learn about human reproductive anatomy and the process of gametogenesis, where sperms are produced in the testes and eggs in the ovaries. They study the menstrual cycle, its hormonal regulation, and fertilisation, which forms a zygote that develops into an embryo. The chapter also covers plant reproduction, including the structure of flowers, pollination, and double fertilisation in angiosperms. Genetic inheritance is explained through Mendel’s laws, showing how traits like flower colour or blood group are passed from parents to offspring using Punnett squares. Sexual and asexual reproduction in organisms like Hydra and Amoeba are also discussed.

Flower-to-fruit sexual reproduction; human male-female systems; gametogenesis; menstrual cycle; fertilisation-pregnancy-parturition; reproductive health-contraception-MTP-ART outline.

## Biology: Genetics and evolution
Students learn the principles of inheritance and variation by studying Mendel’s laws of segregation and independent assortment using pea plant experiments. They explore the chromosomal basis of inheritance, understanding how genes are located on chromosomes and how mutations can lead to variations. The topic covers the mechanism of sex determination in humans and the process of evolution, including Darwin’s theory of natural selection and the evidence from palaeontology, embryology, and molecular biology. Students also learn about the concept of gene flow, genetic drift, and how speciation occurs, with examples like antibiotic resistance in bacteria. The classification of organisms based on evolutionary relationships and the role of genetic drift in small populations are also discussed.

Mendelian genetics with problems; linkage, crossing over; sex-linked inheritance (haemophilia, colour blindness); genetic disorders (sickle cell, Down); DNA as genetic material; replication-transcription-translation code; lac operon idea; genome-human genome project; evolution: Hardy-Weinberg, origin ideas.

## Biology: Biotechnology, ecology and health
Students learn about genetic engineering and how bacteria are used to produce human insulin, which is a key application of biotechnology. In ecology, they study the structure and functioning of ecosystems, including food chains and food webs, and how energy flows through different trophic levels. The topic also covers environmental issues like pollution and its effects on human health, such as respiratory problems caused by air pollution. Students understand the importance of biodiversity and conservation of natural resources to maintain a healthy ecosystem and human well-being. They also explore how public health measures, like vaccination programmes, help in controlling the spread of infectious diseases.

rDNA tools (restriction enzymes, plasmid), PCR; Bt cotton, insulin; ecology: organisms-populations interactions; ecosystems energy flow, pyramids; biodiversity-hotspots; environmental issues; human health-diseases (AIDS, cancer basics); microbes in human welfare.
