# CBSE Class 8 Science - NCERT

## Crop production and management
In Class 8, students learn that crops need space to grow, so farmers follow different patterns like row cropping or mixed cropping for better use of land. They understand that choosing the right season and healthy seeds, like wheat in winter or paddy in the rainy season, is important for a good harvest. To help plants grow well, farmers use manure and fertilisers, which are natural and chemical ways to add nutrients to the soil. Finally, students learn about protecting crops from insects and diseases by using methods like crop rotation and pesticides to keep the plants healthy and safe.

Agricultural steps: ploughing, sowing, manuring-fertilising, irrigation, weeding, harvesting, storage; kharif-rabi crops; nitrogen fixation by pulses; granaries and silos.

## Microorganisms: friend and foe
Microorganisms are tiny living things that can be seen only with a microscope. They are found all around us, in air, water, soil and even inside our bodies. Some microorganisms are friends of humans as they help in making curd from milk, producing medicines like antibiotics and fixing nitrogen in the soil for plants. Other microorganisms are foes as they can cause diseases in humans, plants and animals, like cholera in humans and rust in wheat plants. We can prevent the spread of harmful microorganisms by keeping our surroundings clean, drinking boiled water and covering our food.

Bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa; uses: curd (lactobacillus), bread, antibiotics, vaccines; diseases: cholera, TB, malaria, dengue; food preservation (pasteurisation, pickling); nitrogen cycle role.

## Materials: metals and non-metals
In Grade 8 Science, you will learn to differentiate between metals and non-metals based on their properties, such as lustre, malleability, ductility, and conductivity. Metals are usually solid, hard, and good conductors of heat and electricity, like iron and copper, while non-metals are generally dull, brittle, and poor conductors, like sulphur and carbon. You will understand the chemical behaviour of metals, which react with acids to produce hydrogen gas, and non-metals, which often gain electrons to form negatively charged ions. The chapter also explains the formation of ionic compounds when metals combine with non-metals, such as sodium chloride (common salt), where sodium donates an electron to chlorine.

Physical: lustre, malleability, ductility, sonority, conductivity; chemical: reaction with oxygen, water, acids; displacement; uses and safety (sodium in water).

## Coal and petroleum
Coal and petroleum are non-renewable resources that are found deep inside the earth. These are called fossil fuels because they were formed from the dead remains of plants and animals that lived millions of years ago. We mine coal from underground mines and extract petroleum from oil wells. These fuels are very important because they give us energy to run factories, vehicles, and generate electricity. However, using them causes air pollution and we need to save these resources by using them carefully.

Fossil fuels as exhaustible resources; coal → coke, coal tar, coal gas; petroleum refining products; natural gas, CNG; conservation: alternate sources, efficiency.

## Combustion and flame
In Class 8 Science, you learn about combustion, which is a chemical process that produces heat and light, and the flames that result from it. A flame is the visible, gaseous part of a fire that forms when a fuel burns in the presence of oxygen. For a fire to start and continue burning, it needs three things: a combustible material like wood or paper, air to supply oxygen, and heat to raise the fuel’s temperature to its ignition point. Different fuels burn differently; for example, a wax candle produces a yellow, sooty flame due to incomplete combustion, while a gas stove gives a clean blue flame because of complete combustion. If any one of these three elements—fuel, oxygen, or heat—is removed, the fire will go out.

Combustion needs fuel + air + ignition temperature; types: rapid, spontaneous, explosion; flame zones (dark, luminous, non-luminous); control fire by removing one need; inflammable substances.

## Conservation of plants and animals
Plants and animals are our precious natural resources, and we must protect them for the future. We learn about deforestation, which is cutting down too many forests, and how it destroys homes for animals like the tiger and plants like neem. Conservation means protecting our environment by growing more trees, saving wild animals from being hunted, and not polluting rivers and lakes. National Parks like Jim Corbett and Sanctuaries are safe homes for animals where they can live freely. We must all try to save water, avoid plastic, and not harm nature to keep our Earth beautiful and healthy.

Deforestation causes-effects; conservation: national parks, sanctuaries, biosphere reserves; Red Data Book; migration; reforestation vs afforestation.

## Cell: structure and functions
A cell is the building block of all living things, like tiny rooms that make up a house. Inside the cell, the jelly-like material is called cytoplasm, and the control room is the nucleus which tells the cell what to do. The nucleus has important thread-like structures called chromosomes, which carry all the information needed to build an animal or a plant, just like a recipe book. Different cells have different jobs, such as red blood cells which carry oxygen in your body. A group of similar cells working together forms tissue, like skin tissue that protects your body.

Discovery; unicellular-multicellular; parts: cell membrane, wall (plants), cytoplasm, nucleus (control centre), vacuole; plant vs animal cell; tissues preview.

## Reproduction in animals
Animals reproduce to make new young ones like themselves. Some animals lay eggs, like birds and frogs, while others give birth to live babies, like cats and humans. Young ones grow and change into adults, a process called life cycle. Males produce small cells called sperms, and females produce larger cells called eggs. When a sperm and an egg join together, they form a zygote, which grows into a baby. Different animals have different ways to care for their young ones.

Sexual: male-female gametes, fertilisation internal/external (frog); viviparous-oviparous; metamorphosis in frog; asexual in hydra, amoeba (binary fission); reproductive health basics.

## Reaching the age of adolescence
Adolescence is the period of life when a child grows into a young adult and changes happen in their body. Boys begin to develop deeper voices and their bodies become broader, while girls start their menstrual cycle, a monthly process where blood flows from the uterus. Glands inside our bodies release special chemicals called hormones, which are like messengers that control these changes and also make emotions feel stronger, sometimes making one happy or angry. It is very important to keep the body clean by bathing regularly and wearing clean clothes to stay healthy during this time. Eating good food like fruits, vegetables, and milk gives the body the energy it needs to grow strong.

Puberty changes: height, voice, hair; hormones: testosterone, oestrogen, thyroid; menstrual cycle basics with respect; personal hygiene; balanced diet for teens.

## Force and pressure
Force is a push or pull that can make an object move, stop, or change its shape. Pressure is the force acting on a unit area of a surface, and it increases when the force is greater or the area is smaller. We measure force in newtons (N) and pressure in newtons per square metre (N/m²), also called pascals. For example, when you sharpen a knife, its small blade area concentrates the force, making it cut things easily. A wide school bag strap puts less pressure on your shoulder because the force is spread over a large area.

Force as push-pull: change state, shape, direction; contact vs non-contact (gravity, magnetic, electrostatic); pressure = force/area (nails, knives); pressure in liquids; atmospheric pressure demonstrations.

## Friction
Friction is a force that stops things from moving smoothly. It is the resistance between two surfaces when they rub against each other, like when you try to push a heavy box on the floor. The roughness of surfaces causes friction, so smoother surfaces have less friction, like ice on a path. Friction helps us in many ways, it allows us to walk without slipping and makes a vehicle stop when we apply brakes. It also produces heat, which you can feel when you rub your palms together quickly.

Causes of friction; depends on surfaces and weight; static-sliding-rolling; advantages (walking, brakes) and reduction (oil, ball bearings); drag in fluids.

## Sound
Sound is a form of energy that travels in the form of waves through a medium like air, water or solids. It needs a medium to travel and cannot travel in a vacuum. The loudness of sound depends on the amplitude of the vibration, while the pitch depends on the frequency. For example, a drum produces a low-pitched sound, whereas a whistle produces a high-pitched sound. The reflection of sound creates an echo, which we can hear in large empty spaces like a cave or a valley. Sound can be produced by vibrating objects, such as a guitar string when it is plucked.

Sources are vibrations; needs a medium (no sound in vacuum); amplitude-loudness, frequency-pitch; human range 20 Hz-20,000 Hz; noise pollution and control.

## Chemical effects of electric current
Electric current can produce chemical changes in liquids, a process called electrolysis. When electricity is passed through certain liquids like salt water, they break down into new substances. For example, passing current through water produces bubbles of oxygen and hydrogen gas. The liquids that allow electricity to pass through them are called conductors of electricity, like lemon juice. Substances that do not conduct electricity are called insulators, such as pure water or plastic. This effect is used in processes like electroplating to coat objects with a thin layer of metal.

Conducting liquids; electrodes-electrolyte; electroplating (copper on iron); testing conductivity safely; LED tester.

## Light
In the topic of Light, students learn that light travels in a straight line, which is why shadows are formed when objects block its path. They study the laws of reflection, where the angle of incidence is always equal to the angle of reflection, using mirrors as examples. Lenses are introduced, showing how convex and concave lenses can bend light to form different kinds of images, such as magnifying glasses and the human eye's lens. The colour spectrum is explained, demonstrating how white light splits into seven colours when passed through a prism, much like a rainbow. Finally, students learn about the importance of light for visibility and how our eyes help us see objects by reflecting light into them.

Reflection laws again with angles; human eye parts; dispersion through prism; atmospheric refraction idea; the eye's defects preview; care of eyes.

## Some natural phenomena
In Class 8, students learn about some natural phenomena like lightning and earthquakes. Lightning is a spark of electricity that happens when clouds have opposite charges and a discharge occurs. Earthquakes are caused by the sudden shaking of the Earth’s crust due to the movement of tectonic plates deep inside. The theory of plate tectonics explains that the Earth’s outer layer is made of large plates that float and move, causing phenomena when they collide or slide past each other. Other phenomena such as cyclones are also discussed, which are violent rotating winds with low pressure at the centre.

Lightning: charges in clouds, safety rules during storms; earthquake: seismic waves, plates, safety (drop-cover-hold); seismograph idea.

## Light (applications) and pollution wrap
In Class 8 Science, you learn about the many uses of light in our daily lives, such as seeing objects with our eyes and using mirrors to reflect it. You study how light travels in straight lines and can be bent by lenses, which helps us see clearly with spectacles and make magnifying glasses. You also understand that light makes things visible and can form shadows when an object blocks it. The topic also covers different types of pollution, including air pollution from smoke and noise pollution from loud sounds, and how they harm our environment and health. You learn about simple ways to reduce pollution, like planting more trees and not burning waste.

Water-air pollution recaps; greenhouse gases and global warming basics; individual action plan.
