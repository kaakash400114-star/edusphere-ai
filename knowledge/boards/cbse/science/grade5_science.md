# CBSE Class 5 Science - NCERT-aligned EVS (Looking Around 5)

## Super senses: how animals sense the world
Animals have special senses like sight, smell, and hearing to help them find food and stay safe from danger. A snake uses its tongue to smell the air and sense where other animals are, while a bat uses sound to find food in the dark. Elephants have big ears to hear far away sounds, and their trunks can feel, smell, and touch things very well. Some animals, like dogs, can smell things that humans cannot even notice. These strong senses help animals live and move around in their homes like the forest, water, or fields.

Tiger's night vision, eagle's sharp eyes, dog's smell, bat's hearing (echolocation), snake's vibration sense - compare with human senses.

## From tasting to digesting
When we eat food, our tongue helps us taste different flavours like sweet or salty. Our teeth chew the food into small, soft pieces, and saliva in our mouth helps break it down. Food then goes down a pipe called the food pipe to our stomach, where strong juices make it softer. From the stomach, it moves to the small intestine, which absorbs all the good nutrients to give us energy. The leftover waste goes out of our body, completing the process of digestion.

Tongue taste zones, saliva starts digestion; teeth roles; the journey: food pipe, stomach, small intestine absorbs, large intestine removes; good food habits.

## Seeds and seeds: germination and dispersal
Seeds are small parts of plants that grow into new plants when they get water, air, and warmth. The process of a seed growing into a new plant is called germination, where the first root and then the shoot come out of the seed. Seeds travel to new places through different methods like wind, water, or by sticking to animal fur, which is called dispersal. For example, coconut seeds float on water to reach faraway beaches, and burrs stick to clothes or animal fur to spread. Seeds have different shapes and sizes, like tiny mustard seeds or large mango seeds, but all need the right conditions to grow.

Seed parts (seed coat, baby plant, food store); germination needs water-air-warmth; dispersal by wind, water, animals, explosion (castor).

## Every drop counts: water conservation
Water is a precious natural resource that we must save because it is needed by all living things. We learn that clean water comes from rivers, lakes, and underground wells, but it can become dirty if we waste it. Simple habits like turning off the tap while brushing teeth, taking shorter showers, and using a bucket instead of a hose to save water. Rainwater can also be collected in pits or tanks to store it for later use, just like a small pond holding raindrops. By sharing these methods, we help ensure that every drop of water is used wisely.

Where water comes from; old step-wells to modern tanks; evaporation and clouds; conserve at home and school; rainwater harvesting.

## Experiments with water: solutions
When we mix things in water, some dissolve completely and make a solution, while others do not dissolve. Things like sugar and salt disappear in water to form a clear solution, but sand or pebbles settle down at the bottom. The amount of a substance that can dissolve in water is called its solubility, and hot water can dissolve more sugar than cold water. We can separate the dissolved substance from water by boiling the water away, like getting salt back from salt water. If we add more salt than water can dissolve, the extra salt stays as a solid at the bottom because water has become full.

Soluble and insoluble; solution, sediment, filtration; evaporation leaves salt; floating-sinking with shape and weight.

## A treat for mosquitoes: disease and environment
Mosquitoes spread diseases like malaria, dengue, and chikungunya when they bite us. They lay their eggs in clean standing water, like in buckets, pots, or coolers, which becomes their home. To stop them, we should cover water containers and clean them every week. Mosquitoes grow in four stages: egg, larva, pupa, and adult, and they need water for all stages except the last one. Keeping our surroundings clean and not letting water stand helps stop mosquitoes from multiplying and keeps us healthy.

Mosquito life cycle in standing water; malaria/dengue spread; prevent by clean surroundings, nets, covers; community health habits.

## Up you go: mountains and adventure
Mountains are huge landforms that rise high above the surrounding areas. They are formed by powerful forces deep inside the Earth that push the land upwards. We learn about different types of mountains, like fold mountains and block mountains, and how they change the shape of the land. Mountains are important because they give us water through rivers and are home to many plants and animals. People who love adventure enjoy climbing mountains to see beautiful views and feel proud of reaching the top.

Mountain landforms, weather there; climbing spirit and teamwork; respect for nature and safety.

## Space travel and the solar system
In Class 5, we learn about our solar system, which is made of the Sun, eight planets, and many other celestial bodies. The Sun is the biggest star at the centre, and it gives light and heat to all the planets. The planets—Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune—move around the Sun in paths called orbits; Earth takes one year to complete its orbit. We also study Earth’s Moon, which revolves around our planet and gives us light at night. Space travel helps astronauts explore these amazing objects in space using special machines called rockets and satellites.

Planets of the sun's family; the moon's phases; satellites help us (weather, TV); India's space milestones (ISRO) in simple words.

## What if it finishes: fuels and energy
Fuels are things that give us heat and light, like wood, coal, and cooking gas. They burn and make energy, but we must save them because they will finish one day. Renewable energy, like sun and wind, never ends and helps us save fuels. Solar panels use sunlight to make electricity for our homes and schools. Using buses and bicycles instead of cars saves petrol and protects our Earth.

Wood, coal, petrol, LPG are limited; saving energy at home; sun and wind as never-ending sources; small actions matter.

## Forests, wildlife and our duty
Forests are homes to many animals like tigers and elephants, and they give us clean air and water. We must not cut down trees too much or hurt wild animals, as it is against the law to hunt them or hurt their homes. We should plant more trees, like neem and banyan, to help the forests grow and give animals safe places to live. Saving forests and wildlife is important because they are part of our nature and we need them to stay healthy. Our duty is to protect them by not littering and by always being kind to animals and plants.

Forests as homes and water banks; food chains in a forest (grass→deer→tiger); protect wildlife: parks and sanctuaries; say no to plastic litter.

## Growing food: from field to plate
Plants need water, sunlight, and good soil to grow food like wheat, rice, and vegetables. Farmers prepare the soil by ploughing it and then sow seeds using tools like a seed drill. Weeds are unwanted plants that take food and water from crops, so farmers remove them by hand or with a tool called a khurpi. After the crops grow, farmers harvest them using sickles or machines. The harvested food is then cleaned, packed, and sent to markets where we buy it to eat at home. This long journey from the farm to our plate ensures we get fresh and healthy food.

Crop seasons (kharif, rabi in simple words); irrigation; farmer's work calendar; storing and transport; respect for food and farmers.
