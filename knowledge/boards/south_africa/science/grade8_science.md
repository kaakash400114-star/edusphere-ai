# South African (CAPS) Grade 8 Science
## The States of Matter
Learners are able to describe the three common states of matter: solid, liquid, and gas, based on the arrangement, movement, and energy of their particles. A solid like the rock at Table Mountain has a fixed shape and volume because its particles are tightly packed and vibrate in place. A liquid, such as water in the Berg River, has a fixed volume but takes the shape of its container because particles are close but can move past one another. A gas, like the air in a football before a Proteas match, has no fixed shape or volume as its particles are far apart and move randomly at high speeds.

## Chemical and Physical Changes
Learners are able to distinguish between physical and chemical changes. A physical change alters the form of a substance but not its chemical composition, for example, melting ice at a braai or dissolving sugar in tea. In a chemical change, a new substance with different properties is formed, such as burning wood which produces ash and smoke, or iron rusting on a gate near the sea. The law of conservation of mass states that in any change, the total mass of the substances involved remains the same.

## The Solar System
Learners are able to describe the relative positions and movements of the Sun, Earth, and Moon. The Sun, the centre of our solar system, is orbited by the Earth. The Earth takes one year to complete its orbit. The Moon orbits the Earth, completing its cycle in about 29.5 days. These movements cause phenomena like day and night and the phases of the Moon. The Southern Cross, a prominent constellation in the South African night sky, is used for navigation and helps us locate the South Celestial Pole.

## Electric Circuits
Learners are able to build simple electric circuits and understand their components. A complete circuit requires a power source (like a battery), conductors (wires), a load (such as a light bulb or buzzer), and a switch to control the flow of electric current. The current flows from the positive terminal of the battery, through the components, and back to the negative terminal. In South Africa, the voltage supplied to homes is 220 volts. An open circuit, where a switch is off, stops the current from flowing.

## Weather and Climate
Learners are able to describe the difference between weather and climate. Weather refers to the state of the atmosphere at a specific time and place, for example, a hot, sunny day suitable for a braai. Climate describes the average weather patterns of a region over a long period, such as the warm, dry summers typical of the Western Cape. Factors influencing local climate include altitude, proximity to the ocean, and prevailing winds. The Cape Doctor, a strong southeasterly wind, is a regular feature of Cape Town's climate.

## Natural Resources and Sustainability
Learners are able to identify renewable and non-renewable resources and discuss the importance of sustainable living. Non-renewable resources, like coal and oil used to generate electricity in South Africa, are finite and will eventually be depleted. Renewable resources, such as solar and wind power, can be replenished naturally. Sustainable practices include conserving water and electricity, which helps save the country money and protects the environment. A simple action like switching off lights when not in the room helps reduce the national demand for electricity.
