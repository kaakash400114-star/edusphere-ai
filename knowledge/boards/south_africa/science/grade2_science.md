# South African (CAPS) Grade 2 Science
## Living and Non-living Things
Learners are able to group objects as living or non-living. Living things grow, eat, breathe, reproduce and move. A dog, a proteas flower, and a baobab tree are living. Non-living things do not grow or eat. A toy car, a rock from Table Mountain, and a classroom desk are non-living. Learners sort pictures of animals, plants, cars, and buildings into correct groups. They learn that living things need air, water, and food to survive, like a bird finding worms to eat.
## Materials and their Properties
Learners are able to describe simple properties of everyday materials. They explore materials like wood, metal, plastic, and cloth by feeling, seeing, and listening. Wood is hard and comes from trees. Metal is shiny and strong, like a rand coin. Plastic is light and can be many colours. Cloth is soft. Learners sort objects by their properties, such as grouping all hard things together. They understand that materials are chosen for jobs based on these properties, like a metal pot for cooking on a braai.
## Energy and Change
Learners are able to identify sources of energy and how it makes things move or work. They learn that the sun is a main source of energy that warms us and helps plants grow. Electricity makes a light bulb shine and a fan spin. Food gives us energy to run and play. Learners explore toys that move, like a wind-up car, and identify the energy source (muscle power). They understand that energy makes changes, like making water hot for tea, but we must use it carefully.
## The Senses and Our Body
Learners are able to name the five senses and how they help us learn about the world. We use our eyes to see, our ears to hear, our nose to smell, our tongue to taste, and our skin to touch. A Proteas cricket match is seen, heard, and felt. We smell braai fires and taste pap and wors. Learners play games like 'I Spy' using sight and 'Guess the Smell' using smell. They learn to use their senses to gather information, like using touch to find a smooth stone or hearing a bird chirping.
## Weather and Day and Night
Learners are able to describe daily and seasonal weather changes and relate them to day and night. They observe the weather each day, noting if it is sunny, cloudy, rainy, or windy. They learn that the sun rises in the morning (day) and sets in the evening (night). Weather changes with the seasons; summer is hot and rainy, winter is cold and dry. They connect these changes to their lives, like wearing a sun hat in summer or a warm jacket in winter.
## Caring for Our Environment
Learners are able to identify ways to care for their immediate environment. They learn that people must keep their surroundings clean to be healthy. They know to put rubbish in bins and not to litter in places like Kruger National Park or their schoolyard. Learners understand that plants and animals need a clean place to live. They discuss simple actions, like using both sides of paper to save trees, and why we must protect beautiful places like Table Mountain for everyone to enjoy.
