# South African (CAPS) Grade 9 Science
## The Nature of Matter
Learners explore the particle model of matter, distinguishing between atoms, molecules, and compounds. They understand that matter exists in three states—solid, liquid, and gas—and can change state through processes like melting and boiling. For example, ice (solid) melts into water (liquid) at 0°C and boils into steam (gas) at 100°C. The kinetic theory explains these changes: particles gain energy, move faster, and spread out. Matter can also be classified as pure (e.g., gold) or a mixture (e.g., air), with mixtures like a braai braai sauce being separable by physical means.
## Energy Transfer in Systems
Learners investigate different forms of energy and their transformations. They learn that energy cannot be created or destroyed but only converted, as stated by the law of conservation of energy. Examples include a Proteas cricket ball gaining kinetic energy when hit, or electrical energy converting to light and heat in a lamp. Heat transfer occurs via conduction, convection, and radiation; for instance, a metal spoon handle heats up by conduction in a pot of boiling water used for a traditional meal. Efficiency is calculated as useful output energy divided by total input energy, expressed as a percentage.
## Electric Circuits
Learners build and analyse both simple and parallel electrical circuits. They learn to use circuit symbols and draw circuit diagrams. Ohm’s Law (V = IR) is applied, where voltage (V) is in volts, current (I) in amps, and resistance (R) in ohms. For example, a 9 V battery connected to a 3 Ω resistor produces a current of 3 A. Learners also understand safety aspects like fuses and circuit breakers. The cost of electricity is calculated in kilowatt-hours (kWh); running a 2 kW appliance for 5 hours uses 10 kWh, costing about R24 at a rate of R2.40 per kWh.
## The Earth in the Solar System
This topic covers the Earth's position and motion in the solar system. Learners explain seasons in terms of the Earth’s 23.5° axial tilt and its orbit around the Sun. They understand that the Southern Hemisphere experiences summer in December because the Earth is tilted towards the Sun. The Moon's phases are caused by its revolution around the Earth. Observational skills are developed by tracking celestial objects; for example, the constellations visible from South Africa change with the seasons, and Table Mountain is a landmark for stargazing. The concept of light-years is introduced as a unit for measuring astronomical distances.
## Classification and Biodiversity
Learners classify living organisms into five kingdoms: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia. They use a dichotomous key to identify plants and animals based on observable characteristics. Biodiversity refers to the variety of life in an area, such as the unique fynbos biome in the Cape. Factors threatening local biodiversity include habitat loss from urban expansion and pollution. Learners are able to discuss the importance of conservation, like protecting the Table Mountain National Park, to maintain ecosystem services and genetic resources for future generations.
## Chemical Reactions and Equations
Learners define a chemical reaction as a process where substances change into new ones with different properties. They identify evidence of reactions, such as colour change, gas production, or temperature change. For instance, baking soda and vinegar react to produce carbon dioxide gas. Chemical equations are balanced to show the conservation of atoms; the reaction between hydrogen and oxygen to form water is 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. Learners distinguish between elements (e.g., gold, Au), compounds (e.g., water, H₂O), and mixtures, and are able to write word and balanced symbol equations for reactions like the rusting of iron.
