# South African (CAPS) Grade 11 Science
## Mechanical Energy and Work
Learners are able to calculate work and energy in different systems. Work is done when a force causes displacement, calculated as W = F∆x cosθ, with the unit the joule (J). Gravitational potential energy (Ep = mgh) is energy due to position, like a Proteas cricketer at the top of a jump. Kinetic energy (Ek = ½mv²) is energy of motion, such as a rand delivery van moving. The Principle of Conservation of Mechanical Energy states that total mechanical energy in a closed system is constant if only conservative forces act.

## Electric Circuits and Fields
Learners are able to analyse series and parallel circuits and understand electric fields. Ohm's Law (V = IR) relates voltage, current, and resistance. In a series circuit, current is constant and voltage divides. In a parallel circuit, voltage is constant and current divides. An electric field is a region where a charge experiences a force, represented by field lines. A capacitor stores charge, and its capacitance is C = Q/V. Common examples include the electrical wiring in a home or the battery in a cell phone.

## Chemical Bonding and Intermolecular Forces
Learners are able to explain the types of chemical bonds and intermolecular forces. Covalent bonds form through the sharing of electrons, like in water (H₂O) or methane (CH₄). Ionic bonds form through the transfer of electrons, creating ions, such as in sodium chloride (NaCl). Metallic bonds involve a sea of delocalised electrons. Intermolecular forces are forces *between* molecules, including London forces, dipole-dipole forces, and hydrogen bonding. Hydrogen bonding is strong and is seen in water, which affects its boiling point.

## Rates of Chemical Reactions
Learners are able to determine and explain factors affecting reaction rates. Reaction rate is the change in concentration of a reactant or product per unit time. Factors affecting rates include temperature (increased temperature increases rate), concentration (higher concentration increases rate), surface area (greater surface area increases rate), and the presence of a catalyst. A catalyst speeds up a reaction by providing an alternative reaction pathway with lower activation energy, without being used up, like enzymes in the human body.

## Human Impact and Environmental Sustainability
Learners are able to evaluate the impact of human activities on the environment and propose solutions. Air pollution from industries and vehicles contributes to acid rain and global warming. Water pollution from mining and agricultural runoff harms ecosystems. Soil degradation from poor farming practices reduces fertility. Sustainable solutions include using renewable energy sources like solar power, implementing effective waste management systems to recycle materials, and promoting conservation efforts to protect biodiversity, such as in the Table Mountain National Park.

## Transverse and Longitudinal Waves
Learners are able to describe the properties and behaviour of transverse and longitudinal waves. A wave transfers energy without transferring matter. In transverse waves, particles vibrate perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation, like light or waves on a string. In longitudinal waves, particles vibrate parallel to the direction of wave propagation, like sound. The speed of a wave is given by v = fλ, where f is frequency and λ is wavelength. Properties include reflection, refraction, diffraction, and interference, which explain phenomena like hearing a sound at a braai or seeing a rainbow after rain.
