# South African (CAPS) Grade 3 Science
## Materials and their Properties
Learners are able to group materials by observing their properties. They learn that materials can be hard (like a rock) or soft (like a sponge), or strong (like steel) or weak (like cardboard). They are able to sort materials into categories like transparent (clear, like a window), translucent (like a frosted bulb), and opaque (not see-through, like a wall). They identify that some materials, like plastic or metal, are waterproof and useful for making containers for liquids, while others, like fabric, absorb water.
## Changes in Materials
Learners are able to understand that materials can change, but they can often be changed back. They learn that mixing flour and water makes a paste, which can be dried and turned back into flour. They identify that some changes are permanent, like melting wax into a candle shape. They observe that physical changes (like tearing paper or folding metal) do not make a new substance. They are able to describe how a braai changes wood into ash and smoke, a change that cannot be reversed to get the wood back.
## Living Things and their Habitats
Learners are able to classify animals by where they live. They learn about animals living in water (fish), on land (zebra, lion), and in the air (eagle). They are able to describe a habitat as the place where an animal lives and gets its food. They identify that animals in the Karoo desert habitat are different from those in a wetland habitat. They learn that Table Mountain in Cape Town is a habitat with unique plants and animals, like the dassie, that are suited to its rocky slopes.
## The Human Body
Learners are able to identify the basic external parts of the human body. They learn the functions of key organs, such as the lungs for breathing and the heart for pumping blood. They are able to name the five senses (sight, hearing, smell, taste, touch) and link each to a body part (eyes, ears, nose, tongue, skin). They understand that looking after their bodies by eating healthy food and getting enough sleep helps them grow strong and stay healthy.
## Weather and the Seasons
Learners are able to describe different types of weather. They learn to measure weather using simple tools, like a thermometer to measure temperature in degrees Celsius (°C). They are able to identify the four seasons and link them to different weather patterns in South Africa, like hot, wet summers and cool, dry winters. They observe how the seasons affect their lives, such as wearing a jersey in winter and shorts in summer, and how the weather affects activities like a braai.
## Money and Exchange
Learners are able to identify and understand the purpose of South African money. They learn to recognise the rand (R) and cent (c) notes and coins. They are able to count money to make small purchases, like buying a sweet for R5. They understand that exchanging money is how people buy goods and services, similar to how the Proteas cricket team is paid to play for the country. They learn that people must earn money through work or receive it as a gift.
