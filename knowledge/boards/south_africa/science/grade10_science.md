# South African (CAPS) Grade 10 Science

## Chemical Change
Learners are able to define chemical change as the formation of new substances with different properties. They learn to identify signs of chemical change, such as colour change, gas production, temperature change, or precipitate formation. For example, burning wood changes it to ash and smoke, a new substance. The reaction between vinegar (acetic acid) and baking soda (sodium bicarbonate) produces carbon dioxide gas, a clear sign of a chemical reaction. These changes are usually difficult or impossible to reverse.

## Newton's Laws of Motion
Learners are able to apply Newton's three laws of motion to explain everyday phenomena. Newton's First Law (inertia) states that an object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion unless acted upon by an unbalanced force, like a Proteas cricket ball stopping when the fielder catches it. The Second Law (F=ma) explains that force equals mass times acceleration, such as pushing a trolley at the shops. The Third Law states that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction, like the recoil felt when firing a rifle.

## The Water Cycle and Management
Learners are able to describe the processes of evaporation, condensation, and precipitation in the water cycle. They learn that evaporation from places like Table Mountain's forests turns water into vapour, which cools and condenses to form clouds. Precipitation then returns this water to Earth as rain, vital for agriculture. In South Africa, managing this cycle is critical. Water is a precious resource, and understanding it helps prevent shortages, ensuring enough water for household use, industries, and the ecosystem.

## Transverse and Longitudinal Waves
Learners are able to differentiate between transverse and longitudinal waves. A transverse wave, like a wave on a rope or light, oscillates at a right angle to the direction of energy transfer. A longitudinal wave, like sound travelling through air, oscillates parallel to the direction of energy transfer. Learners learn that sound cannot travel through a vacuum, like space, because it needs a medium. The speed of sound in air is approximately 343 m/s, which is why you see lightning before you hear thunder during a storm over the Highveld.

## Chemical Bonding and Reactions
Learners are able to explain how atoms form chemical bonds to achieve stable electron configurations. They learn about ionic bonding, where electrons are transferred (e.g., sodium and chlorine forming table salt), and covalent bonding, where electrons are shared (e.g., two hydrogen atoms forming H₂ gas). They understand how to balance simple chemical equations to show the conservation of mass. For example, the reaction between hydrogen and oxygen to form water must be balanced as 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O.

## Electricity and Circuits
Learners are able to construct and draw simple electric circuits, identifying components like cells, batteries, resistors, and bulbs. They learn Ohm's Law (V = I × R), which states that voltage equals current times resistance. Using this, they can calculate the current in a circuit with a 12-volt battery and a 6-ohm resistor (I = 2A). They understand the difference between series circuits (one path for current) and parallel circuits (multiple paths), which is why the lights in your house use parallel circuits—if one fails, the others stay on.
