# South African (CAPS) Grade 6 Science
## Classification of Living Things
Learners are able to classify living organisms into five main kingdoms: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia. This is based on shared characteristics such as cell type, number of cells, and how they obtain energy. For example, the proteas cricket is an animal, while the mushroom growing near a braai is a fungus. Bacteria, which can cause illnesses, are single-celled organisms found in the Monera kingdom. This system helps us understand the diversity of life on Earth, from the smallest bacteria to the largest animals.

## Mixtures and Solutions
Learners are able to identify and distinguish between mixtures and solutions. A mixture is when two or more substances are combined but not chemically joined, like sand mixed with water. A solution is a special type of mixture where one substance dissolves completely in another, like sugar dissolving in water to make a refreshing drink. Many things we use are mixtures, such as air and the alloy in a South African rand coin. Understanding this helps in separating materials for different uses, like purifying water.

## The Solar System
Learners are able to name and describe the main features of the planets in our solar system. The sun is the star at the centre, with planets orbiting around it in a specific order. The inner planets, like Mercury and Venus, are made of rock and are very hot. The outer planets, like Jupiter, are gas giants. Earth is the only planet known to have life, and it has one moon. This system is held together by gravity, which keeps all the planets in their paths, just like Table Mountain is held on the Earth.

## Energy Transfer and Electricity
Learners are able to understand that energy can be transferred in different ways, including through electricity. Electricity is a form of energy that flows through materials like copper wires. It can be made in power stations and is used to power homes and schools for light and appliances. A simple circuit has a power source, like a battery, and a path for the electricity to flow, such as a bulb. Conductors allow electricity to pass, while insulators, like plastic, stop it. This knowledge helps us use electricity safely and efficiently.

## Weather and Climate
Learners are able to describe the difference between weather and climate. Weather refers to the atmospheric conditions over a short period, like today being sunny and hot. Climate, however, is the average weather pattern in a place over many years, like South Africa’s climate being generally warm and dry. Weather elements include temperature, rainfall, and wind. These are measured using tools like a thermometer and a rain gauge. Understanding weather helps us plan for activities, like having a braai on a clear day.

## Materials and Their Properties
Learners are able to describe the properties of different materials and relate them to their uses. Materials can be natural, like wood from trees, or man-made, like plastic. Important properties include whether a material is strong, transparent, or waterproof. For instance, metal is strong and used to build cars, while glass is transparent and used for windows. We choose materials based on these properties; a waterproof raincoat keeps you dry, just like the sandstone of Table Mountain weathers erosion over time.
