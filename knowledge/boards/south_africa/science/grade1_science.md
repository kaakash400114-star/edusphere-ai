# South African (CAPS) Grade 1 Science
## Living things
Learners are able to identify common living and non-living things in their environment. Living things grow, eat, breathe, reproduce and move on their own, like the birds at the park or their puppy at home. Non-living things, like a toy car or a rock on the Table Mountain path, do not do these things on their own. Learners learn to sort objects into these two groups by checking for these life processes. They use simple observations to classify things in their classroom and schoolyard, understanding the basic difference between a living Protea flower and a plastic one.

## My body
Learners are able to name and identify the main external parts of their body. They learn that their body has different parts, each with a special job, like eyes for seeing and hands for holding. They use songs and games to learn the names: head, shoulders, knees, and toes. Learners also learn to name their five senses: sight, hearing, smell, taste, and touch, and identify which body part is used for each sense, like using their nose to smell a braai and their ears to hear the Proteas cricket team on the radio.

## Materials and their properties
Learners are able to identify and sort everyday materials based on their properties. They learn to observe and describe materials like wood, metal, plastic, and cloth. For example, a metal spoon is hard and cold, while a wool jersey is soft and warm. They explore how materials feel and look, sorting them into groups like 'hard' and 'soft'. Learners can use simple terms to describe materials, such as 'smooth' for a glass or 'rough' for a tree bark, and explain why a rand coin is made of metal.

## Weather and the seasons
Learners are able to observe and describe daily weather patterns. They learn to identify different types of weather like sunny, cloudy, rainy, or windy. They use simple weather symbols to show the day's weather. Learners link seasons to weather: summer is hot and dry (perfect for a braai), winter is cold and wet. They can describe clothing for different weather, like wearing a jersey on a cold day or a hat in the sun, and observe changes in the environment, like how the Table Mountain looks different in summer rain versus winter mist.

## Light and shadow
Learners are able to understand that light comes from a source. They identify that the sun is a natural source of light and a torch is a man-made one. They learn that light travels in straight lines and is blocked by objects, creating shadows. Learners make shadows using their hands and a light source, noticing how a shadow is bigger or smaller depending on how close the object is to the light. They can explain why their shadow is long in the morning sun and shorter at midday.

## Caring for the environment
Learners are able to identify ways to care for their environment at home and school. They learn that litter must be put in a bin to keep the community clean, just like the volunteers who clean up beaches. They understand that plants and animals need to be protected. Learners can explain why water should not be wasted and list things they can do, like turning off a tap or reusing a box for toys. They learn about the importance of respecting nature, like not picking all the Proteas in a field so that more can grow for everyone to see.
