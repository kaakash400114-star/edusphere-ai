# South African (CAPS) Grade 5 Science

## Matter and Materials
Learners are able to describe different materials and their properties. They learn that materials can be grouped according to their characteristics like strength, transparency, and conductivity. For example, glass is transparent but strong, while plastic is transparent but less strong. They explore natural materials like wood from trees and man-made materials like plastic bottles. They understand that materials can be changed physically, like melting ice, or chemically, like burning wood. Using a local example, they compare the properties of clay for making pots and the plastic used for making a school bag.

## Energy and Change
Learners are able to explain different forms of energy and how they change from one form to another. They identify light, heat, and sound energy in their daily lives. For instance, a light bulb changes electrical energy into light and heat energy. They learn about renewable energy sources, like the sun which gives solar power. A practical example is feeling the warmth from a braai, which is chemical energy changing into heat and light. They also explore how energy is conserved, meaning it is not lost but only changes forms, like when you eat food (chemical energy) to run (kinetic energy).

## Life and Living
Learners are able to classify living things and describe their basic needs. They group animals into vertebrates (with a backbone) and invertebrates (without a backbone), using examples like a kudu (vertebrate) and a snail (invertebrate). They learn that all living things need food, water, air, and a suitable place to live. They understand the role of producers (like grass) and consumers (like a rabbit) in a simple food chain. A South African example is the Proteas flower, a producer that provides food for insects, which are then eaten by birds like a hadeda.

## Earth and Beyond
Learners are able to describe the Earth’s features and movements. They identify the main layers of the Earth: the crust, mantle, and core. They learn that the Earth rotates on its axis, causing day and night, and revolves around the sun, causing the seasons. They study major landforms like Table Mountain, which is a famous mountain in South Africa. They also understand the importance of weather and how it changes, describing different types of weather like sunny, rainy, or windy days experienced across the country.

## Structures and Systems
Learners are able to identify and explain different systems in the human body and simple structures. They explore the skeletal system, learning that it provides support and protection, with the skull protecting the brain. They also study the digestive system, understanding how food moves from the mouth to the stomach and intestines. They link this to health, explaining the importance of eating a balanced meal with local foods like pap and morogo. They also analyse simple structures like a bridge, identifying how its design helps it support weight, much like the cables of the Table Mountain Aerial Cableway support its carriages.

## The Material World in Society
Learners are able to understand how science and technology are used in society and affect the environment. They learn about the use of money, like the rand, in buying goods and services, linking it to economic activities. They explore simple technologies, such as a kettle that uses electricity to boil water. They discuss the positive and negative impacts of technology, such as cars providing transport but causing pollution. They understand the importance of caring for the environment by recycling waste materials, showing how human actions can help protect natural resources like clean water and air for everyone.
