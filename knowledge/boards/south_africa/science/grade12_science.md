# South African (CAPS) Grade 12 Science

## Electrochemical Cells
Learners are able to construct and analyse voltaic and electrolytic cells. In a voltaic cell like the Daniell cell, zinc oxidises at the anode, releasing electrons that flow through an external circuit to reduce copper ions at the cathode. The cell potential (E°) is calculated as E°cathode - E°anode. For example, a standard cell using zinc and copper has a potential of approximately +1.10 V. The electrolyte provides ions for charge transfer. In South Africa, such principles are vital for understanding battery technology in local energy solutions, including those used for portable devices and backup power in homes.

## Organic Chemistry: Functional Groups and Reactions
Learners are able to identify and predict reactions of key functional groups. Alkanes like propane undergo substitution reactions with halogens under UV light, forming halogenoalkanes such as chloroethane. Alkenes like ethene undergo addition reactions; for example, with bromine water, they form dibromoethane, decolourising the solution. Alcohols like ethanol can be oxidised to carboxylic acids. Understanding these reactions is essential for the pharmaceutical and agricultural sectors in South Africa, which contribute significantly to the economy and food security.

## Newton's Laws and Momentum
Learners are able to apply Newton's laws to analyse motion. Newton's first law states that an object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion unless acted upon by a net force. The second law is expressed as Fnet = ma. Momentum is conserved in a closed system. For instance, during a Proteas cricket match, when a ball is hit, the momentum is transferred from the bat to the ball. The impulse experienced by the ball is equal to the change in its momentum, determining how far it travels towards the boundary.

## Chemical Equilibrium and Reaction Rates
Learners are able to explain factors affecting equilibrium and rates. Le Chatelier’s principle states that a system at equilibrium will shift to counteract a change in concentration, pressure, or temperature. Increasing the temperature of an endothermic reaction shifts the equilibrium to the right. The reaction rate is influenced by temperature, concentration, and surface area. In industrial processes in South Africa, like the production of ammonia via the Haber process, these principles are optimised to maximise yield and efficiency, impacting the fertiliser industry critical for agriculture.

## The Human Eye and Optical Phenomena
Learners are able to describe the structure of the eye and correct optical defects. The eye has a cornea, lens, and retina that focus light. Short-sightedness (myopia) is corrected by a concave lens, while long-sightedness (hypermetropia) uses a convex lens. Total internal reflection occurs when light travels from a denser to a less dense medium at an angle greater than the critical angle. This principle is applied in fibre optics, which are used in high-speed internet infrastructure, connecting businesses and homes across South Africa, from Table Mountain to rural areas.

## Electrostatics and Electric Fields
Learners are able to calculate electrostatic forces and describe electric fields. Coulomb's law states that the force between two point charges is F = kq1q2/r². An electric field is a region where a charge experiences a force, represented by field lines pointing from positive to negative charges. For example, the static charge from rubbing a balloon on hair can create a small electric field capable of picking up small pieces of paper. This understanding of static electricity is relevant for preventing damage to electronic equipment during a braai, where static discharge could interfere with sensitive devices.
