# South African (CAPS) Grade 4 English

## Listening and Speaking
Learners are able to listen attentively and speak clearly in formal and informal situations. They practise taking turns in group discussions, sharing ideas about topics like their favourite braai foods or the Proteas cricket team. Learners learn to express their opinions and ask relevant questions. They are introduced to giving short, prepared oral presentations, for example, describing a place like Table Mountain or explaining how to buy a snack at the tuck shop with rand. The focus is on using correct pronunciation and audible voice projection.

## Reading and Viewing
Learners are able to read and understand a variety of texts. They read stories, poems, and informational texts with increasing fluency and accuracy. Learners are taught to identify the main idea and supporting details in a paragraph. They use context clues to understand the meaning of unfamiliar words. Learners also learn to read maps, charts, and pictures, interpreting information to answer questions, such as finding a route to a local landmark or understanding a diagram of a rand coin.

## Writing and Presenting
Learners are able to write for different purposes and audiences. They learn to write short, coherent paragraphs with a topic sentence and supporting details. They are introduced to writing different text types, such as friendly letters, simple stories, and descriptions. Learners practise using correct grammar, punctuation, and spelling. For example, they might write a letter to a friend describing a weekend braai or a short story set in a place like Durban, using simple and compound sentences.

## Language Structures and Conventions
Learners are able to understand and use the basic structures of English. They revise and extend their knowledge of nouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs. They learn to use tenses correctly, for example, changing a sentence from present ("I play") to past ("I played"). Learners are introduced to punctuation, such as using question marks and exclamation marks appropriately. They also focus on subject-verb agreement, ensuring that sentences like "The boys play soccer" are grammatically correct.

## Language Usage and Usage
Learners are able to use language effectively in a variety of contexts. They learn to use common homophones correctly, such as "their," "there," and "they're." They are introduced to alphabetical order for words and simple dictionary skills. Learners learn to use connecting words like "and," "but," and "because" to join ideas. They practise using descriptive language to create pictures with words, for example, describing the view from Table Mountain or the sound of a crowd at a Proteas match.

## Language in Context
Learners are able to use language to explore and understand their environment and other learning areas. They learn to discuss topics from other subjects, such as explaining a science project or describing a historical event. They use language to follow instructions, such as those for a recipe for vetkoek or a game. Learners also learn to interpret and respond to advertisements or simple news reports, connecting language to real-life situations like buying a cool drink with rand.
