# South African (CAPS) Grade 5 English

## Language Structures
Learners are able to identify and use parts of speech correctly in sentences. They will understand nouns (proper and common, e.g., Table Mountain, mountain), verbs (action words, e.g., run, braai), adjectives (describing words, e.g., fast, delicious), adverbs (how actions are done, e.g., quickly, loudly), and conjunctions (joining words, e.g., and, but). They learn to construct sentences with a subject, verb, and object. For example, 'The Proteas team bats quickly.' They are also able to use pronouns like 'he', 'she', and 'it' to replace nouns.

## Listening and Speaking
Learners are able to listen actively for specific information in stories, poems, and conversations. They will follow multi-step instructions, such as those for a braai. They are able to express opinions clearly and participate in group discussions. They learn to ask and answer questions about a topic, using examples from South Africa. They practice telling short stories using descriptive words and correct pronunciation of words like 'rand' and 'veld'. They also learn to speak confidently in front of the class, sharing ideas about local places like the Kruger National Park.

## Reading and Phonics
Learners are able to read and understand a variety of texts with increasing fluency. They will read stories, poems, and informational texts with South African themes, such as the history of the rand or the life of Nelson Mandela. They learn to decode unfamiliar words using phonics rules, including vowel sounds and consonant blends. They are able to identify the main idea and supporting details in a paragraph. For example, after reading about a Proteas match, they can state the main idea, which is the team's victory. They also practice their reading skills aloud with proper expression.

## Writing
Learners are able to write a variety of texts for different purposes. They will write short paragraphs, friendly letters, and simple stories using correct sentence structure and punctuation. They learn to plan their writing by brainstorming ideas and organizing them into a logical sequence. They are able to describe a place like Table Mountain using adjectives and sensory details. They also practice writing instructions, for example, how to make a traditional potjie, using imperative verbs. They are encouraged to use correct spelling of common words and to edit their work for mistakes.

## Viewing and Visual Literacy
Learners are able to interpret and analyse visual texts such as advertisements, photographs, and cartoons. They will identify the main message in a poster advertising a local festival. They learn to understand how images and words work together to convey meaning. For example, they can describe the mood of a photograph of a braai with family and friends. They are able to compare and contrast different versions of the same image. They also learn to create their own simple visual texts, like a poster for a school event, using symbols and pictures that are relevant to South African culture.

## Language in Context
Learners are able to use language to explore and express ideas about their environment and experiences. They will use topic-specific vocabulary related to subjects like Geography and History. They learn to engage with texts that reflect South African diversity and heritage. They are able to discuss the values shown in a story about sharing a meal. They practice using idiomatic expressions common in South African English, such as 'now now' or 'lekker'. They are also able to use reference materials, like a simple dictionary, to find the meaning of new words like ' Proteas ' or 'rand '.
