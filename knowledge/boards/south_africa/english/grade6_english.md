# South African (CAPS) Grade 6 English

## Listening and Speaking
Learners are able to listen actively for main ideas and supporting details in a variety of texts. They can follow multi-step oral instructions, such as those used for a traditional braai. Learners are able to give well-structured oral presentations using a logical sequence, maintaining eye contact with their audience. They participate effectively in group discussions, sharing personal viewpoints on topics like the performance of the Proteas national cricket team, while also respecting and listening to the opinions of their peers.

## Reading and Viewing
Learners are able to read and view a wide range of texts for pleasure and information. They understand and can explain the main idea of a text using their own words. Learners can identify the setting, characters, and plot in a story. They use strategies like skimming and scanning to find specific information quickly. When viewing a video about Table Mountain, they can identify the main purpose and point of view of the creator, distinguishing between fact and opinion.

## Writing and Presenting
Learners are able to write a variety of texts for different purposes and audiences. They can write a descriptive paragraph using sensory details to describe a scene, for instance, a bustling market. Learners can write a formal letter of complaint to a service provider. They use appropriate punctuation, including commas in lists and for direct address. They are able to edit and proofread their own writing to correct spelling, grammar, and punctuation errors.

## Language Structure and Conventions
Learners are able to understand and use parts of speech correctly. They identify and use verbs in different tenses (past, present, future). Learners can identify and use adjectives and adverbs to add detail. They understand and use conjunctions like 'and', 'but', and 'because' to join sentences. Learners can correctly use subject-verb agreement and pronouns in their writing. They are introduced to the correct use of dialogue marks to show what a character is saying.

## Language in Context
Learners are able to use language to function effectively in a variety of contexts. They understand and interpret advertisements, identifying the target audience and persuasive techniques used. Learners can read and interpret information from graphs, charts, and timetables, such as a household budget showing expenses in Rands. They are able to read maps and identify key features. Learners understand the conventions of different text types, like recipes, instructions, and news reports.

## Thinking and Reasoning
Learners are able to read between the lines and infer information that is not directly stated in a text. They can predict what might happen next in a story based on clues provided. Learners distinguish between fact and opinion in a news article about a sporting event. They identify the author's purpose in writing a text. Learners can sequence events in a story in the correct order and categorise information into groups based on shared characteristics.
