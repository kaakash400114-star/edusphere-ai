# South African (CAPS) Grade 8 English
## Language Structures and Conventions
Learners are able to identify and apply the correct use of the future simple and continuous tenses in writing and speech. They learn to distinguish between 'will' + base verb ('The Proteas will win the match') and 'will be' + present participle ('My family will be braaiing this weekend'). The curriculum requires understanding how to form questions and negatives in both tenses. Students practice constructing sentences to express predictions, promises, and future plans, using local contexts like 'We will visit Table Mountain during the holidays' to ensure practical application.
## Reading and Viewing
Learners are able to read and view a variety of texts to identify main ideas and supporting details. They focus on understanding the purpose and audience of different texts, such as news articles about local events or advertisements promoting South African products. Students are taught to use skimming and scanning techniques to find information efficiently. The curriculum exposes them to examples from diverse sources, including digital media and printed materials, and requires them to summarise a passage in their own words, demonstrating comprehension of a text about the rand's fluctuating value.
## Writing and Presenting
Learners are able to write a well-structured paragraph that includes a clear topic sentence, supporting sentences with evidence, and a concluding sentence. They learn to use appropriate transitional words to create cohesion. This involves planning a paragraph using a mind map or bullet points before drafting. The curriculum requires formal writing conventions, including correct punctuation and varied sentence structures. A typical task is to write a descriptive paragraph about a local experience, such as 'A busy day at a local market in Cape Town', using sensory details.
## Literature
Learners are able to analyse and respond to literary texts, including a prescribed short story and poem from South African or African writers. They learn to identify elements such as character, setting, theme, and plot. Students are required to use evidence from the text to support their interpretations in written or oral responses. The curriculum emphasizes comparing the experiences of characters in the texts to their own lives, and a key example involves discussing themes of courage and resilience in a story about a young person overcoming challenges in a South African community.
## Thinking and Reasoning Skills
Learners are able to gather, evaluate, and synthesise information from various sources to form a reasoned opinion. They learn to distinguish between fact and opinion in media texts, such as news reports or online articles. This involves identifying the writer's bias and assessing the reliability of information. A practical application is evaluating a source on the economic impact of tourism in South Africa, presenting a balanced view that acknowledges both benefits and challenges using specific data points.
## Language in Context
Learners are able to understand and explain the connotations of words and their influence on meaning. They explore how word choice can evoke positive or negative feelings in a reader or listener. The curriculum requires identifying figurative language, such as similes and metaphors, in texts. A key activity is analysing advertisements to see how words like 'premium' or 'authentic' are used to persuade consumers, and students are tasked with rewriting sentences to change their emotional impact from a positive description of a braai to a neutral one.
