# South African (CAPS) Grade 10 English
## Transactional Writing: Formal and Informal Texts
Learners are able to produce a variety of written texts for different purposes and audiences. This includes writing formal business letters using correct structure and language, as well as crafting detailed and coherent emails. For informal writing, learners create expressive personal narratives and engaging social media posts. A key skill is adapting tone, style, and structure based on the context. For example, a letter to the City of Cape Town about a pothole near Table Mountain requires formal language, while a post about the Proteas’ latest win can be informal and celebratory.
## Literature: Novel Study
Learners are able to analyse a prescribed novel, such as *Cry, the Beloved Country* by Alan Paton, through critical engagement. They explore the author’s use of character, theme, setting, and symbolism to convey meaning. Learners investigate how social issues like injustice and resilience are portrayed. They compare and contrast characters and motivations, and discuss the novel’s relevance to the South African context. This deepens their understanding of how literature reflects and critiques society, enabling them to form and articulate personal interpretations with textual evidence.
## Grammar and Language Structures
Learners are able to apply grammatical rules with precision in their speech and writing. This includes correctly using parts of speech such as nouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs to build effective sentences. A key focus is on mastering sentence structure, including simple, compound, and complex sentences. Learners also analyse and use parts of speech, clauses, and phrases to avoid common errors. For instance, they learn to correctly place commas and use subject-verb agreement in sentences about local events or when discussing the value of the rand.
## Comprehension and Critical Reading
Learners are able to read and comprehend a variety of texts with increasing depth. They identify and explain the main idea, supporting details, and implicit meaning. Learners analyse the writer’s purpose, tone, and audience. They are taught to differentiate between fact and opinion and to make inferences based on the text. For example, when reading a newspaper article about national economic growth, learners can critically evaluate the information presented, identify the writer’s perspective, and draw conclusions about its impact on everyday life in South Africa.
## Creative Writing: Poetry and Stories
Learners are able to create original imaginative texts. They write expressive poems using a variety of figurative language techniques such as simile, metaphor, personification, and imagery to evoke emotion and create vivid pictures. They also craft short stories with a clear plot, engaging characters, and a well-developed setting. Learners experiment with different narrative perspectives and moods. For example, a poem could capture the spirit of a traditional braai with friends, while a short story might explore a young person’s experience of overcoming a challenge in a local community.
## Oral Communication and Presentation
Learners are able to communicate information, ideas, and opinions effectively through oral presentations. They structure and deliver well-organised speeches, reports, and oral arguments using appropriate language and non-verbal communication. They learn to speak confidently and audibly, and to listen actively and respond thoughtfully. Learners also participate in group discussions and debates, presenting arguments clearly while respecting the views of others. For instance, they might prepare a speech on the importance of heritage sites or debate the merits of national sports teams like the Proteas.
