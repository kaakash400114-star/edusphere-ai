# South African (CAPS) Grade 7 English

## Language Structures: Parts of Speech
Learners identify and use the eight parts of speech in sentences. A noun like 'braai' or 'Proteas' names a person, place, or thing. A verb such as 'travel' or 'win' expresses an action. An adjective, for example, 'beautiful' or 'red', describes a noun. A pronoun like 'he' or 'they' replaces a noun. An adverb, such as 'quickly', describes a verb, adjective, or another adverb. A preposition like 'over' or 'at' shows a relationship between nouns. A conjunction like 'and' or 'but' connects words or groups of words. An interjection like 'Wow!' expresses strong emotion.

## Writing: Narrative Composition
Learners write imaginative stories with a clear plot, characters, and setting. They plan using a story map, focusing on a conflict and resolution. They write paragraphs, ensuring each develops a single idea. They use descriptive language, for example, describing Table Mountain's silhouette or the taste of boerewors. They use dialogue to show characters interacting. They write with a consistent tense, usually past tense. They conclude their stories effectively, reflecting on the events and learning a valuable lesson about friendship, courage, or honesty.

## Comprehension and Close Reading
Learners read and understand a variety of texts, including poems, stories, and factual reports. They answer literal questions about who, what, where, and when. They infer information by reading between the lines to understand characters' feelings or unstated reasons. They identify the main idea and supporting details in a paragraph. They understand the effect of figurative language like similes ('as brave as a lion') and metaphors ('a sea of faces'). They use context clues to determine the meaning of unfamiliar words and phrases.

## Language Conventions: Punctuation and Spelling
Learners apply punctuation rules correctly to improve clarity in their writing. They use full stops, question marks, and exclamation marks to end sentences. They use commas to separate items in a list, for example, 'chakalaka, pap, and wors'. They use commas to separate clauses in complex sentences. They use apostrophes for contractions like 'it's' and for showing possession, for example, 'the Proteas' captain'. They spell common words correctly and use a dictionary to check unfamiliar words, ensuring financial terms like 'rand' are spelled accurately.

## Vocabulary Development
Learners expand their vocabulary through reading, writing, and discussion. They learn the meaning and spelling of new words related to different themes, for example, 'heritage', 'democracy', and 'biodiversity'. They find synonyms and antonyms to enrich their language. They identify and use words with similar meanings, like 'big', 'large', and 'enormous'. They understand and use idiomatic expressions common in South Africa, such as 'howzit' or ' lekker '. They learn prefixes and suffixes to determine the meaning of unfamiliar words, for example, 'unhappy' or 'careless'.

## Viewing and Presenting
Learners interpret and respond to visual texts like advertisements, cartoons, and photographs. They identify the main message and the target audience in an advert for a local brand. They analyse the use of images, colours, and text to convey meaning. They plan and deliver short oral presentations, such as a report on a national hero or a persuasive speech on why recycling is important. They speak clearly and audibly, using appropriate language and body language. They listen actively to others and ask relevant questions.
