# South African (CAPS) Grade 12 English
## Literary Genres and Contexts
Learners are able to analyse the formal elements and historical contexts of novels, plays, and poetry prescribed by CAPS. They examine character development, narrative structure, and symbolic devices. For instance, when studying a play set during apartheid, learners will analyse how characters' dialogue reflects societal pressures. They apply literary terms like irony, foreshadowing, and metaphor to deepen their understanding of texts set in diverse South African locales, from the urban sprawl of Johannesburg to the rural landscapes of the Eastern Cape.

## Essay and Transactional Writing
Learners are able to compose coherent, well-structured essays and various transactional texts. They develop arguments with clear theses, supported by relevant textual evidence and logical reasoning. For formal letters, such as a job application or a complaint to a local municipality, they adhere to specific formats and tones. They learn to write for different purposes, for example, drafting a persuasive speech about conservation efforts in the Kruger National Park or a formal report on the economic impact of hosting a Proteas cricket series.

## Language Structures and Conventions
Learners are able to apply complex grammatical rules and conventions accurately in their writing. They master the correct use of tenses, particularly the subjunctive mood in conditional statements. They analyse and use advanced punctuation, such as semicolons and colons, to structure complex sentences. This includes understanding the nuances of South African English idioms and phrases, ensuring their writing is polished and error-free for both academic and professional contexts, like a business proposal valued in Rands.

## Oral Communication and Presentation Skills
Learners are able to deliver well-researched, persuasive oral presentations for a variety of audiences. They structure speeches with clear introductions, logically developed bodies, and impactful conclusions. They learn to employ appropriate vocal techniques, such as tone, pace, and emphasis, to engage listeners. Practical exercises include preparing and delivering a formal debate on a current social issue or giving an informative presentation about the cultural significance of a traditional braai, demonstrating confident and articulate public speaking.

## Comprehensive and Close Reading Strategies
Learners are able to employ strategies for both comprehensive and close reading of demanding texts. They identify main ideas, supporting details, and an author's implicit or explicit purpose. During close reading, they dissect figurative language, connotation, and syntax to uncover deeper layers of meaning. For example, they might analyse a poem about Table Mountain, not just for its description of the landmark, but for the symbolic representations of hope or isolation embedded within its verses.

## Critical Thinking and Media Analysis
Learners are able to critically evaluate information from a range of media sources, distinguishing between fact and opinion. They identify bias, propaganda, and logical fallacies in articles, advertisements, and news broadcasts. They learn to deconstruct how media shapes public perception on local issues, such as political campaigns or social justice movements. This involves analysing visual and textual elements to understand the power of language and imagery in constructing a particular view of reality in the South African context.
