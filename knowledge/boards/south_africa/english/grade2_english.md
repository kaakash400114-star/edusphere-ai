# South African (CAPS) Grade 2 English

## Listening and Speaking
Learners are able to listen attentively for a sustained period and respond appropriately. They learn to follow two-step instructions, such as 'Take out your red book and open to page five'. They practice greeting others formally and informally, and are encouraged to share news about their weekend using complete sentences. Learners begin to use descriptive words, like 'big' and 'loud', when describing objects or events, such as the Proteas winning a cricket match. They participate in class discussions, raising their hands to speak and listening to their friends' ideas.

## Reading and Phonics
Learners are able to recognise all letter sounds and blend them to read simple CVC (consonant-vowel-consonant) words. They learn to read high-frequency sight words like 'the', 'was', and 'said'. They identify and read words with common vowel digraphs such as 'ai' in 'rain' and 'ee' in 'see'. Learners read aloud with increasing fluency and expression. They can point to each word as they read and make sense of simple sentences about familiar topics, like visiting a braai with their family.

## Writing Handwriting and Spelling
Learners are able to form all upper and lower-case letters correctly, using a standard print style. They learn to write their own name and address clearly and neatly. They practice spelling simple, common words using their knowledge of letter sounds. Learners copy simple sentences from the board, using correct letter formation and spacing between words. They are introduced to punctuation, learning to use a capital letter at the beginning of a sentence and a full stop at the end. An example sentence is 'We see Table Mountain from the car'.

## Language Structures
Learners are able to understand and use basic grammatical structures in speaking and writing. They learn to identify and use nouns for people, places, and things, like 'teacher', 'school', and 'Table Mountain'. They practice using plural forms, adding '-s' or '-es', for example, 'rand' becomes 'rands'. Learners learn to ask questions using question words like 'What', 'Where', and 'When'. They begin to form simple past tense sentences, using words like 'played' or 'watched'.

## Comprehension
Learners are able to understand information read to them and from simple texts. They listen to a short story and can answer literal questions, such as 'What was the main character's name?'. They identify the main idea of a story or a simple paragraph. Learners can recall details from a text, like 'What colour was the ball?'. They make simple predictions about what might happen next in a story. They can also match pictures to words or simple sentences from a familiar text, like one about a braai.

## Language in Use
Learners are able to use language to interact effectively in different situations. They practice polite phrases, such as 'please', 'thank you', and 'excuse me'. They learn to give simple descriptions of objects using words like 'soft' and 'round'. Learners can share their likes and dislikes, for example, 'I like eating mieliepap'. They begin to use language to solve simple problems in the classroom, like 'Can you please pass me the blue pencil?' They are introduced to counting in isiXhosa and isiZulu during routine activities.
