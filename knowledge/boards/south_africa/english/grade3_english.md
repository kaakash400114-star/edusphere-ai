# South African (CAPS) Grade 3 English

## Phonics and Spelling
Learners are able to use phonics to decode and spell unfamiliar words. They learn to recognise and use long vowel sounds like in 'cake', 'me', and 'bike'. They are introduced to consonant digraphs 'ch', 'sh', 'th', and 'wh' and blend them with vowels to build words. Learners practice spelling common words using these patterns, such as 'chair', 'shop', 'this', and 'what'. They apply this knowledge to spell simple two-syllable words like 'tabletop' and 'football', connecting written symbols to their corresponding sounds accurately.

## Reading Comprehension
Learners are able to read and understand short texts with simple story structures. They read stories set in South African contexts, like a family having a braai. Learners are able to identify the main character, setting, and sequence of events in a story. They answer direct questions about who, what, where, and why. They can also find specific information in a text, such as the names of Proteas players or the animals seen near Table Mountain, demonstrating literal comprehension of the content read.

## Writing Sentences and Paragraphs
Learners are able to write complete sentences that start with a capital letter and end with a full stop. They are able to write a short paragraph of three to five sentences on a given topic. Learners use linking words like 'and' and 'because' to join their ideas. They write about personal experiences, such as a fun day at the beach, or familiar topics, using simple vocabulary and correct subject-verb agreement to express their thoughts clearly and in sequence.

## Language Structures
Learners are able to use nouns, verbs, and adjectives correctly in sentences. They identify nouns for people (teacher), places (school), and things (ball). They use action verbs like 'run', 'read', and 'play' and can describe nouns using adjectives, for example, 'a big ball' or 'a happy dog'. Learners are able to form the plural of nouns, adding 's' or 'es', such as 'rand' to 'rands' or 'box' to 'boxes', showing an understanding of basic grammatical rules for word formation.

## Language in Use
Learners are able to listen to and follow simple, two-step oral instructions, for instance, 'Please pick up the book and put it on the table'. They are able to speak clearly in short turns, sharing their ideas about familiar topics using simple sentences. They practice using common greetings and polite expressions. They are able to participate in class discussions by answering questions and asking simple ones, using complete sentences to express their thoughts and opinions respectfully.

## Language Awareness and Vocabulary
Learners are able to expand their vocabulary by learning new words related to different themes. They learn topic-specific words for subjects like Mathematics (add, subtract, number) and Life Skills (healthy, exercise, family). They are able to match words to pictures, for example, matching 'Table Mountain' to its image. Learners practice using these new words in sentences. They are also able to identify and use high-frequency words that appear often in reading texts, such as 'the', 'and', and 'said'.
