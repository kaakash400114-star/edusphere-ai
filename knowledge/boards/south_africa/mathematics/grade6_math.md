# South African (CAPS) Grade 6 Mathematics

## Whole Number Operations
Learners are able to solve problems involving whole numbers using all four operations. They consolidate knowledge of addition and subtraction, including regrouping. For multiplication, they use standard written methods for multi-digit numbers, and for division, they divide by single and double-digit divisors. Learners apply the commutative, associative, and distributive properties to simplify calculations. They solve word problems, such as calculating the total cost of Proteas cricket match tickets for a family or determining how many packets of boerewors are needed for a braai for 20 people.

## Fractions and Decimals
Learners are able to work with common fractions and decimal fractions. They compare and order fractions and find equivalent fractions using common multiples. They perform addition and subtraction with fractions that have the same denominator. Learners convert between common fractions and decimal fractions, and vice versa. They interpret decimals in practical contexts, like calculating the change from R100 after buying a souvenir at the Kirstenbosch market or comparing the prices of different brands of juice in decimal rand values.

## Patterns, Functions, and Algebra
Learners are able to recognise, describe, and extend numeric and geometric patterns. They complete and generate input-output tables for given rules. They use letters as variables to represent unknown numbers in simple equations, and they solve for the unknown using inverse operations. Learners apply this to real-life situations, like finding the number of rows needed to seat 56 learners if each row has 7 seats, or determining the cost of multiple Table Mountain cable car tickets based on a fixed price per ticket.

## Measurement
Learners are able to use standard units of measurement. They solve problems involving length, mass, and capacity, including conversions between units (e.g., mm to cm, g to kg, ml to l). They calculate the perimeter and area of squares and rectangles. Learners also work with time, calculating elapsed time and converting between 12-hour and 24-hour clock formats. They apply this by calculating the distance to a relative’s house in kilometres or measuring the ingredients needed for a large batch of cookies.

## Space and Shape (Geometry)
Learners are able to recognise and name geometric properties of 2D and 3D shapes. They identify and describe the properties of triangles, quadrilaterals, and prisms. They draw 2D shapes on grid paper, using a ruler and protractor to construct lines and angles. Learners perform transformations, including reflections and translations. They identify lines of symmetry in shapes like the Proteas cricket logo or flags of South African provinces, and they describe their geometric properties.

## Data Handling
Learners are able to collect, organise, and represent data using tally tables, tables, and bar graphs. They interpret the information presented in graphs and answer questions related to the data. They find the mode (the most frequent value) and the range (the difference between the highest and lowest values) of a data set. Learners engage in practical projects, such as conducting a survey on favourite braai foods and representing the findings in a bar graph to determine the most popular option among their classmates.
