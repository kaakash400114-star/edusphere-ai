# South African (CAPS) Grade 12 Mathematics

## Algebraic Expressions
Learners are able to simplify and factorise algebraic expressions using the sum and difference of cubes, factor theorem, and remainder theorem. They master the manipulation of complex fractions, including those with surds in the denominator. For instance, simplifying (8x³ - 27y³) / (2x - 3y) results in 4x² + 6xy + 9y² using the difference of cubes formula. Learners apply these skills to solve for unknowns in rational equations, ensuring the solution is always checked against the denominator to avoid division by zero, a critical step in real-world problem-solving involving financial models.

## Functions and Inverses
Learners are able to identify and sketch functions, focusing on the domain, range, asymptotes, and intercepts of graphs. Key functions include exponential, logarithmic, and inverse relations. They determine the inverse of a function algebraically and interpret its practical meaning. For example, the inverse of the function C(F) = (5/9)(F - 32), which converts Fahrenheit to Celsius, is F(C) = (9/5)C + 32, allowing conversion back to Fahrenheit. Learners use this to solve problems, such as determining the cost of a braai (barbecue) based on fluctuating ingredient prices over time.

## Financial Mathematics
Learners are able to perform calculations involving simple and compound interest, including effective and nominal interest rates. They calculate present and future value annuities to solve problems related to loans, investments, and savings plans. A practical example is calculating the monthly instalment required to finance a new Proteas cricket jersey priced at R1,200 over 12 months at an interest rate of 15% per annum. Learners use the formula for the present value of an annuity to determine the total repayment amount and understand long-term financial implications.

## Euclidean Geometry
Learners are able to apply circle geometry theorems to solve complex geometric problems, including theorems on angles, chords, tangents, and cyclic quadrilaterals. They calculate angles and prove geometric properties using these theorems. A typical problem involves finding the angle of elevation from the base of Table Mountain to its peak, using the theorem that the angle between a tangent and a chord is equal to the angle in the alternate segment. Learners apply this to real-world scenarios, such as determining optimal viewing angles for tourists.

## Trigonometry
Learners are able to solve two- and three-dimensional problems using trigonometric ratios, identities, and the sine and cosine rules. They apply the area rule to find the area of triangles. For example, a problem might involve calculating the height of a flagpole at a sports stadium by measuring the angle of elevation from two different points on the ground and using the sine rule to find the unknown height. Learners also use trigonometry to model periodic phenomena, like the daily temperature fluctuations in Johannesburg, using sinusoidal functions.

## Statistics and Probability
Learners are able to analyse univariate and bivariate data, including calculating measures of central tendency and dispersion. They use scatter plots and linear regression to model relationships between variables. For instance, they might investigate the correlation between the number of tourists visiting Cape Town and the monthly revenue generated by local businesses. Learners also apply probability rules, including conditional probability, to solve real-world problems, such as determining the likelihood of the Proteas winning a cricket match based on historical data and current conditions.
