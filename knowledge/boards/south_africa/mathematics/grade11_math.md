# South African (CAPS) Grade 11 Mathematics

## Functions and Inverses
Learners are able to investigate the properties of functions, including the domain, range, and intercepts, and sketch graphs of functions. They learn to determine the equations of asymptotes and to use transformations to sketch graphs of functions. Learners find and interpret the inverse of a function algebraically and graphically. For example, if a function f(x) models the cost of a braai in rand, f⁻¹(x) would determine the number of items bought for a given cost. The graphical relationship between a function and its inverse is that they are reflections of each other across the line y = x.

## Financial Mathematics
Learners are able to solve problems involving simple and compound interest, including effective and nominal interest rates. They calculate present and future values of annuities. For instance, they can determine how much needs to be invested now to have enough for Proteas cricket tour tickets in five years. Learners use the formulae A = P(1 + i)ⁿ for compound growth and the annuity formula to solve for payment periods, total amounts, and present values, applying their skills to personal financial planning and investment scenarios in the South African context.

## Euclidean Geometry
Learners are able to prove and apply theorems concerning the geometry of circles. They understand and use theorems about angles subtended by arcs, chords, and tangents. For example, they can calculate the angle at which a cable must be anchored to a tower on Table Mountain, given the distance from the base. Learners use the converse of these theorems and apply the theory to solve for unknown lengths and angles within complex geometric figures, strengthening their deductive reasoning skills.

## Analytical Geometry
Learners are able to calculate the equation of a line given its slope and a point, or two points. They find the equation of a perpendicular bisector and the point of intersection of two lines. For example, they could find the optimal location for a new community hall equidistant from two suburbs in a town. Learners use the distance, midpoint, and gradient formulae to solve problems involving the properties of quadrilaterals and other polygons on the Cartesian plane, connecting algebra with spatial relationships.

## Trigonometry
Learners are able to prove and apply the sine, cosine, and area rules to solve problems in two and three dimensions. They use the trigonometric identities sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 and the reduction formulae. For instance, they can calculate the height of Table Mountain from a known distance on the ground by using angles of elevation. Learners apply the cosine rule to find the length of a cable required to span a gorge and the sine rule to determine unknown angles in non-right-angled triangles.

## Statistics
Learners must analyse univariate data by calculating measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) and dispersion (range, variance, standard deviation), using these statistics to interpret and compare data sets, such as comparing the average monthly rainfall in Cape Town and Durban. They apply the normal distribution to model real-world phenomena like student heights, standardising values using the formula z = (x - μ) / σ to determine probabilities and percentiles. To make informed conclusions, learners interpret statistical outputs in context, for example, using the standard deviation of a factory's product weights to assess quality control and process consistency.
