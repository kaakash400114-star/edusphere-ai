# South African (CAPS) Grade 4 Mathematics
## Numbers, Operations and Relationships
Learners are able to recognise, read and write number names and symbols for whole numbers up to 10 000. They build number sense through ordering, comparing and representing numbers in different ways, including using number lines. They develop mental strategies and apply rounding off to the nearest 10, 100 and 1 000. Learners solve number puzzles and real-life problems, such as calculating the total cost of Proteas team merchandise or sharing rands for a school fundraiser, using all four operations.
## Patterns, Functions and Algebra
Learners are able to investigate, extend and describe numeric and geometric patterns. They look for relationships in patterns using tables and flow diagrams, creating their own rules. For example, they might complete a pattern like 2, 4, 6, ..., 20, describing the rule as 'adding 2' or 'multiplying by 2'. They also work with number sentences, balancing them with missing numbers, such as 15 + ____ = 25, applying these to problems involving sharing sweets or arranging seating for a braai.
## Space and Shape
Learners are able to recognise, name and describe 2D and 3D objects in their environment. They identify properties like the number of sides, vertices, faces and edges. They build models with objects like blocks and draw 2D shapes. Learners explore symmetry by folding shapes and completing patterns. They use directional language to describe positions on a grid, such as moving from the base of Table Mountain to the top, and understand concepts like left, right, and compass directions (North, South, East, West).
## Measurement
Learners are able to measure, compare and order objects using standard units. They measure length in millimetres, centimetres and metres, mass in grams and kilograms, and capacity in millilitres and litres. They read and tell time accurately to the minute and calculate elapsed time. They read temperature in degrees Celsius. Learners solve practical problems, like measuring ingredients for a recipe in grams or calculating how long a cricket match will last, using real-world South African contexts.
## Data Handling
Learners are able to collect, organise, display and interpret data. They conduct simple surveys, asking questions like, 'What is your favourite fruit?' They record data using tally marks and represent it using pictographs and bar graphs where each symbol or block stands for a value. They interpret the information to answer questions, for example, 'Which animal got the most votes from the class?' or 'How many more learners prefer soccer than rugby?', developing skills for making informed decisions.
## Financial Mathematics
Learners are able to solve problems involving money. They identify and perform calculations with coins and banknotes of the South African rand. They add and subtract amounts of money to find totals and change, such as calculating the cost of buying a snack and a drink from the tuck shop. They also work with simple budgets, planning for an event, like a class braai, by estimating costs and ensuring they have enough money, building essential financial literacy skills.
