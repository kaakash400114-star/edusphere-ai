# South African (CAPS) Grade 1 Mathematics

## Number, Operations and Relationships
Learners are able to count to at least 35 objects and order numbers from 1 to 34. They build number sense by counting forwards and backwards, and recognise number symbols. They demonstrate addition and subtraction by combining and taking away objects, using concrete materials. For example, combining 3 proteas and 5 proteas makes 8 proteas, or taking away 2 rugby balls from 7 leaves 5. They solve simple number stories and begin to record their work using drawings and symbols.

## Patterns, Functions and Algebra
Learners are able to recognise, describe, copy and extend patterns. They use concrete objects like beads, shells or toy cars to create patterns based on colour, shape or size. For example, a pattern of red, blue, red, blue, or big, small, big, small. They identify and name the core of a repeating pattern. They also extend simple number patterns, such as counting in 2s (2, 4, 6) or 5s (5, 10, 15), and are able to create their own patterns with a clear rule.

## Space and Shape (Geometry)
Learners are able to name, sort and describe common 3D objects like balls, boxes, and cylinders, and 2D shapes like circles, triangles, and squares. They use these shapes to build pictures and structures, such as a house from a square and a triangle. They recognise and name shapes in their environment, seeing a round manhole cover, a triangular road sign, or the square windows of a classroom. They describe shapes using simple words like big, small, long, short, and curved.

## Measurement
Learners are able to use non-standard units to measure length, mass and capacity. They measure the length of a table using their hands, the mass of a book using blocks, and the capacity of a cup using beads. They compare and order objects using words like longer, shorter, heavier, lighter, more, and less. They sequence events in their day, like morning, afternoon, and night, and recognise times linked to their school routine, such as break time and home time. They use South African money, recognising R1, R2, and R5 coins.

## Data Handling
Learners are able to collect, sort, and organise objects according to given criteria, such as sorting fruit into apples and bananas. They represent their data in concrete pictographs using pictures or blocks, with one symbol standing for one object. For example, they might draw a picture of a protea for each learner supporting a certain team. They interpret the information from their simple displays by answering questions like "Which has the most?" or "Which has the least?"

## Problem Solving
Learners are able to solve problems in meaningful contexts using their number, operations, and other mathematical skills. They act out a story about sharing 15 boerewors at a braai with friends, or work out how many tickets are needed for a family of four to visit the top of Table Mountain. They use drawings, counting, and trial-and-error strategies to find solutions. They communicate their thinking by explaining how they solved the problem, using simple language and supporting their answers with concrete objects.
