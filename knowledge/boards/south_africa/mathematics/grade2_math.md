# South African (CAPS) Grade 2 Mathematics

## Numbers, Operations and Relationships
Learners are able to count forwards and backwards in ones from any number between 0 and 200. They build up and break down numbers up to 200 using concrete objects like counters, ensuring they understand place value (hundreds, tens, ones). They use addition and subtraction number sentences with numbers up to 100, solving practical problems. For example, buying 2 cooldrinks for R8 each means calculating 8 + 8 or 8 x 2 to find the total cost of R16. Mental strategies like using doubles are key.

## Patterns, Functions and Algebra
Learners are able to recognise, describe, copy and extend simple patterns using physical objects, sounds, numbers and drawings. They identify patterns in numbers, such as counting in 2s, 5s, and 10s, connecting this to everyday life like counting fingers or toes. They also investigate patterns in shapes and colours. For instance, using the red and blue Proteas cricket kit colours to create a repeating pattern on a chart. The goal is to find and explain the rule that continues the sequence.

## Space and Shape (Geometry)
Learners are able to identify, sort and name common 3D objects like spheres, cubes, and cylinders found in their environment, such as a rugby ball or a tin of mielies. They describe 2D shapes by naming circles, triangles, squares and rectangles, recognising them in pictures of the Table Mountain cable car or a braai grid. They use positional language to describe where things are, placing a toy 'behind' the chair or 'next to' the table to build spatial awareness.

## Measurement
Learners are able to estimate, measure, compare and order objects using non-standard units. They measure length using hands or strides, comparing who is taller or the length of a classroom table. They measure capacity using cups, filling containers to see which holds more water. They use time language like 'yesterday', 'today' and 'tomorrow', and sequence events in a day. For example, understanding that a braai happens in the evening after the afternoon sun.

## Data Handling
Learners are able to collect data by asking simple questions and sorting objects into categories. They tally information like favourite fruits or types of shoes worn to school. They represent this data using pictographs, where one picture stands for one object or person. For instance, they could draw a picture of a rand coin for every learner who saves money, creating a visual graph that shows how many save R5 or R10 each week.

## Problem Solving
Learners are able to solve simple word problems using addition and subtraction, drawing on their own experiences. They read a problem, decide whether to add or take away, and use objects or drawings to find the answer. For example: 'Sipho has 15 oranges. He gives 5 to his friend. How many does he have left?' The process encourages logical thought and applying mathematical skills to real-life situations they understand.
