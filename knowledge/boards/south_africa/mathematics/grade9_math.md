# South African (CAPS) Grade 9 Mathematics
## Algebraic Expressions
Learners are able to simplify algebraic expressions using the laws of exponents and factorisation. They simplify expressions like (2x²y³)⁴ to 16x⁸y¹² and factorise binomials such as 4x² - 16 to 4(x + 2)(x - 2). They also factorise trinomials like x² + 5x + 6 into (x + 2)(x + 3). The distributive property is used to expand expressions like 3(x² - 2x). These skills are applied to simplify complex expressions and solve for unknowns in simple equations, using a combination of multiplication and division.
## Equations and Inequalities
Learners are able to solve linear equations and inequalities, including those with variables on both sides. They solve for x in equations like 3(x - 2) + 5 = 2x + 7, showing all steps. For inequalities, they solve 2x - 4 < 10 and represent the solution on a number line. Word problems involving rates and costs, such as calculating the cost of a braai where the total is R350 for 10kg of meat, are translated into algebraic equations. The solutions are verified by substitution, ensuring they are logical in the context of the problem.
## Exponents and Surds
Learners are able to simplify expressions using the laws of exponents, such as multiplying like bases (aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ) and simplifying powers of powers ((aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ). They simplify expressions like (2³ × 2⁴) / 2² to 2⁵. They also work with rational exponents and simplify surds, like expressing √45 as 3√5. Rationalising denominators is taught, for example, simplifying 5/√2 to (5√2)/2. These concepts are applied to solve equations involving exponents and to simplify complex algebraic expressions.
## Number Patterns, Sequences, and Series
Learners are able to identify patterns, determine the nth term of linear and quadratic sequences, and calculate the sum of arithmetic series. For the pattern 2, 5, 8, ..., the nth term is 3n - 1. They calculate the sum of the first 20 terms of the sequence 4, 7, 10, ..., using the formula Sₙ = n/2 (2a + (n-1)d), where a is the first term and d is the common difference. Real-world contexts, like calculating the total money saved over several months or the seating arrangements in a stadium shaped like Table Mountain, are modelled using sequences.
## Financial Mathematics
Learners are able to solve problems involving simple and compound interest, hire purchase, and exchange rates. They calculate the simple interest on an investment of R5,000 at 7% per annum over 3 years. Compound interest is calculated using A = P(1 + i)ⁿ, finding the amount after 4 years on R10,000 invested at 5.5% p.a. They determine the hire purchase price for a new cricket bat for the Proteas team, calculating total repayments. Exchange rate problems involve converting between rands and other currencies, such as calculating the cost of goods bought online from an international store.
## 2D and 3D Geometry and Measurement
Learners are able to solve problems involving the area, perimeter, and volume of 2D and 3D shapes. They calculate the area of a circle to find the size of a braai area and the volume of a rectangular prism to determine the size of a water tank. The Pythagorean theorem (a² + b² = c²) is used to find unknown sides in right-angled triangles, such as determining the length of a ladder against a wall. Surface area formulas for prisms and cylinders are applied, for instance, calculating the amount of paint needed to cover a large silo.
