# South African (CAPS) Grade 5 Mathematics
## Number, Operations and Relationships
Learners are able to recognise, order and represent numbers to at least 100 000. They build fluency in adding, subtracting and multiplying whole numbers using appropriate calculation strategies. For example, to calculate 350 + 278, they can break down the numbers: 350 + 200 = 550, 550 + 70 = 620, and 620 + 8 = 628. They also learn division as sharing or grouping. If the Proteas cricket team scores 420 runs and they are shared equally among 7 players, each player gets 60 runs (420 ÷ 7 = 60).
## Patterns, Functions and Algebra
Learners are able to investigate, extend and describe numeric and geometric patterns. They can input and output values in a simple function machine, like adding 15 each time. For instance, an input of 20 becomes an output of 35. Geometrically, they can continue a pattern where one Table Mountain outline is followed by two circles, and then repeat. Learners also describe these patterns using words, like "add three" or "one mountain, two circles".
## Space and Shape (Geometry)
Learners are able to identify and name 2D shapes like pentagons, hexagons, and octagons. They can draw these shapes on grid paper, ensuring all sides and angles are correct. They also recognise and describe 3D objects, focusing on their properties. For example, a braai grid is a rectangular prism with flat faces and sharp edges. Learners can distinguish between lines (straight or curved) and angles (bigger or smaller than a right angle), making this practical for building or design.
## Measurement
Learners are able to convert between different units of measurement. They know that 1 000 millimetres (mm) make 1 metre (m) and 1 000 metres make 1 kilometre (km). For mass, 1 000 grams (g) equal 1 kilogram (kg). They can measure the length of a classroom in metres or the weight of a rugby ball in grams. They also read time on analogue and digital clocks, calculating elapsed time. If a rugby match starts at 14:30 and ends at 16:10, the duration is 1 hour and 40 minutes.
## Data Handling
Learners are able to collect, organise and display data using tally tables, pictograms, and bar graphs. They can conduct a survey on learners' favourite Proteas players and show the results. A pictogram might use a picture of a cricket ball to represent every two votes. They then interpret this information by answering questions like, "Which player got the most votes?" or "How many more votes did Player A get than Player B?"
## Financial Mathematics
Learners are able to solve problems involving money, using South African rand (R). They add and subtract amounts to find totals or change. For example, buying a soccer jersey for R250 and a pair of shorts for R120 costs a total of R370. If you pay with R500, you get R130 back. They can also work with simple budgets, like planning a braai for friends, calculating the total cost of five burgers at R30 each and two litres of cold drink at R25 per litre.
