# South African (CAPS) Grade 10 Mathematics

## Algebraic Expressions
Learners are able to simplify and manipulate algebraic expressions using the laws of exponents and operations. They expand and factorise binomials and trinomials, including the difference of squares. For example, expanding 3x(2x - 5) gives 6x² - 15x, while factorising x² - 16 results in (x - 4)(x + 4). They apply the distributive property and identify common factors. A practical application is calculating the area of a braai grid with dimensions expressed in terms of a variable, such as length l and width w.

## Equations and Inequalities
Learners are able to solve linear and quadratic equations using factorisation, completing the square, and the quadratic formula. They solve linear simultaneous equations algebraically and graphically. They also solve linear inequalities and represent the solution on a number line. For instance, solving 2x + 10 > 30 gives x > 10, meaning a Proteas team needs to score more than 10 runs per over to win. They interpret solutions in real-world contexts like budgeting with rand values.

## Number Patterns, Sequences and Series
Learners are able to identify and describe number patterns and determine the formula for the nth term of linear and quadratic sequences. They calculate the sums of arithmetic and geometric series. For example, the cost of a stadium tour that increases by R50 each year forms an arithmetic sequence. A savings plan where the amount doubles each month is a geometric series. They use these concepts to model financial growth and analyse trends related to population or ticket sales for events at Table Mountain.

## Functions and Inverses
Learners are able to interpret and work with function notation, f(x). They identify the domain and range of functions and sketch graphs of linear, quadratic, and exponential functions. They determine the inverse of a function where it exists. For example, the function f(x) = 2x + 5 can be inverted to find the original input from an output, which is useful for converting a final price back to an original rand amount before VAT was added. They understand the vertical line test to determine if a graph represents a function.

## Euclidean Geometry
Learners are able to apply the properties of geometric figures, including triangles, quadrilaterals and circles. They prove theorems related to the geometry of the circle, such as the tangent-chord theorem. They calculate angles and lengths using properties like the Pythagorean theorem and similar triangles. An example is calculating the height of Table Mountain using trigonometric ratios, or determining the optimal angle for a roof structure to ensure stability during strong winds in Cape Town.

## Measurement
Learners are able to solve problems involving surface area and volume of right prisms, pyramids, spheres, and cones. They convert between units of measurement and apply formulae to real-world scenarios. For instance, calculating the volume of concrete needed for a new stadium foundation or determining the amount of paint required to repaint the walls of a classroom. They also apply trigonometric ratios to solve problems involving heights and distances, such as finding the length of shadow a Proteas player casts on the field during a midday match.
