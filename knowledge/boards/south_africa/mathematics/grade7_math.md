# South African (CAPS) Grade 7 Mathematics

## Whole Numbers and Operations
Learners are able to perform operations on whole numbers, including squaring and cubing. They use the order of operations (BODMAS/PEMDAS) to solve multi-step problems. Learners calculate and apply percentages of whole numbers in real-life contexts, like calculating a 15% tip on a R350 restaurant bill. They also solve problems involving ratio, sharing R180 in the ratio 2:1, and rate, such as determining the cost of 5 litres of petrol at R17 per litre.

## Integers
Learners are able to compare and order integers on a number line and perform addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. They apply these operations to real-world contexts like temperature changes, where a drop of 8°C is represented as -8. Learners solve problems involving debt and savings, for example, if a Proteas team spends R2 million on equipment and has R1.5 million in sponsorship, their financial standing is represented as -R500,000.

## Common Fractions
Learners are able to add, subtract, multiply, and divide common fractions, including mixed numbers. They apply these skills to solve problems like finding the total flour needed for a braai recipe that requires 2 ½ cups for the pap and ¾ cup for the vetkoek. Learners also convert between common fractions and percentages, such as determining what fraction of the school represents the 60% of learners who support the rugby team.

## Decimal Fractions
Learners are able to perform calculations with decimal fractions and apply them to money. They solve problems like calculating the cost of 3.5kg of mielie meal at R12.50 per kilogram. Learners round decimal numbers to a specified number of decimal places and to the nearest whole number, which is useful for estimating the total number of hikers who might visit Table Mountain in a day, based on an average of 125.8 visitors per hour.

## Patterns, Functions, and Algebra
Learners are able to identify, describe, and extend numeric and geometric patterns. They use simple tables to represent relationships between variables and write algebraic expressions, such as representing the cost of 'x' tickets to a soccer match at R80 per ticket. Learners solve simple equations, for example, finding the value of 'x' in 2x + 10 = 30, which could represent sharing prize money.

## Measurement and Space
Learners are able to convert between different units of length, mass, and capacity, such as converting 3 500ml into litres and millilitres. They calculate the perimeter and area of squares and rectangles, like finding the area of a braai grid that is 80cm long and 50cm wide. Learners also recognise and draw 2D shapes and identify 3D objects from different viewpoints, describing the shapes of the Table Mountain cable car stations.
