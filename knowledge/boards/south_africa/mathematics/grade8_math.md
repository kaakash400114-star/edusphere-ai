# South African (CAPS) Grade 8 Mathematics
## Whole Numbers and Number Operations
Learners are able to perform operations on whole numbers using the four operations and the order of operations (BODMAS). They solve problems involving ratio and rate, such as sharing the cost of braai supplies in a 3:2 ratio. Learners also work with exponents, applying the laws like \(a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}\). They solve word problems involving speed, for instance, calculating the time it takes to drive 180 km at an average speed of 90 km/h, and use practical examples like splitting a R500 prize among four friends.

## Integers, Exponents, and Roots
Learners are able to work with integers, performing addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division on the number line. They understand concepts like debt and profit, calculating a team’s score after gaining 15 runs and then losing 8 runs. Learners apply exponent laws for integers and solve problems like \((2^3)^2\). They also calculate square and cube roots, identifying the square root of 144 and the cube root of 64, and relate this to real-world contexts like finding the side length of a square with an area of 121 m².

## Common Fractions and Decimal Fractions
Learners are able to perform operations with common and decimal fractions. They add and subtract fractions with different denominators, like \(\frac{3}{4} + \frac{2}{5}\), and multiply and divide fractions, such as \(\frac{4}{5} \times \frac{3}{8}\). They convert between fractions and decimals, understanding that \(\frac{3}{8} = 0.375\). Learners solve problems involving money, like calculating the cost of 2.5 kg of apples at R12.50 per kg, and use terminating and recurring decimals in practical contexts.

## Financial Mathematics
Learners are able to solve problems involving financial transactions. They calculate profit, loss, and discount, such as finding the selling price of a R200 Proteas shirt after a 15% discount. Learners work with budgets, planning a school trip with R5000 allocated for transport and meals. They calculate simple and compound interest, determining the amount after 3 years if R1000 is invested at 5% simple interest annually, and understand concepts like VAT and hire purchase agreements.

## Measurement
Learners are able to solve problems involving length, area, volume, and time. They convert between units, like 1.2 km to 1200 m, and calculate the perimeter and area of shapes, such as finding the area of a rectangular field at Table Mountain that is 50 m long and 30 m wide. Learners calculate the volume of rectangular prisms, like a water tank measuring 2 m by 1.5 m by 1 m, and solve problems involving time, such as determining the duration of a 2-hour and 45-minute cricket match.

## Data Handling
Learners are able to collect, organise, and interpret data using tables, bar graphs, and pie charts. They represent data, like the favourite sports of 100 learners, in a bar graph and calculate the mean, median, and mode for data sets. Learners interpret data, such as finding the average score of the Proteas cricket team over five matches, and draw conclusions, like identifying the most popular type of food at a school braai based on a pie chart. They distinguish between categorical and numerical data.
