# Matriculation Class 10 Mathematics

## Real numbers

In this topic, you learn about real numbers and how to classify them into rational and irrational numbers. You understand that rational numbers can be written as a fraction p/q where q is not zero, like ½ or -7, while irrational numbers cannot be written this way, like √2 or π. You will learn to represent these numbers on a number line and find the decimal expansions of fractions to see if they terminate, repeat, or continue endlessly. The laws of exponents, including positive and negative powers, are also studied with numbers like 2³ and 5⁻², making sure you can apply these rules to simplify calculations and solve problems involving real numbers.

## Polynomials

In this topic, you learn about polynomials, which are expressions made up of variables and constants using operations like addition, subtraction, and multiplication. You identify the degree of a polynomial, which is the highest power of the variable, for example, the polynomial \(x^2 - 3x + 2\) has a degree of 2. You also study different types of polynomials, such as linear (degree 1), quadratic (degree 2), and cubic (degree 3), like \(x - 1\) (linear) and \(x^3 + 4x - 5\) (cubic). The topic includes factorising polynomials into simpler terms, for instance, \(x^2 - 9\) becomes \((x + 3)(x - 3)\). Finally, you learn about the relationship between the zeroes of a polynomial and its coefficients, using simple methods to find them.

## Pair of linear equations

Students learn to solve pairs of linear equations in two variables using methods like substitution and elimination. They understand that these equations represent straight lines on a graph, and the solution is their point of intersection. For example, given equations like 2x + y = 5 and x - y = 1, students find the values of x and y that satisfy both equations simultaneously. They also learn to check if the equations are consistent (have one solution), inconsistent (no solution), or dependent (infinitely many solutions). The topic helps students understand real-life problems like finding costs or speeds where two conditions must be true at the same time.

## Quadratic equations

Students learn to recognise quadratic equations, which are polynomial equations of degree two, and write them in the standard form ax² + bx + c = 0, where a, b, and c are numbers and a is not zero. They practice solving these equations using the quadratic formula, x = [-b ± √(b² - 4ac)] / 2a, and by the method of factorisation. For example, they can solve x² - 5x + 6 = 0 by finding two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to -5, which are -2 and -3, giving the solutions x = 2 and x = 3. The concept of the discriminant (b² - 4ac) is introduced to determine the nature of the roots, whether they are real and equal, real and distinct, or not real.

## Arithmetic progressions

In this topic, you learn about arithmetic progressions, which are number patterns where the difference between consecutive terms is always the same, called the common difference. You find any term using the formula nth term = first term + (n – 1) × common difference, for example, in 3, 7, 11, 15,… the 5th term is 3 + (5 – 1) × 4 = 19. You also learn to find the sum of the first n terms with the formula sum = n/2 × (first term + last term), like sum of first 5 terms in the above series is 5/2 × (3 + 15) = 45. These rules help you solve problems involving sequences and series in real-life situations.

## Triangles: similarity

Students learn that two triangles are similar if their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding sides are proportional. They discover the criteria for similarity, such as AA (Angle-Angle), SSS (Side-Side-Side), and SAS (Side-Angle-Side), which help identify similar triangles without measuring all sides and angles. For example, if one triangle has angles 40°, 60°, and 80°, and another has the same angles, they are similar by AA. The ratio of corresponding sides in similar triangles is called the scale factor, which can be used to find unknown lengths. This concept is useful in real-life situations, like determining the height of a tree using its shadow and a smaller object's measurements.

## Coordinate geometry

In coordinate geometry, you learn to find the position of points using numbers called coordinates, written as (x, y). You also discover how to calculate the distance between two points, such as between (2, 3) and (5, 7), using a special formula that looks like the Pythagoras theorem. Another key idea is finding the midpoint, or middle point, between two points, like halfway between (1, 4) and (5, 8). You will practice drawing these points and lines on a graph called the Cartesian plane to see how they connect. Finally, you explore how to check if three points lie in a straight line using their coordinates.

## Introduction to trigonometry

In this topic, students learn about the three main trigonometric ratios—sine, cosine, and tangent—for a given angle in a right-angled triangle. These ratios are defined as the side opposite the angle divided by the hypotenuse (sin), the side adjacent to the angle divided by the hypotenuse (cos), and the opposite side divided by the adjacent side (tan). They use these ratios to find the missing sides or angles in right-angled triangles, for example, finding the height of a building when given its distance from the observer and the angle of elevation. Students also learn to remember these ratios easily using the mnemonic 'SOH CAH TOA'. Finally, they apply these concepts to solve simple real-life problems involving heights and distances.

## Applications of trigonometry

In Class 10, students learn to use trigonometry to solve real-life problems involving heights and distances. They apply the sine, cosine, and tangent ratios to find unknown sides or angles in right-angled triangles. For example, they can calculate the height of a building or the width of a river using these rules. The concept of line of sight and angle of elevation is introduced to model such problems. Students also practice drawing diagrams to represent given situations and applying the correct trigonometric formula.

## Circles

In Class 10, you learn about circles, their parts like radius, diameter, chord, and tangent. You study theorems such as the perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord, and the angle subtended by an arc at the centre is double the angle at the circumference. You also learn that the lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal. The area and circumference of a circle are calculated using formulas, like Area = πr² and Circumference = 2πr. Practical problems involve finding the distance of a chord from the centre or proving the properties of cyclic quadrilaterals.

## Constructions

In Class 10, you will learn to construct geometric figures using only a compass and ruler. You will practice drawing perpendicular bisectors, angle bisectors, and triangles when the lengths of their sides and angles are given. You will also construct triangles based on specific conditions like the sum of two sides and an angle between them, or two angles and a side. For example, you might draw a triangle with sides of 5 cm, 7 cm, and the angle between them equal to 60 degrees. These constructions help you understand shapes and their properties accurately.

## Areas related to circles

In this topic, students learn to find the area of a circle using the formula πr², where 'r' is the radius. They also calculate the area of sectors of a circle, which is a slice shaped like a pizza, using (θ/360) × πr² with θ as the central angle. The area of a segment, the area between a chord and the arc, is found by subtracting the triangle’s area from the sector’s area. They solve problems involving areas of combinations of plane figures like circles inside squares or rectangles, learning to break down complex shapes into simpler parts to find the total area.

## Surface areas and volumes

In this topic, children learn to calculate the surface areas and volumes of common 3D shapes like cubes, cuboids, cylinders, cones, and spheres. They use specific formulas, such as finding the total surface area of a cuboid by adding the areas of all six faces or calculating the volume of a cylinder by multiplying its base area with its height. For example, they might find how much paint is needed to paint a cube or how much water a cylindrical tank can hold. Children also understand how to convert units between litres and cubic centimetres for real-life problems. The lessons focus on applying these formulas correctly to solve practical problems involving measurement.

## Statistics

In Class 10, you learn about statistics to collect and understand information called data. You practise making and reading different types of graphs like bar graphs, histograms, and frequency polygons to show data clearly. A key idea is finding the mean, median, and mode to understand what is typical or common in the data, for example, the average marks of students in a class. You also learn to group large data into class intervals and calculate the mean using a special formula to make calculations easier. Finally, you study probability to understand the chance of something happening, like the likelihood of getting a head when you toss a coin.

## Probability

In Class 10 probability, you learn to find the chance of something happening using numbers between 0 and 1, where 0 means impossible and 1 means sure. You study the formula Probability = (Number of favourable outcomes) / (Total number of possible outcomes), like finding the chance of getting a head when tossing a coin is 1/2. You also understand terms like equally likely outcomes, such as rolling a die where each number has the same chance of 1/6. The sum of probabilities of all possible outcomes is always 1, for example, the chance of getting an odd or even number on a die is 1/2 + 1/2 = 1.
