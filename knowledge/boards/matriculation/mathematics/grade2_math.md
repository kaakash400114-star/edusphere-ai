# Matriculation Class 2 Mathematics

## Numbers to 999

Children learn to read, write, and count numbers up to 999, understanding place value for hundreds, tens, and ones. They use numerals to represent numbers like 450, which has 4 hundreds, 5 tens, and 0 ones. Children compare numbers to find which is bigger or smaller, for example, 785 is greater than 680. They also learn to arrange numbers in order from smallest to largest, like 200, 500, 800. This helps them build a strong foundation for working with bigger numbers in the future.

## Addition with carry

In Class 2, children learn to add two-digit numbers where the sum of the ones place is 10 or more, called addition with carry. When adding ones, if the total is 10 or more, they write the ones digit in the answer and carry over the tens digit to the tens place. For example, adding 28 and 5: first, add the ones (8 + 5 = 13), write 3 in the ones place and carry over 1 to the tens. Then, add the tens (2 + 0 + 1 = 3) and write 3 in the tens place, making the answer 33. This method ensures correct addition of larger numbers by managing place values carefully.

## Subtraction with borrow

Children learn subtraction with borrow when the digit in the ones place of the smaller number is less than the digit in the ones place of the larger number. They must borrow one group of ten from the tens place, adding it to the ones place before subtracting. For example, in 42 - 15, they borrow 1 ten from 4, making it 3 tens, and add 10 ones to 2, making it 12 ones. Then, they subtract 5 ones from 12 ones to get 7 ones and subtract 1 ten from 3 tens to get 2 tens, resulting in 27. This method helps in correctly solving subtraction problems when the ones digit of the minuend is smaller.

## Multiplication as repeated addition

Multiplication is a quick way to add the same number many times, for example, 3 + 3 + 3 is the same as 3 × 3. We write multiplication using the × sign, and the first number tells us how many things there are, like 4 mangoes, while the second number tells us how many groups there are, like 2 groups. So, 4 × 2 means we have 4 mangoes in each of the 2 groups, and we can find the answer by adding 4 + 4. We also use skip counting to help us, like counting 2, 4, 6, 8 to solve 2 × 4. Learning multiplication helps us solve problems faster without adding the same number over and over again.

## Division as sharing

Children learn that division means sharing things equally among a group. For example, if we have 8 candies and share them between 2 friends, each friend gets 4 candies. They understand that division splits a big group into smaller, equal parts. We use the division sign (÷) for sharing; so 8 ÷ 2 = 4 means 8 shared into 2 equal groups gives 4 each. They practice simple sharing problems like sharing 6 stickers among 3 children, finding each gets 2. This helps them see how division makes groups fair and equal.

## Money: rupees and paise

In Grade 2, children learn about money using rupees (₹) and paise (p). They identify and count coins of 50p, ₹1, ₹2, ₹5, and ₹10, and notes of ₹10, ₹20, ₹50, and ₹100. They understand that 100 paise make one rupee and use this to convert small amounts, like 50p is half of one rupee. Students practice adding and subtracting simple amounts, such as finding the total of ₹5 and ₹10, or calculating change from a ₹20 note after buying a ₹5 item. They also learn to write amounts in figures, like ₹15.50, and understand real-life uses of money.

## Length, weight, capacity

Children learn to measure length using non-standard units like hand spans and paper clips, comparing the lengths of objects such as pencils and ribbons. They explore weight by hefting objects to find which is heavier or lighter, like a book and a ball, and use balances for simple comparisons. Capacity is introduced through filling and pouring activities, using containers like cups and jugs to understand which holds more or less, such as water in a bottle versus a glass. The children also learn to order objects based on their size, weight, and capacity using simple words like long, short, heavy, and light. These activities help them understand the basic concepts of measurement in everyday life.

## Time: days, months, clock

Children learn to name the days of the week in order, like Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, up to Sunday, and the months of the year, such as January, February, March, and so on. They understand that a week has 7 days and a month has about 30 days, with some months having 31 days. On a clock with hands, they learn to tell the time to the hour, for example, when the big hand is on 12 and the small hand is on 3, it is 3 o’clock. They also learn to identify activities that happen in the morning, afternoon, and evening, like eating breakfast in the morning.

## Shapes and patterns

Children learn to identify and name common shapes like circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles in their surroundings. They observe and create simple patterns using shapes, colours, or objects, such as circle-square-circle-square. Students understand that shapes have different properties, such as the number of sides and corners, like a triangle has three sides and three corners. They also learn to differentiate between straight lines and curved lines while drawing these shapes. Through sorting and matching activities, children develop an early understanding of classification based on shape and size.

## Data: picture charts

Children learn to collect simple information like their favourite fruits or animals using tally marks. They sort and count the collected data to draw picture charts, where each picture represents one item. For example, a chart might show four banana pictures and two apple pictures to display the data. Students also learn to read these charts to answer basic questions like "Which fruit is the most liked?" by counting the pictures. The main rule is that every picture must stand for one thing, and all pictures must be the same size to make the chart easy to understand.
