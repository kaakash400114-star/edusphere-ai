# Matriculation Class 4 Mathematics

## Large numbers to 10,00,000

In Class 4, children learn to read, write, and understand numbers up to ten lakhs (10,00,000). They learn the place value system, identifying ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten thousands, and lakhs. They practice writing numbers in both words and numerals, like 5,32,100 as "five lakh thirty-two thousand one hundred". Children also compare and arrange large numbers in order from smallest to largest. They learn to form numbers using given place values, such as making 7,45,000 from 7 lakhs, 4 ten thousands, and 5 thousands.

## Big additions-subtractions

In Grade 4 Mathematics, students learn to solve big additions and subtractions using the column method for numbers up to five digits. They place numbers in columns of ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, and ten thousands, adding or subtracting from right to left, carrying over if needed. For example, in 45678 + 12345, they add 8+5=13, write 3 and carry over 1 to the tens place. Similarly, in subtraction like 67890 - 34567, if 0 is smaller than 7, they borrow from the tens place. This method helps children understand place value and accurately solve big problems.

## Multiplication 2-digit

In Class 4, children learn to multiply two-digit numbers by one-digit numbers using the column method. They first multiply the ones digit of the two-digit number with the one-digit number, writing down the ones place and carrying over the tens if needed. Then, they multiply the tens digit of the two-digit number with the one-digit number, adding any carried-over number to the result. For example, to find 24 × 3, they multiply 4 × 3 to get 12, write down 2 and carry over 1, then multiply 2 × 3 and add 1 to get 7, making the final answer 72.

## Long division

In Class 4, children learn long division to divide large numbers step by step. They follow the rule: divide, multiply, subtract, and bring down the next digit. For example, to divide 96 by 4, first divide 9 by 4 to get 2, multiply 2 by 4 to get 8, subtract 8 from 9 to get 1, and bring down 6 to make 16. Then, divide 16 by 4 to get 4, and the final answer is 24. They use the division symbol (÷) and write the quotient and remainder clearly. This method helps them solve big sums accurately and builds a strong foundation for harder problems later.

## Factors-multiples

In this topic, children learn about factors and multiples of numbers. A factor is a number that divides another number exactly, without leaving a remainder. For example, the factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 12 because each of these numbers divides 12 completely. A multiple is the result of multiplying a number by another whole number, so the multiples of 4 are 4, 8, 12, 16, and so on. Children practice finding factors and multiples using simple tables and number grids to understand these concepts clearly. They also learn to identify common factors and common multiples between two small numbers.

## Fractions: equivalent and like

In Grade 4, we learn about equivalent fractions, which are different names for the same value. We find equivalent fractions by multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator by the same number, like how 1/2 is the same as 2/4 or 4/8. We also learn about like fractions, which have the same denominator, such as 3/5 and 1/5. We can easily add or subtract like fractions by just adding or subtracting their numerators, like 3/5 + 1/5 = 4/5. This helps us understand fractions better and solve problems easily.

## Decimals: tenths-hundredths

In Class 4, children learn about decimals, focusing on tenths and hundredths. They understand that a decimal point separates the whole number from its fractional parts, where tenths represent one part out of ten and hundredths represent one part out of hundred. For example, 0.3 means 3 tenths, and 0.07 means 7 hundredths. They practice writing, reading, and comparing such decimals, like 0.5 being greater than 0.05. Using place value charts, they learn to express fractions with denominators 10 and 100 as decimals, such as 3/10 becoming 0.3. This helps them build a strong foundation for understanding more complex decimal concepts in higher grades.

## Measurement conversions

In Grade 4 Mathematics, we learn to convert measurements from one unit to another within the same system, like metres to centimetres and litres to millilitres. We understand that 1 metre is equal to 100 centimetres, so to change metres to centimetres, we multiply the number by 100. Similarly, we know that 1 litre is equal to 1000 millilitres, so we multiply the number of litres by 1000 to find the amount in millilitres. For example, 3 metres is the same as 300 centimetres, and 2 litres is the same as 2000 millilitres. We practice these conversions to understand how different units of the same measurement are related to each other.

## Perimeter and area

Children learn to measure the distance around a shape, which is called the perimeter, by adding the length of all its sides together, like finding the total length of a fence around a garden. They also discover how much space a shape covers, called the area, by counting the number of square units that fit inside it, for example, counting square tiles to find the area of the floor. For simple shapes like rectangles, they learn to use the formula length plus width plus length plus width to find the perimeter. By practising with everyday objects like notebooks and handkerchiefs, children become confident in calculating both perimeter and area using addition and multiplication.

## Time, money problems

In Class 4, children learn to solve problems involving time and money using addition and subtraction. They read time from clocks in hours and minutes, calculate durations like "how long a movie lasts," and find the difference between two times. For money, they add and subtract rupees and paise, such as finding the total cost of two items or the change left after buying something. They also compare amounts, like figuring out who has more money, and solve simple word problems about saving or spending. These lessons help them understand real-life situations involving time and money.
