# Matriculation Class 9 Mathematics

## Number systems

In this topic, children learn about different types of numbers like natural numbers, whole numbers, integers, rational numbers, and their properties. They understand how to represent these numbers on the number line and perform operations like addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division on them. They also learn the rules for comparing numbers and finding the absolute value of an integer. For example, they will know that -5 is less than 3 and that the absolute value of -7 is 7. This helps them build a strong foundation for solving problems involving different types of numbers.

## Polynomials

In Class 9, you learn about polynomials, which are expressions with variables and numbers, like 3x² + 2x – 5. You will identify the degree, coefficients, and constant terms, and understand the difference between monomials, binomials, and trinomials. The chapter teaches you how to add, subtract, and multiply polynomials, for example, (x + 2) + (3x – 4) = 4x – 2. You also learn to factorise simple polynomials, such as breaking x² + 5x + 6 into (x + 2)(x + 3). These rules help you solve equations and understand algebra better.

## Coordinate geometry

In Coordinate Geometry, we learn to plot points on a graph using two numbers called coordinates, written as (x, y). We find the distance between two points using a special formula like √[(x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)²], for example, the distance between (2, 3) and (5, 7). We also divide a line segment joining two points in a given ratio, like m:n, using the midpoint formula and section formula. We use these ideas to identify shapes like triangles and quadrilaterals on a graph by checking distances and slopes. Finally, we learn the slope of a line, which shows how steep it is, using the formula (y₂ - y₁)/(x₂ - x₁).

## Linear equations two variables

In this topic, you learn to solve linear equations in two variables, such as 2x + 3y = 7, by finding pairs of values for x and y that make the equation true. You understand that each equation has infinitely many solutions, which can be shown as points on a graph. You practice solving these equations using methods like substitution, where you express one variable in terms of the other, and elimination, where you add or subtract equations to remove one variable. For example, for the equations x + y = 5 and x - y = 1, you can find x = 3 and y = 2 as the solution. You also learn to represent these equations as straight lines on a graph, where the point where the lines meet is the solution.

## Euclid geometry

In Euclid geometry, we learn about the basic building blocks of shapes like points, lines, and planes. We study the properties of different angles, such as acute, obtuse, and right angles, and how they are formed when two lines meet. We also explore the rules for parallel lines and a transversal, learning about corresponding angles, alternate angles, and co-interior angles. For example, when a line crosses two parallel lines, the corresponding angles are always equal. This helps us understand why shapes look the way they do and how they fit together.

## Lines-angles

In Class 9, you learn about different types of angles like acute, obtuse, and right angles, and how to measure them using a protractor. You also study pairs of angles such as complementary angles, which add up to 90 degrees, and supplementary angles, which add up to 180 degrees. When two lines intersect, opposite angles are always equal, and adjacent angles form a linear pair. When a line crosses two parallel lines, corresponding angles are equal, and alternate angles are also equal. You use these rules to solve problems involving angles in shapes like triangles and quadrilaterals.

## Triangles

In Grade 9, you will learn about the different types of triangles based on their sides and angles, such as equilateral, isosceles, and scalene triangles, and acute, right, and obtuse triangles. You will study the properties of triangles, including the Angle Sum Property which states that the sum of all three interior angles of a triangle is always 180 degrees, and the Pythagoras theorem for right-angled triangles. You will also learn to apply these properties to solve problems, like finding missing angles in a triangle given two angles or checking if a set of three numbers can form the sides of a right-angled triangle. For example, in a triangle with angles 60°, 60°, and 60°, you can verify that 60° + 60° + 60° equals 180°. Additionally, you will explore the concept of the congruency of triangles and the rules to prove it, such as SSS (Side-Side-Side), SAS (Side-Angle-Side), and ASA (Angle-Side-Angle).

## Quadrilaterals

In Class 9, you learn about different shapes with four sides called quadrilaterals, such as squares, rectangles, parallelograms, rhombuses, and trapeziums. You discover their special properties, like opposite sides being equal and parallel in parallelograms, or all sides being equal in a rhombus. The topic also teaches you to find the area of these shapes using formulas, like length × breadth for rectangles and ½ × base × height for trapeziums. You will see examples, like measuring the area of a playground shaped as a rectangle or a field shaped as a parallelogram. Finally, you explore how the sum of angles in any quadrilateral is always 360 degrees.

## Circles

In Class 9, you learn about circles and their important parts like the centre, radius, diameter, and circumference. You discover that the circumference of a circle is always a little more than three times its diameter, which we call Pi (π). The formula to find the circumference is C = 2πr or C = πd, where 'r' is the radius and 'd' is the diameter. You also learn about the area of a circle using the formula A = πr², which means area is Pi multiplied by the radius squared. For example, if a circle has a radius of 7 cm, its area is π × 7 × 7 cm².

## Heron's formula

Students learn Heron’s formula to find the area of a triangle when all three sides are known. The formula states that the area is equal to √[s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)], where 's' is the semi-perimeter (half the perimeter) of the triangle, and 'a', 'b', and 'c' are the lengths of its sides. To use the formula, first calculate the semi-perimeter by adding all three sides and dividing by 2. For example, a triangle with sides 6 cm, 8 cm, and 10 cm has a semi-perimeter of 12 cm, and its area is √[12(12 - 6)(12 - 8)(12 - 10)] = √[12 × 6 × 4 × 2] = 24 cm². This method is very useful when the height of the triangle is not given.

## Surface areas-volumes

In this topic, you learn how to find the surface area and volume of common 3D shapes like cubes, cuboids, cylinders, and cones. For a cube, you calculate the area of all six square faces to get its total surface area, and find its volume by multiplying length by width by height. A cylinder’s total surface area includes the areas of its two circular bases and its curved side, found using the formula 2πr(r + h). The volume of a cylinder is calculated as πr²h, while a cone’s volume is one-third of that of a cylinder with the same base and height, given by (1/3)πr²h. You also learn to solve word problems involving these shapes, like finding the area of a tent or the water a tank can hold.

## Statistics

In Class 9, you learn to collect, arrange, and show data in pictographs, bar graphs, and double bar graphs. You also find the mean, median, and mode of ungrouped data to understand the central value of a collection of numbers. For example, to find the mean marks of your class, you add all the marks and divide by the number of students. A frequency distribution table helps you count how many times each number appears in a set of data. These methods make it easy to understand and share information about numbers and groups.

## Probability

In Class 9, you learn about probability, which tells us how likely something is to happen. You find probability by using the formula Probability = (Number of favourable outcomes) / (Total number of possible outcomes), like when you find the chance of getting a head when tossing a coin. You understand that probability is always a number between 0 (impossible) and 1 (sure to happen). You also learn to describe chance using words like 'certain', 'likely', 'unlikely', and 'impossible'. For example, when you pick a red marble from a bag with only blue marbles, the probability is zero.
