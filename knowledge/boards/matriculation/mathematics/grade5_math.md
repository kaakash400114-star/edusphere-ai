# Matriculation Class 5 Mathematics

## Very large numbers and estimation

Children learn about very large numbers like thousands, lakhs, and crores, and how to read and write them in words and numerals. They understand the place value system up to lakhs, knowing the value of each digit in a number, such as 1 in 1,00,000 being one lakh. Estimation helps them round off numbers to the nearest ten or hundred to make calculations easier, like estimating 48 as 50 to add quickly. They also learn to compare large numbers using symbols like >, <, or =, for example, 5,00,000 > 4,99,999. These skills build a strong foundation for handling bigger numbers in everyday life.

## Angles and shapes

Children learn to identify and name different angles like right angles, acute angles, and obtuse angles. They understand that a right angle looks like the corner of a square or rectangle. Pupils explore different shapes like triangles and quadrilaterals and count their sides and corners. They learn that a triangle has three sides and three corners, while a square has four equal sides and four right angles. Children also discover how to draw these shapes using a scale and a protractor. They practice grouping shapes based on their properties, such as the number of sides or the presence of right angles.

## Area-perimeter

In Grade 5, we learn to find the area and perimeter of simple shapes like squares and rectangles. Perimeter is the total distance around a shape, found by adding the lengths of all its sides. For a square, all four sides are equal, so we multiply one side length by 4. Area is the space inside a shape, measured in square units; for a rectangle, we multiply the length by the breadth. We also compare these concepts—perimeter is the boundary, while area is the space covered inside.

## Fractions: unlike operations

In this topic, children learn to add and subtract fractions with different denominators. First, they find a common denominator by identifying the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of the two numbers. They then convert each fraction to an equivalent fraction with the new denominator. For example, to add ½ and ⅓, they find the LCM of 2 and 3, which is 6. They change ½ to ³⁄₆ and ⅓ to ²⁄₆. Finally, they add the new fractions: ³⁄₆ + ²⁄₆ = ⁵⁄₆. The same steps are used for subtraction.

## Decimals operations

Students learn to add and subtract decimal numbers by first writing them in a column, ensuring the decimal points are one below the other, just like in whole numbers. For example, to add 2.5 and 1.25, we write them with the decimal points aligned, making it easier to calculate 3.75. They also learn to multiply decimals by whole numbers, treating them like regular whole numbers and then placing the decimal point correctly in the product. For instance, multiplying 4.2 by 3 gives 12.6. Division of decimals by whole numbers is introduced by performing the division as usual and placing the decimal point directly above its position in the dividend.

## Multiples-factors-HCF-LCM

In Class 5, children learn about multiples and factors. Multiples are numbers you get when you count in jumps of a number, like the multiples of 5 are 5, 10, 15, and so on. Factors are numbers that divide another number exactly, so the factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 12. They also find the Highest Common Factor (HCF), which is the biggest number that is a factor of two numbers, for example, the HCF of 8 and 12 is 4. Lastly, they learn about the Lowest Common Multiple (LCM), which is the smallest number that is a multiple of two numbers, like the LCM of 4 and 6 is 12. These ideas help solve problems with numbers and groups.

## Number patterns

Children learn to observe and extend number patterns using simple addition or subtraction rules. They identify the pattern by finding the difference between consecutive numbers, like in the sequence 2, 5, 8, 11, where we add 3 each time. Similarly, they work with patterns that decrease, such as 20, 15, 10, 5, where we subtract 5 each time. They also practice completing number grids and filling in missing numbers based on the given rule. These activities help them understand how numbers change in a regular way and predict the next numbers in a sequence.

## Maps and directions

In this topic, children learn to read simple maps and identify basic directions like north, south, east, and west. They understand symbols and colours used on maps, such as blue for water and green for parks, and how they show real places. For example, a map of their school might show classrooms, the playground, and the gate. They also learn to follow directions to move from one place to another, like "walk north from the gate to reach the library". By practising with maps and charts, they can find locations and describe the position of objects using simple directional words.

## Volume with unit cubes

Children learn to find the volume of 3D shapes by counting unit cubes, which are small, identical cubes that fill the space inside a shape completely. Each cube has a volume of 1 cubic unit, so the total number of cubes inside the shape gives its volume. For example, if a cuboid is made of 24 unit cubes, its volume is 24 cubic units. They also learn that the volume of a cuboid can be calculated using the formula length × breadth × height. By practising with models and drawings, children understand how to measure and compare the space occupied by different objects.

## Bar graphs

In Class 5, children learn to read and draw simple bar graphs using a graph sheet. A bar graph has a horizontal axis for categories, like fruits, and a vertical axis for numbers, like how many children like each fruit. Bars are drawn as rectangles of equal width with gaps between them, and their height shows the value using a scale. For example, a bar of height 5 units can show 5 children liking apples. We use a ruler to draw straight bars and label them correctly.
