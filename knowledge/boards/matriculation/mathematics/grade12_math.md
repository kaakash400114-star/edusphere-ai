# Matriculation Class 12 Mathematics

## Relations-functions

In Class 12, you learn that a relation is a set of ordered pairs linking two sets, while a function is a special relation where every input has exactly one output. You identify functions using vertical line tests and notations like f(x) = 2x + 1, which means for any x, the output is 2 times x plus 1. You also study different types of functions, such as one-one (injective), onto (surjective), and bijective functions, which tell us how inputs and outputs are paired together. For example, the function f(x) = x² is not one-one because both 2 and -2 give the output 4.

## Inverse trigonometry

In Class 12, you learn about inverse trigonometric functions, which are the opposite of sine, cosine, and tangent. These functions, written as sin⁻¹x, cos⁻¹x, and tan⁻¹x, give you the angle whose sine, cosine, or tangent is x. You study their domains and ranges, for example, sin⁻¹x has a domain from -1 to 1 and a range from -π/2 to π/2. You also learn important formulas, like sin⁻¹x + cos⁻¹x = π/2, and how to solve equations using these functions, such as finding the angle θ where sinθ = 1/2.

## Matrices

In the Matrices chapter, you learn about rectangular arrays of numbers called elements, with rows and columns. You will practice adding or subtracting matrices of the same order, like [[2, 3], [1, 4]] + [[1, 0], [5, 2]] = [[3, 3], [6, 6]], and multiplying a matrix by a scalar, such as 2 × [[1, 0], [0, 1]] = [[2, 0], [0, 2]]. The rules for matrix multiplication are special; you multiply rows of the first matrix by columns of the second, as in [[1, 2], [3, 4]] × [[5, 6], [7, 8]] = [[19, 22], [43, 50]]. You will also find the transpose of a matrix by flipping its rows into columns.

## Determinants

In Class 12 Mathematics, students learn about determinants, which are special numbers calculated from a square matrix using specific rules, such as the diagonal method for 2x2 matrices and the general formula for larger matrices. They study how to find the determinant of a 3x3 matrix by expanding it along a row or column using minors and cofactors. Key concepts include properties like the determinant of a triangular matrix and the effect of row operations on its value. For example, the determinant of matrix [[a, b], [c, d]] is ad - bc, while for [[1, 2, 3], [0, 4, 5], [0, 0, 6]], it is 1×4×6 = 24. Students also explore applications, like solving systems of linear equations using Cramer’s rule.

## Continuity-differentiability

Students learn that a function is continuous at a point if it is defined there, its limit exists, and both are equal, like the function f(x) = x² everywhere. They explore differentiability, understanding that a function must be continuous first to be differentiable, but continuity alone doesn't guarantee differentiability, as seen in sharp corners of functions like |x| at x=0. Using first principles and standard rules, they find derivatives of sums, differences, products, quotients, and composite functions (chain rule). Examples include differentiating sin(2x) using the chain rule or finding the derivative of x³ + 2x using sum and power rules.

## Derivative applications

In this topic, students learn how to use the derivative to find the rate of change of one quantity with respect to another, like the speed of a moving object. They also discover how to find the equation of a tangent line to a curve at a given point using the derivative. The derivative is used to check if a function is increasing or decreasing and to find the maximum and minimum values of a function, which helps in solving real-life problems like finding the least amount of material needed for a box. For example, if a function represents the profit of a company, the derivative can help find the production level for maximum profit.

## Integrals

In Class 12, you learn to find the integral, or anti-derivative, of a function, which is the reverse of differentiation. You study the basic rules, like the integral of a constant, and the power rule for integrating terms like x². You also learn about definite integrals, which are calculated using the difference between the values of the anti-derivative at two points, and indefinite integrals, which have a constant of integration ‘C’. For example, the integral of 2x is x² + C. This topic helps you find areas under curves, like the area of a shape drawn on graph paper.

## Integral applications

In Class 12 Mathematics, students learn to use integrals to find areas enclosed by curves and regions between two curves, applying definite integrals with proper limits. They calculate the area of the region bounded by the parabola y² = 4x and the line x = 4 as a practical example. The topic also covers finding the volume of solids of revolution, like spheres and cones, by rotating curves around the x or y-axis. Students derive and apply the standard formulas for volume, such as π∫[a to b] y² dx for rotation about the x-axis. Additionally, they explore the concept of the average value of a function over an interval using the integral formula (1/(b-a))∫[a to b] f(x) dx.

## Differential equations

Students learn to identify and solve first-order differential equations, which are equations involving a function and its derivatives. They study methods like separation of variables to solve equations of the form dy/dx = f(x)g(y), where variables can be moved to opposite sides. The concept of integrating factors is introduced to solve linear differential equations, such as dy/dx + Py = Q, by multiplying both sides with a special factor. Students also explore applications of these equations in real-life situations, like finding population growth models or cooling of objects, to understand how they describe change over time.

## Vectors

In this topic, you learn about vectors as quantities having both magnitude and direction, represented by directed line segments. You will study vector operations like addition (parallelogram and triangle laws), subtraction, and scalar multiplication, which change a vector’s magnitude or direction. Important concepts include the position vector of a point, the section formula to divide a line segment internally or externally, and the dot product (scalar product) to find the angle between two vectors or check perpendicularity. For example, the dot product of vectors **a** = 3**i** + 4**j** and **b** = 2**i** - 3**j** is calculated as (3 × 2) + (4 × -3) = -6.

## 3D geometry

In three-dimensional geometry, students learn about the distance between two points in space using the formula √[(x₂-x₁)² + (y₂-y₁)² + (z₂-z₁)²]. They study the section of a sphere by a plane and find its area when the plane is at a distance 'd' from the centre. The concept of the angle between two lines in space is introduced using their direction ratios. Students also learn to find the shortest distance between two skew lines and the equation of a line in space. For example, they calculate the distance between the lines (x-1)/2 = (y-2)/3 = (z-3)/4 and (x-4)/5 = (y-5)/6 = (z-6)/7.

## Linear programming

In Class 12 Mathematics, you will learn linear programming to solve real-life problems by finding the best possible outcome. You will identify variables, frame objective functions (like maximising profit or minimising cost), and write linear inequalities as constraints. These constraints are graphed on a coordinate plane to form a feasible region, which is a polygon. The optimal solution is always found at one of its corner points (vertices), which you find by solving the equations of the boundary lines. For example, a company might use this to decide how many of two products to make to get the highest profit without exceeding limits on materials and labour.

## Probability

In Class 12 Probability, you learn about independent and dependent events, where the occurrence of one does or does not affect the other. You study the addition rule for mutually exclusive events, P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B), and the multiplication rule for independent events, P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). Conditional probability, P(A|B), is the chance of event A happening given that event B has already happened. You also explore Bayes’ Theorem, which helps find the probability of a cause given an effect, and solve real-world problems like finding the probability of drawing a king from a deck of cards.
