# Matriculation Class 1 Mathematics

## Numbers 1-100

Students learn to count numbers from 1 to 100 in both ascending and descending order. They understand the concept of place value, identifying numbers as ones, tens, and hundreds (e.g., 25 has 2 tens and 5 ones). Children learn to read and write numerals and corresponding words for numbers up to 100. They compare numbers using symbols like 'greater than', 'less than', and 'equal to' (e.g., 45 > 32). Students also learn to skip count by 2s, 5s, and 10s to build number sense.

## Addition up to 20

Children learn to add two numbers up to 20 using objects, fingers, and mental methods. They understand addition as combining groups to find a total, for example, 5 apples and 3 apples make 8 apples. The plus sign (+) shows addition, and the equal sign (=) shows the result, as in 5 + 3 = 8. They practice adding one-digit numbers and simple two-digit numbers without carrying over, like 10 + 5 = 15. Using number lines and pictorial representations helps them visualize and understand the concept of addition clearly.

## Subtraction up to 20

Children learn to subtract numbers up to 20 using objects, pictures, and counting back. They understand subtraction as taking away from a group, like removing 3 apples from 5, leaving 2. The concept of 'remaining' is introduced through simple examples such as 7 minus 2 equals 5. Students practice writing subtraction sentences using the minus (−) sign and equals (=) sign, like 10 − 4 = 6. They also learn to use the number line to count backwards to find the difference between two numbers.

## Shapes around us

Children learn to recognise and name simple shapes like circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles in their surroundings. They understand that shapes have different features, such as round sides for circles and straight sides for squares. For example, a ball is a circle and a book is a rectangle. Students also learn to identify these shapes in objects like coins, plates, windows, and toys. The focus is on visual recognition and naming shapes correctly without measuring sides or counting angles.

## Measurement: long-short, heavy-light

Children learn to compare objects using words like long, short, heavy, and light based on their size and weight. They identify which object is longer or shorter than another, like comparing a pencil with a book. For weight, they understand which object feels heavier or lighter, such as comparing a stone with a feather. No formal formulas are used; instead, children rely on direct observation and simple comparisons. This helps them develop basic measurement skills and descriptive language for everyday objects.

## Money: rupees and coins

Children learn to identify and distinguish between different coins (like 1 rupee, 2 rupees, 5 rupees, 10 rupees) and notes (like 10 rupees, 20 rupees, 50 rupees). They count and add coins and notes of the same type, for example, two 5 rupee coins make 10 rupees. Children also understand that money is used to buy things and that we need to pay the correct amount. They learn to make small amounts using different coins, such as making 6 rupees with a 5 rupee coin and a 1 rupee coin.

## Time: day-night, o'clock

Children learn to observe the difference between day and night. They understand that day time is for being awake and playing, and night time is for sleeping. They are introduced to the hours on a clock face and learn to tell the time for the hour, for example, 8 o’clock in the morning for school. They learn to read the time when the long hand points to 12 and the short hand points to a number. They also learn to associate their daily activities with the time, such as eating lunch at 12 o’clock in the day.

## Sorting and patterns

In Class 1, children learn to sort objects based on a single attribute, such as colour, size, or shape. They identify and create simple patterns like ABAB using beads, blocks, or pictures. For example, they can arrange red and blue beads as red, blue, red, blue. They also understand and complete patterns with sounds, actions, or numbers. This helps them recognise the rule of a sequence, such as clap, stomp, clap, stomp. Sorting and patterns build early skills for grouping and logical thinking.
