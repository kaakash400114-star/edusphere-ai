# Matriculation Class 6 Mathematics

## Knowing numbers

In this topic, children learn to read, write, and compare numbers up to ten lakh using place value charts. They understand the number system with periods like ones, thousands, and lakhs, and can identify the place value of each digit. For example, in the number 45,671, the digit 5 is in the thousands place. Students also learn to form numbers using digits and compare them using symbols like >, <, and =. They practice writing numbers in both words and numerals, such as seventy-three thousand and 73,000. This helps them build a strong foundation for working with larger numbers in future lessons.

## Whole numbers

In Class 6, we learn about whole numbers, which are counting numbers starting from 0 and going on forever without any fractions or decimals. We understand how to arrange these numbers in order from smallest to largest or from largest to smallest, like 10, 20, 30, and so on. We also learn how to add, subtract, multiply, and divide whole numbers using simple methods, such as using the number line or standard algorithms. For example, when we add 5 and 3, we get 8, and when we subtract 7 from 12, we get 5. Finally, we explore the properties of whole numbers, like commutativity, which means the order of adding or multiplying two numbers does not change the result, such as 4 + 5 = 5 + 4.

## Playing with numbers (HCF-LCM)

In Class 6, we learn about HCF and LCM of numbers. HCF is the biggest number that divides two or more numbers exactly, like the HCF of 12 and 16 is 4. LCM is the smallest number that can be divided by two or more numbers, like the LCM of 4 and 6 is 12. We find HCF by listing factors or using the prime factorisation method. We find LCM by listing multiples or prime factorisation. We also use HCF to divide things equally and LCM to find when events will happen together.

## Basic geometry

In Class 6, students learn about basic shapes like circles, squares, rectangles, and triangles, and how to count their sides and corners. They understand the difference between straight lines and curved lines, and how to draw shapes using a ruler and compass. Children are taught to find the perimeter of shapes by adding the lengths of all their sides, like finding the border of a playground. They also learn about the area of squares and rectangles by counting the small squares inside them. This helps them measure space, like the floor area of a room.

## Elementary shapes

In Class 6, we learn about different elementary shapes like circles, squares, rectangles, and triangles. We identify these shapes in our classroom and surroundings, such as a round clock or a square book. We also study the properties of these shapes, like counting their sides and corners. A square has four equal sides and four corners, while a triangle has three sides and three corners. We draw these shapes using a ruler and a compass to make neat and accurate pictures. Understanding these shapes helps us see maths in the world around us.

## Integers

In Class 6, we learn about integers, which are positive and negative whole numbers, including zero. We use the number line to understand how positive numbers are to the right of zero and negative numbers are to the left. We learn simple rules: adding two positive integers gives a positive sum, like 3 + 2 = 5, and adding two negative integers gives a negative sum, like -4 + (-1) = -5. When adding a positive and a negative integer, we subtract their smaller absolute value and take the sign of the larger number, for example, -6 + 3 = -3. We also compare integers using symbols like > (greater than) and < (less than), such as -2 < 1 because -2 is to the left of 1 on the number line.

## Fractions

In Class 6, children learn about fractions, which are parts of a whole number. They study how to identify and name fractions like ½ (one-half) and ¼ (one-quarter), where the bottom number (denominator) shows how many equal parts the whole is divided into, and the top number (numerator) shows how many parts are taken. Students learn to add and subtract fractions with the same denominator, such as ⅖ + ⅕ = ⅗, by simply adding the top numbers. They also practice comparing fractions to see which is bigger or smaller, like knowing ½ is greater than ¼. Real-life examples, like sharing a chocolate bar or cutting a pizza into equal slices, help them understand fractions better.

## Decimals

In this topic, children learn about decimals, which are numbers with a point called a decimal point that separates the whole number part from the fractional part. They understand that numbers like 0.5, 2.75, and 15.25 are decimals, where the digits after the decimal point represent tenths, hundredths, and so on. For example, 0.5 means five tenths, and 2.75 means two whole numbers and seventy-five hundredths. Children also learn to compare decimals by looking at the digits from left to right, just like whole numbers, and they practice writing decimals in expanded form, such as 3.4 = 3 + 0.4. Additionally, they start to add and subtract decimals with the same number of decimal places, ensuring the decimal points are aligned correctly.

## Data handling

In Class 6, we learn to collect and organise information called data using tally marks and tables. We make simple bar graphs using squares or lines to show numbers, like counting how many fruits are liked by children in the class. We also learn to read and understand the information shown in pictographs, where pictures stand for numbers. The main idea is to find the answer to questions by looking at the organised data, like finding which is the most liked fruit.

## Mensuration

In Class 6, students learn mensuration to find the area and perimeter of simple shapes like squares and rectangles. They use formulas such as perimeter = 4 × side for squares and perimeter = 2 × (length + breadth) for rectangles. For area, the formula area = side × side is used for squares, while area = length × breadth is used for rectangles. These concepts help in measuring the boundary and surface of objects, like finding the perimeter of a classroom floor or the area of a notebook. Students also solve real-life problems, such as calculating the length of a fence needed for a garden or the area of a playground.

## Algebra introduction

In this topic, children learn to use letters like a, b, c or x, y, z to show unknown numbers. They understand that a letter can stand for any number, like in '5 + x = 10', where x is the number 5. They learn to write simple number statements as algebraic expressions, for example, writing '5 times a number' as '5 × x'. Children also solve simple equations by finding the missing number, such as solving 'x + 3 = 7' to get x = 4. This helps them understand how numbers and letters work together in maths problems.

## Ratio-proportion

In Class 6, children learn about ratios to compare two quantities of the same kind, like the number of boys to girls in a class. They understand that a ratio, written as 3:4, means for every 3 items of one thing, there are 4 of another. The topic also introduces proportion, which checks if two ratios are equal, such as 2:3 and 4:6, using the rule "product of means equals product of extremes". Children solve simple problems to find missing values in proportions, like figuring out how many apples can be bought with a certain amount of money. These concepts help them compare things fairly and solve real-life problems involving sharing and comparing.
