# Matriculation Class 7 Mathematics

## Integers

In Class 7, you learn about integers, which are positive and negative whole numbers, including zero. You discover how to add and subtract integers using the number line, where moving right adds and moving left subtracts. For example, 3 + (-2) is 1, and 5 - (-1) is 6. You also learn rules for multiplying and dividing integers: the product of two negatives is positive, while the product of a positive and a negative is negative. Division follows the same rule, so 12 ÷ (-3) is -4. These rules help you solve real-life problems like temperatures or scores in games.

## Fractions-decimals

In Class 7, you will learn how to change fractions to decimals and decimals to fractions. To turn a fraction like 1/4 into a decimal, you divide the top number by the bottom number: 1 ÷ 4 = 0.25. You will also learn about place values in decimals, like the tenths, hundredths, and thousandths places. For example, 0.25 is read as "twenty-five hundredths". You will add, subtract, multiply, and divide decimals by lining up the decimal points and using the same rules as whole numbers, like 0.50 + 0.25 = 0.75.

## Data handling-mean

In Class 7, we learn about the mean, which is the average of a group of numbers. To find the mean, we add all the numbers together and then divide the total by how many numbers there are. For example, if we have the numbers 4, 6, and 8, we add them to get 18, then divide by 3 to find the mean is 6. This helps us understand the middle value of the data when all numbers are shared equally. The mean is useful in everyday life, like finding the average score in a test or the average height of students in a class.

## Simple equations

In Class 7, we learn to solve simple equations like x + 5 = 12, where we need to find the value of x. The main rule is to do the same thing on both sides to keep the equation balanced. For example, if we subtract 5 from both sides, we get x = 7. We also solve equations with subtraction, like y – 3 = 8, by adding 3 to both sides. Another important idea is using letters for unknown numbers and learning how to check our answers by putting the number back into the equation.

## Lines-angles

Children learn about different types of lines like straight, curved, and slanty lines. They study angles, such as right angles (like a corner of a book), acute angles (smaller than a right angle), and obtuse angles (bigger than a right angle). They also learn about pairs of angles like complementary angles (which add up to 90 degrees) and supplementary angles (which add up to 180 degrees). For example, if one angle is 60 degrees, its complementary angle will be 30 degrees. Children identify these angles in everyday objects like door corners and clock hands.

## Triangle properties

Children learn that a triangle has three sides and three angles, and that the sum of its angles is always 180 degrees. They identify different types of triangles, like equilateral (all sides equal), isosceles (two sides equal), and scalene (all sides different), and also acute, right-angled, and obtuse based on their angles. They understand the properties of triangles, such as the sum of the lengths of any two sides being greater than the third side. For example, a triangle with sides 3 cm, 4 cm, and 5 cm is a right-angled triangle because it follows the Pythagorean rule. They also learn to draw triangles using a ruler and compass, making sure all three sides connect properly.

## Comparing quantities-percent

In Class 7, we learn to compare numbers using percentages, which means parts of one hundred. We convert fractions and decimals into percentages to see them as out of 100, like turning ½ into 50% or 0.25 into 25%. To find the percentage of a given number, we multiply the number by the percentage and divide by 100. For example, to find 20% of 50, we calculate (20 × 50) ÷ 100 = 10. We also learn to increase or decrease a number by a percentage, such as adding 15% to the price of an item to find the new cost.

## Rational numbers

In this topic, children learn about rational numbers, which are numbers written as fractions where both the top number (numerator) and bottom number (denominator) are whole numbers, and the denominator is not zero. They practice comparing two rational numbers by finding a common denominator or cross-multiplying to see which is larger, like comparing 3/4 and 5/8. Kids also learn to add and subtract rational numbers using the same denominator, for example, 2/5 + 1/5 = 3/5. They explore how to convert mixed numbers like 2 1/3 into improper fractions such as 7/3. Finally, they solve simple problems using rational numbers, like sharing 3 pizzas equally among 4 friends, where each gets 3/4 of a pizza.

## Perimeter-area

In Class 7, you learn to find the perimeter and area of simple shapes like squares, rectangles, and triangles. The perimeter is the total distance around a shape, and for a rectangle, you add the length and breadth and then multiply by 2 (P = 2(l + b)). The area is the space inside a shape, and for a rectangle, you multiply the length by the breadth (A = l × b). For a square, since all sides are equal, the perimeter is 4 times the side (P = 4s) and the area is the side multiplied by itself (A = s²). You also learn to calculate the area of a triangle by multiplying the base by the height and then dividing by 2 (A = ½ × b × h).

## Expressions

Students learn to write and understand mathematical expressions using numbers, variables, and operations like addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. They practise replacing variables with numbers to find the value of an expression, such as finding the value of 3x + 5 when x = 2. The concept of like and unlike terms is introduced to help them combine or simplify expressions, like adding 4x and 2x to get 6x. They also learn to form expressions from simple word problems, such as writing 2n + 3 for "twice a number increased by three". Proper use of brackets is emphasised to show the correct order of operations in an expression.

## Exponents

In Class 7, we learn about exponents, which are small numbers written above and to the right of a bigger number, telling us how many times to multiply the bigger number by itself. For example, in 3², the small number 2 means we multiply 3 by 3, which is 9. We also learn rules like multiplying exponents (2³ × 2² = 2⁵) and dividing exponents (5⁴ ÷ 5² = 5²). Another important rule is that any number with an exponent of 0 is always 1, like 7⁰ = 1. We use these rules to solve simple problems and understand how exponents make big numbers easier to write and work with.

## Symmetry-solids

In Class 7, children learn about symmetry in solid objects like cubes, cylinders, and cones. They identify if a solid looks the same after rotation, called rotational symmetry, and count the number of matching positions it can take. Solids like cubes have four rotational symmetries, while cylinders have infinite symmetries when spun around their axis. Children also explore plane symmetry by cutting solids with a flat sheet to see if both sides are equal, like a sphere being divided into two identical hemispheres. These activities help them understand how shapes and objects are balanced and uniform in different ways.
