# Matriculation Class 6 Science

## Food components

Food is made of different parts that give us energy and help us grow. We learn about three main food parts called carbohydrates, proteins, and fats. Carbohydrates give us energy and are found in rice and chapatis. Proteins help build our body and repair it, like dal and eggs. Fats give us energy and are found in ghee and nuts. We also learn about vitamins and minerals from fruits and vegetables that keep us healthy. Fibre from fruits and vegetables helps clean our stomach and keeps us strong.

## Sorting materials

We learn to group objects based on their properties like colour, size, shape, and material. For example, we can sort fruits into round and round or sweet and sweet. We also sort materials into natural, like wood and cotton, and man-made, like plastic and glass. Objects can be sorted into living and non-living things, like plants and animals versus stones and toys. We sort materials based on whether they float or sink in water, like a boat floating and a stone sinking.

## Separation methods

We learn to separate mixtures using different methods like handpicking, winnowing, sieving, and filtration. Handpicking helps us separate big things like stones from rice by picking them with our hands. Winnowing uses wind to separate lighter husk from heavier grains like wheat. Sieving helps separate things of different sizes, like flour from bigger impurities, using a sieve with holes. Filtration separates solid particles from liquids using a filter paper, like cleaning muddy water. Evaporation helps remove salt from salty water by heating, leaving the salt behind.

## Plants around us

Plants grow in different places like gardens, forests, and even in water. They all have parts like roots, stem, leaves, and flowers which help them live. Roots take water from the soil, the stem carries it to the leaves, and leaves make food for the plant with sunlight. Flowers make seeds, which grow into new plants. Some plants are small like grass, and some are tall like trees, and they give us air, food, and wood.

## Body movements

Our body helps us move with the help of bones and muscles. Bones are hard and form our skeleton, like the skull that protects our brain. Muscles are soft and pull the bones to help us walk, run, and pick up things. Joints are where two bones meet, and they allow movement, like the hinge of a door in our elbows and knees. We can move our body because our brain sends messages to the muscles to pull the bones.

## Living organisms-habitats

Living organisms live in different places called habitats where they find food, water, and shelter. A habitat can be a forest, a pond, a desert, or even the soil in your garden. Plants and animals are adapted to their habitats; for example, cactus plants have thick stems to store water in deserts, and fish have gills to breathe in water. Different animals have different homes, like a bird nest on a tree or a rabbit burrow under the ground. All living things depend on their habitat to survive and grow.

## Motion and measurement

In Class 6 Science, we learn about motion and how to measure different things. Motion means an object moving from one place to another, like a car on the road or a ball rolling. We discover that to measure the length of a moving object, we use a standard unit like the metre. Speed tells us how fast something moves; it is how much distance it covers in a certain time, like a bicycle covering 10 metres in 1 minute. We also learn about slow and fast motion, comparing a walking tortoise to a running rabbit.

## Light-shadows

Light always travels in a straight line and helps us see things. When light is blocked by an object, a dark area called a shadow is formed on the opposite side. The size and shape of the shadow change when we move the object closer or farther away from the light source. For example, a small stick held near a torch makes a big shadow, but a big tree far away makes a small shadow. Shadows help us understand how light works and are formed because objects do not let light pass through them.

## Electricity-circuits

Electricity travels in a path called a circuit. We need three things to make a circuit work: a source like a battery, wires to connect everything, and a bulb that glows. When the circuit is complete, the electricity flows and the bulb lights up. If the circuit is broken, like by opening a switch, the bulb goes off because the electricity stops. A switch is a small device that can open or close the circuit to control the flow of electricity.

## Magnets

Magnets have two ends called poles, a north pole and a south pole, which is where their pulling power is strongest. When two magnets are brought together, similar poles repel or push away from each other, while opposite poles attract or pull towards each other. Some common objects made of materials like iron, such as nails and paper clips, are attracted to magnets because they can become temporary magnets themselves. The area around a magnet where it can pull things is called its magnetic field, which is stronger the closer you are to the magnet. Not all metals are magnetic; for example, a coin or an aluminium foil does not get pulled by a magnet.

## Water

Water is a very important liquid that we need for life. We learn that water can exist in three forms: solid (ice), liquid (water), and gas (water vapour). When water is heated, it turns into steam, and when it is cooled, it becomes ice. Plants and animals also need water to live, just like we do. Water helps us clean things, and we drink it to stay healthy. We must always save water and not waste it because it is very precious for everyone.

## Air

Air is all around us, and it is a mixture of gases. We learn that air has weight, and it presses on us from all sides, creating atmospheric pressure. Air is necessary for living things to breathe; without air, we cannot survive. Plants help keep the air clean by taking in carbon dioxide and giving out oxygen. For example, when we blow air into a balloon, we can see it expand because air takes up space.

## Waste management

In Class 6, we learn about waste management and how we can keep our surroundings clean. We understand different types of waste, like biodegradable (like peels and dry leaves) and non-biodegradable (like plastic and metal), and where they should be thrown. The three R’s—Reduce, Reuse, and Recycle—are important rules to follow to make less waste and protect our environment. We also learn about segregating waste into separate bins for dry and wet garbage, and how composting can turn wet waste into useful manure for plants.
