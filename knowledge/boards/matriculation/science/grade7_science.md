# Matriculation Class 7 Science

## Nutrition in plants

Plants make their own food using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide through a process called photosynthesis. The green pigment in leaves called chlorophyll captures the sunlight energy needed to turn these ingredients into food. Roots absorb water from the soil, and leaves take in carbon dioxide from the air. This food, mainly in the form of sugar, gives plants the energy to grow, make flowers, and produce fruits like mangoes and apples. For example, a banyan tree uses these ingredients to become very big and strong.

## Nutrition in animals

Animals get their food from eating other plants or animals to stay alive. Different animals have different ways of eating; cows chew grass with their flat teeth while snakes swallow their food whole. After eating, the food is broken down in the body to give energy for all activities. This process is called digestion, and it happens in special parts of the body like the stomach. Solid waste is left from the body after all the useful food is taken out.

## Heat

Heat is a form of energy that flows from a hotter object to a colder one. We feel heat through our skin, and we measure its temperature using a thermometer. Heat always moves from things that are warm to things that are cool, like when a hot cup of tea cools down in a room. Heat makes materials expand or grow bigger, like when a metal spoon left in hot water becomes slightly longer, and contract or shrink when they get cold. We use heat every day for cooking food on a gas stove and to keep our bodies warm by wearing woollen clothes in winter.

## Acids-bases-salts

In class 7, we learn about acids, bases, and salts. Acids are sour, like lemon juice and tamarind, and turn blue litmus paper red. Bases are bitter and soapy, like soap solution and baking soda, and turn red litmus paper blue. When an acid and a base mix, they neutralise each other to form salt and water, for example, hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide make sodium chloride salt. Salts are used in our daily lives, such as sodium chloride (table salt) and calcium chloride (used in making tofu). We also test for acids and bases using indicators like litmus paper or turmeric paste.

## Physical-chemical changes

In Class 7, we learn that changes around us can be physical or chemical. A physical change only alters the shape or size of a substance, like tearing a paper or melting ice, but does not create a new material. A chemical change, however, produces one or more new substances with different properties, such as burning a candle which makes wax vapour and smoke. We also see this when we fry an egg, as the liquid egg changes into a solid, cooked form. The key difference is that physical changes are usually reversible, while chemical changes are not.

## Respiration

Respiration is the process where living things take in air to get oxygen and give out air to remove carbon dioxide. We breathe through our nose or mouth, and air passes through the windpipe to reach the lungs. In the lungs, oxygen moves into the blood to give us energy, while carbon dioxide is released out. Plants also respire; they take in oxygen at night and give out carbon dioxide, which is why we should not sleep under trees. Our body needs energy for all activities like walking, eating, and thinking, which is made by respiration.

## Transport in plants-animals

Plants move water and food through special pipes called xylem and phloem. Water travels up from the roots to the leaves through xylem, like water in a straw. Animals move from place to place using different body parts, such as legs for walking and wings for flying. Fish use fins and tails to swim in water, while birds use their beaks to find food. These ways of moving help plants and animals grow, eat, and stay safe in their homes.

## Reproduction in plants

Plants make new plants like we do, but they have special ways. They can grow from seeds, like sunflowers from black seeds, or from their leaves, like bryophyllum plants making tiny plants at the edges. Some plants grow from their stems, like potatoes growing from the main potato. They can also make new plants from their roots, like ginger or grass. Flowers are important for making seeds. A flower has parts like petals, sepals, stamens, and pistils. The stamen has pollen, and the pistil has ovules. When pollen from the stamen goes to the ovule in the pistil, it helps make a seed, which can grow into a new plant.

## Motion-time

In Class 7 Science, you will learn about motion and how it changes over time. An object is in motion when it moves from one place to another, like a car on the road. You will study different types of motion, such as straight-line motion and circular motion, and what makes an object slow down or speed up. Time is used to measure how long it takes for an object to travel a certain distance. For example, if you walk to school, you can measure the time you take and the distance you cover to understand your speed.

## Electric current effects

We learn about the effects of electric current in our daily life. When current flows through a wire, it produces heat, like in an electric heater or an iron. Current also creates a magnetic effect, which helps in making electric bells and motors work. We understand that for a current to flow, we need a complete path, called a circuit, made of a battery, wires, and a device like a bulb. If a circuit has more than one device, they can be connected in two ways: series or parallel. In a series circuit, all devices are connected one after the other, but if one stops, the whole circuit breaks.

## Light

Light is a form of energy that helps us see objects. It travels in a straight line and can be reflected when it bounces off a smooth surface, like a mirror. When light passes through a prism, it splits into seven colours, forming a rainbow. Opaque objects cast shadows because they do not let light pass through them, while transparent objects like glass allow light to go through. We use concave mirrors to make things look bigger and convex mirrors to see a wider area, like in a car.

## Forests-water-wastewater

Forests are homes for plants and animals and also catch rainwater, helping rivers and ponds stay full. Roots of trees hold the soil together, so soil does not wash away when it rains, preventing floods. Wastewater comes from homes, schools, and factories, and it contains dirty things that must be cleaned before it goes back to rivers. We clean wastewater in treatment plants by removing dirt through filtering and using helpful bacteria to eat harmful substances. Keeping forests and water clean helps all living beings stay healthy and gives us clean water for drinking, bathing, and farming.
