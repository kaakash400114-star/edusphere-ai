# Matriculation Class 5 Science

## Plant and animal interdependence

Plants and animals depend on each other to live and grow. Plants give animals oxygen to breathe and food to eat, like when a goat eats grass. Animals help plants by spreading their seeds, for example, when a bird eats a fruit and drops the seed somewhere else. They also give plants carbon dioxide, which they need to make food. Animals help plants by dropping their droppings on the ground, which acts as a natural fertilizer, helping the plants grow strong and healthy.

## Seed germination and dispersal

Seeds are tiny baby plants that need water, air, and warmth to grow into new plants, which is called germination. A seed has a baby plant (embryo) and a food store (cotyledon) to help it grow until it can make its own food. Seeds travel far from their parent plant in different ways, like floating on water (coconut), sticking to animals (burrs), or being carried by wind (dandelion seeds). After landing in good soil, the seed sprouts a root downwards to find water and a shoot upwards to reach sunlight, turning into a small seedling.

## Water conservation

Water is a precious natural resource that we must save every day. We learn simple rules to conserve water like turning off the tap tightly while brushing teeth or taking a bucket bath instead of a shower. Rainwater is collected in pits and tanks to fill the groundwater, which is called recharging. We should fix leaking taps at home and in school to stop water wastage. Farmers use special methods like drip irrigation to give water directly to plant roots. Every small action saves water and helps us keep this important resource for our future.

## Simple experiments with water

Water can exist in three states: solid, liquid, and gas. Ice is solid water, liquid water fills a glass, and water vapour is the gas we see as steam. When you heat ice, it melts to become liquid water, and when you boil water, it turns into steam. Water can change back to liquid when steam cools down. We can make water dirty by mixing mud in it, and we can clean it by using a filter. Water always flows downwards due to gravity, and it takes the shape of the container it is kept in.

## Health and hygiene

We learn about keeping our body clean to stay healthy. We wash our hands with soap and water before eating and after using the toilet to remove germs. We also learn that brushing our teeth twice a day keeps them strong and prevents cavities. Eating healthy food like fruits, vegetables, and dal gives us energy and helps us grow. Keeping our surroundings clean by not littering and using a dustbin stops the spread of sickness.

## Crops and farmers of our state

Farmers in our state grow many crops like rice, wheat, and maize. They need fertile soil, sunlight, and water for their crops to grow well. Farmers plough the land, sow seeds, and add manure to help plants grow strong. Different crops grow in different seasons, like kharif crops in monsoon and rabi crops in winter. Farmers also use tools like ploughs and tractors to work on their fields. They take care of crops by protecting them from insects and weeds to get a good harvest.

## Simple machines

Simple machines help us do work with less force. We learn about six types: lever, pulley, wheel and axle, inclined plane, wedge, and screw. A lever is a bar that turns on a fixed point, like a see-saw. An inclined plane is a sloped surface, like a ramp used to push a heavy box into a truck. A pulley is a wheel with a rope, used to lift a flag up a pole. These machines make tasks easier for us by changing how we use force.

## Solar system tour

In Grade 5 Science, children learn about our solar system, which is the Sun and all the planets that move around it in big circles called orbits. They discover that the Sun is the biggest star in the middle, and the eight planets—Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune—travel around it in order, from the closest to the farthest. Each planet is a different colour and size; for example, Earth is blue and green, and Jupiter is the largest planet with many spots. They also learn that some planets have moons, like Earth has one moon, and Jupiter has many, which are small rocky objects that circle them.

## Energy saving

Energy is what helps us do work, like running a fan or lighting a bulb. We should save energy to not waste it and help our planet. Simple ways to save energy are switching off lights and fans when we leave a room. Using natural light instead of electric bulbs during the day also saves energy. Saving energy means using only the amount we need, so it lasts for a long, long time.

## Forests and wildlife

Forests are homes for many plants and animals like tigers, elephants, and peacocks. The plants in the forest, such as trees and shrubs, make their own food using sunlight, a process called photosynthesis. Forests give us important things like wood for furniture, oxygen to breathe, and clean water. Animals and plants depend on each other; for example, bees help flowers make seeds, and flowers give bees sweet nectar. People must protect forests by not cutting too many trees and by planting new ones, so that plants and animals can live safely.
