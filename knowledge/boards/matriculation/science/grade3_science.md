# Matriculation Class 3 Science

## From farm to plate: our food

Food travels from farms to our plates in many steps. Farmers grow crops like rice and wheat in fields, and animals like cows and hens give us milk and eggs. Fruits and vegetables are picked from trees and plants, then washed and packed. At markets, shopkeepers sell these fresh foods. Finally, families bring them home to cook delicious meals. This helps us learn about healthy eating and where our food comes from.

## Plant parts and jobs

Plants have different parts like roots, stem, leaves, flowers, and fruits, and each part does a special job. The roots hold the plant in the ground and drink water from the soil, just like a straw for the plant. The stem carries water and food from the roots to the other parts, like a little pipe inside the plant. Leaves make food for the plant using sunlight, and flowers help make seeds for new plants to grow. Fruits protect the seeds and sometimes are eaten by animals and humans, helping the seeds travel far away.

## Animals: food and coverings

Animals eat different foods, which we call their food. Some animals eat plants like leaves and grass, for example, the cow and elephant. Other animals eat other animals, like the lion eating a deer, or insects, like the frog eating a fly. All animals have a body covering to protect themselves. Many animals have fur or hair, like the cat and monkey. Some animals have feathers, like the parrot, and others have scales, like the fish. This covering helps them stay warm, dry, and safe.

## Birds: beaks and nests

Birds have different kinds of beaks to eat different foods. Parrots have strong, curved beaks to crack hard nuts, while hummingbirds have long, thin beaks to drink nectar from flowers. Birds use twigs, grass, mud, and feathers to build their nests. Sparrows make cup-shaped nests in trees, and weaver birds weave beautiful nests using grass. Nests are safe homes where mother birds lay their eggs and take care of their babies.

## Bones and muscles

Our body has many strong bones inside us that help us stand, walk, and sit properly. Bones are hard and work like a strong frame to hold our body together. Muscles are soft and stretchy parts that help us move, like when we bend our arms or run in the playground. Our fingers have small bones called phalanges, and our legs have strong thigh muscles called quadriceps. When we feel hungry, our stomach muscles help in digesting food, just like our bones help us hold our pencil while writing.

## Sense organs

We learn about our five sense organs: eyes, ears, nose, tongue, and skin. Our eyes help us see things like colourful flowers and bright sun. Our ears help us hear sounds like the chirping of birds and our teacher’s voice. The nose lets us smell nice things like jasmine flowers and food cooking. Our tongue helps us taste sweet candies and salty chips. The skin helps us feel soft blankets, rough stones, and hot sunlight. All these sense organs work together to help us understand the world around us.

## Water cycle basics

Water on Earth moves in a cycle called the water cycle. The Sun heats water in ponds, rivers, and seas, and it turns into water vapour. This water vapour goes up into the air and forms clouds. When the clouds become heavy, the water falls back to the ground as rain. Rain water flows into rivers and collects in the ground, ready to start the cycle again.

## Air and weather

Air is all around us, even though we cannot see it. It moves and pushes things, which we feel as wind. Wind can be gentle or strong, and it helps in bringing rain. Rain, snow, and sun are all parts of weather. We wear different clothes like woollens in winter and cotton in summer to protect ourselves from changing weather. Some days are hot and some are cool, and this change is called weather.

## Earth, day-night

The Earth is a big round ball that moves all by itself. It spins around very, very fast, but we do not feel it moving. This spin is called rotation. When our part of the Earth faces the Sun, it is daytime. When that same part turns away from the Sun, it becomes night. This cycle of day and night happens every 24 hours, which is our one full day. For example, when you are playing in the park during the day, children on the other side of the world are fast asleep at night.

## Caring for environment

Children learn that keeping our environment clean is very important. They understand that we must not throw waste, like plastic bags or paper, on the ground. Instead, we use dustbins and always put garbage in the right place. Children learn about different materials and can sort them into groups like paper, plastic, and metal. They also learn that plants and trees are our friends and we should not pluck their leaves or flowers. Water should not be wasted, and we should turn off the tap when not in use. These small actions help us take good care of our Earth.
