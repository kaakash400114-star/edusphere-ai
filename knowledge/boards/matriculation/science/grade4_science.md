# Matriculation Class 4 Science

## Green plants make food

Green plants make their own food through a process called photosynthesis. They use sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide to prepare food in their leaves. The green pigment in leaves, called chlorophyll, helps capture sunlight from the air. Plants also get water from the soil through their roots. For example, a mango tree uses sunlight to make food for its own growth and to make mangoes. This process helps plants stay healthy and gives us fruits, vegetables, and oxygen to breathe.

## Plant and animal adaptations

Plants and animals change to live in their homes. A cactus stores water in its thick stem. A lotus has wide leaves to float. Animals have body coverings like feathers, fur, or scales. A camel has a hump to store food energy. A chameleon changes colour to hide from enemies. These changes help them find food, water, and stay safe.

## Teeth and digestion

Teeth help us to chew and break down our food into small, soft pieces. There are different kinds of teeth for different jobs: the front teeth, called incisors, help us bite into food like apples. The pointed teeth, called canines, help us tear tough food like rotis. The flat teeth at the back, called molars, help us grind and crush hard food like rice and dal. Food passes through our mouth, food pipe, stomach, and intestines to be digested and give us energy.

## Safety-first aid-cleanliness

We learn that safety means keeping ourselves and others safe from harm. We follow simple rules like looking both ways before crossing the road and not touching hot things. First aid is the help we give to someone who is hurt, like putting a small bandage on a cut. We also learn to keep ourselves clean by washing our hands with soap and water before eating food and after playing. Cleanliness helps us stay healthy and prevents germs from making us sick.

## Air-water pollution basics

Air is all around us and water is essential for life. Pollution means making air and water dirty and not clean. We learn that smoke from vehicles and factories makes the air bad to breathe. Dumping garbage and chemicals in rivers and ponds makes the water unsafe for drinking and for fish. Burning trash and bursting fireworks also harm the air we breathe, just like spitting or washing dirty things in the well makes the water dirty. Keeping our surroundings clean helps stop pollution.

## Sun-earth-moon

The Sun is a big, hot star that gives us light and heat. The Earth is our planet, which moves around the Sun in a path called an orbit; it also spins around itself to make day and night. The Moon is a smaller, rocky body that goes around the Earth, and we see its different shapes because sunlight falls on it differently each night. The Sun, Earth, and Moon are connected: the Earth orbits the Sun, and the Moon orbits the Earth, and this is why we have day, night, and the phases of the Moon. For example, when the Moon is between the Sun and Earth, we see it as a new Moon, and when it is on the other side, we see a full, bright Moon.

## Food and nutrients

Food is our body’s fuel, giving us energy to play and grow. Nutrients are like tiny helpers in food that help us stay strong and healthy. We learn about the five main nutrients: carbohydrates for quick energy (like rice and chapati), proteins for building muscles (like dal and eggs), fats for long-lasting energy (like ghee), vitamins and minerals for fighting sickness (like oranges and spinach), and water for keeping us hydrated. Eating a variety of foods, like fruits, vegetables, grains, and dairy, helps our body get all these important nutrients.

## Force and simple machines

We learn that force is a push or a pull, like when we push a toy car or pull a swing. Gravity is a force that pulls everything down towards the earth. Simple machines make work easier for us; a lever is a machine like a seesaw or a nutcracker, and an inclined plane is a slope like a ramp or a staircase. A wheel and axle, like the one in a bicycle or a trolley, helps us move heavy things with less force. We see that force and machines are all around us in our daily life.

## Our state's resources

Children in Class 4 learn about the different resources found in their state, such as water from rivers and lakes, soil for farming, and minerals like iron and coal. They understand that these resources are important for daily life, like using water for drinking and cooking, and soil for growing crops like rice and wheat. The children also learn about renewable resources, like forests which give us wood and fresh air, and non-renewable resources, which are limited and cannot be replaced once used. They are taught to respect and protect these resources, such as not wasting water and not cutting down too many trees, to ensure they are available for everyone in the future.

## Water saving

We learn why water is important and how to save it. Water is precious, so we must not waste it. We turn off the tap tightly while brushing our teeth and washing hands. We should take a bucket bath instead of a shower to use less water. Rainwater can be collected in pots and tanks to use later. Small actions, like fixing a dripping tap, help save a lot of water. Saving water helps everyone and keeps our rivers and lakes full.
