# Matriculation Class 9 English

## Unseen comprehension passages

In this topic, children learn to read and understand unseen passages by identifying the main idea, supporting details, and the writer’s purpose. They practise finding answers directly stated in the text and making simple inferences using clues like words such as "but" or "however" to understand contrasts. For example, they might answer a question like "Why was the character happy?" by pointing to a sentence that says, "He won the race and clapped his hands with joy." Students also learn to pick out the meaning of new words from the context of the surrounding sentences to improve their vocabulary.

## Descriptive paragraph and diary writing

In Class 9 English, students learn to write descriptive paragraphs that paint a picture with words by using our five senses to describe people, places, or things in detail. They practise structuring paragraphs with a clear topic sentence, followed by supporting sentences that add specific details, and a concluding sentence to wrap up the description. For diary writing, they learn to express personal feelings, thoughts, and experiences in a first-person perspective, using the past tense and emotive language to make entries authentic. For example, a descriptive paragraph might describe the bustling scene of a local market, focusing on sounds like vendors shouting and smells of spices, while a diary entry could describe feeling excited before a school sports day.

## Story writing and picture composition

In Class 9, students learn to write original stories and picture compositions using a clear structure with a beginning, middle, and end. They practice developing characters and settings with descriptive details, such as describing a brave character or a stormy forest to make the story interesting. For picture compositions, they are taught to observe the scene carefully and write a logical sequence of events, including what happened before and after the picture, like describing why a child is crying and what happens next. Grammar and vocabulary are strengthened by using correct tenses, punctuation, and new words to express ideas clearly. The focus is on creating imaginative stories that connect to real-life situations, such as helping a friend or solving a small problem.

## Tenses in depth

In Class 9, you will learn to use tenses correctly in your writing and speaking. You will study the simple, continuous, perfect, and perfect continuous forms of the present, past, and future tenses. For example, you will know the difference between "I play" (present simple) and "I am playing" (present continuous). You will also learn to change the form of the verb to show when an action happens. Practice helps you use these tenses accurately in sentences and stories.

## Clauses and sentence transformation

In Class 9, you will learn to identify and use different kinds of clauses in sentences. A clause is a group of words with a subject and a verb. An independent clause can stand alone as a complete sentence, like "The cat sat". A dependent clause cannot stand alone and begins with words like "because," "if," or "when," such as "because it was hungry." You will also practice combining simple sentences into complex ones using these clauses. For example, you can change "The sun set. The birds flew back to their nests" to "The birds flew back to their nests when the sun set".

## Reported speech, voice and determiners

In Grade 9, students learn to change sentences from direct speech to reported speech, adjusting pronouns and verb tenses correctly. They understand that the reporting verb is in the past tense and words like ‘now’ become ‘then’. Active voice, where the subject performs the action, is contrasted with passive voice, where the subject receives it. Determiners such as articles (a, an, the), demonstratives (this, that), and possessives (my, your) are taught to show quantity, specificity, and ownership. For example, "He said, 'I like this book'" becomes "He said that he liked that book".

## Literature: prose themes and characters

Students learn to identify and explain the main themes and characters in stories, such as friendship, courage, and honesty. They understand how characters are developed through their actions, dialogue, and feelings, like Ravi in "The Last Leaf" showing bravery. Children also explore how the setting and events influence the story's central ideas. For example, in the story "The Fun They Had," they compare characters to understand different perspectives on school. This helps them describe how characters change and why the author chooses certain themes to teach a lesson.

## Poetry: central idea and devices

In this topic, students learn to identify the central idea, or main message, of a poem by reading its lines carefully. They study common poetic devices like simile (comparing two things using “like” or “as”), metaphor (a direct comparison without “like” or “as”), and personification (giving human qualities to non-human things). For example, a poem might use a simile to say the sky is “as blue as a clear lake.” Students also learn about rhyme and rhythm, which make poems sound musical and help the poet express feelings more strongly. By understanding these tools, students can better appreciate what the poet is trying to say.

## Vocabulary building

In Grade 9, students learn to understand and use new words from their English textbooks, stories, and general reading. They are taught to find the meanings of words using a dictionary and to group words into sets called synonyms and antonyms. Children also learn about prefixes and suffixes, which are small word parts added to the beginning or end of a base word to change its meaning. For example, adding the prefix 'un-' to the word 'happy' creates 'unhappy', which means not happy. They practice using these new words in their own sentences to improve their writing and speaking skills.

## Speaking and listening skills

Students learn to listen carefully to their friends and teachers, paying attention to the main points and important details while others speak. They practice speaking in clear and complete sentences, using correct grammar and expressing their own thoughts and ideas confidently in front of the class. During group discussions, students take turns to speak and listen to others, building on what they say to share different points of view. They also learn to give simple instructions or directions clearly to a partner and ask relevant questions to get more information. For example, they might describe their favourite book or explain how to make a simple snack.
