# Matriculation Class 6 English

## Nouns-pronouns in use

Students learn to name people, places, animals, and things as nouns, like "teacher," "Delhi," and "cat." They also understand that pronouns replace nouns to avoid repetition, such as using "he" for "Rohan" and "she" for "Priya." Singular nouns name one person or thing, like "book," while plural nouns name more than one, like "books." Students practise changing singular nouns to plural by adding "s" or "es," like "box" to "boxes." They also learn about possessive nouns, which show ownership, like "Ravi's pencil" or "the dog's tail.

## Tenses simple-continuous

In Class 6, we learn about two important tenses called the Simple Present and the Present Continuous. The Simple Present tense is used for habits, routines, and general truths, like "I eat rice every day" or "The sun rises in the east." We form this tense by using the subject with a verb that ends in 's', like "He plays" or "She reads." The Present Continuous tense is used for actions happening right now or around this time, like "They are playing football" or "I am eating my lunch." We make this tense by using the verb "am", "is", or "are" with a verb that has "ing" at the end.

## Adverbs-prepositions

In Class 6, we learn about adverbs and prepositions to make our sentences clear and interesting. Adverbs are words that tell us more about verbs (like *quickly run*), adjectives (*very tall*), or other adverbs (*quite fast*). Prepositions are words that show the relationship between nouns or pronouns, such as *in the box*, *on the table*, or *under the chair*. We also learn to use prepositions correctly to describe where something is or when something happens, like *We play football at school* or *She finished her homework before dinner*. By practising these, we can write better sentences and understand how words work together.

## Conjunctions

In Class 6, we learn about conjunctions, which are words that join two words, phrases, or sentences together. We use words like ‘and’, ‘but’, ‘or’, ‘so’, and ‘because’ to make our sentences longer and more interesting. For example, we can say, "Rani likes to play football and cricket," or "It was raining, so we played indoors inside." We also learn to choose the right conjunction to show the correct meaning, like using ‘because’ to give a reason. By practising these, we can write sentences that are clear and make sense to everyone.

## Sentences: phrases-clauses

In Class 6, we learn to recognise phrases and clauses in sentences. A phrase is a group of words that makes sense but does not have a subject and predicate, like “the tall tree” or “in the garden.” A clause is a group of words that has a subject and a verb, like “the dog barks.” A clause can be a full sentence if it makes complete sense, like “The sun rises.” We also learn the difference between main clauses, which can stand alone, and subordinate clauses, which depend on the main clause to make sense, like “when I go to school.”

## Punctuation mastery

In Class 6, you learn to use punctuation marks like the full stop, comma, and exclamation mark correctly to make your writing clear and easy to read. A full stop ends a telling sentence, like "The cat sat on the mat," while an exclamation mark shows strong feeling, such as "Wow! What a big dog!" You also use commas to separate words in a list, for example, "I bought apples, bananas, and oranges." Apostrophes are taught to show missing letters in words like "don't" for "do not," which helps in writing short and correctly. These rules help you write neatly and others understand your writing without confusion.

## Comprehension

In Class 6, students learn to read and understand different kinds of texts like stories, poems, and factual passages. They practice finding the main idea and important details in a paragraph, for example, identifying who, what, where, when, and why in a short story. Children also learn to answer direct questions based on the text and make simple inferences, like guessing how a character feels from what they do or say. They are taught to distinguish between facts and opinions, such as knowing that "the sun rises in the east" is a fact, while "ice cream is the best dessert" is an opinion. This helps them become better readers and thinkers.

## Informal letter-application

In Class 6, students learn to write informal letters using simple language and a friendly tone, following a clear format with date, salutation, body, closing, and signature. They practice including personal details like their name and address in the letterhead, and begin with greetings such as "Dear Mummy" or "Dear Rohan" to show warmth. The body of the letter is written in short, simple sentences, sharing news or asking questions like "How are you?" or "I missed you." Students end with polite closings such as "With love" or "Yours sincerely," followed by their name. They focus on making the letter clear, neat, and easy to read for someone they know well.

## Story writing

In Class 6, children learn to write a short story by first choosing a simple idea, like a lost puppy or a surprise birthday, and then planning it using a beginning, middle, and end. They must describe the characters and setting clearly, for example, "Rani was a small girl with long hair who lived in a green house with a big garden." The story should have a clear problem, like the puppy getting stuck, and a solution, like Rani helping it out. Finally, children use simple sentences and connect them with words like 'and' or 'because' to make the story flow smoothly.

## Speaking fluency

Students learn to speak fluently by practising clear and complete sentences without long pauses. They are taught to use joining words like 'and', 'but', and 'because' to connect their ideas smoothly. For example, instead of saying "I like apples. I like bananas", they learn to say "I like apples and bananas". Children also learn to speak at a steady pace, not too fast or too slow, and to use their voice loudly enough for everyone to hear. They practise taking turns to speak and listening carefully when others are talking to ensure their conversations are smooth and easy to understand.
