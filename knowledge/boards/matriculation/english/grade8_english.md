# Matriculation Class 8 English

## Determiners-quantifiers

Determiners are words that come before nouns to tell us about them. Quantifiers are a type of determiner that tells us the number or amount of something. We use words like many, much, few, and several to count nouns, and some, any, and a few to talk about small amounts. We also learn to use quantifiers like plenty of and a number of to talk about larger amounts. For example, we say "many books" for countable nouns and "much water" for uncountable nouns. This helps us speak and write clearly about the quantity of things around us.

## Tense narration

In this topic, students learn to identify and correctly use different tenses in their writing. They understand that tenses show when an action happens—past, present, or future. For example, "He played cricket" is past tense, "He is playing cricket" is present continuous, and "He will play cricket" is future tense. They learn to change the form of the verb to match the correct tense, like adding "ed" for past tense or using "am/is/are" for present continuous. This helps them tell clear and interesting stories about things that happened before, are happening now, or will happen later.

## Passive-reported mastery

In Class 8, students learn to change direct speech sentences into passive-reported speech, which shows an action being done to something. For example, “The teacher corrected my mistakes” becomes “My mistakes were corrected by the teacher” in passive voice. The main rules include keeping the past tense, using “was” or “were” with the past participle (like “corrected”), and adding “by” to show who did the action. Students also practice changing questions and commands into passive-reported form, like “Who opened the door?” becoming “The door was opened by whom?”

## Clauses: complex sentences

In Class 8, you will learn to identify and write complex sentences using a main clause and one or more subordinate clauses. A main clause makes sense by itself, like "The cat slept," while a subordinate clause, beginning with words such as "because," "if," or "when," adds extra information but cannot stand alone. For example, in "The cat slept because it was tired," the main clause is "The cat slept" and the subordinate clause is "because it was tired." You will practise joining clauses using conjunctions like "although," "since," and "unless" to build longer, more interesting sentences. This helps you express ideas clearly and show how different ideas are connected.

## Non-finites

In Class 8, we learn about non-finite verbs, which are words that look like verbs but do not show any tense. They are of three types: infinitives (to read), gerunds (reading), and participles (reading). An infinitive is used to express purpose, like "She studies to pass the exam." A gerund acts as a noun in a sentence, such as "Swimming is fun." A participle, either present (reading) or past (read), is used to describe a noun or to form continuous and perfect tenses, for example, "The reading girl is my sister." We practice using these non-finites correctly in our sentences.

## Modals deduction

Students learn to use modals like must, could, and might to make educated guesses based on evidence. For example, if a desk is wet, we say, “Someone must have spilled water.” They also learn to express possibility with may and might, such as, “It might rain today.” The class practices forming sentences with these modals to show how sure or unsure a guess is. This helps students describe situations logically when they are not completely certain. They understand that must shows strong belief, while could and might show weaker possibilities.

## Prefixes-suffixes-idioms

Students learn to add prefixes like un-, dis-, and re- to the start of words to change their meaning, for example, making "happy" into "unhappy". They also study suffixes like -less, -ful, and -ly, which are added to the end of words to form new ones, like turning "care" into "careless". Additionally, children understand common idioms, such as "it’s raining cats and dogs," which are phrases that mean something different from their individual words. They practise identifying these prefixes, suffixes, and idioms in sentences to improve their vocabulary and reading skills. This helps them become better readers and writers by building stronger language foundations.

## Literary devices

In Class 8, students learn about literary devices which are special techniques writers use to make stories more interesting and powerful. They study figures of speech like similes, which compare two things using 'as' or 'like', for example, "as brave as a lion". Another important device is the metaphor, which directly says one thing is another, like "time is money". Pupils also explore alliteration, the repetition of the same initial sound in words close together, such as "the big blue balloon bounced by the beach". Finally, they learn about personification, where human qualities are given to non-living things, like "the wind whispered secrets through the trees".

## Formal letter-email-article-speech

In Class 8, you learn to write formal letters, emails, articles, and speeches using correct structure and language. A formal letter has a date, address, subject, salutation like ‘Dear Sir/Madam’, body paragraphs, and a closing such as ‘Yours faithfully’. An email includes a clear subject line and a polite sign-off, while an article or speech uses an introduction, main points in order, and a conclusion. Always use formal words and avoid slang to share ideas clearly and respectfully. For example, a speech on cleanliness would begin with a greeting, explain the importance, and end with a thank you.

## Listening-speaking

In Class 8, students learn to listen carefully and speak clearly in different situations. They practice understanding main ideas and important details from what teachers say or from audio recordings. For speaking, they learn to express their own thoughts in proper sentences, using the correct words and grammar. Students are also taught to speak in small groups, sharing their ideas politely and listening to others without interrupting. For example, they might have a class discussion about their favourite story book, taking turns to speak and answering questions.
