# Matriculation Class 2 English

## Phonics blends and sight words

Children learn two-letter and three-letter blends like 'bl' in 'blue' and 'spl' in 'splash'. They also memorise common sight words such as 'the', 'and', and 'is' that cannot be sounded out easily. These skills help them read simple sentences by blending sounds and recognising words quickly. The practice involves reading short texts with these blends and sight words to build fluency. Teachers use flashcards and picture books to make learning engaging and fun for young readers.

## Naming words: common and special

In Class 2, children learn to identify naming words, which are words for people, places, animals, and things. They learn about common naming words, which name general things like 'dog', 'cat', or 'school', and special naming words, which name specific people, places, or things, such as 'Raj' or 'India'. A special naming word always begins with a big letter, even in the middle of a sentence, to show it is special. By understanding this difference, children can use words correctly when they talk and write. For example, 'animal' is a common word, but 'Lion' is a special word.

## Describing words

Children learn about describing words, which are called adjectives. Adjectives are words that tell us more about a noun, such as what it looks like, how it feels, or what colour it is. For example, we can say a big ball, a soft doll, or a red car. We also learn to use describing words to compare things, like a tall boy or a short girl. This helps us make our sentences more interesting and clear when we talk or write about things around us.

## Action words and tenses (is/are, -ed)

Children learn action words, called verbs, in this lesson. They are introduced to the present tense using ‘is’ and ‘are’ to describe actions happening now. For example, “The cat is sleeping” and “The birds are flying.” They also learn the past tense by adding ‘-ed’ to action words that end with a simple sound, showing actions that have already happened. For instance, “walk” becomes “walked” as in “She walked to school” and “jump” becomes “jumped” like “The frog jumped.” These simple rules help them talk about actions happening at different times.

## Am-is-are, has-have

Children learn to use the verb 'am' with the pronoun 'I' and 'is' with singular nouns like 'the cat' or 'he'. They use the verb 'are' with plural nouns like 'the dogs' or 'they' and with 'you'. For possession, they use 'has' with singular subjects like 'the boy has a ball' and 'have' with plural subjects like 'the boys have books'. This helps them make simple sentences correctly, such as 'I am happy' or 'She has a doll'. They understand that 'is' and 'has' are for one, and 'are' and 'have' are for two or more.

## Position words: in-on-under

In Class 2, students learn to use position words like 'in', 'on', and 'under' to describe where something is. The word 'in' shows that an object is inside another object, for example, "the pencil is in the box." The word 'on' shows that an object is on the top surface of another object, like "the book is on the table." The word 'under' shows that an object is below another object, for instance, "the cat is under the chair." Students practice these words by identifying and naming the position of different objects around them. This helps them build a strong vocabulary for describing the world accurately.

## Plurals with -s and -es

In Class 2, we learn to change words from singular (one) to plural (more than one) by adding ‘-s’ or ‘-es’. Most words add ‘-s’ to show plural, like ‘cat’ becomes ‘cats’ or ‘book’ becomes ‘books’. Some words, like those ending with ‘s’, ‘sh’, ‘ch’, ‘x’, or ‘z’, need ‘-es’ to make plural, such as ‘bus’ becoming ‘buses’ or ‘box’ becoming ‘boxes’. We also add ‘-es’ to words ending with a consonant + ‘y’, like ‘baby’ becoming ‘babies’. These rules help us speak and write correctly when we talk about more than one thing.

## A-an-the

We learn to use 'a' before a word that starts with a consonant sound, like 'a cat' or 'a dog'. We use 'an' before a word that starts with a vowel sound, like 'an apple' or 'an umbrella'. The words 'a' and 'an' are called 'indefinite articles' and they tell us about one thing. We do not use 'a' or 'an' with plurals, like 'cats' or 'apples'. We also learn that 'the' is a 'definite article' and we use it when we talk about a specific thing, like 'the red ball'.

## Capital letters and stops

In Class 2, we learn to use capital letters at the start of sentences and for names of people, places, and days. We also learn to put a full stop at the end of every sentence to show where it stops. For example, we write "Rahul goes to school." with a capital 'R' and a full stop at the end. We always start a new sentence with a big capital letter, like "Anita plays in the park." This helps us write correctly and makes our writing easy to read. We remember these rules every time we write in our notebooks.

## Reading and picture writing

In this topic, children learn to read simple sentences and understand that pictures help tell a story. They learn to match words with pictures, like seeing the word 'cat' next to a picture of a cat. Children also learn to tell a short story using pictures in order, such as showing a cat drinking milk. They learn to identify the beginning, middle, and end of a story with the help of pictures. For example, they can look at three pictures to know what happened first, next, and last. This helps them understand how stories are put together and how pictures and words work together.
