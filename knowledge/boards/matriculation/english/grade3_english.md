# Matriculation Class 3 English

## Nouns: common-proper-collective

In Class 3, we learn that words are names for people, places, animals, and things. We study three kinds of nouns: common nouns (general names like boy, school, dog), proper nouns (specific names like Ramesh, Delhi, Bruno), and collective nouns (groups of things like team, herd, bunch). A common noun does not start with a capital letter, but a proper noun always starts with a capital letter. Collective nouns tell us about many things together as one group. For example, a flock of sheep is a collective noun, and Mumbai is a proper noun.

## Pronouns

Pronouns are special words that take the place of names of people, places, animals, or things. They help us avoid repeating the same noun again and again in a sentence, making our speech and writing clearer. The common pronouns we learn are ‘I’, ‘you’, ‘he’, ‘she’, ‘it’, ‘we’, and ‘they’. For example, instead of saying “Rahul is playing football”, we can say “He is playing football” where ‘he’ stands for Rahul. We also use pronouns like ‘this’ and ‘that’ to point out things near us and far from us.

## Adjectives and comparison

In Class 3, children learn about adjectives, which are describing words that tell us more about nouns. They understand that adjectives tell us how things look, feel, sound, or taste, such as "a big ball" or "a sweet mango". Students are introduced to comparing two things using comparative adjectives, where we add "-er" to short words, like "bigger" or "sweeter", and use "more" for longer words, like "more beautiful". They also learn about superlative adjectives to compare three or more things, adding "-est" to short words, like "biggest" or "sweetest", and using "most" for longer words, like "most beautiful". This helps them describe things better and understand how to compare different objects clearly.

## Verbs: -ing actions

Verbs are action words that tell us what someone or something is doing. We add -ing to many action words to show the action is happening now, like walking, talking, or playing. Some verbs change their spelling when we add -ing, for example, run becomes running and dance becomes dancing. We use these -ing words to make sentences about what we are doing right now, such as "The cat is sleeping" or "We are eating". This helps us describe actions in a fun and simple way.

## Simple past

In Grade 3, we learn about the simple past tense to talk about things that happened earlier. We add ‘ed’ to the end of many action words, like ‘walk’ becomes ‘walked’ or ‘play’ becomes ‘played’. For some words that end with ‘e’, we only add ‘d’, such as ‘smile’ turning into ‘smiled’. We also learn that some special words do not follow this rule, like ‘go’ becomes ‘went’ and ‘eat’ becomes ‘ate’. We use this tense to share stories about our day or events from the past, for example, “I played cricket yesterday.”

## Adverbs of manner

Adverbs of manner describe how an action is done, telling us more about the verb. They usually come after the verb in a sentence. For example, in the sentence "The cat runs quickly," the word "quickly" is an adverb of manner. We use adverbs like slowly, softly, and loudly to explain how something happens. These words help us make our sentences more clear and interesting when we speak and write.

## Prepositions

Children learn about prepositions, which are small words that show the position or place of things. They understand words like ‘on’, ‘in’, ‘under’, ‘behind’, and ‘next to’ to describe where objects are. For example, a book is ‘on’ the table, a cat is ‘under’ the chair, and a ball is ‘behind’ the door. They also learn to use prepositions to show time, such as ‘in’ the morning, ‘at’ night, and ‘on’ Sunday. By practising simple sentences, children can correctly place prepositions to describe the location of people, animals, and things around them.

## Commas and apostrophes

Children learn to use commas in lists, like "I bought apples, bananas, and oranges," and before the word "and" in a list. They also use commas to separate words in a series, such as "She runs, jumps, and plays." For apostrophes, they learn to show possession, like "Riya's book" or "the dog's bone," to show something belongs to someone. They also learn to use apostrophes in contractions, such as "don't" for "do not" and "can't" for "cannot." These small marks help make writing clear and easy to read.

## Reading a passage

Children learn to read short, simple passages with familiar words and clear pictures. They read from left to right and top to bottom to follow the story correctly. They find the main idea, like what the passage is mostly about. They also notice small details, such as "the dog is big" or "the cat is black". They answer simple questions about the story, like "Who is in the passage?" or "What is happening?". They also find and read words that are opposite, like "big" and "small", to understand meaning.

## Paragraph writing

In Class 3, students learn to write a paragraph by grouping related sentences together to express one main idea. They understand that a paragraph begins with a topic sentence that tells the main idea, followed by 2-3 supporting sentences which give details or examples. Children are taught to arrange these sentences in a logical order, using simple connecting words like 'and' or 'but' to make their writing smooth. For example, a paragraph about 'My Pet Dog' might start with the topic sentence, "My pet dog is my best friend," and then add sentences about its colour, what it eats, and how it plays.
