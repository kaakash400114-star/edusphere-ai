# Matriculation Class 7 English

## Perfect tenses

In Class 7, you learn about perfect tenses to talk about actions that are finished or have a connection to the present. The present perfect tense is formed with has/have and the past participle, like "She has finished her homework," to show an action is complete recently. The past perfect tense uses had with the past participle, such as "He had eaten his lunch before the bell rang," to describe an action that happened before another action in the past. These tenses help you share events clearly, whether they just happened or happened long ago but are important now.

## Modals

Students learn about modal auxiliary verbs like can, could, may, might, should, and must, which help express ideas like ability, permission, possibility, and necessity. Modal verbs are always used with a main verb to form sentences, and they do not change for tense, number, or person. For example, we use "can" to show ability ("She can run fast"), "must" for necessity ("You must finish your homework"), and "may" for permission ("May I go out?"). The main verb always comes after the modal verb, and it is usually in its base form. Students practice using these modals in sentences to express different ideas clearly.

## Voice: active-passive

In class seven, you learn to change sentences between active and passive voice. In active voice, the subject does the action, like "The cat chases the mouse". In passive voice, the subject receives the action, and we use the verb "to be" with the past participle, like "The mouse is chased by the cat". To make a passive sentence, we find the object of the active sentence and make it the new subject. We add the correct form of "to be" and the past participle of the main verb, and we use "by" to show who did the action.

## Reported speech

In Class 7, students learn to change direct speech into reported speech by using reporting verbs like "said" or "told". The rules include moving the tense back one step (present becomes past, past becomes past perfect) and adjusting pronouns and time words (today becomes that day, here becomes there). Statements use "that" to introduce the reported part, while questions lose their question word order and use "if" or "whether". Commands turn into requests or orders using "to" or "not to", like "He asked me to close the door".

## Clauses

In Class 7, we learn to identify different kinds of clauses in sentences. A clause is a group of words that has a subject and a verb. A main clause makes complete sense by itself and can stand alone as a sentence, like "The boy is playing football". We also learn about subordinate clauses, which do not make sense by themselves and must be attached to a main clause; for example, "who is wearing a blue cap" is a subordinate clause. When we join a subordinate clause to a main clause, we use words like "who", "which", "because", "if", and "since", such as "The boy who is wearing a blue cap is playing football".

## Determiners

In Class 7, students learn about determiners, which are words used before nouns to give more information. They study articles like ‘a’, ‘an’, and ‘the’, and learn that ‘a’ is used before consonant sounds (a book), ‘an’ before vowel sounds (an apple), and ‘the’ for specific things (the sun). Pupils also explore possessive determiners such as ‘my’, ‘your’, ‘his’, ‘her’, ‘its’, ‘our’, and ‘their’, which show who the noun belongs to. Demonstrative determiners like ‘this’, ‘these’, ‘that’, and ‘those’ are taught to point out nouns near or far (this pen, those books). Finally, students practice using quantifiers such as ‘some’, ‘many’, ‘few’, and ‘all’ to tell the quantity of nouns.

## Prepositions fine points

In Class 7, students learn about prepositions and their correct use in sentences. They understand that prepositions show the relationship between a noun or pronoun and other words, like 'The book is on the table'. They identify common prepositions such as 'in', 'on', 'under', 'between', and 'for'. They also learn about prepositional phrases, which begin with a preposition and end with a noun or pronoun, like 'She ran to the park'. Students practice using prepositions to describe time, place, and direction in their writing to make their sentences clearer.

## Editing errors

In Class 7, we learn to spot and fix errors in sentences like spelling mistakes, using words like ‘their’ and ‘there’ correctly, and checking for full stops. We also correct punctuation marks like commas and question marks to make sentences clear. Another important rule is finding subject-verb agreement errors, such as “The boys is playing” which should be “The boys are playing”. Additionally, we learn to arrange words in the right order to form a proper sentence, like changing “Running fast dog the is” to “The dog is running fast”. We practice editing sentences by correcting these mistakes to make writing correct and easy to understand.

## Poetry-prose comprehension

Students learn to find the difference between poetry and prose by looking at features like rhyme and rhythm in poems and sentences in prose. They understand that poetry uses lines and stanzas, while prose has paragraphs and tells a story like in a book. For example, they can spot rhyming words like 'cat' and 'hat' in a poem or the beginning of a sentence with a capital letter in prose. Students also learn to find the main idea in both types of writing by reading carefully and asking questions. They practice this by reading short poems and stories and answering simple questions about what they read.

## Story-dialogue-descriptive writing

Students learn to write dialogues using quotation marks to show exactly what characters say, along with action words that describe their movements and expressions. Descriptive writing helps them create clear pictures in the reader’s mind by adding details about how people, places, and things look, sound, or feel. For example, they write sentences like, *“Ravi shouted, ‘Help me!’ while running fast,”* to combine action with speech. They also practice writing longer paragraphs that connect dialogues and descriptions to tell a small, interesting story. These skills make their writing lively and easy for others to understand.
