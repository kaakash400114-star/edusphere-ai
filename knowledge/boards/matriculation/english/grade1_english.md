# Matriculation Class 1 English

## Tamil-English alphabet sounds (phonics)

Children learn to identify and match the sounds of Tamil and English letters, focusing on simple consonants and short vowel sounds. They practice linking these letter sounds to their corresponding symbols in both scripts through picture-word associations. For example, the sound 'k' is associated with the Tamil letter க and the English letter K, both linked to words like 'kite' or 'kalam'. Students also begin to blend two letter sounds to form simple words, such as 'ma' + 'pa' = map. The goal is to build a strong phonetic foundation in both languages for early reading and writing skills.

## Naming words around us

Children learn to identify and name things around them, like tables, chairs, books, and pencils. They understand that these naming words are called nouns and are used to name people, animals, places, and things. Students practice naming familiar objects in their classroom and home, such as door, window,黑板, and water bottle. They learn to use these nouns in simple sentences, for example, "This is a book" or "I see a cat." This helps them build a strong foundation for reading and writing.

## One and many

In this topic, children learn to understand the difference between singular and plural forms of nouns. They discover that singular nouns refer to one object, like a "ball" or "a book", while plural nouns refer to two or more objects, like "balls" or "books". Young learners are taught to make plurals by adding "s" to most nouns, such as "cat" becoming "cats" and "dog" becoming "dogs". They also encounter a few common nouns that change their form to become plural, like "foot" to "feet" and "child" to "children". Through everyday examples and activities, children practice identifying and using singular and plural nouns correctly in simple sentences.

## A and an

We learn to use the little words ‘a’ and ‘an’ before a noun, which is the name of a thing. We use ‘a’ when the noun starts with a consonant sound, like ‘a ball’ or ‘a dog’. We use ‘an’ when the noun starts with a vowel sound, like ‘an apple’ or ‘an egg’. These little words help us to talk about one thing at a time. For example, we say ‘a cat’ and not ‘an cat’, and we say ‘an owl’ and not ‘a owl’.

## This-that, these-those

Children learn to use 'this' and 'that' to point to things near and far. They use 'these' for many things close to them and 'those' for many things far away. For example, they say 'this pen' when a pen is near and 'that pencil' when a pencil is far. They also say 'these books' for books nearby and 'those toys' for toys far away. This helps them talk clearly about things they see and where they are.

## Simple sentences with capital letter and full stop

Children learn to write a simple sentence. A simple sentence tells one complete idea. It must start with a capital letter. We use the first letter of the name of a person, place, or animal for this rule. Every sentence must end with a full stop. The full stop looks like a small dot (.). For example, "Rahul runs." is a correct sentence. It has a capital letter 'R' at the start and a full stop at the end. We also write "The cat is black." and "I play ball." following the same rules.

## Action words

In Grade 1, children learn about action words, also called verbs. Action words are words that tell us what someone or something is doing, like ‘run’, ‘jump’, ‘sing’, or ‘read’. These words help to make sentences more interesting by showing actions and activities. For example, in the sentence "The cat runs fast," the word "runs" is the action word because it tells what the cat is doing. Children practice identifying and using simple action words to complete sentences and describe actions in their daily lives.

## Rhymes and poems

In Class 1, children learn to identify and enjoy rhymes and simple poems through listening and repetition. They explore rhyming words, which are words that end with the same sound, such as ‘cat’ and ‘mat’ or ‘sun’ and ‘fun’. Teachers introduce short, rhythmic poems with repetitive lines and sound patterns, like ‘Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star’, to help children hear and appreciate the musical quality of language. Students practice reciting rhymes aloud, recognising rhyming pairs, and filling in missing words in familiar verses. This builds phonemic awareness, vocabulary, and a love for poetic expression through playful and engaging activities.

## Opposites

Children in Class 1 learn to identify and understand pairs of opposite words, which describe contrary meanings. They learn that opposites are words that have opposite meanings, such as 'hot' and 'cold', 'big' and 'small', and 'up' and 'down'. This helps them describe the world around them more clearly. Through pictures and simple examples, they learn to use these opposite words in everyday sentences. By the end of the lesson, they can recognise and correctly use common opposite pairs to compare and describe objects and actions.

## Picture reading and speaking

Children learn to observe pictures carefully and identify simple objects, actions, and people. They use simple words and short sentences to describe what they see in the picture, such as "a red ball" or "the cat is running". They listen to others’ descriptions and respond with related words or questions. Through practice, children build their vocabulary and improve their speaking skills by sharing their ideas clearly. This helps them understand how pictures and words work together to tell a story.
