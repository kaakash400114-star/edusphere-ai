# Advanced Placement (AP) Grade 1 English
## Foundational Phonics Skills
Students learn the relationship between letters and their sounds, focusing on consonant and short vowel sounds. They practice the alphabetic principle, learning to associate a grapheme (e.g., the letter 'b') with its corresponding phoneme (/b/ sound). Instruction uses explicit, systematic phonics methods, such as blending sounds to decode simple CVC (consonant-vowel-consonant) words like "cat," "sun," and "pen." Students also practice segmenting words into individual sounds to build phonemic awareness, a critical precursor to reading.
## High-Frequency Sight Word Recognition
Students master reading and writing a set of high-frequency sight words, also known as "heart words," that do not follow standard phonetic rules. These are common words that appear frequently in texts, such as "the," "and," "is," "to," and "of." Instruction involves repeated exposure through daily practice, flashcards, and contextual reading. Students learn to recognize these words automatically by sight, which is essential for reading fluency and allows them to focus on decoding more complex vocabulary in texts.
## Narrative Text Comprehension
Students develop foundational comprehension skills by listening to and discussing narrative texts, including classic fables and folktales. They learn to identify the basic story elements: characters, setting, and the main events of the plot. They practice retelling stories in a sequential order, using words like "first," "next," "then," and "last." Students also make simple personal connections to the characters and events within the stories, demonstrating a basic understanding of narrative structure and theme.
## Informational Text Features
Students learn to recognize and use basic features of informational texts, such as the title, cover, pictures, and captions. They understand that these features provide information to help them comprehend the text. For example, students learn that the title tells the topic, and the caption explains a picture. They practice answering explicit questions based on the information provided in simple photographs and illustrations, building skills for locating facts in non-fiction texts like science books or biographies.
## Foundational Writing Skills
Students develop beginning writing skills through a process modeled on the AP exam's free-response questions. They practice constructing simple, complete sentences with a subject (who or what) and a predicate (what they do or are). Using graphic organizers, students plan and write short, descriptive sentences about familiar topics, such as their family or a pet. They apply grade-level phonics knowledge to spell simple words conventionally and practice using correct end punctuation, such as a period (.).
## Speaking and Listening Collaboration
Students practice collaborative skills by participating in small and whole-class discussions. They learn to listen attentively to others and take turns speaking, using basic conversation protocols like raising a hand to be recognized. Students practice speaking clearly and in complete sentences to share ideas and ask simple questions. They also learn to respond to questions from peers and adults with relevant information, building the foundational discourse skills necessary for later analytical discussions like those found in AP seminars.
