# Advanced Placement (AP) Grade 4 English
## Reading for Author's Craft and Purpose
Students analyze text structures, including cause/effect, compare/contrast, and problem/solution. They identify the author's purpose: to persuade, inform, or entertain. Learners recognize literary devices such as similes ("as brave as a lion") and metaphors ("the classroom was a zoo"). They determine the main idea of a multi-paragraph passage and distinguish it from key supporting details. For example, after reading a history article about the Boston Tea Party, they can state the main event and list three specific actions taken by the colonists.

## Foundational Writing Skills: Informative and Narrative
Fourth graders write multi-paragraph texts that clearly state a topic. In informative writing, they group related information into paragraphs and use facts and definitions to develop their points. For narrative writing, they establish a situation, introduce characters, and sequence events using temporal words like "first," "next," and "then." Students use dialogue and descriptive details to convey experiences and events. A student might write a personal narrative about a family trip to a national park, describing a hike and the animals seen.

## Expanding Vocabulary and Academic Language
Students determine the meaning of unknown words and phrases, including those that allude to significant characters found in mythology (e.g., "Herculean task"). They use resources like glossaries and dictionaries to confirm word meanings. Learners understand and use grade-appropriate academic and domain-specific words (e.g., "erosion," "democracy") in their speaking and writing. They identify prefixes (un-, re-) and suffixes (-ful, -less) to determine the meaning of unfamiliar words, such as understanding that "unhappiness" means not being happy.

## Comprehension and Analysis of Literary Texts
Students analyze literature, focusing on characters, setting, and plot events. They identify a character's motivations and how their feelings change over the course of a story. Learners make inferences about the text using explicit details and evidence. For instance, while reading a fable like "The Tortoise and the Hare," they can infer the moral of the story from the characters' actions and outcomes. Students also describe the major differences between poems, drama, and prose.

## Grammatical Conventions and Sentence Structure
Fourth graders apply a growing knowledge of grammar and usage conventions. They form and use the progressive verb tenses (e.g., "I was walking," "I will be walking") and ensure subject-verb agreement. Students correctly use commas in a list, dates, and to separate introductory elements from the rest of the sentence. They correctly spell grade-level words and use resources to check spelling. A student can write a sentence like, "After we visited the museum on Tuesday, we bought souvenirs," with correct punctuation.

## Foundational Research Skills and Media Literacy
Students conduct short research projects to build knowledge about a topic. They gather information from provided sources and quote or paraphrase findings to answer a specific question. Learners use digital tools, such as kid-safe search engines, to find information. They identify the main idea of a multimedia presentation, such as a short video or podcast, and distinguish their own point of view from that of an author or speaker. Students also practice evaluating the credibility of simple, age-appropriate sources.
