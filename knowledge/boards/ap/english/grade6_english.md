# Advanced Placement (AP) Grade 6 English


## Foundational Reading Comprehension
Students analyze texts using a structured approach. They identify central ideas, key details, and textual evidence to support claims. A focus is placed on distinguishing between literal and inferential meanings. For example, students might read a non-fiction article about American history and be required to locate a specific historical fact (literal) and then infer the author's opinion on the event (inferential). They learn to synthesize information from multiple paragraphs to answer complex questions about the text's overall meaning and purpose.

## Narrative Structure and Plot Analysis
Students examine the components of narrative fiction, including exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, and resolution. They use text evidence to identify these elements within a story. A key skill is analyzing how events contribute to the plot's development and the protagonist's motivation. For example, after reading a novel, students will map out the story arc, describing the main conflict, the point of highest tension, and how the conflict is ultimately resolved, using specific quotes to support their analysis of each stage.

## Argumentative Writing Fundamentals
Students learn to construct an argumentative claim supported by relevant evidence and logical reasoning. The writing process includes crafting a clear thesis statement, organizing body paragraphs with topic sentences, and including analysis that connects the evidence to the claim. An example task involves writing an essay arguing whether a character in a novel made a responsible choice, using three specific actions from the text as evidence and explaining why each action proves or disproves the claim.

## Foundational Grammar and Syntax
Students analyze sentence structures to differentiate between simple, compound, and complex sentences, constructing each using coordinating conjunctions and subordinating clauses. They apply strict rules for subject-verb agreement, ensuring correct verb forms agree with their subjects in number and person. Pronoun usage is mastered by establishing clear antecedents and ensuring pronouns match their antecedents in person, number, and gender. Punctuation rules are applied specifically to correctly use commas in series, after introductory elements, and with non-restrictive clauses, while apostrophes denote possession and form contractions. For example, they learn to correct a comma splice like "The cat slept, the dog barked" by rewriting it as "The cat slept, but the dog barked" or "The cat slept; the dog barked.

## Academic Vocabulary and Context Clues
Students will expand academic vocabulary by applying systematic methods for interpreting meaning through context. This includes identifying and using four primary types of context clues: definitions embedded within sentences, examples that illustrate a word's meaning, contrasts that show opposition, and inferences drawn from surrounding information. For example, when encountering "photosynthesis" in a science text, a student would identify the definition clue in the preceding sentence: "the process by which green plants use sunlight to synthesize foods from carbon dioxide and water." A student would also identify an example clue for the word "frugality" by reading, "She avoided expensive vacations and bought items only when necessary.

## Annotating and Close Reading
Students develop techniques for annotating a text to track their comprehension and analysis. They mark the text to identify main ideas, ask questions, define unknown words, and note patterns or significant literary devices. For example, while reading a poem, students might circle unfamiliar words, place a star next to a powerful image, and write questions in the margins about the speaker's tone. This active reading practice forms the foundation for the more sophisticated textual analysis required in later AP English courses.
