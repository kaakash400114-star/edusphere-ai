# Advanced Placement (AP) Grade 5 English

## Reading Comprehension and Literary Analysis
Students will read and analyze grade-level literary and informational texts. They will identify a text's main idea, key supporting details, and author's purpose using specific evidence from the passage. For a story like "The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe," students will identify the central conflict and analyze character motivations using direct quotes. In a non-text like "The Boston Tea Party," they will summarize the main events and explain the causes and effects of the colonists' actions, requiring precise text-based support for all claims.

## Foundational Reading Skills: Vocabulary and Word Parts
This unit focuses on building vocabulary through explicit instruction. Students will learn to determine the meaning of unknown words by using context clues within a sentence or paragraph. They will also analyze common Greek and Latin roots and affixes to decode unfamiliar terms. For example, when encountering the word *transport* in a science text, they will use the root *port* ("to carry") to determine its meaning. Students will also distinguish between multiple meanings of words, such as the different definitions for "bat" in a sports article versus a nature article.

## Writing: Opinion Pieces with Evidence
Students will learn to write opinion essays that clearly state a claim and support it with logical reasoning and relevant evidence. They will structure their writing with an introduction that presents the topic and opinion, body paragraphs with reasons and examples, and a conclusion that restates the opinion. A student might argue that recess is important by providing evidence for better focus and physical health. All supporting points must be backed by facts, details, or specific examples from credible sources, strengthening their overall argument.

## Grammar and Mechanics for Clear Communication
This unit covers the foundational rules of Standard American English grammar necessary for clear and effective writing. Students will correctly use punctuation, including commas in a series and with introductory elements. They will understand and apply subject-verb agreement and recognize the difference between commonly confused words like their/there/they're and your/you're. Students will practice these skills by editing sentences from drafts and their own writing, ensuring proper capitalization, spelling, and punctuation to enhance clarity.

## Foundational Reading Skills: Fluency and Prosody
Students will develop oral reading fluency, focusing on accuracy, rate, and prosody (expression). They will read grade-level prose and poetry aloud with sufficient accuracy and expression to support comprehension. For example, a student reading a patriotic speech would use appropriate pacing, volume, and emphasis to convey the author's tone. They will also learn to use context to self-correct misread words, demonstrating an ability to decode words automatically and maintain meaning while reading aloud to an audience.

## Research and Inquiry Skills
Students will conduct short research projects to build knowledge about a topic. This includes generating relevant questions about a subject, such as the life of a notable American like Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. They will gather information from provided texts and digital sources, using simple search terms. Students will sort evidence into categories, paraphrase information in their own words, and create a brief report or presentation to share their findings, citing the sources they used to support their information.
