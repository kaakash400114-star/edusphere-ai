# Advanced Placement (AP) Grade 2 English

## Foundational Reading: Consonant Blends and Digraphs
Students learn to decode words containing consonant blends and digraphs. Blends are two or three consonants blended together, such as in the word "stop" where /s/, /t/, /o/, and /p/ are blended. Digraphs are two letters that make one new sound, like the /sh/ sound in "ship" or the /th/ sound in "this". Students practice identifying these sounds in the beginning, middle, and end of words through word sorts and flashcards. They apply this knowledge to read simple stories and build vocabulary.

## Comprehension: Identifying Story Elements
Students learn to identify the core elements of a narrative text. These elements include the characters (who the story is about), the setting (where and when the story takes place), and the major events (what happens in the story). Using picture books and simple short stories, students practice identifying these elements. They might draw a picture of the setting or list the characters' names. This skill is essential for understanding the structure and meaning of a story.

## Writing: Informative Writing About a Topic
Students learn to write simple informational texts about a familiar topic. They are taught that informative writing explains something using facts. Students choose a topic like their favorite animal or a holiday. They then state a topic sentence, such as "My favorite animal is a dog." They provide at least two supporting details or facts to explain why, using simple sentences and correct capitalization and punctuation to complete their paragraph.

## Foundational Writing: Spelling High-Frequency Words
Students master the spelling and use of high-frequency words. These are common words that appear often in written text, such as "the", "and", "to", "of", "said", "was", and "were". Students practice these words through various activities, including tracing, writing in sentences, and using them in word games. They are expected to spell these words correctly in their daily writing, as they are often phonetically irregular and cannot be sounded out easily using standard phonics rules.

## Foundational Reading: Long Vowel Spelling Patterns
Students explore long vowel sounds, where the vowel says its name (e.g., the 'a' in "cake"). They are taught the most common spelling patterns for each long vowel: the vowel-consonant-e pattern (e.g., "make", "like"), vowel pairs (e.g., "boat", "rain"), and open syllables where the vowel at the end of a syllable is long (e.g., "me", "go"). Students practice decoding and encoding words with these patterns to build their reading accuracy and fluency with more complex text.

## Speaking and Listening: Following Multi-Step Directions
Students must accurately process and execute a series of commands in the specific order given, such as "First, take out your science journal from your desk, then open it to page 12, and finally, write your name at the top." Instruction begins with simple two-step directions and progressively increases in complexity with three to five steps, sometimes incorporating temporal markers like "before" and "after." To support memory and comprehension, teachers often introduce directions verbally and provide a written visual aid on the board, requiring students to wait for the complete instruction before acting. This practice reinforces sequential memory and active listening. Mastery is demonstrated when students can successfully complete the task in the correct sequence without constant repetition, as seen when following multi-step instructions for a science experiment.
