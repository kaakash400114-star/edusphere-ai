# Advanced Placement (AP) Grade 11 Mathematics
## Limits and Continuity
Students explore the foundational concepts of calculus by analyzing the behavior of functions as inputs approach specific values. They learn to evaluate limits using algebraic manipulation, graphical analysis, and numerical methods. Key techniques include direct substitution, factoring, and rationalizing expressions to resolve indeterminate forms like 0/0. The formal epsilon-delta definition of a limit is introduced, emphasizing precise mathematical language. Students distinguish between continuous and discontinuous functions, identifying points of discontinuity (removable, jump, infinite) and applying the Intermediate Value Theorem to prove the existence of roots within an interval.

## Differentiation
Students develop the ability to determine the instantaneous rate of change of a function. They master the limit definition of the derivative and apply differentiation rules, including the Power, Product, Quotient, and Chain Rules, for polynomial, rational, exponential, logarithmic, and trigonometric functions. The application of derivatives to real-world contexts is emphasized, such as calculating velocity and acceleration. Implicit differentiation is used to find derivatives of equations not explicitly solved for one variable. Students also learn to differentiate inverse trigonometric functions and understand the relationship between differentiability and continuity.

## Applications of Derivatives
This unit focuses on using derivatives to analyze and solve problems involving functions. Students apply the First and Second Derivative Tests to find local maxima and minima and determine intervals of increase, decrease, and concavity. They use derivatives to sketch the graphs of functions and identify points of inflection. Optimization problems are solved by finding maximum or minimum values, such as minimizing surface area for a given volume. Related rates problems involve finding the rate of change between two related quantities over time. The Mean Value Theorem is applied to establish the existence of a tangent line parallel to a secant line.

## Integration
Students learn the process of integration as the inverse operation of differentiation, also known as finding the antiderivative. They master techniques of integration, including substitution, integration by parts, and partial fractions for rational functions. The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus connects differentiation and integration, showing how to evaluate definite integrals. Students calculate the area between curves and the volume of solids of revolution using methods like the disk, washer, and shell methods. Definite and indefinite integrals are distinguished, and the properties of definite integrals are explored.

## Differential Equations
Students are introduced to the language and methods of solving differential equations, which model rates of change. They focus on first-order separable equations and linear differential equations of the form dy/dx + P(x)y = Q(x). The method of integrating factors is used to solve linear equations. Applications include exponential growth and decay models, such as population change and Newton's Law of Cooling. Slope fields are used to visualize the family of solutions to a differential equation. Students learn to set up and solve initial value problems to find particular solutions.

## Sequences and Series
This unit covers infinite sequences and series, which are sums of the terms of a sequence. Students apply various tests for convergence, including the Ratio, Root, Integral, Comparison, and Limit Comparison Tests for series with positive terms. The Alternating Series Test identifies convergent alternating series. Power series are introduced, and students learn to find the radius and interval of convergence. Taylor and Maclaurin series are used to represent functions as infinite polynomials, allowing for approximation of functions near a specific point. Geometric series and their sums are emphasized.
