# Advanced Placement (AP) Grade 8 Mathematics

## Linear Functions and Equations
Grade 8 students learn to define a linear function as $y = mx + b$, where $m$ is the slope representing the rate of change and $b$ is the y-intercept. They calculate slope from a graph or two points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ using the formula $m = (y_2 - y_1) / (x_2 - x_1)$. Students write equations of lines parallel or perpendicular to a given line and solve linear equations in one and two variables. For example, they find the intersection of $y = 2x + 3$ and $y = -x + 9$ by solving the system.

## Systems of Linear Equations
Students analyze and solve systems of two linear equations with two variables. They master three methods: graphing to find the point of intersection, substitution to solve for one variable and substitute into the other, and elimination to add or subtract equations to eliminate a variable. For instance, to solve $x + y = 10$ and $2x - y = 5$, they can add the equations to eliminate $y$, finding $x = 5$ and then substituting to find $y = 5$.

## Functions and Their Notation
Students formally define a function as a rule that assigns each input to exactly one output. They use function notation, $f(x)$, to represent output for a given input, such as $f(x) = 3x - 7$. Students evaluate functions for specific values (e.g., find $f(4)$) and distinguish between linear and non-linear functions by analyzing their graphs and rates of change. They also explore real-world contexts, like modeling the cost of a taxi ride as a function of distance.

## Exponents and Scientific Notation
Grade 8 students extend exponent rules to include negative and zero exponents, using properties like $a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}$ and $(a^m)^n = a^{m \cdot n}$. They perform operations with numbers in scientific notation, such as $(3 \times 10^5) \cdot (2 \times 10^3) = 6 \times 10^8$. Students convert between standard and scientific notation to represent very large or small numbers, like expressing the speed of light as $3 \times 10^8$ meters per second.

## Pythagorean Theorem and Its Applications
Students apply the Pythagorean Theorem, $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, to find unknown side lengths in right triangles. They use the theorem to solve real-world problems, such as determining the diagonal of a rectangular prism or the distance between two points on a coordinate plane. For example, to find the distance between (0,0) and (3,4), they calculate $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5$. Students also explore proofs of the theorem using area or similar triangles.

## Statistical Analysis and Probability
Students calculate and interpret measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) and spread (range, interquartile range) for a data set. They use box plots and histograms to represent and analyze data distributions. Students apply the Fundamental Counting Principle to determine possible outcomes and calculate probabilities of compound events, like the chance of rolling a sum of 7 with two dice. For example, the probability is 6/36 or 1/6, as there are 6 favorable outcomes out of 36 total.
