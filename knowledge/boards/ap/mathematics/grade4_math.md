# Advanced Placement (AP) Grade 4 Mathematics

## Multiplication with Multi-Digit Numbers
Students build fluency in multiplying whole numbers. They use the standard algorithm to solve problems like 34 x 27, multiplying ones and tens separately before adding the partial products. Place value is essential; they must understand that 34 x 27 is (30 + 4) x (20 + 7). Estimation skills are used to check for reasonableness, for example, knowing that 34 x 27 should be close to 30 x 30, which is 900. Students solve multi-step word problems involving multiplication, such as calculating the total number of seats in a 25-row theater with 42 seats per row.

## Fraction Equivalence and Operations
Students deepen their understanding of fractions by generating equivalent fractions using visual models like number lines and fraction bars. They compare fractions with different numerators and denominators by finding a common denominator or using benchmark fractions like 1/2. Adding and subtracting fractions with like denominators is a primary skill, for instance, 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/8. They also add and subtract mixed numbers, solving problems such as finding the total distance of a 2 1/4-mile hike and a 1 3/4-mile bike ride.

## Division of Whole Numbers and Concepts
Students interpret division as the inverse of multiplication and as equal sharing. They divide multi-digit dividends by single-digit divisors using various strategies, including area models and the standard algorithm. For example, solving 72 ÷ 3 involves breaking 72 into 60 and 12, and then dividing each part by 3. Students solve division word problems with and without remainders, such as determining how many groups of four students can be made from a class of 31. They must interpret the remainder as either a whole number or a fraction.

## Understanding Place Value to 1,000,000
Students extend their place value understanding to numbers in the millions. They recognize that each digit in a number represents a value ten times the digit to its right. They read, write, and compare multi-digit numbers, such as 7,482,165, using place value charts and inequality symbols. Students practice rounding numbers to any place, for instance, rounding 345,678 to the nearest hundred thousand. They also explore patterns in place value, like how multiplying a number by 10 shifts its digits one place to the left.

## Geometric Measurement and Concepts
Students explore area and perimeter of two-dimensional shapes. They use formulas to find the area of rectangles, such as multiplying a 12 cm by 8 cm rectangle to find an area of 96 square cm. Perimeter is calculated by adding the side lengths, like finding the perimeter of a rectangular garden measuring 10 ft by 15 ft. Students also recognize and draw lines of symmetry in various shapes, including capital letters like 'A' and 'M'. They classify two-dimensional figures based on properties like the number of sides and angles.

## Representing and Interpreting Data
Students create and interpret scaled bar graphs and picture graphs to represent data. They analyze a bar graph showing the number of books read by each student in a class and solve problems like finding the difference between the highest and lowest values. Students solve problems involving addition and subtraction of whole numbers based on the data displayed, such as finding the total number of books read by all students. They also learn to create a line plot to show measurements to the nearest quarter inch.
