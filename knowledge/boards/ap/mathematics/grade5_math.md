# Advanced Placement (AP) Grade 5 Mathematics

## Place Value and Number Forms
Students understand that in the number 874, the digit 8 represents 8 hundreds, or 800. They practice reading, writing, and comparing multi-digit whole numbers and decimals. For example, students compare 3.75 and 3.8 by aligning the decimal points and noting that 80 hundredths is greater than 75 hundredths. They write numbers in expanded form, such as 2,405 = 2,000 + 400 + 5, and in word form, like "one thousand, two hundred thirty-four." Students are expected to be precise in their language and notation.

## Operations with Whole Numbers and Decimals
Students master the standard algorithms for adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing multi-digit whole numbers. They apply these skills to solve word problems, such as calculating the total cost of 24 items priced at $15 each. For decimals, students learn to add and subtract by aligning decimal points, like 4.5 + 3.25 = 7.75. They multiply decimals using whole-number methods and adjust the decimal point, as in 0.3 × 0.4 = 0.12.

## Multiplication and Division Strategies
Students develop fluency in multiplying two- and three-digit numbers by two-digit numbers using the standard algorithm, for example, 345 × 12 = 4,140. They explore various strategies, such as partial products, to check their work. For division, they learn to divide up to four-digit dividends by two-digit divisors, finding whole-number quotients and remainders, as in 8,464 ÷ 16 = 529. Students interpret remainders in the context of a problem, such as rounding up for seating arrangements.

## Fractions and Fraction Operations
Students understand that fractions represent parts of a whole and can be equivalent, like 1/2 and 3/6. They compare fractions by finding common denominators or using benchmark fractions. Students add and subtract fractions and mixed numbers with like denominators, for example, 2 1/4 + 3 3/4 = 6. They also multiply fractions by whole numbers, such as 3 × 1/5 = 3/5. These skills are applied to solve real-world problems, like dividing a pizza among friends.

## Measurement and Data
Students convert among different-sized standard measurement units within a given system, such as converting 5 feet to 60 inches. They practice measuring length to the nearest eighth-inch and volume to the nearest milliliter. Students organize and display data in line plots, graphs, and charts, interpreting the information to answer questions. For example, they might analyze a line plot showing the heights of students to determine the most common height range.

## Geometric Concepts
Students recognize and classify two-dimensional shapes, such as quadrilaterals, based on their properties like parallel and perpendicular sides. They calculate the area of rectangles and right triangles using formulas, for example, finding the area of a rectangle that is 7 units long and 4 units wide (A = 7 × 4 = 28 square units). Students also explore volume by counting unit cubes to find the volume of a solid figure, understanding that volume is the amount of space a three-dimensional shape occupies.
