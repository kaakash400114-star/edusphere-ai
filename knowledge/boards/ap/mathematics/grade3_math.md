# Advanced Placement (AP) Grade 3 Mathematics

## Multiplication and Division Facts
Students master multiplication and division facts within 100. They use properties of operations (like the commutative property: 3 x 4 = 4 x 3) to solve problems. For multiplication, they understand it as repeated addition (e.g., 4 x 7 means adding 7 four times: 7 + 7 + 7 + 7). For division, they see it as equal sharing or grouping (e.g., 24 ÷ 8 means splitting 24 into 8 equal groups of 3). Fluency is developed using strategies such as skip-counting, drawing arrays, and relating known facts to unknown ones.

## Fractions on a Number Line
Students understand a fraction, like 1/4, as the quantity formed when a whole is partitioned into equal parts. They represent fractions on a number line by marking equal intervals between 0 and 1. For example, to show 3/4, they divide the space from 0 to 1 into four equal parts and point to the third mark. Students also compare fractions with the same denominator (e.g., 2/5 < 4/5) and recognize that a fraction 1/b is a multiple of 1/b, such as 3/4 = 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4.

## Area and Perimeter
Students learn that the area of a shape is the amount of space it covers, measured in square units. They find the area of rectangles by multiplying side lengths (e.g., a 4 x 6 rectangle has an area of 24 square units). Perimeter is the distance around a shape, found by adding the lengths of all sides (e.g., a 3 cm by 5 cm rectangle has a perimeter of 16 cm). Students solve real-world problems, such as finding the area of a garden or the fencing needed for a yard, to distinguish between these two concepts.

## Data Analysis and Graphing
Students create and interpret scaled bar graphs, where one symbol or mark represents more than one object. For example, in a graph showing pet ownership, one picture of a cat might represent 3 cats. Students solve problems by "reading" the data, such as finding the total number of pets or how many more dogs there are than cats. They also use line plots to show measurement data, like the heights of students in inches, marking an X for each data point to display the frequency of each value.

## Multi-Step Word Problems
Students solve two-step word problems using all four operations. They represent problems with equations that have a letter for an unknown number (e.g., 3 x n = 21). For example, a problem might ask: "There are 8 bags with 6 apples in each. If 10 apples are eaten, how many are left?" Students must first find the total apples (8 x 6 = 48) and then subtract the eaten apples (48 - 10 = 38). They must identify the correct operations to use and check their answers for reasonableness.

## Geometric Shapes and Attributes
Students understand that shapes can be classified based on their attributes. They recognize rhombuses, rectangles, and squares as examples of quadrilaterals. Students partition shapes into equal parts with equal areas, such as dividing a circle into fourths or a rectangle into sixths. They also identify lines of symmetry in two-dimensional shapes, like a butterfly or a capital letter 'A', where one side is a mirror image of the other, helping them build a foundation for more advanced geometric reasoning.
