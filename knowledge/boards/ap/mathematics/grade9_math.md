# Advanced Placement (AP) Grade 9 Mathematics

## Linear Functions and Equations
Students master linear functions, written as f(x) = mx + b, where 'm' is the slope and 'b' is the y-intercept. They calculate slope using the formula m = (y₂ - y₁)/(x₂ - x₁) from two points on a line. Students learn to graph linear equations by plotting the y-intercept and using slope to find additional points. They also solve linear equations, including those with variables on both sides, and use them to model real-world scenarios, such as calculating the cost of a taxi ride where a base fee is added to a per-mile charge.

## Systems of Linear Equations and Inequalities
Students solve systems of two linear equations with two variables using graphing, substitution, and elimination methods. They identify systems with one solution, no solution, or infinitely many solutions. Additionally, they graph systems of linear inequalities in two variables, shading the solution region that satisfies all inequalities. Applications include finding break-even points for businesses and optimizing resource allocation. This skill is foundational for understanding the intersection of constraints in multi-variable problems on the AP Calculus and AP Statistics exams.

## Introduction to Exponents and Radicals
Students extend their understanding of exponents to include zero and negative integer rules, such as a⁰ = 1 and a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ. They simplify expressions using the laws of exponents, including product, quotient, and power rules. Students also learn about radicals, expressing them as fractional exponents and simplifying square roots and cube roots of perfect numbers. For example, they learn that √(x²) = |x|. This unit prepares students for the more complex exponential and logarithmic functions encountered in Algebra II and AP Precalculus.

## Polynomial Expressions and Factoring
Students perform arithmetic operations with polynomials, including addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division using long division. They factor polynomials using various techniques: greatest common factor, grouping, difference of squares (a² - b² = (a - b)(a + b)), trinomials, and sum/difference of cubes (a³ ± b³ = (a ± b)(a² ∓ ab + b²)). Proficiency in factoring is essential for solving polynomial equations, which is a prerequisite for success in AP Calculus, where it is used to find limits and derivatives.

## Quadratic Functions and Equations
Students explore quadratic functions, f(x) = ax² + bx + c, analyzing their graphs as parabolas. They find the vertex, axis of symmetry, and intercepts to sketch the graph. Students solve quadratic equations by factoring, completing the square, and using the quadratic formula, x = [-b ± √(b² - 4ac)] / 2a. They also solve quadratic inequalities by finding the roots and testing intervals. This topic is a major focus of the SAT and ACT and is a building block for understanding conic sections in AP Precalculus and motion in AP Physics.

## Data Analysis and Probability Basics
Students analyze one-variable data by calculating measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) and spread (range, interquartile range, standard deviation). They represent data using box plots, histograms, and bar graphs. For probability, students determine the likelihood of events using basic rules, such as the complement rule P(A) = 1 - P(A'), and understand conditional probability. This foundational data literacy is critical for success in AP Statistics, where it forms the basis for experimental design and hypothesis testing.
