# Advanced Placement (AP) Grade 7 Mathematics

## Rational Number Operations
Students develop fluency with positive and negative fractions, decimals, and integers. They master all four operations with these numbers, applying the standard order of operations (PEMDAS). This includes converting between fractions, decimals, and percentages. For example, students learn to multiply -⅖ by 1.5 and divide -3.4 by ½, understanding that dividing by a fraction is equivalent to multiplying by its reciprocal. They solve multistep problems like finding the total cost of items with discounts and sales tax, where calculations involve both decimal and fractional values.

## Proportional Relationships and Percentages
This unit focuses on identifying, representing, and solving problems involving proportional relationships. Students use tables, graphs, and equations (y = kx) to model direct proportionality. They analyze the constant of proportionality (k) as the unit rate. The unit extends to solving real-world percentage problems, including markups, markdowns, gratuities, commissions, and percent increase/decrease. For instance, students calculate the final price of a $48 item after a 15% discount and a 7% sales tax, applying the formula: final price = original price × (1 ± rate).

## Algebraic Foundations and Linear Expressions
Students begin formal algebra by writing, evaluating, and simplifying linear expressions and equations. They learn to use the distributive property to combine like terms (e.g., 3(x + 2) - 5x simplifies to -2x + 6). The curriculum emphasizes translating verbal phrases into algebraic expressions and vice-versa. Students solve one- and two-step linear equations and inequalities with rational coefficients. An example is solving 3(x - 1) = 15 for x and graphing the solution on a number line, demonstrating an understanding of inverse operations to isolate the variable.

## Probability and Statistics
Students learn to use random sampling to draw inferences about a population. They understand that a valid sample must be representative. They compare two populations by analyzing the measures of center (mean, median) and variability (interquartile range, mean absolute deviation). In probability, students calculate the likelihood of simple and compound events. For example, they find the probability of drawing a red marble then a blue one without replacement from a bag containing five red and three blue marbles, distinguishing between independent and dependent events.

## Two- and Three-Dimensional Geometry
This unit covers formulas for the area and circumference of circles, and the volume of cylinders, cones, and spheres. Students use the formulas A = πr², C = 2πr, V = πr²h (cylinder), V = ⅓πr²h (cone), and V = ⁴⁄₃πr³ (sphere). They solve for a missing dimension given the area or volume. A practical application involves finding the volume of a cone-shaped cup and comparing it to a cylinder with the same base and height to understand the 1:3 volume relationship. Students also practice composing and decomposing complex geometric figures to find their total area or volume.

## Analyzing and Solving Problems
Students apply their knowledge of ratios, percentages, expressions, and geometry to solve complex, multi-step problems. They use a variety of strategies, including making tables, drawing diagrams, and writing equations. The problems often involve real-world contexts, such as designing a room with given floor area and volume constraints or optimizing packaging dimensions to minimize material cost. This culminating unit emphasizes perseverance, logical reasoning, and the ability to communicate mathematical arguments clearly, both verbally and in writing, preparing them for the rigorous problem-solving expected in subsequent AP courses.
