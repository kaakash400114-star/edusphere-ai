# Advanced Placement (AP) Grade 10 Science
## Chemical Equations and Stoichiometry
Students learn to write and balance chemical equations, ensuring the law of conservation of mass is upheld. They calculate quantities of reactants and products using mole ratios derived from balanced equations, applying the formula: moles = mass / molar mass. For example, to find how many grams of oxygen are needed to combust 36 grams of glucose (C6H12O6), they convert grams to moles, use the 6:1 mole ratio from the equation C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O, then convert oxygen moles back to grams, yielding 38.4 grams.

## Newton's Laws of Motion and Forces
This topic covers Newton's three laws: an object at rest stays at rest unless acted upon by a net force (First Law, F=0 for equilibrium); the acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force and inversely proportional to its mass (Second Law, F=ma); for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction (Third Law). Students apply these to solve problems, such as calculating the tension in a cable holding a 500 kg elevator accelerating upward at 2 m/s². The net force is (500 kg)(2 m/s²) = 1000 N, so tension equals weight (mg = 4900 N) plus net force, totaling 5900 N.

## Energy Conservation and Thermodynamics
Students study the conservation of mechanical energy (PE + KE = constant) and the first and second laws of thermodynamics. The first law states that the change in a system's internal energy equals heat added to the system minus work done by the system (ΔU = Q - W). For example, if 200 J of heat are added to a gas and it does 75 J of work, its internal energy increases by 125 J. The second law explains entropy, noting that natural processes increase the total entropy of the universe.

## Genetic Inheritance and DNA Structure
This unit explores Mendelian genetics, including principles of segregation and independent assortment. Students use Punnett squares to predict genotypic and phenotypic ratios from monohybrid and dihybrid crosses. For instance, crossing two heterozygous parents (Aa x Aa) yields a 1:2:1 genotypic ratio and a 3:1 phenotypic ratio for a dominant trait. The curriculum also covers DNA's double-helix structure, composed of nucleotides with deoxyribose sugar, a phosphate group, and a nitrogenous base (adenine, thymine, guanine, cytosine), linked by hydrogen bonds between complementary bases.

## Ecosystem Dynamics and Biodiversity
Students analyze energy flow in ecosystems, starting with producers (autotrophs) that convert solar energy into chemical energy via photosynthesis. They study trophic levels and the 10% rule, where only about 10% of energy is transferred between levels. For example, if producers capture 10,000 kcal of solar energy, primary consumers obtain approximately 1,000 kcal. The unit also examines biodiversity, measuring species richness and evenness, and how factors like habitat loss and climate change impact ecosystem stability and resilience.

## Electromagnetic Waves and Optics
This topic covers the electromagnetic spectrum, including visible light, radio waves, and X-rays, all of which travel at the speed of light (c ≈ 3.0 x 10⁸ m/s). Students apply the wave equation c = λf, where λ is wavelength and f is frequency. For example, green light with a wavelength of 550 nm has a frequency of f = (3.0 x 10⁸ m/s) / (550 x 10⁻⁹ m) ≈ 5.5 x 10¹⁴ Hz. The unit also covers reflection, refraction (Snell's Law, n₁sinθ₁ = n₂sinθ₂), and the lens equation (1/f = 1/do + 1/di).
