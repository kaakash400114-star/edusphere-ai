# Advanced Placement (AP) Grade 2 Science

## Properties of Objects
Students observe and describe the physical properties of objects, such as color, shape, size, and texture. They learn to group objects by these shared attributes. For example, students can sort a collection of buttons based on their material (plastic, metal) or their number of holes (two, four). This hands-on activity develops foundational skills in observation and classification, which are essential for scientific analysis. Students record their findings by drawing or writing down the properties they identify, connecting their concrete experiences to the language of science.

## Forces and Motion
Students explore the concept of a push or pull, which they learn is called a force. They conduct experiments to see how forces can make objects start moving, stop moving, change direction, or change shape. For instance, a student can push a toy car to make it roll (a force causing motion) or squeeze a rubber ball to change its shape. This grade-level introduction to Newton's Laws of Motion focuses on cause and effect, helping students understand the relationship between forces and the changes they produce in the motion of everyday objects.

## Plant Life Cycles
Students study the life cycle of a common plant, such as a bean or sunflower, from seed to mature plant. They learn about the key stages: germination (the seed sprouting), growth (developing roots, stems, and leaves), and reproduction (producing new seeds). By planting a seed and observing its development over weeks, students gain a concrete understanding of how living things grow and change. They identify the basic needs of plants—sunlight, water, and air—and recognize how these factors affect their growth rate and health.

## Animal Habitats
Students investigate the different habitats where animals live, such as forests, oceans, deserts, and ponds. They learn that a habitat provides the things an animal needs to survive, including food, water, shelter, and space. For example, a rabbit in a forest habitat eats plants, drinks from a stream, and lives in a burrow for protection. Through observation of models or videos, students can identify various animals and match them to their correct habitats, beginning to understand the concept of interdependence within an ecosystem.

## Weather Patterns
Students observe and describe daily weather conditions. They learn to measure and record temperature using a thermometer and to describe precipitation, such as rain or snow, and wind. They identify common types of clouds, like cumulus (fluffy) and stratus (flat), and associate them with different kinds of weather. This unit helps students recognize patterns, like sunny days being warmer and rainy days being cooler, and introduces them to how scientists collect data to understand and predict changes in the environment.

## Earth's Materials
Students identify and classify Earth's materials, including rocks, soil, and water. They learn that rocks are made of different minerals and can be sorted by properties like hardness or texture (e.g., rough granite vs. smooth sandstone). They explore soil by examining its components, such as tiny rocks, sand, clay, and organic matter. By testing the properties of water, including its state (solid, liquid, gas), students understand how this essential resource moves through the environment and shapes the land.
