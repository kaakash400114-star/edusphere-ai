# Advanced Placement (AP) Grade 8 Science
## Introduction to Matter and Its Interactions
Students learn that matter has mass and volume and is made of tiny particles called atoms and molecules. They classify matter based on its composition, distinguishing between pure substances (elements and compounds) and mixtures (solutions, suspensions, colloids). A key concept is the Law of Conservation of Mass, which states that mass is neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. For example, when 50 grams of hydrogen react with 400 grams of oxygen, they produce exactly 450 grams of water, demonstrating that the total mass remains constant.

## Forces and Motion
Students explore the relationship between forces and an object’s motion using Newton's Laws of Motion. An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion at a constant velocity unless acted upon by an unbalanced force (Newton's First Law). Force is calculated as F = ma, where mass (m) is in kilograms and acceleration (a) is in meters per second squared. For instance, a 10-kg cart accelerated at 2 m/s² experiences a net force of 20 newtons.

## Energy: Its Forms and Transformations
This section covers the different forms of energy, including kinetic, potential, thermal, chemical, and electrical. Students learn that energy can be transformed from one form to another, but the total amount of energy in an isolated system remains constant, as stated by the Law of Conservation of Energy. A practical example is a roller coaster: at the highest point, it has maximum gravitational potential energy, which converts to kinetic energy as it descends, and some energy is lost to heat and sound due to friction.

## Waves and Their Properties
Students investigate the properties of mechanical and electromagnetic waves. All waves transfer energy through a medium (mechanical) or vacuum (electromagnetic). Key characteristics include wavelength (λ), frequency (f), and speed (v), related by the formula v = fλ. For example, a sound wave traveling at 343 m/s with a frequency of 440 Hz has a wavelength of approximately 0.78 meters. They also learn about the electromagnetic spectrum, from long radio waves to short gamma rays.

## Earth's Systems and Interactions
Students examine the four major Earth systems: the geosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere, and biosphere. They explore how these systems interact, such as the water cycle, where water from the hydrosphere evaporates into the atmosphere, condenses into clouds, and precipitates back to the surface. Human impacts, like the addition of carbon dioxide to the atmosphere from burning fossil fuels, are studied as they contribute to climate change by enhancing the greenhouse effect.

## Heredity and Inheritance
This topic focuses on how traits are passed from parents to offspring. Students learn about DNA as the molecule that carries genetic instructions. They study Mendelian genetics, including concepts of dominant and recessive alleles. For example, in pea plants, the allele for purple flowers (P) is dominant over the allele for white flowers (p). A cross between two heterozygous parents (Pp) can result in offspring with genotypes PP, Pp, or pp, yielding a phenotypic ratio of 3 purple flowers to 1 white flower.
