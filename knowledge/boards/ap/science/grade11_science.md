# Advanced Placement (AP) Grade 11 Science
## Cellular Respiration and Photosynthesis
Students learn how cells harvest energy from glucose through aerobic respiration, a process occurring in mitochondria. The overall equation is C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + ATP. They study the three main stages: glycolysis, the Krebs cycle, and the electron transport chain. For photosynthesis, they analyze how light energy is converted to chemical energy in chloroplasts, described by the equation 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂. Key concepts include the Calvin cycle, light-dependent reactions, and the role of pigments like chlorophyll.
## Mendelian Genetics and Patterns of Inheritance
This section covers Gregor Mendel's foundational laws of inheritance. Students apply the law of segregation to monohybrid crosses, using Punnett squares to predict phenotypic ratios, such as a 3:1 ratio in pea plant height (dominant tall vs. recessive short). They also use the law of independent assortment for dihybrid crosses, observing 9:3:3:1 ratios. The content includes distinguishing between genotype and phenotype, and analyzing examples like human blood types (ABO system) to understand codominance and multiple alleles.
## Chemical Equilibrium and Kinetics
Students explore the dynamic state of chemical equilibrium, where the forward and reverse reaction rates are equal. They learn Le Chatelier’s principle to predict how a system responds to changes in concentration, pressure, or temperature, using an example like the Haber process (N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃). For kinetics, they study how reaction rates are affected by concentration, temperature, and catalysts, applying the rate law (Rate = k[A]^m[B]^n) to determine the order of a reaction and the meaning of the rate constant, k.
## Constitutional Law and Federalism
This unit examines the structure and powers of the U.S. government as defined by the Constitution. Students analyze the principles of federalism, using the *McCulloch v. Maryland* (1819) case to illustrate the supremacy of federal law and implied powers. They study the division of authority between national and state governments, exploring the Elastic Clause and the 10th Amendment. Key topics include the Supremacy Clause, the commerce power, and how the Supreme Court's interpretations have shaped the balance of power over time, as seen in landmark rulings.
## The Periodic Table and Periodic Trends
Students learn that the periodic table organizes elements by increasing atomic number and recurring chemical properties. They analyze periodic trends: atomic radius decreases across a period and increases down a group. Electronegativity increases from left to right across a period. For example, fluorine (F) is the most electronegative element, while cesium (Cs) has the lowest ionization energy. They apply these trends to predict chemical reactivity, comparing elements like sodium (Na) and chlorine (Cl) to form ionic bonds.
## Newton's Laws and Applications
This section covers Sir Isaac Newton's three laws of motion. The first law (inertia) states an object remains at rest or in constant motion unless acted upon by a net force. The second law (F = ma) explains how force equals mass times acceleration, used to calculate the force of a 5 kg object accelerating at 2 m/s² (10 N). The third law (action-reaction) is demonstrated when a rocket pushes exhaust gases down, pushing the rocket up. Students apply these laws to solve problems involving friction, gravity, and circular motion.
