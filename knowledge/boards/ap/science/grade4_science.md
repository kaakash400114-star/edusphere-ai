# Advanced Placement (AP) Grade 4 Science
## Earth's Place in the Universe
Students learn that Earth is one of eight planets in our solar system, which orbits the Sun, a medium-sized star. They identify planets as terrestrial (rocky) like Earth and Mars, or gas giants like Jupiter. Using models, students explain that Earth rotates on its axis every 24 hours, causing day and night, and revolves around the Sun every 365 days, causing the year. They can name the planets in order from the Sun: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, noting Pluto as a dwarf planet.

## The Structure and Properties of Matter
Students classify objects by their observable properties such as mass, volume, and states of matter (solid, liquid, gas). They conduct investigations to measure mass using a balance and volume using rulers or graduated cylinders. They learn that matter can change states through physical processes like melting (solid to liquid) and freezing (liquid to solid), but the substance itself does not change. For example, ice melting into water is a physical change because it is still H₂O.

## Energy: Conservation and Transfer
Students identify different forms of energy, including light, heat, sound, electrical, and mechanical. They explore how energy is transferred through conduction, like a metal spoon getting hot in a pot of soup, and radiation, like heat from the Sun warming the Earth. Students demonstrate that energy can be converted from one form to another, such as a battery (chemical energy) powering a lightbulb (light and heat energy). They understand that energy is conserved, meaning it is never created or destroyed, only changed.

## Earth's Systems: Weather and Climate
Students learn that weather is the condition of the atmosphere at a specific time and place, including temperature, precipitation, wind, and cloud cover. They study climate as the average weather patterns in a region over many years, using examples like the humid climate of Florida versus the arid climate of Arizona. Students interpret data from simple weather instruments, such as thermometers for temperature and rain gauges for precipitation, to forecast and describe weather events like thunderstorms or a sunny day.

## Organisms and Their Environments
Students classify organisms based on shared characteristics, such as plants, animals, fungi, and bacteria. They construct models of food chains to show how energy flows from producers (plants) to consumers (herbivores and carnivores) and decomposers. For example, a food chain could be: grass (producer) → rabbit (consumer) → fox (consumer). Students describe how environments provide all the resources an organism needs to survive, including food, water, shelter, and space, and understand that these factors can limit population growth.

## Engineering Design Process
Students apply the engineering design process to solve problems. They define a problem, such as designing a container that keeps an ice cube from melting for 30 minutes. They plan a solution by brainstorming ideas, like using materials such as aluminum foil or foam, and create a prototype. They test their design and collect data, then analyze the results to improve their original idea. This iterative process teaches students how to evaluate and refine solutions based on evidence.
