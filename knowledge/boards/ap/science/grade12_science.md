# Advanced Placement (AP) Grade 12 Science
## Physics C: Mechanics and Electricity & Magnetism
This course uses calculus to explore Newtonian mechanics and electromagnetism. Students apply derivatives and integrals to kinematics, dynamics, and energy. They solve problems involving rotational motion, angular momentum, and simple harmonic oscillators using vector calculus and differential equations. The electricity and magnetism portion covers Coulomb's Law, Gauss's Law, and electric potential. Students analyze circuits with Kirchhoff's laws and use calculus to determine the electric and magnetic fields created by continuous charge distributions and current-carrying wires.

## Chemistry
Students study the structure and properties of matter, including atomic theory and quantum mechanical models of the atom. The curriculum focuses on chemical bonding through molecular orbital theory and VSEPR models, and explores chemical kinetics and equilibrium using the rate law and equilibrium constant expressions (K). Thermodynamics is analyzed using Gibbs free energy. Acid-base chemistry and electrochemistry are examined through the Nernst equation and redox titration calculations. Students apply these concepts to stoichiometric problems involving complex reactions and industrial processes like the Haber process.

## Biology
This course explores the four big ideas: evolution, cellular processes, genetics, and information transfer, and systems interactions. Students analyze Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium to understand population genetics and the effects of selection, migration, and genetic drift. The central dogma of molecular biology is examined through the details of DNA replication, transcription, and translation, including the role of specific enzymes and regulatory sequences like promoters. The immune response is studied, focusing on the structure of antibodies and the cellular mechanisms of humoral and cell-mediated immunity.

## Environmental Science
Students evaluate the interrelationships of the natural world. They analyze biogeochemical cycles, calculating the flux of carbon between the atmosphere and biosphere. Population dynamics are modeled using the logistic growth equation to understand carrying capacity. The course investigates biodiversity through species richness indices and the impact of habitat fragmentation. Students conduct a Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) for a product like a plastic water bottle, quantifying energy consumption and emissions. Environmental policies such as the Clean Air Act and cap-and-trade systems are analyzed for their effectiveness in reducing pollution.

## Calculus BC
This course covers the concepts, methods, and applications of differential and integral calculus. Students learn to use limits to define derivatives and integrals, applying them to model rates of change in motion, growth, and decay. Techniques of integration include u-substitution, integration by parts, and partial fractions. For parametric, polar, and vector functions, students find derivatives and calculate arc length. Infinite series are analyzed for convergence using the ratio and integral tests. Students also explore Taylor and Maclaurin series to approximate functions with specific error margins.

## English Language and Composition
While not a science course, this AP course is often taken by science-focused seniors. Students analyze rhetorical strategies in scientific texts, such as Darwin's "On the Origin of Species," to identify an author's purpose, audience, and argument. They develop evidence-based arguments using scientific data, peer-reviewed journal articles, and case studies, properly citing sources in MLA or APA format. The course teaches the synthesis of multiple sources to construct a cohesive position on a scientific or technological issue, requiring precise language and logical organization to persuade a well-informed reader.
