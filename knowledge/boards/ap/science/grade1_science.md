# Advanced Placement (AP) Grade 1 Science
## Observing Properties
Students learn that objects have properties that can be observed using their five senses. They use descriptive words like hard, soft, smooth, rough, cold, warm, heavy, and light to classify items. For example, a rock feels hard and cold, while a stuffed animal feels soft and warm. They sort classroom objects based on these properties, such as grouping all the smooth shells together or separating the rough blocks. This practice builds foundational skills in scientific observation and data organization.

## Exploring Living Things
Students identify the basic needs of living things: food, water, air, and shelter. They observe how a classroom plant needs water and sunlight to grow, and how a classroom pet needs food and water to be healthy. They compare the needs of plants and animals. For instance, a butterfly needs flowers (food) for nectar, while an oak tree needs sunlight to make its own food through photosynthesis, a key concept they encounter in later AP Biology.

## Forces and Motion
Students learn about push and pull forces that make objects move. They conduct simple experiments, like pushing a toy car across a table (a push force) or pulling a wagon with a friend (a pull force). They explore how the strength and direction of a force affect the motion of an object. A gentle push makes a ball roll slowly, while a strong push makes it roll quickly. This hands-on investigation introduces the foundational physics principle of force and acceleration.

## Daily Cycles and Weather
Students observe and describe daily and seasonal patterns, focusing on the sun and weather. They track the sun's position throughout the day to understand its role in day and night. They learn that the weather can be described using measurable quantities like temperature (e.g., it is 70 degrees Fahrenheit today) and precipitation (e.g., it is raining). They create simple charts to record sunny, cloudy, or rainy days, correlating weather patterns with seasons.

## Earth's Materials
Students identify and sort Earth's common materials, including rocks, soil, and water. They classify rocks based on observable properties like color, texture, and size. For example, they can identify a granite rock as speckled. They explore soil by examining its components (sand, silt, clay) and discuss how soil is important for growing plants like beans or corn, which are common American crops.

## Investigating with Tools
Students use simple scientific tools to make observations and gather data. They use a magnifying glass to closely examine the details of an insect wing or a leaf's veins. They use a ruler to measure the length of objects in standard units, such as the length of a pencil being 15 centimeters long. This practice teaches students that tools enhance their ability to observe and collect accurate information.
