# Advanced Placement (AP) Grade 3 Science
## Properties of Matter
Matter has properties that can be observed and measured. Students identify if a material is a solid, liquid, or gas by its shape and volume. Solids, like a wooden block, keep their own shape and volume. Liquids, like water in a glass, take the shape of their container but keep the same volume. Gases, like the air in a balloon, fill any container they are in. We measure mass using a balance in grams. Temperature is measured using a thermometer in degrees Celsius (°C) or Fahrenheit (°F). For example, water freezes at 0°C (32°F).

## Life Cycles of Organisms
All living things have a life cycle. Plants, like a sunflower, start as a seed. They sprout roots and a stem, grow leaves, then produce flowers and seeds. Animals, like a monarch butterfly, have a complete metamorphosis. The life cycle includes egg, larva (caterpillar), pupa (chrysalis), and adult. Each stage has a different appearance and job. Animals, like frogs, also change from egg to tadpole with gills to frog with lungs. Life cycles show how organisms are born, grow, reproduce, and die.

## Earth's Resources
Earth provides natural resources we use every day. Natural resources are things found in nature that are useful. They are either renewable, like trees that can be replanted, or non-renewable, like coal that must be dug from the ground and cannot be replaced. Rocks and soil are non-renewable used for building. Water is a renewable resource for drinking and growing crops. We must conserve resources by not wasting them and reusing materials, like using scrap paper for notes, to protect our planet for the future.

## Interactions in Ecosystems
An ecosystem is a community of living and non-living things in an area. Living things (biotic) are plants, animals, and insects. Non-living things (abiotic) are water, sunlight, and soil. Plants use sunlight, water, and air to make their own food. This process is called photosynthesis. Animals eat plants or other animals to get energy. A food chain shows this energy transfer, for example: grass → rabbit → fox. Decomposers, like mushrooms and worms, break down dead plants and animals, returning nutrients to the soil for new plants to use.

## Forces and Motion
Forces are pushes or pulls that can change how an object moves or its shape. A strong push can make a ball roll fast. A gentle pull can move a toy car slowly. Gravity is a force that pulls objects toward the center of the Earth, which is why apples fall from trees and we stay on the ground. Friction is a force that slows down moving objects, like a sliding block coming to a stop on a rug. We can use tools, like a ramp or a lever, to make work easier by changing the direction or amount of force needed.

## Weather and Climate
Weather is the condition of the air outside right now, such as sunny, rainy, or windy. It is measured using tools like a thermometer for temperature, a rain gauge for how much rain falls, and a wind vane to show wind direction. Climate is the average weather of a place over many years. For example, Miami, Florida has a warm, wet climate, while Barrow, Alaska has a cold, dry climate. Weather can change from day to day, but a region's climate stays the same for a long time.
