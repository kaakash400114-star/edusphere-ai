# Canadian Curriculum Grade 7 English

## Literary Devices and Figurative Language
Students identify and analyze a range of literary devices to deepen their comprehension of texts. They focus on simile ("as brave as a loonie" to describe courage) and metaphor ("the hockey rink was a battlefield"). Students learn to distinguish between literal and figurative language, examining how authors use hyperbole ("I'm so tired I could sleep for a week") and personification ("the CN Tower watched over the city"). This understanding allows students to appreciate the creative use of language in both classic and contemporary Canadian literature, connecting abstract concepts to concrete experiences.

## Narrative Writing: Structure and Plot Development
Students learn the foundational elements of narrative writing. They focus on structuring stories using the five-part plot: exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, and resolution. Lessons involve creating detailed character profiles and settings, such as a small-town rink in rural Ontario or a bustling market in Vancouver. Students practice writing clear, logical storylines with a central conflict, ensuring each event builds upon the previous one. They apply this knowledge to craft original narratives that reflect personal or imagined Canadian experiences, using dialogue and descriptive language to enhance their stories.

## Reading Comprehension: Inference and Evidence
Students develop advanced reading comprehension skills by making inferences based on textual evidence. They learn to "read between the lines," drawing conclusions that are not explicitly stated in a text. For example, when reading a story about a character longing for maple syrup, students infer a sense of homesickness. Students practice identifying key details and supporting evidence to back up their inferences, moving beyond literal understanding. This skill is applied to a variety of genres, including news articles, short stories, and historical texts related to Canadian events, fostering critical thinking and analytical abilities.

## Grammar: Punctuation and Sentence Structure
Students refine their understanding of formal grammar and punctuation rules. They correctly use commas in complex sentences, such as those with introductory phrases ("After the hockey game ended, we celebrated with a loonie toss") and non-restrictive clauses. Lessons cover the proper use of semicolons to join independent clauses and colons to introduce lists or explanations (e.g., "Our trip included stops in three provinces: Ontario, Quebec, and Manitoba"). Students practice identifying and correcting common grammatical errors, ensuring their writing adheres to Canadian conventions and achieves clarity and precision in formal communication.

## Media Literacy: Analyzing and Creating Messages
Students learn to critically analyze media messages by examining their purpose, audience, and persuasive techniques. They deconstruct advertisements, news segments, and social media content, identifying techniques like emotional appeal or bandwagoning. For example, they might analyze a commercial promoting a hockey tournament. Students then apply this knowledge by creating their own media texts, such as a podcast episode about the cultural significance of the maple leaf or a short video documentary about a Canadian province. This process fosters responsible consumption and creation of media in a digital world.

## Research Skills: Finding and Citing Sources
Students develop essential research skills by gathering information from a variety of sources, including books, websites, and databases. They learn to evaluate sources for credibility, relevance, and bias, such as distinguishing between a government report on Canadian wildlife and a personal blog post. Lessons focus on paraphrasing information in their own words and correctly using in-text citations to avoid plagiarism. Students organize their findings using outlines and begin to format simple bibliographies, preparing them for more extensive research projects in higher grades and building a foundation for academic integrity.
