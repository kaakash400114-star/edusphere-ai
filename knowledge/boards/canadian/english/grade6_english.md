# Canadian Curriculum Grade 6 English
## Reading Comprehension and Analysis
Students analyze a variety of literary texts, including novels, short stories, and poems, to identify and explain themes, characters' motivations, and the author's purpose. They learn to distinguish between fact and opinion and to make inferences based on textual evidence. For example, while reading a story set in rural Alberta, students might infer the protagonist's feelings about moving to the city by examining specific details. They also learn to analyze the use of literary devices such as simile, metaphor, and personification, and to explain their effect on the reader's understanding.

## Writing: Processes and Products
Grade 6 students focus on the writing process: planning, drafting, revising, editing, and publishing. They learn to write for specific audiences and purposes, producing multi-paragraph compositions such as narratives, reports, and persuasive essays. For a writing task, a student might draft a persuasive letter to their local Member of Parliament arguing for a new community rink in their town. They apply rules of grammar and punctuation, including using commas correctly, and expand their vocabulary to use more precise and engaging language in their work.

## Oral Communication and Media Literacy
Students develop their oral communication skills by preparing and delivering presentations for different audiences. They learn to structure their ideas logically, use appropriate volume and pace, and use visual aids effectively. In media literacy, students analyze various forms of media, including advertisements, news articles, and websites. For example, they deconstruct a commercial for a Canadian snack food, identifying its persuasive techniques and target audience. They also learn to recognize how different media platforms can influence public opinion and their own perceptions of the world.

## Grammar, Mechanics, and Spelling
This unit solidifies students' understanding of English grammar and mechanics. They learn to correctly use all parts of speech, including pronouns, adjectives, adverbs, and conjunctions. Key concepts include understanding subject-verb agreement, the correct use of tenses, and forming different sentence structures (simple, compound, complex). Students continue to develop Canadian spelling conventions, which often blend British and American rules, such as writing "colour" and "centre" but using "tire." They also practice using punctuation like apostrophes for possession and quotation marks for direct speech.

## Vocabulary Development
Students expand their vocabulary through reading, word study, and explicit instruction. They learn strategies to determine the meaning of unknown words, using context clues, prefixes, suffixes, and roots. For example, they might analyze the word "multicultural" by breaking it down into "multi-" (many) and "cultural." They are also introduced to academic and domain-specific vocabulary related to other subjects, such as "ecosystem" in science or "democracy" in social studies. Students learn to use these new words accurately in both their speaking and writing.

## Drama and Informational Texts
In drama, students read scripts and explore ways to interpret and perform characters' emotions and motivations. They may perform short scenes or create tableaux to represent key moments from a story. Simultaneously, they learn to effectively comprehend and analyze informational texts, such as articles about Canadian history, scientific reports on local wildlife, or biographies of famous Canadians like Terry Fox. Students practice summarizing key information, identifying main ideas, and distinguishing between primary and secondary sources, becoming more discerning readers of non-fiction.
