# Canadian Curriculum Grade 5 English
## Reading Comprehension
Students will read and understand a variety of texts, including stories, informational articles, and poems about Canadian geography and history. They will learn to identify the main idea, supporting details, and the author's purpose. Students will use context clues to determine the meaning of new vocabulary, such as "precipitation" or "hereditary." A key skill is making inferences, like predicting what happens next in a story about a child's first hockey game or understanding a character's feelings after visiting the CN Tower.

## Spelling and Grammar
Students will master spelling rules for common Canadian spellings, including words with "our" (colour, favourite) and "re" (centre, theatre). In grammar, they will learn to correctly use commas in lists, addresses (like "Toronto, Ontario"), and with introductory words. They will expand their knowledge of punctuation by using apostrophes for contractions (e.g., "don't," "can't") and possessives (e.g., "the dog's leash"). Weekly spelling lists will incorporate Canadian vocabulary, such as "loonie," "maple," and "inukshuk."

## Writing Process
Students will learn the five steps of the writing process: prewriting, drafting, revising, editing, and publishing. They will generate ideas through brainstorming and using graphic organizers. A major focus is organizing paragraphs with a clear topic sentence and supporting details. For example, students might write a paragraph describing a favourite provincial park in Ontario or explaining the rules of lacrosse. They will also learn to write a simple multi-paragraph composition, ensuring ideas connect logically.

## Canadian Literature and Context
Students will explore a range of texts that reflect Canadian culture and identity. This includes stories featuring iconic Canadian symbols like the maple leaf, loons, and beavers, as well as narratives set in different provinces. Students will analyze how settings influence the story, comparing life in rural Alberta to a city like Montreal. They will read poems by Canadian poets and identify themes of nature, multiculturalism, and Canadian pride to foster a deeper connection with their country's literary heritage.

## Oral Communication
Students will develop skills for effective spoken communication. They will learn to deliver clear and organized presentations on topics like a famous Canadian figure (e.g., Terry Fox) or a provincial landmark. A key focus is active listening, where students maintain eye contact, ask clarifying questions, and summarize what they have heard. They will also participate in group discussions, sharing opinions on issues like protecting Canada's national parks while respectfully listening to the perspectives of their classmates.

## Media Literacy
Students will learn to analyze different forms of media, such as websites, advertisements, and news articles. They will identify the purpose and target audience of a piece, for example, an ad for a hockey tournament. Students will practice detecting fact versus opinion and will examine how images and text work together to convey a message. A key skill is recognizing persuasive techniques used in commercials, such as an advertisement featuring a popular Tim Hortons product to encourage consumer spending.
