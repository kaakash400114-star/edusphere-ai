# Canadian Curriculum Grade 9 English

## Literary Analysis
Students analyze texts by identifying literary devices such as symbolism, irony, and foreshadowing. For example, in The Hockey Sweater by Roch Carrier, the narrator’s new Montreal Canadiens jersey symbolizes his cultural identity and the pressure of community expectations. Learners examine character motivation by tracing a character’s decisions to understand their development, like how a character’s choice in a Margaret Atwood story reflects Canadian societal values. Students learn to support their analysis with direct textual evidence to construct well-reasoned arguments.

## Academic Writing
Students develop persuasive essays using the five-paragraph structure: introduction with a clear thesis statement, three body paragraphs with topic sentences and supporting evidence, and a concluding paragraph. They practice incorporating secondary sources to bolster arguments, citing in MLA or APA format. A typical prompt might ask students to argue for or against a change to the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms, requiring them to use logical reasoning and real-world examples, such as debates over freedom of expression in Quebec.

## Grammar and Usage
Students master advanced grammatical concepts, including the correct use of commas in complex sentences and the distinction between its and it’s. They practice identifying and correcting common errors such as dangling modifiers and improper pronoun-antecedent agreement. For example, a student would revise a sentence like “The loonie, which is Canada’s one-dollar coin, is often confused with it’s American counterpart” to “The loonie, which is Canada’s one-dollar coin, is often confused with its American counterpart.”

## Media Literacy
Students deconstruct various media forms, analyzing how television, radio, and social media shape public perception. For instance, they compare a news segment on CBC about a federal election to a satirical clip on This Hour Has 22 Minutes to identify bias and persuasion techniques. Learners evaluate the credibility of sources, distinguishing between peer-reviewed journalism and user-generated content, and discuss the ethical implications of media ownership in Canada, particularly the role of Bell and Rogers in controlling information flow.

## Drama Studies
Students explore Canadian plays, focusing on script analysis and performance. They study works like Billy Bishop Goes to War by John MacLachlan Gray to understand how characters, dialogue, and stage directions convey theme. Students practice monologue and scene study, using vocal techniques and body language to portray characters authentically. For example, they might perform a scene from a play about residential schools, focusing on subtext and emotional depth to convey historical weight.

## Creative Writing
Students craft short stories and poems using Canadian contexts and imagery. They practice writing techniques such as showing, not telling, and incorporating sensory details to create vivid settings. A typical assignment might involve writing a story about a character’s experience at a hockey tournament in Saskatchewan, using elements like the prairie landscape and the sound of a zamboni to evoke atmosphere. Students learn to revise their work for clarity, conciseness, and impact.
