# Canadian Curriculum Grade 4 English

## Reading Comprehension and Fluency
Students read grade-level texts, including stories about Canadian pioneers and animals of the boreal forest. They learn to identify the main idea and supporting details. Students practice reading aloud with expression, focusing on correct phrasing and pace. They use strategies like rereading for understanding and looking at pictures for context. Fluency is developed through repeated reading of familiar texts, ensuring they can read smoothly at a rate of approximately 90 words per minute. This builds confidence and improves comprehension of more complex Canadian stories and informational texts.

## Spelling and Vocabulary
Students explore spelling patterns, focusing on Canadian spellings like 'colour' and 'centre'. They learn to recognize and apply common prefixes and suffixes, such as 'un-' and '-ful', to understand word meanings. Vocabulary is expanded through reading and studying thematic units on Canadian geography and culture, including words like 'poutine', 'loonie', and 'parliament'. Word lists are drawn from literary and informational texts. Students practice spelling through word sorts and dictation, applying phonics rules and understanding how words are built in the Canadian English language.

## Writing: Sentences and Paragraphs
Students construct complete sentences, ensuring they have a subject and a verb. They learn to use a variety of sentence structures, including declarative, interrogative, and exclamatory sentences. Paragraph writing is introduced, with a focus on topic sentences, supporting details, and concluding sentences. Students write about familiar Canadian experiences, like playing hockey or visiting the CN Tower. They use graphic organizers to organize their ideas. Punctuation, including commas, periods, and question marks, is taught to ensure clarity and correctness in their written communication.

## Oral Communication and Listening
Students develop active listening skills by paying attention to the speaker, asking clarifying questions, and summarizing key information. They learn to express ideas clearly and concisely in group discussions, using appropriate volume and tone. Oral presentations are prepared on topics such as Canadian provinces or famous Canadians. Students practice speaking from brief notes, not scripts. They learn to participate in respectful turn-taking conversations, sharing their own viewpoints while considering the perspectives of others, a key skill in Canadian classroom discourse.

## Grammar and Language Conventions
Students study the parts of speech, identifying nouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs in sentences. They learn to use singular and plural nouns correctly. Subject-verb agreement is practiced with Canadian examples like 'The maple leaf is Canada's symbol' versus 'Maple leaves fall in autumn'. Students are introduced to punctuation for dialogue and learn to use commas in a series. Conventions of Canadian English are reinforced, including the correct use of quotation marks and apostrophes in possessive nouns like 'the dog's toy' or 'Mme. Dubois's class'.

## Media Literacy and Digital Awareness
Students learn to identify different types of media, such as news articles, advertisements, and websites. They examine how messages are constructed and who the target audience is. With a focus on Canadian media, students explore how a commercial for Tim Hortons or a CBC Kids segment tries to persuade or inform them. They practice distinguishing between fact and opinion. Students begin to understand the importance of digital citizenship, including creating strong passwords and being respectful online when using educational platforms to share their work.
