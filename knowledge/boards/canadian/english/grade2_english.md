# Canadian Curriculum Grade 2 English
## Phonemic Awareness
Students learn to isolate and manipulate the individual sounds (phonemes) in spoken words. This includes identifying the first, middle, and last sounds in simple, familiar words, such as "hockey" (beginning /h/, middle /o/, ending /kē/). They practice blending sounds together to form words, like combining /m/, /a/, /p/ to make "maple," and segmenting words into their individual sounds. A key skill is identifying rhyming pairs and creating rhyming words, using Canadian examples like "loon" and "moon," or "snow" and "go." This foundational skill is essential for reading and spelling.

## Phonics
Students expand their knowledge of letter-sound correspondences, focusing on short and long vowel sounds. They learn to decode (read) and encode (spell) one-syllable words with initial and final consonant blends, such as "snow," "play," and "truck." Students are taught common letter patterns like "ai" as in "train" and "oa" as in "boat," using Canadian vocabulary. They begin to recognize high-frequency sight words by sight, with examples including "about," "could," "have," and "said" using Canadian spelling conventions.

## Comprehension
Students learn to identify the main idea and recall key details from a short, age-appropriate text they have heard or read. They are taught to make simple connections between the text and their own experiences, such as relating a story about winter to building a snowman. Students practice answering basic questions ("who," "what," "where," "when") about the characters, setting, and events in a story. They also learn to identify the sequence of events in a familiar narrative, like the steps to making a Canada Day craft.

## Writing
Students focus on constructing complete, simple sentences that begin with a capital letter and end with a period. They learn to use the most common punctuation mark, a period, correctly. Spelling is phonetic and inventive, with an emphasis on high-frequency words and grade-appropriate vocabulary. Students are encouraged to write about familiar topics, such as their favourite hockey team, the loon on a coin, or the CN Tower. They use a combination of known words and phonetic spelling to express ideas, developing confidence in written communication.

## Oral Communication
Students learn to listen attentively to others and speak on familiar topics in clear, complete sentences. They practice participating in simple group discussions, sharing ideas and taking turns speaking. The focus is on using clear pronunciation and volume so others can easily understand. Students are taught to retell a simple story, including the beginning, middle, and end, using Canadian content like a tale of the loon. This involves using appropriate vocabulary related to their own experiences and interests.

## Media Literacy
Students learn to identify common forms of media, such as storybooks, posters, and short videos. They are introduced to the concept that media communicates messages and can be used for different purposes, like teaching or advertising. Students practice making simple observations about media, for example, identifying the main subject of a picture of the maple leaf or a hockey player. They begin to understand that people create media content and that different sources may present information in different ways.
