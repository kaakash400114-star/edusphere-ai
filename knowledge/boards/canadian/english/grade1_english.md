# Canadian Curriculum Grade 1 English
## Listening and Speaking
Students develop listening skills by following one-step and two-step directions related to classroom routines, such as "Please put your pencil in the box and then line up." They learn to listen politely to others during sharing times and show understanding by asking simple questions like "What colour was your hockey jersey?" Oral expression focuses on sharing personal experiences using simple sentences. For example, a student might say, "I saw a loonie on the ground." They practice speaking in complete sentences and learn to take turns in conversations, respecting others who are speaking.

## Reading Foundations
Students learn to recognize and name all uppercase (A, B, C) and lowercase (a, b, c) letters. They practice identifying the most common sound each letter makes, understanding that 's' makes the /s/ sound in 'snake'. Children begin blending these sounds to read simple, high-frequency words like 'the', 'and', and 'can'. They learn to track a line of print from left to right. Students recognize their own name and common signs, such as 'Exit' or 'Washroom', connecting written words to their meanings in everyday Canadian life.

## Reading Comprehension
Students listen to and read a variety of simple texts, including stories, poems, and information books about Canadian topics like maple syrup or animals. They demonstrate understanding by retelling the main event in a story, such as "The beaver built a dam." Students learn to make simple predictions about what might happen next in a story, using picture clues. They can identify characters and setting. When asked, they can point to where a story takes place, for example, "The CN Tower is in Toronto." They connect stories to their own experiences.

## Writing Foundations
Students learn to form uppercase and lowercase letters correctly, using proper pencil grip and starting at the top of the letter. They practice writing their first and last names, paying attention to capital letters. Children learn to write simple words and short sentences using their developing knowledge of letter-sound relationships. They use a combination of invented and phonetic spelling, writing 'mapl' for 'maple'. Students learn to leave spaces between words and write from left to right. Their writing focuses on personal topics, such as their family or a favourite toy.

## Conventions of Language
Students understand and use basic rules of grammar and punctuation in Grade 1. They learn that sentences start with a capital letter and end with a period. They recognize and can identify nouns as naming words for people, places, and things (e.g., teacher, school, hockey stick, loonie). Students learn to use common adjectives to describe nouns, such as 'red' for a 'leaf' in the fall. They understand that a sentence expresses a complete thought and practice speaking and writing in complete sentences.

## Media and Digital Literacy
Students begin to understand that different media carry messages. They learn that illustrations in books help tell the story. Students are introduced to digital tools, like tablets or computers, used for learning games or listening to stories. They learn to care for books and devices by handling them gently. They can identify simple signs and symbols in their environment, like a stop sign or a recycling symbol for the blue box. Students are encouraged to ask questions about what they see and hear in various media formats.
