# Canadian Curriculum Grade 3 English
## Spelling Patterns
Grade 3 students learn common spelling patterns. They study vowel teams like 'oa' in boat and 'ai' in train. They review vowel-consonant-e (VCe) patterns, such as in 'make' and 'home'. Students also study the 'ight' pattern as in light and night. They are introduced to Canadian spelling, like 'colour' and 'favourite'. They practice these words in sentences, like "The loonie is a coin" and "I like the colour of the maple leaf".
## Reading Comprehension
Students learn to understand texts by identifying the main idea and key details. They answer questions about who, what, when, where, and why. For example, after reading about the CN Tower, they can state its main purpose and its location in Toronto. They make simple inferences, such as guessing a character's feelings based on the story. They summarize a short paragraph in two or three sentences. They learn to distinguish between fiction and non-fiction texts.
## Writing Structures
Grade 3 students focus on writing clear, organized paragraphs. They learn that a paragraph has a main idea, followed by supporting sentences. For example, a main idea could be "Hockey is a popular sport in Canada" with details about playing on a frozen pond or in an arena. They practice using time-order words like first, next, then, and finally. They learn to write simple friendly letters with a date, greeting, body, and closing, perhaps writing to a pen pal in another province.
## Word Study and Vocabulary
Students expand their vocabulary by learning root words, prefixes, and suffixes. They learn that adding '-un' to a root like 'happy' creates 'unhappy'. They learn that the suffix '-less' means 'without', as in 'friendless'. They are introduced to compound words, like 'sunflower' and 'homework'. They use a dictionary to find the meaning of new words. They learn specific Canadian vocabulary like 'poutine', 'toque', and 'zamboni'.
## Grammar and Punctuation
Students learn to identify and use parts of speech: nouns, verbs, adjectives, and pronouns. They learn that pronouns like 'he', 'she', and 'it' replace nouns. They learn to use commas in a series, as in "We need skates, a puck, and a hockey net". They learn to use apostrophes for contractions, such as "can't" for "can not" and "it's" for "it is". They practice writing simple and compound sentences.
## Listening and Speaking
Students develop their listening skills by following multi-step instructions. They practice active listening by making eye contact and asking clarifying questions. They learn to speak clearly on a topic for a short period, such as describing their favourite provincial park. They practice sharing opinions, like "I think the maple syrup is the best treat". They learn to use appropriate volume and tone when speaking to the class or a small group.
