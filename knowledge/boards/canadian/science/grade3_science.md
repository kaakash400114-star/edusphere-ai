# Canadian Curriculum Grade 3 Science

## Growth and Changes in Plants
Plants are living things that need sunlight, water, and nutrients from soil to grow. They have roots, stems, leaves, flowers, and seeds. A maple tree is a common plant in Canada. Its roots hold it in the ground, the stem (trunk) carries water, and leaves make food from sunlight. Seeds, like those from a dandelion or pinecone, can be carried by wind or animals to grow into new plants. Plants are important for oxygen and are a symbol on our loonie coin.

## Growth and Changes in Animals
Animals grow and change during their life cycles. A butterfly starts as a caterpillar, forms a chrysalis, and then emerges as a butterfly. Many animals, like the Canada lynx or a common frog, are born in one province and may stay there or move. Animals need food, water, shelter, and space to survive. Just like in a hockey game, each animal has a role. Mothers, such as a bear or a robin, care for their babies until they can find food on their own.

## Materials and Structures
Materials have different properties like strength, flexibility, and texture. We use strong materials like wood and steel to build structures. A bridge is a structure that must support a lot of weight. The CN Tower in Toronto is a famous Canadian structure. To make a strong bridge, engineers might use triangles because they are a rigid shape. You can test the strength of materials by stacking books on a piece of paper versus a cardboard tube to see which one holds more.

## Forces and Movement
A force is a push or a pull. Magnets can push or pull without touching. They have two poles, north and south, that can attract or repel. Gravity is a force that pulls everything toward the Earth’s centre. When you play hockey, the ice exerts a low-friction force on the skates, allowing you to glide easily. You can feel forces when you open a heavy door or when a strong wind from the west pushes against you on a prairie day.

## Soils in the Environment
Soil is a mixture of minerals, water, air, and organic matter. It is not all the same; there are different types like clay, sand, and loam. Healthy soil is full of living things, such as worms and insects, that help break down old leaves. In a boreal forest, the soil is thin and acidic, perfect for pine trees. We must protect our soil, as it is a valuable resource for growing crops in provinces like Saskatchewan and for supporting all life.

## Daily and Seasonal Changes
Days and nights are caused by the Earth’s rotation on its axis. Seasons are caused by the Earth’s orbit around the Sun. In Canada, we have four distinct seasons. Spring brings warm weather and melting snow. Summer has long, sunny days perfect for swimming. Fall brings cooler weather and changing leaf colours, especially on maples. Winter is cold and may have a lot of snow, which creates beautiful landscapes and opportunities for activities like skating.
