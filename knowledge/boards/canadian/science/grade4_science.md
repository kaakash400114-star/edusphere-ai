# Canadian Curriculum Grade 4 Science

## Habitats and Communities
Students investigate the interactions between living and non-living things within an ecosystem. They define habitats as the natural home of a plant or animal, such as a beaver's lodge or a maple tree. A community includes all the living things sharing a habitat, like the loons, fish, and reeds in a lake. Producers, such as algae, make their own food, while consumers, like rabbits, eat other living things. Decomposers, such as fungi, break down dead materials. Students learn how animals adapt to their environment, like the polar bear's thick fur for surviving in the cold.

## Rocks and Minerals
Students learn about the three main types of rocks: igneous, sedimentary, and metamorphic. Igneous rocks, like granite, are formed from cooled magma. Sedimentary rocks, such as sandstone, are made from compressed layers of sand and mud. Metamorphic rocks, like marble, are changed by heat and pressure. A mineral is a solid, non-living material found in Earth, like quartz or pyrite. Students identify and sort samples, understanding that the CN Tower is made from concrete and steel, which are manufactured from natural resources.

## Light and Sound
Students explore how light and sound travel. Light travels in a straight line and can be reflected, like seeing your face in a still lake. Objects can be transparent (like a window), translucent (like a frosted glass), or opaque (like a wall). Sound is a form of energy made by vibrations and travels as waves through air, water, or solids. Pitch is how high or low a sound is; a howling loon has a low pitch. Students learn about hearing safety around loud noises, such as a hockey arena.

## Forces and Simple Machines
Students identify forces that push or pull objects. Gravity is a force that pulls everything toward the centre of the Earth, causing a dropped hockey puck to fall. A simple machine helps make work easier. The six types are the inclined plane, wedge, screw, lever, pulley, and wheel and axle. An example is using a ramp (an inclined plane) to pull a heavy load, or a lever like a hockey stick to transfer force and shoot a puck with more power.

## Animal Life Cycles
Students study the life cycles of animals, including complete and incomplete metamorphosis. The life cycle of a butterfly has four stages: egg, larva (caterpillar), pupa (chrysalis), and adult. The life cycle of a frog has three stages: egg, tadpole, and adult. Many animals, including mammals like moose, are born live and grow to adulthood. Students learn about the needs of animals at each stage, such as how a mother bear protects her cubs, and that all living things have basic needs for food, water, shelter, and air.

## Properties of Materials and States of Matter
Students examine the three states of matter: solid, liquid, and gas. Solids have a definite shape and volume, like a hockey skate. Liquids have a definite volume but take the shape of their container, like water in a glass. Gases have no definite shape or volume and expand to fill a space, like the air in a balloon. Students learn that changing temperature can cause a change of state, such as water freezing into ice or boiling into steam, a key concept for understanding maple syrup production.
