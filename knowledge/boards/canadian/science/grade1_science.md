# Canadian Curriculum Grade 1 Science


## Senses and the World Around Us
Children explore their five senses to understand the world. They learn to identify and describe what they see, hear, smell, taste, and touch. For example, a loonie has rough edges and makes a distinct sound, while a hockey puck is smooth and cold. They use these senses to compare materials like a soft maple leaf and a hard hockey puck. Observations are recorded using simple drawings or words, honing their descriptive language skills.

## Weather and Seasons
Grade 1 students observe and describe daily and seasonal weather patterns. They learn about different types of precipitation, like rain, snow, and sleet, and associate them with seasons. Students observe how weather affects their activities, such as needing a coat in winter for playing outdoors or celebrating Canada Day in sunny summer. They learn about the four seasons and their characteristic weather, connecting them to the natural world around them.

## Living Things and Their Needs
Students identify basic needs of living things: air, water, food, and shelter. They classify objects as living (like a squirrel or a tree in a Canadian forest) or non-living (like a rock or a CN Tower model). Children learn that plants need sunlight and water to grow, using examples like a maple tree or a classroom bean plant. They observe how animals, including pets, meet their needs to stay alive.

## Materials and Objects
In Grade 1 Science, students identify and describe the properties of common materials, including wood, metal, plastic, glass, and fabric. They use descriptive vocabulary such as hard, soft, smooth, rough, flexible, and transparent to characterize these materials based on observable attributes. Students sort and classify objects into groups according to shared material properties, for example, grouping items that are hard or items that are soft. They distinguish between the material an object is made from and its function, recognizing that a bookshelf made of wood is hard but designed to hold books. This foundational knowledge allows children to understand the purposeful selection of materials in the objects they encounter daily.

## Earth and Sky
Students identify major landforms and water bodies, recognizing natural features like mountains, rivers, and oceans, as well as human-made structures such as bridges and buildings. They observe and describe the sun, identifying it as a source of light and heat that causes changes in temperature and shadows throughout the day. Learners record the appearance and movement of the moon, noting its phases as a cycle from a thin crescent to a full circle. They explore the daily pattern of day and night, understanding that the Earth's rotation causes the sun to appear to move across the sky, creating these cycles and linking them to familiar activities like eating meals, attending school, and resting at night.

## Forces and Motion
In Grade 1 Science, students learn that a force is a push or a pull, which is the direct cause of any motion or change in an object’s movement. They explore and describe different types of motion, including sliding, like a hockey puck on ice, rolling, such as a ball down a ramp, and bouncing, like a rubber ball on the floor. Through hands-on activities, students discover that the strength and direction of a force affect how an object moves; for example, a harder push makes a toy car go faster and farther. They identify and describe forces in their everyday environment, such as wind blowing leaves from a tree or pulling a wagon, and explain how these forces cause objects to move in specific ways.
