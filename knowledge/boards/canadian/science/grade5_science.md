# Canadian Curriculum Grade 5 Science

## Understanding Matter and Energy
Students explore the properties of solids, liquids, and gases. They learn that all matter is made of tiny particles that are always moving. A solid, like a hockey puck, has a fixed shape because its particles are packed tightly. A liquid, such as maple syrup, flows and takes the shape of its container. A gas, like the air in a soccer ball, fills any space. Matter can change states through processes like melting ice or boiling water, which are examples of physical changes. These changes do not create new substances.

## Forces and Simple Machines
Students investigate how forces, like pushes and pulls, affect objects. A force can start, stop, or change the movement of something. They learn about simple machines that make work easier. A lever, like a see-saw, uses a pivot point to lift a heavy load. An inclined plane, such as a ramp, allows you to pull a heavy object up with less effort than lifting it straight up. Other examples include pulleys used to lift flags and wheelbarrows that act as levers to move garden soil.

## The Canadian Ecosystem
Students focus on the unique ecosystems found across Canada. They learn about the interactions between living things (plants, animals) and their non-living environment (soil, water, sunlight). In a boreal forest, you might find spruce trees, black bears, and loons. In a wetland, cattails and frogs are common. They understand that all living things need energy to survive and that energy often comes from the sun, which is captured by plants through a process called photosynthesis.

## Earth's Materials and Processes
Students identify rocks and minerals that are common in Canada. They learn that the Earth’s surface is constantly changing through slow processes like erosion, where wind and water shape the land over thousands of years, and faster processes like earthquakes. They examine the rock cycle, where igneous rocks (like granite) form from cooled magma, sedimentary rocks (like sandstone) form from compressed sediments, and metamorphic rocks (like marble) are changed by heat and pressure.

## Weather and Water Systems
Students learn about the water cycle and how it affects weather. The cycle includes evaporation (water turning to vapour from lakes like Ontario), condensation (vapour forming clouds), and precipitation (rain or snow falling). They study how temperature and air pressure influence weather patterns. In winter, cold arctic air brings freezing temperatures and snow, while in summer, warmer air can lead to thunderstorms. They also learn about extreme weather events, like blizzards common in the Prairie provinces.

## Humans and the Environment
Students explore the positive and negative impacts of human activities on the environment. They learn about sustainable practices to reduce our footprint, such as recycling paper to save trees and conserving water. They study the importance of renewable resources, like generating electricity from wind or hydro dams. Examples include managing the health of the Great Lakes and protecting the habitats of endangered species, like the polar bear in the North, to ensure a healthy environment for future generations.
