# Canadian Curriculum Grade 6 Science

## Diversity of Living Things
Students classify organisms into five main kingdoms: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia. They examine the characteristics of each, such as Monera's prokaryotic cells and Fungi's chitinous cell walls. The focus is on Canadian biodiversity, comparing the temperate rainforest of British Columbia to the arctic tundra of Nunavut. Students use dichotomous keys to identify local flora and fauna, like the common loon or the sugar maple, understanding that physical and behavioural traits determine an organism's niche.

## Electricity
This topic explores the principles of static and current electricity. Students learn about electrical charges, conductors (like copper), and insulators (like rubber). They construct simple series and parallel circuits using batteries, wires, and bulbs, calculating voltage. A key experiment involves creating a static charge by rubbing a balloon on their hair, demonstrating electron transfer. The curriculum connects this to real-world applications like the hydroelectric dams in Niagara Falls, which power millions of homes, including the iconic CN Tower in Toronto.

## Flight
Students investigate the principles of aerodynamics, including lift, drag, thrust, and weight. They learn how the shape of an airfoil creates a pressure difference, generating lift. Through hands-on activities, students design and test paper airplanes and gliders, measuring how wing surface area affects flight distance. Examples include the powerful downforce generated by hockey players and the aerodynamics of a loon diving into a lake, relating these forces to the controlled flight of aircraft like the Canadair Regional Jet.

## Space
This unit focuses on Earth's place in the solar system and beyond. Students identify the planets and their major characteristics, learning about planetary orbits using astronomical units. They model Earth's rotation and revolution, explaining the cause of seasons and day/night cycles. The curriculum highlights Canada's contributions to space exploration, like the Canadarm used on the Space Shuttle and the James Webb Space Telescope, which has Canadian-designed components. Students also learn about the aurora borealis, a stunning natural light show visible in northern provinces.

## Properties of Air and Flight
Students explore the properties of air, including its mass, pressure, and ability to be compressed. They conduct experiments demonstrating air pressure, such as the Magdeburg hemispheres or a Cartesian diver. These principles are directly linked to the science of flight, explaining how a jet engine generates thrust by compressing and igniting air. Students analyze how the thin air at high altitudes affects aircraft performance and learn about the use of altimeters, which measure air pressure to determine a plane's elevation.

## Matter and Materials
Students examine the three states of matter—solid, liquid, and gas—and the processes of phase change (melting, freezing, vaporization, condensation). They investigate the properties of materials, such as strength, transparency, and magnetism. Using the particle theory, they explain why matter changes state. A key example involves the production of maple syrup, where water is boiled off (evaporation) to concentrate the sugar. Students also test materials for their suitability in building structures, comparing wood from a Canadian forest to steel.
