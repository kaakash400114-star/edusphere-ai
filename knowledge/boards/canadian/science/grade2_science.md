# Canadian Curriculum Grade 2 Science

## Living Things and Their Habitats
Living things have basic needs to grow and stay healthy. These needs are food, water, air, and a suitable place to live, called a habitat. A habitat is where a plant or animal finds everything it needs. For example, a maple tree’s habitat provides soil for its roots, sunlight for its leaves, and air. A loon’s habitat is a lake with clean water for swimming and fish for food. Animals and plants are adapted to their specific habitats, like how a polar bear has thick fur for the cold.

## Materials and Objects
Objects are made from different materials, each with its own properties. We can describe materials by how they look, feel, and sound. For instance, a hockey puck is hard, smooth, and black, while a wool toque is soft, warm, and often colourful. We can test materials to see if they are waterproof or transparent. A clear glass is transparent, allowing us to see through it, but a wooden block is not. We use these properties to choose the right material for an object's job.

## The Senses and the Body
We use our five senses—sight, hearing, smell, taste, and touch—to explore the world. Our eyes help us see the CN Tower and the colours of fall leaves. Our ears help us hear the sound of a hockey game on TV. Our nose helps us smell fresh baking, like maple syrup. Our tongue helps us taste sweet apples. Our skin helps us feel the softness of a cat or the hardness of a rock. Our senses give us information about our environment.

## Daily and Seasonal Changes
Our world changes every day and with the seasons. Days and nights happen because the Earth spins on its axis. Seasons change over the year because of the Earth’s orbit around the sun. In summer in Canada, it is warm and the days are long, perfect for playing outside. In winter, it is cold and the days are short, and we can see frost on the windows. Animals and plants also change with the seasons, like trees losing their leaves in the fall.

## States of Matter
Matter is anything that takes up space and can be solid, liquid, or gas. Solids keep their shape, like a hockey stick or a rock. Liquids flow and take the shape of their container, like water in a glass or maple syrup on pancakes. Gases, like the air we breathe, are all around us but have no fixed shape. Water can change state: it is liquid as rain, solid as ice on a pond in winter, and gas as steam from a kettle.

## Forces and Motion
A push or a pull is a force that can make an object move, stop, or change direction. For example, when you kick a soccer ball, you are applying a force. Gravity is a force that pulls everything down towards the Earth, making a dropped apple fall. Magnets can push or pull certain objects without touching them. We can also use a lever, like a seesaw, to lift something heavy by applying a small force.
