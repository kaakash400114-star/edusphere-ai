# Canadian Curriculum Grade 6 Mathematics

## Number Sense and Numeration
Grade 6 students extend their understanding of large numbers to the millions place and explore fractions, decimals, and percents. They learn to read, write, and represent whole numbers and decimals using expanded notation and place value charts. Students practice multiplying and dividing whole numbers by 10, 100, and 1000, exploring how this affects place value. They compare and order fractions, decimals, and percents, and convert between these representations. A key skill is calculating percentages of whole numbers, such as determining a 15% sales tax on an item from a local hockey arena ticket.

## Patterns and Algebra
Students identify and describe patterns and relationships using charts, tables, and graphs. They learn to write algebraic expressions to represent real-world situations, for example, calculating the total cost of 'n' loonies and toonies. The focus is on understanding the preservation of equality in equations, where performing the same operation on both sides maintains balance. Students solve one-variable equations, like 3x + 5 = 20, using systematic methods. They also analyze patterns in sequences, such as the number of goals scored by a team over several games, to predict future terms.

## Data Management and Probability
In this strand, students collect, organize, and display data using line graphs, bar graphs, and histograms with proper scales and labels. They calculate and interpret the mean, median, and mode of a data set, like the average temperature in their province over a week. Students learn to compare different sets of data to draw conclusions. In probability, they conduct experiments to determine the theoretical likelihood of events, such as flipping a coin or drawing a specific card from a deck, and express probability as a fraction in its simplest form.

## Measurement
Students convert between metric units, such as converting kilometres to metres or litres to millilitres, to solve problems involving length, mass, and capacity. They develop formulas for calculating the perimeter and area of rectangles and the volume of rectangular prisms, applying them to find how much soil is needed for a garden box. A key concept is measuring and constructing angles using a protractor. They also solve problems involving the relationship between area and perimeter, for instance, determining the amount of fencing required to enclose a playground shaped like a rectangle.

## Geometry and Spatial Sense
Grade 6 students sort and classify two-dimensional shapes by their geometric properties, including acute, obtuse, and right angles. They learn to plot and read coordinates on the Cartesian plane, identifying points like the CN Tower's location. Students explore transformations by translating, reflecting, and rotating shapes. They construct three-dimensional figures from nets and determine the type of symmetry, like the reflection symmetry of a maple leaf. A practical application involves analyzing shapes in Canadian architecture, identifying parallel and perpendicular lines in building designs.

## Financial Literacy
This strand focuses on developing responsible money management skills. Students create and interpret budgets for personal or community events, such as planning a school fundraiser. They learn about different payment methods, including credit, debit, and digital wallets. A core concept is understanding interest, both simple and compound, as it applies to savings accounts and loans. Students practice calculating unit prices to find the best value, for example, comparing the cost per gram of different brands of maple syrup. They also analyze how financial decisions affect personal and economic well-being.
