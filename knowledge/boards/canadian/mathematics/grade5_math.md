# Canadian Curriculum Grade 5 Mathematics


## Place Value and Number Sense
Students read, write, and compare numbers up to 100,000. They understand the value of each digit in a number, like in 45,678 where the 4 is in the ten-thousands place. They learn to round numbers to the nearest ten, hundred, or thousand. For example, the population of a small Canadian town like 7,432 would round to 7,000 to the nearest thousand. Students also explore how numbers are used in real-life contexts, like counting the number of loonies in a hockey arena donation box.

## Operations: Whole Numbers and Decimals
In Grade 5, students apply the partial products method to multiply two-digit by three-digit numbers, such as 24 x 356. They also divide three-digit numbers by single-digit divisors, using strategies like long division. For decimals, students add and subtract values to the hundredths place, illustrated with examples like $12.75 + $3.50. They use place value concepts to align decimal points when performing these operations. To apply these skills, students solve practical problems, such as calculating the total cost for multiple family attending a Toronto Maple Leafs game at a ticket price of $54.50 each.

## Fractions
Students develop fluency with equivalent fractions by multiplying or dividing both the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero whole number, such as recognizing 2/3 is equivalent to 4/6. They learn to compare and order fractions by finding a common denominator, for example, ordering 1/2, 1/4, and 3/4 by converting them to 2/4, 1/4, and 3/4. The foundational method for adding and subtracting fractions involves using a common denominator, as seen in the equation 5/8 – 2/8 = 3/8. Students apply these concepts to real-world problems, like calculating that sharing a 3/4-eaten pizza among six friends requires finding 3/4 ÷ 6, resulting in each friend receiving a portion of 3/24, which simplifies to 1/8 of the whole pizza.

## Measurement and Perimeter
In Grade 5 Mathematics, students build proficiency with metric units by measuring and comparing length, mass, volume, and capacity, such as determining the mass of a hockey puck in grams or the capacity of a large water cask in litres. A key concept is perimeter, which is calculated by adding the side lengths of any polygon. For example, to find the perimeter of a rectangular playground measuring 25 m by 15 m, students add all four sides (25 + 15 + 25 + 15) to determine the perimeter is 80 m. Students also apply the formula P = 2(l + w) as a more efficient method for rectangles. Practical exercises include measuring classroom objects in centimetres and calculating the perimeter of irregular shapes like a school's hockey rink.

## Geometry
Students identify, describe, and classify 2D and 3D shapes. They know that a stop sign is an octagon and that a hockey puck is a cylinder. Students learn about lines of symmetry, like the perfect symmetry of a maple leaf. They identify properties of shapes, such as the equal side lengths of a square or the number of faces on a cube, connecting these ideas to building blocks used in class.

## Data Management
Grade 5 students expand their data management skills by planning and conducting surveys, such as collecting primary data on the favourite sports teams of their peers. They learn to organize this data into tally charts and frequency tables to prepare for analysis. Students represent their findings using double bar graphs to compare two data sets and scaled pictographs, where each symbol represents a specific quantity, like one maple leaf for every 25 students. In their analysis, they identify the mode to find the most frequent response and calculate the median to determine the middle value. They also use these graphs to interpret data, solve simple problems, and communicate their findings with clear labels and titles.
