# Canadian Curriculum Grade 5 Mathematics

## Place Value and Number Sense
Students read, write, and compare numbers up to 100,000. They understand the value of each digit in a number, like in 45,678 where the 4 is in the ten-thousands place. They learn to round numbers to the nearest ten, hundred, or thousand. For example, the population of a small Canadian town like 7,432 would round to 7,000 to the nearest thousand. Students also explore how numbers are used in real-life contexts, like counting the number of loonies in a hockey arena donation box.

## Operations: Whole Numbers and Decimals
Students multiply 2-digit by 3-digit numbers, such as 24 x 356, using strategies like partial products. They divide 3-digit numbers by 1-digit divisors. Students also add and subtract decimals to the hundredths, like $12.75 + $3.50, often using money examples. They practice solving problems with tickets to a Toronto Maple Leafs game, calculating the total cost for several family members at $54.50 each.

## Fractions
Students compare and order fractions with like and unlike denominators. They learn to find equivalent fractions, for example, understanding that 3/5 of a maple syrup bottle is the same as 6/10. Students practice adding and subtracting fractions with the same denominator, like 1/4 + 2/4. They solve problems involving sharing pizzas at a birthday party, determining how much each of six friends gets from a 3/4-eaten pizza.

## Measurement and Perimeter
Students measure length, volume, and capacity using metric units. They might measure the height of the CN Tower in metres (553 m) or the volume of a two-litre bottle of milk. Students calculate the perimeter of polygons, such as a rectangular rink in Winnipeg that is 30 metres long and 20 metres wide, adding the sides (30 + 20 + 30 + 20) to find the 100-metre perimeter.

## Geometry
Students identify, describe, and classify 2D and 3D shapes. They know that a stop sign is an octagon and that a hockey puck is a cylinder. Students learn about lines of symmetry, like the perfect symmetry of a maple leaf. They identify properties of shapes, such as the equal side lengths of a square or the number of faces on a cube, connecting these ideas to building blocks used in class.

## Data Management
Students collect data, like the number of students who support each of Canada’s ten provinces. They create and interpret bar graphs and pictographs, with a symbol representing a certain number of people (e.g., one hockey stick for every 50 fans). Students analyze the data to answer questions, such as which province has the most supporters, and find the mode to determine the most popular choice.
