# Canadian Curriculum Grade 7 Mathematics
## Number Sense and Numeration
Students will represent, compare, and order rational numbers, extending their understanding from whole numbers to fractions and decimals. They will perform addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division with positive fractions and decimals, applying these skills to solve real-world problems. For example, they might calculate the cost of hockey equipment after a 15% discount or determine the average speed of a car travelling between Toronto and Ottawa. Students will also use exponents to represent repeated multiplication, such as writing 3 x 3 x 3 as 3³, and apply the order of operations (BEDMAS) to evaluate multi-step expressions correctly.

## Patterning and Algebra
Students will identify and describe patterns and relationships using words, tables of values, graphs, and algebraic expressions. They will model real-life situations with linear equations, like determining the number of loonies needed to make up a specific dollar amount. Students will evaluate algebraic expressions by substituting numerical values for variables, for example, finding the value of 3x + 5 when x is 4. They will also solve simple linear equations of the form ax = b or x + a = b using algebraic techniques and verify their solutions by substitution, connecting abstract math to concrete Canadian examples like calculating the number of provinces in Canada.

## Data Management and Probability
Students will collect, organize, and display data using a variety of graphs, including circle, bar, and line graphs. They will analyze these graphs to determine central tendencies like the mean, median, and mode, perhaps using data on the heights of students in their class. Students will calculate the probability of single events, such as the likelihood of drawing a red maple leaf from a bag of coloured leaves. They will also make inferences and draw conclusions from data, distinguishing between primary and secondary data sources, like comparing a survey of favourite Canadian sports to statistics published by a national organization.

## Measurement
Students will solve problems involving perimeter, area, and volume of composite shapes. They will use formulas to calculate the area of rectangles and triangles, and the volume of right prisms, perhaps finding the volume of a hockey rink's ice resurfacing machine. Students will convert metric units between different prefixes (e.g., millimetres to centimetres, litres to kilolitres). They will also estimate and measure physical attributes in contexts relevant to Canada, such as estimating the height of the CN Tower in metres or calculating the area of a provincial park.

## Spatial Sense
Students will identify and construct geometric patterns and tessellations, which might involve designing a quilt pattern using shapes. They will plot and read coordinates in all four quadrants of the Cartesian plane, and perform dilations (enlargements and reductions) on 2D shapes. Students will also build and analyze three-dimensional models, like creating a scale model of a Canadian landmark using cubes. They will identify and sort quadrilaterals and triangles by their geometric properties, including parallel sides and angle measures, applying this knowledge to real-world structures.

## Financial Literacy
Students will develop skills to make informed financial decisions. They will create and interpret simple budgets, perhaps planning for a family vacation to a national park. Students will calculate unit costs to find the best value when purchasing items, like comparing the price of maple syrup in different-sized bottles. They will also understand concepts of interest, savings, and debt, learning how to calculate simple interest on an amount of money saved to buy a pair of hockey skates. This practical math helps them understand the value of money and the importance of responsible financial planning for their future.
