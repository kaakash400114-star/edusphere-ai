# Canadian Curriculum Grade 9 Mathematics
## Algebraic Expressions and Equations
Students will simplify polynomials using the exponent laws, such as multiplying terms like 3x² and 2x⁴ to get 6x⁶. They will add and subtract polynomials, like combining (2x² + 5x) and (x² - 3x) to make 3x² + 2x. Students solve multi-step linear equations, like finding the value of x in 4x - 7 = 9. Factoring simple trinomials is key, for instance, breaking down x² + 5x + 6 into (x + 2)(x + 3). These skills are foundational for all future math, much like knowing how to skate is fundamental to playing hockey.

## Spatial Sense and Measurement
This unit focuses on metric units for length, area, volume, and capacity, converting between them like 2.5 kilometres to 2,500 metres. Students calculate the areas and perimeters of complex 2D shapes, such as finding the surface area of a rectangular prism representing a hockey rink with specific dimensions. Volume calculations for prisms, cylinders, and pyramids are performed, using the formula V = Bh. Students also understand the relationship between imperial and metric units, converting a player's height from feet and inches to centimetres.

## Data Literacy and Probability
Students collect, organize, and analyze data using various graphs like bar graphs and histograms to represent the number of loonies in different piggy banks. They calculate and interpret measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) for data sets. For probability, students determine theoretical probabilities of events, like the chance of a random Canadian province being Ontario. They conduct experiments and use tree diagrams to solve problems involving independent events, such as flipping a coin and rolling a die, calculating the combined probability.

## Financial Literacy
This module introduces students to unit pricing to compare the cost of items, like determining which of two maple syrup brands offers the better value. They create and interpret budgets for personal scenarios, such as planning a trip to the CN Tower with an allowance. Students calculate simple and compound interest on savings accounts, using formulas like I = P r t. They also analyze different payment methods, understanding the true cost of using a credit card versus cash for a purchase, fostering responsible financial decision-making.

## Proportional Reasoning
Students solve problems involving ratios and rates, such as determining the scale on a map of Canada where 5 cm represents 500 km. They apply these concepts to solve proportional equations, like finding the amount of flour needed to bake a larger batch of Nanaimo bars. Students convert between various units, including currency exchange rates between Canadian and US dollars. They also solve problems involving percent increase and decrease, like calculating the tax on a new pair of hockey skates in Ontario.

## Geometric Properties and Relationships
Students analyze the properties of angles and parallel lines, using a protractor to find angles formed by a hockey stick's blade and shaft. They will study the properties of triangles and quadrilaterals, classifying them based on their sides and angles. The Pythagorean theorem is used to solve for a missing side length, like finding the distance between bases on a baseball diamond. Students will also perform dilations on geometric shapes, understanding how scaling works in real-world contexts like architectural blueprints.
