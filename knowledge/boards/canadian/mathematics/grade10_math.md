# Canadian Curriculum Grade 10 Mathematics

## Algebraic Expressions and Equations
Students will manipulate and simplify polynomial expressions using the laws of exponents and distributive property. They will factor binomials (difference of squares) and trinomials (simple trinomials, perfect square trinomials). Students solve linear equations (e.g., 3x - 5 = 16) and simple quadratic equations by factoring. For example, to solve x² + 5x + 6 = 0, students factor to (x + 2)(x + 3) = 0, finding the solutions x = -2 or x = -3. Techniques to isolate the variable are emphasized.

## Linear Relations and Analytic Geometry
Students explore slope as a rate of change, distinguishing between positive, negative, zero, and undefined slopes using the formula m = (y₂ - y₁) / (x₂ - x₁). They write equations of lines in various forms, including slope-intercept (y = mx + b), standard (Ax + By + C = 0), and point-slope forms. Geometric concepts include calculating the midpoint and distance between two points on a coordinate grid. Real-world applications include modeling the cost of a hockey arena visit with a fixed fee and a per-ticket charge.

## Measurement and Trigonometry
Students will apply the Pythagorean theorem, a² + b² = c², to solve problems involving right-angled triangles. The primary trigonometric ratios (sine, cosine, tangent) are used to find unknown side lengths and angles in right triangles. For instance, to find the height of the CN Tower from a point 150 m away with a 65° angle of elevation, students use tan(65°) = height / 150, rearranging to find the height. The concepts of similar triangles and the metric system for area and volume are reinforced.

## Quadratic Functions
Students investigate the properties of quadratic functions, represented by f(x) = ax² + bx + c. They identify the key features: the vertex, axis of symmetry, and intercepts. By completing the square or using the formula x = -b / 2a, students determine the vertex of a parabola. They sketch graphs, understanding that a positive 'a' value produces an upward-opening parabola. Word problems model scenarios like the height of a loonie toss over time, demonstrating how quadratics represent real-world phenomena.

## Financial Literacy and Applications
This unit focuses on practical mathematical skills for personal finance. Students calculate simple and compound interest using the formulas I = Prt and A = P(1 + i)ⁿ. They solve problems involving the costs of purchasing a car, including calculating monthly payments based on a loan principal, interest rate, and amortization period. Concepts of budgeting, comparing bank account options with different interest rates, and understanding the effect of different payment frequencies (e.g., monthly vs. bi-weekly) on total interest paid are central to this module.

## Geometry and Spatial Sense
Students will solve problems using geometric properties. This includes the application of circle theorems, such as the perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord. The volume and surface area of composite 3-D figures, like a combination of a cylindrical maple syrup can and a conical lid, are calculated. The use of ratios in similar polygons to solve for unknown side lengths is practiced. Students use dynamic geometry software to explore transformations and properties of shapes, such as those found in the design of a hockey rink.
