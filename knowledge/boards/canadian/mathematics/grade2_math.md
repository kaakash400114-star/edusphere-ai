# Canadian Curriculum Grade 2 Mathematics
## Number Sense and Numeration
Students read, represent, compare, and order whole numbers up to 100. They use concrete materials like base-ten blocks to show numbers as tens and ones. For example, the number 45 is 4 tens and 5 ones. Students explore place value, understanding that in 87, the 8 means 8 tens. They use terms like 'more than', 'less than', and 'equal to' to compare numbers, such as 56 is less than 63. Counting by 2s, 5s, and 10s to 100 is a key skill, often using real-life examples like counting loonies or hockey players on a team.
## Measurement
Students measure, compare, and order objects using non-standard units like paper clips or cubes, and standard units like centimetres and metres. They measure the length of a hockey stick or the height of the CN Tower in pictures using these units. They measure capacity using containers, comparing how many cups fill a jug. Time is measured to the hour, half-hour, and quarter-hour using both analog and digital clocks. Understanding the seasons and relating daily activities to times, like eating lunch at 12:00 p.m., is part of this learning.
## Geometry and Spatial Sense
Students identify and describe two-dimensional shapes (triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons, hexagons, circles) and three-dimensional figures (cubes, cones, cylinders, spheres, prisms). They find these shapes in everyday Canadian objects, like a hockey puck (circle) or a dice cube. They locate objects using positional language such as 'over', 'under', 'beside', and 'inside'. Students explore symmetry by folding paper maple leaves or drawing symmetrical patterns. Building with blocks helps them understand how 3D shapes fit together and move.
## Patterning and Algebra
Students identify, describe, create, and extend growing and shrinking patterns. They use concrete materials, actions, and numbers to show patterns. For instance, a pattern of red and white cubes could be 'red, red, white' repeated. Students find the missing number in equations like 3 + __ = 7 and solve simple addition and subtraction problems using word problems. They also explore equalities, understanding that 4 + 2 is the same as 6 + 0. A real-world example is sharing a box of 10 Tim Bits equally among 2 friends.
## Data Management and Probability
Students collect, organize, and display data, often using tally marks and bar graphs. They conduct surveys about favourite animals or provinces and create graphs to show the results. They interpret the data by answering questions like "Which has the most?" or "Which has the fewest?". Students use probability vocabulary like 'certain', 'possible', and 'impossible' to describe events. For example, it is 'certain' that a hockey puck will fit inside a net, and 'impossible' for a loonie to fly like a bird.
## Financial Literacy
Students identify different Canadian coins and bills (penny, nickel, dime, quarter, loonie, toonie) and state their values. They count combinations of coins and bills to make amounts up to $100, like saving up for a hockey jersey. They understand the purpose of money as a medium of exchange for goods and services. Students begin to make simple financial decisions by comparing prices, such as determining if a 25-cent chocolate bar or a 50-cent chocolate bar is a better deal.
