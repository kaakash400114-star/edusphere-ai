# Canadian Curriculum Grade 11 Mathematics  
## Rational Expressions and Equations  
Students learn to simplify, add, subtract, multiply, and divide rational expressions. A key skill is factoring polynomials to find non-permissible values, which are values that make a denominator zero. For example, to simplify (x²-9)/(x+3), students factor to (x-3)(x+3)/(x+3), simplifying to x-3, provided x ≠ -3. They solve equations like 2/(x-1) = 3/x by cross-multiplying to get 2x = 3(x-1), leading to x=3.  

## Trigonometric Functions  
Students explore the unit circle and special angles (0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°) to determine exact sine, cosine, and tangent values. They graph y = sin(x) and y = cos(x), identifying amplitude, period, and phase shifts. For instance, y = 2sin(3x) has amplitude 2 and period 120°. Applications include calculating the height of the CN Tower using angles of elevation, linking trigonometry to real-world Canadian landmarks.  

## Exponential and Logarithmic Functions  
Students analyze exponential growth and decay, such as population models or investment interest. The formula A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt) is used for compound interest, with Canadian examples like calculating loonie growth. They convert between exponential and logarithmic forms (e.g., 10^x = y becomes log₁₀(y) = x) and solve equations like 2^x = 8 using logarithms, reinforcing the relationship between exponents and logs.  

## Quadratic Relations  
Students graph conic sections, including parabolas, circles, and ellipses, using standard forms. For example, (x-2)² + (y+3)² = 16 represents a circle with centre (2,-3) and radius 4. They analyze real-world scenarios, like the trajectory of a hockey puck, using quadratic equations to model height and distance. Completing the square and the quadratic formula (x = [-b ± √(b²-4ac)]/2a) are essential tools for solving these equations.  

## Financial Mathematics  
Students apply mathematics to personal finance, including compound interest, annuities, and loans. The formula FV = PV(1 + i)^n calculates the future value of an investment, such as saving for a maple syrup business. They determine payments for mortgages or car loans using annuity formulas, incorporating Canadian examples like interest rates from Canadian banks. Budgeting and understanding the time value of money are emphasized for real-world decision-making.  

## Geometry and Measurement  
Students solve problems involving perimeter, area, and volume of 2D and 3D shapes, including prisms, pyramids, and spheres. For example, calculating the volume of a hockey puck (a cylinder) or the surface area of the Rogers Centre dome. The metric system is used exclusively, with conversions between units like metres and centimetres. Trigonometry is integrated to solve for unknown sides or angles in right triangles, such as determining the height of a maple tree using shadow measurements.
