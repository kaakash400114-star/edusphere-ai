# Canadian Curriculum Grade 1 Mathematics

## Number Sense and Numeration
Grade 1 students learn to count to 100 by 1s, 2s, 5s, and 10s, and to read and print whole numbers to 20. They recognize numbers in real-world contexts, like a loonie (dollar coin) or the number of players on a hockey team. Students explore parts of a whole, such as dividing a chocolate bar into equal pieces. They use concrete materials like blocks and counters to understand that numbers represent a quantity. For example, they can group 10 maple leaves to understand the number 10, connecting quantity to a familiar Canadian symbol.

## Measurement
Students learn to measure length, mass, and time using non-standard units. They might measure the height of the CN Tower using their own hands or the weight of a hockey puck using blocks. Time is introduced through the school day and calendar, focusing on hours and days. Students use words like "longer," "shorter," "heavier," and "lighter" to compare objects. An example could be comparing the length of two hockey sticks or measuring how long it takes to skate one lap on a rink using non-standard units like skating strides.

## Geometry and Spatial Sense
Grade 1 students sort, classify, and build 2D and 3D shapes. They learn to identify common shapes like triangles, squares, circles, and cubes. Students explore these shapes in their environment, such as recognizing a province's shape on a map or seeing a cylinder in a roll of coins. They describe the position of objects using words like "over," "under," "beside," and "inside." An example activity could be building a maple leaf shape using pattern blocks or sorting Canadian coins by shape and size.

## Patterning and Algebra
Students identify, describe, and create repeating patterns. They use concrete objects like coloured beads, blocks, or natural items like pinecones to make patterns. For example, they might create an A-B pattern with a hockey stick and a puck. Students extend existing patterns and predict what comes next. Patterns can involve sounds, actions, or objects, such as clapping a rhythm related to the number of provinces in Canada. This skill helps them understand relationships and make predictions.

## Data Management and Probability
Grade 1 students collect, organize, and interpret data using simple graphs. They might gather data by asking classmates their favourite type of syrup or the province they live on. Students use tallies, lists, or pictographs to show their findings. They compare groups of data using words like "more," "less," and "the same." For example, they could create a pictograph to see how many students prefer apples or maple syrup for a snack, learning to read basic information from a graph.

## Financial Literacy
Students learn about Canadian coins and bills. They identify the penny, nickel, dime, quarter, loonie, and toonie and understand their value. They use these coins to make simple purchases, like buying a chocolate bar for 75 cents. Concepts of saving and spending are introduced through classroom activities, such as earning "play money" for good behaviour. An example could be sorting a group of Canadian coins or calculating the cost of two hockey stickers using real coin values.
