# Canadian Curriculum Grade 4 Mathematics

## Number Sense and Numeration
Students read, represent, and compare whole numbers to 10,000. They use base-ten blocks and place value charts to understand that 3,478 is 3 thousands, 4 hundreds, 7 tens, and 8 ones. Students round numbers to the nearest ten, hundred, or thousand, like rounding 2,346 to 2,300 for the hockey arena capacity. They also compare numbers using symbols >, <, or =, recognizing that 4,580 is less than 4,585. Learning to write numbers in words, such as "seven thousand, two hundred," builds foundational literacy for math.

## Addition and Subtraction
Students solve four-digit addition and subtraction problems with and without regrouping, using the standard algorithm. For example, they calculate the cost of two items: $12.75 for a loonie coin set plus $7.45 for a maple syrup sample, finding the total is $20.20. They also subtract to find the difference, like 8,432 - 3,219 = 5,213. Students use estimation to check if their answers are reasonable, such as rounding to the nearest hundred before adding. Real-world problems include tracking money saved over several weeks.

## Multiplication and Division
Students multiply two-digit numbers by one-digit numbers using strategies like partial products and arrays. For instance, they might calculate 4 x 24 to find the total number of hockey pucks in 4 packs of 24. Students explore the relationship between multiplication and division, using fact families like 6 x 7 = 42, so 42 ÷ 7 = 6. They divide two-digit numbers by one-digit with and without remainders, such as 45 ÷ 6 = 7 with a remainder of 3, which could represent sharing 43 loonies among 6 friends.

## Data Management and Probability
Students collect, organize, and interpret data using tally charts, line plots, and bar graphs. They might survey classmates on their favourite province and display the results. Students find the median and mode in a data set, like finding the most common temperature (mode) over a week in Winnipeg. They also explore probability using spinners or coin flips, describing outcomes as likely, unlikely, or equally likely, such as the chance of picking a red maple leaf from a mixed bag.

## Measurement
Students measure length, mass, and capacity using metric units like millimetres, centimetres, metres, grams, kilograms, millilitres, and litres. They might measure the length of a hockey stick in centimetres or the mass of a CN Tower souvenir in grams. Students tell time to the nearest minute and calculate elapsed time, such as how long a hockey game lasts from 7:00 PM to 9:30 PM. They also estimate and measure perimeter and area, using grid paper to find the area of a Canadian flag.

## Geometry and Spatial Sense
Students identify, describe, and compare two-dimensional shapes like quadrilaterals and triangles, noting properties like the number of sides and angles. They might sort shapes based on whether they have right angles. Students explore lines of symmetry in objects like a maple leaf or a hockey rink. For three-dimensional figures, they name and describe prisms and pyramids, using building blocks to construct models. Students also describe and create patterns using shapes, rotations, and reflections, like designing a pattern with provincial flags.
