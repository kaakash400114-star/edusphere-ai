# Canadian Curriculum Grade 8 Mathematics

## Number Systems and Operations
Students extend their understanding of numbers to include all rational numbers. They learn to perform the four operations—addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division— fluently with positive and negative fractions and decimals. This includes understanding the rules for multiplying and dividing integers (same signs result in a positive, different signs in a negative) and applying the order of operations (BEDMAS: Brackets, Exponents, Division, Multiplication, Addition, Subtraction) in multi-step problems. Students practice solving real-world problems, such as calculating temperature changes in Winnipeg or determining the profit or loss from a hockey card transaction.

## Algebraic Expressions and Equations
Students develop skills in simplifying and evaluating algebraic expressions involving variables. They learn to expand and factor algebraic expressions, including the difference of squares (e.g., x² - 9 = (x + 3)(x - 3)). The focus moves to solving linear equations in one variable, including those with variables on both sides (e.g., 3x + 5 = 2x - 4). Students translate word problems into algebraic equations, like determining the cost of items at a Tim Hortons based on a total bill, and solve for the unknown variable.

## Ratios, Rates, and Proportional Reasoning
Students deepen their understanding of proportional relationships. They solve problems involving ratios, rates, and percentages, applying them to practical Canadian contexts. This includes calculating tax rates (like the GST/HST in different provinces), determining sale prices at a Hudson's Bay store, or mixing paint for a cottage in Nova Scotia. Students convert between metric units, such as converting kilometres to metres for a marathon in Toronto, and solve direct and inverse proportion problems, like calculating the number of loonies needed to make up a specific value.

## Geometry and Measurement
Students explore properties of two-dimensional and three-dimensional shapes. They calculate the area and perimeter of complex composite figures, such as the layout of a hockey rink, and the surface area and volume of prisms, pyramids, and cylinders. Students learn to use formulas like V = πr²h for the volume of a cylindrical sugar maple sap bucket. They also learn about the Pythagorean theorem to find a missing side length in right-angled triangles, which could be applied to the cross-section of the CN Tower's observation decks.

## Data Management and Probability
Students focus on the analysis of data sets. They learn to select appropriate graphs (bar, circle, line, or scatter plots) to represent and interpret data, such as attendance at Blue Jays games or polling results during a federal election. Students calculate and analyze measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) and range. For probability, they determine theoretical probabilities of single events, like the chance of a randomly selected Canadian quarter being a loonie versus a toonie.

## Financial Literacy
Students build essential financial literacy skills. They create and interpret budgets for various scenarios, like planning a school trip to Ottawa. Students learn about simple and compound interest and how they apply to savings accounts or loans from a Canadian bank. They explore concepts like sales tax, discounts, and gratuities, calculating the final cost of a meal at a restaurant in Montreal, including 15% tip and the 15% Quebec sales tax. They also analyze different payment options, such as using a credit card versus paying with cash for a new piece of hockey equipment.
