# Canadian Curriculum Grade 12 Mathematics  

## Advanced Functions
Students analyze polynomial functions using the Remainder Theorem and Factor Theorem, rational functions by identifying vertical and horizontal asymptotes, and trigonometric functions through graphing transformations of sine and cosine. They apply key identities, such as the Pythagorean identity, to solve trigonometric equations and connect angle measures to arc length using the formula s = rθ. Composition of functions, denoted as f(g(x)), is explored using practical examples like modelling population growth. The concept of a limit, written as lim(x→∞) f(x), is used to describe end behaviour and predict long-term growth patterns in real-world Canadian contexts.

## Calculus and Vectors
Students develop differential calculus rules for polynomial, rational, and sinusoidal functions, using derivatives to determine slopes of tangents and rates of change. They apply optimization to minimize material costs in a cylindrical maple syrup can. Integral calculus is introduced for areas under curves, such as distance travelled by a hockey puck. Vector operations model forces and motion, while dot and cross products solve problems involving direction, like calculating wind effects on a flight from Toronto to Vancouver.

## Mathematics of Data Management
Students apply statistical methods to analyze data sets, including measures of central tendency and variability. They use probability rules for dependent events, like the likelihood of a Toronto Maple Leafs victory. Binomial and normal distributions model real-world scenarios, such as survey results on loonie usage. Students apply hypothesis testing to make inferences, using confidence intervals to estimate the average height of students in British Columbia. Data management skills are vital for interpreting economic trends and research findings.

## Geometry and Discrete Mathematics
This unit covers geometric proofs using deductive reasoning, focusing on properties of circles and triangles. Students solve problems involving vectors in 3D space, such as calculating the shortest path between the CN Tower and a Rogers Centre event. Discrete mathematics includes combinatorics, determining the number of ways to arrange provinces in a Canadian flag mosaic. Graph theory is used to model networks, like optimizing delivery routes through the prairies, emphasizing logical problem-solving and spatial reasoning.

## Financial Mathematics
Students apply compound interest formulas to calculate the future value of investments and the present value of loans, such as determining the total interest paid on a $300,000 Alberta mortgage with a 5-year fixed rate. They utilize annuity formulas to solve for periodic payments, like calculating monthly contributions needed to save $50,000 for a post-secondary education fund over 15 years. Bond valuation concepts involve calculating a bond's price based on its coupon rate, yield to maturity, and time to maturity. Amortization schedules are constructed to detail the principal and interest components of each loan payment, demonstrating how equity builds over time. Finally, students assess investment risk by comparing the potential returns and volatility of diverse assets like GICs, stocks, and bonds to build a balanced portfolio.

## Mathematical Modelling
Students create and refine mathematical models to represent real-world situations. They use functions to describe population growth in Canadian cities or the trajectory of a puck in a hockey game. Linear programming optimizes resource allocation, like scheduling staff for a Tim Hortons franchise. Modelling involves validating results against data, ensuring predictions align with observed phenomena, such as maple syrup production yields. This unit emphasizes critical thinking and the application of mathematics to solve complex, contextual problems.
