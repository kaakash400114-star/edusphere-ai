# British Year 7 Mathematics - National Curriculum/GCSE

## Key Stage 3: number operations

In Key Stage 3, you will learn how to add and subtract numbers with up to four digits, including using formal written methods like column addition and subtraction to find the correct answer. You will also practise multiplying and dividing, including using times tables and written methods such as short multiplication and bus stop division to solve problems involving larger numbers. It is important to use inverse operations, like checking addition with subtraction, to make sure your answers are correct. For example, if 24 + 36 = 60, you can check by doing 60 – 24 = 36 to confirm your result.

## Fractions-decimals-percentages

In Year 7, you learn how to change fractions into decimals and percentages, and the other way around. To change a fraction to a decimal, you divide the top number by the bottom number, like turning 1/4 into 0.25. A percentage is just a fraction out of 100, so 1/4 is the same as 25 per cent. You also practise ordering fractions, decimals and percentages to see which is biggest, for example, putting 1/2, 0.4 and 30% in order. This helps you see how they are all different ways to show the same amount.

## Ratio-proportion

In ratio-proportion, you learn to compare amounts using ‘for every’ and to share things fairly. You can find equivalent ratios, like 2:3 being the same as 4:6, and simplify them to their smallest form. For proportion, you solve problems where one amount changes in the same way, such as working out how many sweets 6 children get if 3 children get 9 sweets. You also use the unitary method to find one part first, like calculating the cost of 5 pencils if 2 pencils cost £1.10. These skills help you with everyday problems like recipes or shopping.

## Algebra: expressions-equations-sequences

In Year 7, you will learn how to write and understand algebraic expressions using letters like *a* or *x* to stand for numbers, for example, *3a + 5* means 3 times *a* plus 5. You will solve simple one-step equations, such as *x + 4 = 10*, by finding the value of the letter that makes the sentence true, in this case, *x = 6*. You will also explore number patterns and sequences, spotting rules like adding 2 each time to get from 3 to 5 to 7, and writing these rules as expressions. By the end, you will be able to collect like terms in expressions, so *2x + 3x* becomes *5x*, making maths simpler and more organised.

## Angles-polygons

In Year 7, you learn how to find missing angles in triangles and quadrilaterals. You discover that the angles inside a triangle always add up to 180 degrees, so if two angles are 70° and 60°, the last one must be 50°. For four-sided shapes like squares and rectangles, all four corners add up to 360 degrees, and each right angle is exactly 90°. You also explore regular polygons, where all sides and angles are equal, like a pentagon with five identical corners. By knowing these rules, you can solve puzzles to measure any unknown angle in a shape.

## Probability-intro

In Year 7, you learn to understand what probability means, like how likely something is to happen. You discover that all possible outcomes are called the sample space. You also learn to identify events and describe how likely they are using words like certain, likely, unlikely, and impossible. For example, you might say it is impossible for the sun not to rise, but it is unlikely to snow in July. You start to use fractions to show probability, like a 1 in 2 chance is the same as 1/2.

## Statistics-averages

In Year 7, you will learn how to find the average, called the mean, by adding up all the numbers in a group and then dividing by how many numbers there are. For example, to find the mean height of four friends who are 140cm, 142cm, 145cm, and 143cm tall, you add them together (570cm) and divide by 4 to get 142.5cm. You will also learn about the median, which is the middle number when you line all the numbers up in order, and the mode, which is the number that appears most often. These different types of average help you understand information and data in maths.

## Area-volume

In Year 7, you will learn how to find the area of shapes like rectangles and triangles by counting squares or using simple formulas. For a rectangle, you multiply its length by its width, and for a triangle, you multiply its base by its height and then divide by 2. You will also explore volume by counting cubes in cuboids, learning that volume is found by multiplying length, width, and height together. These skills help you measure the space inside and around objects, like working out how much carpet a room needs or how much sand a box can hold. You will use rulers and squared paper to practise these ideas with real-world examples.

## Coordinates

In Year 7, you will learn how to describe a position on a grid using pairs of numbers called coordinates. The first number in the pair tells you how many steps to take to the right from the middle, which is called the origin, and the second number tells you how many steps to go up. For example, the coordinate (3, 2) means you move 3 squares to the right and 2 squares up to find the point. You will also learn to read coordinates from a grid to find where a picture or shape is placed. These numbers are always written in a specific order, like (x, y), to help everyone find the same spot on the graph.

## Transformations

In Year 7, you will learn how to move shapes around on a grid using three types of transformations. For translation, you slide a shape left, right, up, or down, remembering to keep it the same size and orientation; for example, you might move a square three places to the right. When reflecting, you create a mirror image of a shape across a line, such as a vertical line of symmetry where each point matches the other side. Finally, you will practise rotating a shape by turning it around a point, like a corner, by a quarter turn or a half turn, keeping the size the same.
