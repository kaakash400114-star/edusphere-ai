# British Year 11 Mathematics - National Curriculum/GCSE

## GCSE Higher: algebra fractions-functions

In this topic, you will learn how to simplify algebraic fractions by factorising the numerators and denominators to cancel out common terms, such as simplifying \(\frac{2x+4}{x^2-4}\) to \(\frac{2}{x-2}\). You will also explore how to add and subtract algebraic fractions by finding a common denominator, like calculating \(\frac{3}{x} + \frac{1}{2x}\) which becomes \(\frac{7}{2x}\). Furthermore, you will practice solving equations involving algebraic fractions, ensuring you multiply through by the lowest common denominator to eliminate the fractions first. Finally, you will study how to manipulate and simplify more complex fractions, including those with quadratic expressions, to build confidence in algebraic reasoning.

## Quadratic-formula-completing-square

In this topic, you learn how to solve quadratic equations by two methods: completing the square and using the quadratic formula. Completing the square involves rewriting a quadratic expression like x² + 6x + 5 into a perfect square form, (x + 3)² - 4, to solve for x. Alternatively, you use the formula x = [-b ± √(b² - 4ac)] / 2a for any equation in the form ax² + bx + c = 0, such as solving 2x² - 4x - 6 = 0 by finding the values of a, b, and c first. Both methods help you find the roots, or solutions, of quadratic equations when factoring isn’t possible.

## Trigonometry: graphs-identities

In this GCSE topic, you will explore how to draw and interpret graphs of sine, cosine, and tangent functions, understanding their key features like amplitude and periodicity. You will learn and apply fundamental trigonometric identities, such as sin²θ + cos²θ = 1, to simplify expressions and solve equations, often using the acronym SOHCAHTOA for right-angled triangles. For example, you might use the identity to prove that (1 - cosθ)(1 + cosθ) is equal to sin²θ. You will also investigate how transformations, such as stretches and reflections, affect these trigonometric graphs. Finally, you will use these skills to model and solve real-world problems involving waves and repeated patterns.

## Circle theorems-mastered

In this topic, you learn the rules that help you find angles in circles, such as the fact that angles in the same segment are equal and that the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference. You also discover that the angle in a semicircle is always a right angle (90 degrees). Another key rule is that opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180 degrees. These theorems let you solve problems involving circles and triangles by using angle properties without needing to measure. For example, you might use the fact that a tangent is at right angles to the radius at the point of contact to find a missing angle.

## Probability-advanced-venn-trees

In this topic, you learn how to use Venn diagrams and tree diagrams to work out probabilities, especially for more complicated events. A Venn diagram uses overlapping circles to show which outcomes are in different events, helping you find probabilities like P(A or B) by adding them and then taking away the overlap to avoid double-counting. Tree diagrams are brilliant for listing all possible outcomes of a two-step experiment, like flipping a coin and then picking a card, and you multiply the probabilities along each branch to find the chance of a specific path. You also learn about the Addition Rule and the Multiplication Rule to make sure your probability calculations are always correct.

## Statistics-histogram-cumulative

In this topic, you will learn to construct and interpret histograms to display continuous data that is grouped into unequal class intervals, understanding that the area of each bar represents the frequency. You will calculate the frequency density using the formula frequency density = frequency / class width to ensure the histogram is accurate. Additionally, you will create and use cumulative frequency tables and curves to estimate values like the median and quartiles, helping you analyse data spread. For example, you might use a histogram to show the heights of students and a cumulative frequency curve to find the height of the median student.

## Area-volume-mixtures

In Year 11, you will learn how to calculate the area and volume of more complex 2D and 3D shapes. You will use formulas for the area of trapezoids and the volume of pyramids, cones, and spheres. A key skill is working with scale factors, for example, how changing the length of a shape affects its area and volume. You will also solve problems involving compound measurements, like finding the density of an object by dividing its mass by its volume. Finally, you will tackle mixture problems, such as working out the new concentration when two liquids with different strengths are combined.

## Vectors-geometry

In vectors-geometry, you learn to describe movements using numbers and directions, like writing 3i + 2j to mean three steps right and two steps up. You practise adding and subtracting vectors by combining their steps, and you also learn how to multiply a vector by a number, which makes it longer or shorter. Vectors can show the position of points from a starting point, and you use them to solve problems involving lines and shapes, such as proving if two lines are parallel. For example, if one line has a vector 2i + 4j and another has 5i + 10j, you can see they are parallel because one is a scaled-up version of the other.

## Proof-argument

In Class 11, you learn to use formal proof-arguments to show that mathematical statements are always true. This means writing clear, logical steps to prove things like angle properties in shapes or rules for number sequences. For example, you might prove that the sum of the angles in any triangle is always 180 degrees by using facts about angles on a straight line and parallel lines. You also practice writing proofs about even and odd numbers, showing that adding two odd numbers always gives an even number. The key is to use known facts and a step-by-step approach to convince someone that your answer is definitely correct.

## Iteration-functions-inverse

In the topic of iteration, functions and inverse, you will learn how to use iterative methods to find approximate solutions to equations, such as using the formula \( x_{n+1} = g(x_n) \) with a starting value like \( x_0 = 2 \) to find a root. You will also study functions, understanding how to map inputs to outputs and represent them using mappings, graphs, or algebraic rules like \( f(x) = 2x + 3 \). Additionally, you will explore inverse functions, which undo the effect of the original function, and learn to find them by swapping \( x \) and \( y \) and rearranging, such as finding \( f^{-1}(x) \) for \( f(x) = 4x - 1 \). Finally, you will practice using iterative techniques to refine your approximations and check whether a function and its inverse are reflections of each other across the line \( y = x \).

## Growth-decay-compound

In this topic, you learn to calculate growth and decay, such as how money grows with interest or how value decreases over time. The key formula is \( P \times (1 + \frac{r}{100})^n \) for growth, where \( P \) is the starting amount, \( r \) is the percentage change, and \( n \) is the number of time periods. For decay, you use \( P \times (1 - \frac{r}{100})^n \). For example, if you save £100 with 5% annual interest for 3 years, it grows to £115.76. You also compare different rates to see which gives the best return or biggest loss.

##  Exam-prep-practice-papers

In this exam-prep-practice-papers topic, you will apply your knowledge to solve problems just like in your real GCSE Maths exam. This includes using formulas for area and volume, such as working out the volume of a cuboid using length × width × height. You will also practise interpreting charts, like finding the median from a list of numbers or calculating probabilities from a spinner. The main goal is to get better at showing all your working out and answering questions in a clear, step-by-step way to earn full marks.
