# British Year 5 Science - National Curriculum/GCSE

## Living things: life cycles (mammals-insects-amphibians)

Living things grow and change in special patterns called life cycles, just like a caterpillar turning into a butterfly. Mammals, such as humans or cats, are born live and grow bigger until they are adults ready to have babies. Insects, like ladybirds, start as eggs, become caterpillars that eat a lot, then wrap themselves in a cocoon before emerging as a flying insect. Amphibians, such as frogs, begin as eggs in water, grow into tadpoles with tails and then legs, and finally become adult frogs that can live on land. All these life cycles help living things make more of their own kind.

## Animals: puberty changes

During puberty, animals like dogs and cats grow bigger and their bodies change. Male animals may start to mark their territory by spraying, while female animals begin having seasons, called seasons, where they can have babies. These changes happen because special chemicals, called hormones, tell their bodies when to grow and change. For example, a young puppy will lose its fluffy baby fur and grow thicker adult fur. These changes are normal and help animals become grown-ups and have their own babies.

## Properties-changes of materials

Materials can be grouped by what they are like, such as stretchy or hard. We see changes when things get hotter, colder, or mixed together, like ice melting into water. Some changes are forever, like baking bread, but others can go back, like freezing water into ice. Different materials have different jobs, like wood for strong tables or plastic for bottles. We learn to sort materials by what they can do and how they change when we play with them in class.

## Earth and space: movement-moon

The Moon moves around the Earth, and we see it change shape over about four weeks. The Moon looks different because different parts are lit by the Sun as it travels. We see a full Moon when the whole side facing us is bright, and a new Moon when we see the dark side. Sometimes the Moon passes through Earth’s shadow, making a lunar eclipse. The Moon’s pull causes the sea to go up and down, making the tides on our beaches.

## Forces: gravity-friction-air resistance-levers

Gravity is a force that pulls things down towards the ground, like when an apple falls from a tree. Friction is a force that stops things from sliding, such as how our shoes grip the floor so we don’t fall over. Air resistance is a push from the air that slows down moving things, like a parachute gently floating down. A lever is a simple machine with a pivot that helps us lift heavy things, like using a seesaw to lift a friend. These forces are all around us and help us understand how things move and stay still.
