# British Year 4 Science - National Curriculum/GCSE

## Living things: classification keys

Children in Year 4 learn to use simple classification keys to sort living things into groups. These keys ask yes-or-no questions about features like the number of legs, wings, or whether an animal has a backbone, helping to identify it. For example, a key might ask, “Does it have six legs?” and if the answer is “yes,” it leads to questions about colour or habitat to find the insect. By following these steps, they can separate creatures like spiders from beetles or fish from frogs. This method teaches them how scientists organise the huge variety of life on Earth.

## Animals: digestion-teeth-food chains

Animals use their teeth to bite and chew food, breaking it into smaller, softer pieces so their bodies can use it for energy and growth. Different animals have different types of teeth: humans have incisors for biting, canines for tearing, and molars for grinding, while lions have sharp teeth for tearing meat and cows have flat teeth for grinding grass. Food chains show how living things get energy; they start with plants (which make their own food) and go to plant-eaters (herbivores), then to meat-eaters (carnivores), and finally to things that eat dead animals (decomposers), like worms or fungi. For example, a food chain could be: grass → rabbit → fox.

## States of matter: solids-liquids-gases

Solids, liquids, and gases are the three states of matter. Solids keep their shape because their particles are packed tightly together, like a wooden block. Liquids can flow because their particles are close but can move around, like water in a cup. Gases have lots of space between their particles, which move freely and spread out, like the air you blow from your mouth. We can see these changes when ice melts into water or when water turns into steam when it gets hot.

## Sound: vibrations-pitch-volume

Sound is made when things vibrate, like a guitar string wiggling fast to make a noise. Pitch tells us if a sound is high or low; a big drum vibrates slowly to make a low-pitched sound, but a tiny bell vibrates quickly for a high-pitched sound. Volume is how loud or soft a sound is, and we make it louder by hitting things harder, like bashing a drum to make a big boom. Sounds need to travel through things like air or water to reach our ears, and if there’s no air, like in space, we can’t hear anything at all.

## Electricity: circuits-conductors

Electricity travels around a special path called a circuit to make things work like a lightbulb glow. A circuit needs a power source, such as a battery, to push the electricity along. Materials that let electricity pass through them easily are called conductors, like metal wires and coins. Materials that stop electricity are called insulators, such as plastic, rubber, and wood. For a circuit to work, it must be a complete loop with no gaps.
