# British Year 1 Science - National Curriculum/GCSE

## Working scientifically: observing

Pupils learn to use their senses to make careful observations about objects and events around them. They explore how to describe what they see, hear, smell, feel, and taste using simple words. For example, they might observe and describe the colour, shape, size, and texture of different leaves or fruits. Children also learn to record their observations through drawings or simple labels. By comparing observations, they begin to notice similarities and differences between items. This skill helps them gather information and ask simple questions about the world.

## Plants: basic structure

Plants have roots that grow underground to take in water and nutrients, and a stem that supports the plant and carries these things to the leaves. Leaves make food for the plant using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide in a process called photosynthesis. Flowers contain parts like petals that help attract insects to help the plant make seeds, and some plants also have fruits that protect the seeds as they grow. Simple examples of these parts can be seen in a common daisy or a sunflower, where the roots, stem, leaves, and flower are all clearly visible.

## Animals including humans

In Year 1, children learn to identify and name the basic parts of the human body, such as the head, arms, legs, and eyes, and they compare them to the parts of other animals. They understand that animals, including humans, need food, water, and air to stay alive and grow, and that baby animals grow into adults. Pupils explore the differences between animals, like grouping them by whether they have fur, feathers, or scales, and they recognise that humans are animals with unique features. They also learn about the senses, such as sight, hearing, and touch, and how animals use them to interact with their environment.

## Everyday materials

Pupils learn to identify and name common materials such as wood, plastic, metal, glass, and fabric, and they explore their simple uses like chairs, cups, and clothes. They understand that materials have properties that make them suitable or unsuitable for jobs, for example, metal is strong for a bridge but plastic might be better for a bottle because it is lighter. Children also investigate how the shape of some materials can be changed by squashing, bending, or stretching, like modelling clay, while others like a wooden block stay the same. They begin to group materials into categories based on these observable features, such as things that are hard or soft.

## Seasonal changes

Children learn that weather changes across the seasons, with winter being cold and summer being warm. They observe that trees lose their leaves in autumn and grow new ones in spring, and that the amount of daylight we get is longer in summer and shorter in winter. Pupils also learn about seasonal clothing, like wearing warm coats in winter and t-shirts in summer, and how animals, such as hedgehogs, might sleep through winter or build nests for their babies in spring. These changes happen in a regular cycle each year as the Earth moves around the Sun.
