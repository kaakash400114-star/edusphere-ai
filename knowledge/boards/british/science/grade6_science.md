# British Year 6 Science - National Curriculum/GCSE

## Living things: classification-microorganisms

Microorganisms are tiny living things that are so small we need a microscope to see them. They are classified into three main groups: bacteria, which are single-celled and can be good or bad, like the ones that make you sick; fungi, which can grow on bread or mushrooms and help things rot; and viruses, which are even smaller and cause colds and flu. We must be careful with some microorganisms because they can spread germs and make us ill, but others are very helpful and are used to make food like yogurt.

## Animals: circulation-diet-exercise-drugs

Animals need blood to carry food and oxygen around their bodies, like a delivery van taking supplies to different parts of the body. They eat different foods to get energy, such as plants for rabbits or meat for lions, and exercise keeps their hearts strong, like running makes a dog fit. Some drugs can help animals feel better when they are sick, like medicine from the vet, but other drugs are bad for them and can make them very poorly. Animals get their energy from the food they eat, and their blood takes this energy to their muscles so they can play and move.

## Light: how we see-evolution-adaptation

We see light from things like the Sun or a lamp, which travels in straight lines to our eyes. When light hits an object, it can pass through it (like a window), bounce off (like a mirror), or get blocked (like a wall). Our eyes have special parts like the pupil that lets light in, and it sends a message to our brain to make a picture. Over a very long time, animals changed to see better, like cats with big eyes to see in the dark, or fish with eyes on the sides to spot danger. This helps them find food and stay safe.

## Electricity: voltage-brightness

In this topic, we learn how the voltage in a circuit makes light bulbs shine brighter or dimmer. Voltage is like the push of electricity from the battery, and more voltage means a brighter bulb. We find out that bulbs in a series circuit share the voltage, so they are not as bright as in a simple circuit. For example, if you add more bulbs, each one gets less voltage and shines less brightly. This helps us understand why circuits work the way they do and how to make lights shine as bright as we want.

## Evolution and inheritance

In this topic, you learn about how living things change over very long times to get better at surviving in their homes. We call this ‘evolution’, and it happens because creatures with helpful traits, like a giraffe’s long neck for reaching leaves, are more likely to have babies and pass those traits on. You also explore ‘inheritance’, which is how parents give special features, such as eye colour or hair type, to their young through tiny things called ‘genes’. Finally, you understand why some animals look similar but are different, like dogs and wolves sharing an old ancestor.
