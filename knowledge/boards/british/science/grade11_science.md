# British Year 11 Science - National Curriculum/GCSE

## Biology: inheritance-variation-evolution-ecology

You learn how inherited characteristics, such as eye colour or blood type, are passed from parents to offspring through genes found on chromosomes. Variation within a species, like different fur patterns in rabbits, arises from genetic differences and environmental influences. This variation is essential for evolution, as individuals with advantageous traits are more likely to survive and reproduce in a process called natural selection. Over time, these successful adaptations can lead to the formation of new species. You also study how living things interact with each other and their environment in habitats and ecosystems, such as a woodland or a pond.

## Chemistry: rates-equilibrium-organic-analysis

In GCSE Chemistry, you learn about how fast chemical reactions happen and what affects their speed, like temperature and concentration. You also discover reversible reactions and dynamic equilibrium, where the forward and backward reactions occur at the same rate, such as the haber process for making ammonia. The topic introduces organic chemistry, including the names and structures of different hydrocarbons, like alkanes and alkenes, and how to test for functional groups using simple chemical tests. Finally, you explore analytical techniques, like chromatography to separate mixtures and identify unknown substances.

## Physics: forces-motion-revised-magnetism-EM

In this Physics topic, you learn how forces, like gravity and friction, can make objects start moving, stop moving, or change direction. You discover how to calculate speed using the formula: speed = distance divided by time, and understand that unbalanced forces cause acceleration. You explore magnetic fields around permanent magnets and how opposite poles attract while like poles repel. Finally, you study how a changing magnetic field can create an electric current, which is the basic principle used in generators to produce electricity.

## Required-practicals: all-collated

In this topic, you learn and practise a set of required experiments for GCSE Science, which helps you develop key scientific skills like observation, measurement, and analysis. You will investigate different variables, such as how changing the concentration of acid affects the rate of reaction, and use equipment like a stopwatch or thermometer to collect accurate data. You will also learn to calculate results, for example, finding the speed of a toy car by dividing the distance it travels by the time taken. Finally, you practise writing clear method and conclusion sections for your practical work, ensuring your results are reliable and you can explain what you found.

## Homeostasis: nervous-endocrine

Homeostasis is the maintenance of stable internal body conditions, like temperature and blood sugar, and is controlled by the nervous and endocrine systems. The nervous system sends rapid electrical impulses to create quick responses, such as pulling your hand away from a hot stove, while the endocrine system releases slower-acting chemical hormones into the blood, like insulin, to control blood sugar levels after a meal. The brain acts as the central control centre, receiving information via sensory receptors and coordinating the correct response to maintain balance. These two systems work together to keep your body functioning correctly despite external environmental changes.

## Chemical-analysis-identification

In this topic, you learn how to identify unknown chemicals using tests for specific ions and gases, which tell you what a substance might be. For example, adding silver nitrate solution produces different coloured precipitates to identify chloride, bromide, or iodide ions. You also discover how to test for positive ions, such as adding sodium hydroxide solution to see if you get a coloured precipitate for copper or iron. Flame tests are another method, where you heat a compound and look for characteristic colours like lilac for potassium or brick red for calcium. These tests follow precise rules to help you safely figure out the identity of a substance.

## Forces-motion-mastered

In GCSE Science, you learn how forces cause motion, from pushing a shopping trolley to a planet orbiting the Sun. Newton’s First Law explains that objects stay still or move in a straight line unless a force, like friction, acts on them. His Second Law tells us that force equals mass times acceleration (F = ma), so a bigger force or lighter object speeds up faster. You also explore balanced forces, where objects stay at constant speed, like a car cruise control. Finally, you study gravity and air resistance, like how a parachute slows a skydiver by increasing drag.

## Waves-light-mastered

In this topic, you will learn how light behaves as a transverse wave and understand key terms like amplitude, wavelength, and frequency. You will explore the electromagnetic spectrum, discovering that visible light is just one type of wave and that others, like radio waves and X-rays, have different wavelengths and uses. A key rule is that the speed of light is constant in a vacuum, and you will use the formula wave speed = frequency × wavelength to calculate its properties. You will also investigate how light can be reflected, refracted, and absorbed, explaining why objects appear certain colours or why a pencil looks bent in water.

## Organic-chemistry-reactions

In this topic, you learn how to name organic compounds using rules from the International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC), such as identifying the longest carbon chain and any functional groups like alcohols (-OH) or carboxylic acids (-COOH). You explore different structures, including straight-chain, branched-chain, and ring-based molecules like cyclohexane. Key reactions include combustion, where substances like methane burn to produce carbon dioxide and water, and substitution reactions, such as bromine reacting with methane to create bromomethane. You also study addition reactions, like when ethene reacts with bromine to form 1,2-dibromoethane.

## Space-revision

In this topic, you will learn about the Earth-Moon-Sun system and explain why we experience day and night, as well as the changing seasons. You will discover how gravity keeps planets in orbit around the Sun and satellites in orbit around Earth, and you can use the equation for weight (weight = mass × gravitational field strength) to calculate the weight of an object on different planets. For example, your mass stays the same on Earth and the Moon, but your weight would be much less on the Moon because its gravity is weaker. Finally, you will explore the life cycle of a star, from a nebula to a main sequence star and, in some cases, to a red giant, white dwarf, and supernova.
