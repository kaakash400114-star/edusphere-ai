# British Year 7 Science - National Curriculum/GCSE

## Cells-organisation

In Year 7, you will learn about how living things are made up of tiny building blocks called cells. You will find out that all plants and animals are made of cells and that different cells have different jobs, like muscle cells helping you move or leaf cells helping a plant make food. You will explore the basic parts of a plant cell, such as the cell wall, which gives it its shape, and the nucleus, which acts like the cell's control centre. By looking at diagrams and models, you can see how cells are organised into tissues, like skin, and tissues are organised into organs, like your heart.

## Particles-model states

In Science, you will learn about tiny particles and how they behave in solids, liquids, and gases. In solids, particles are packed tightly in a fixed shape and cannot move around, like in a wooden block. In liquids, particles are close but can flow past each other, taking the shape of their container, like in a glass of water. In gases, particles are far apart and move quickly, filling any space, like the air in a balloon. You will explore how adding or removing heat energy can change how particles move and change the state of a material.

## Energy-transfers

Energy is the ability to make things happen or make them work. We learn that energy can be stored, like in a battery or a stretched rubber band, and it can be moved from one place to another. When energy moves, we call this an energy transfer, like when a ball rolls down a hill and its stored energy turns into movement energy. Another example is when we eat food, our bodies use the energy to help us run and jump. We also learn that when energy is transferred, some of it can be changed into heat, like when you rub your hands together to warm them up.

## Forces-effects

Forces are pushes or pulls that can make things start moving, stop moving, change direction, or change shape. A force can also squash or stretch things, like when you squeeze a sponge or stretch a rubber band. We measure forces in units called newtons, and a bigger force makes a bigger change. Gravity is a force that pulls everything down towards the Earth, so when you drop a ball, gravity pulls it to the ground. Friction is a force that stops things from sliding easily, like when you rub your hands together and they feel warm.

## Sound-light waves

Sound and light are both types of energy that travel as waves. Sound waves need to travel through a material, like air or water, and they make our ears vibrate so we can hear things, such as a bell ringing. Light waves, however, can travel through empty space, like from the Sun to the Earth, and they help us see when they bounce off objects into our eyes. Big objects make long sound waves with low sounds, like a drum, while small objects make short waves with high sounds, like a whistle. Light can also be reflected off mirrors or change direction when it moves from air into water, making a straw in a glass look bent.

## Reproduction

In Science, we learn about how living things make babies, which is called reproduction. Animals have babies in different ways, like when a dog gives birth to puppies or a hen lays eggs that hatch into chicks. Plants also reproduce, for example, when an apple tree grows from a seed or when a strawberry plant sends out runners to make new plants. People reproduce too, but babies grow inside their mummies' tummies for nine months before they are born. It is how all living things make sure their kind continues.

## Acids-alkalis

Acids are sour substances like lemon juice and vinegar, while alkalis feel soapy and are found in toothpaste. We use indicators like litmus paper to test if something is an acid or alkali, as acids turn it red and alkalis turn it blue. Some acids are very strong, like battery acid, and alkalis like oven cleaner can be dangerous, so we must always be careful with them. Water is a special liquid that is neither an acid nor an alkali; it is called neutral. Acids and alkalis can cancel each other out in a reaction to make water and a salt.

## Electricity-magnetism intro

In this topic, you will learn about electricity and how it can make magnets work. You will discover that magnets have two poles, north and south, and that opposite poles attract while like poles repel, like when two magnets snap together or push apart. You will also find out how an electric current can make a piece of iron into a temporary electromagnet, such as the kind inside a doorbell. Simple circuits, including the flow of electricity through a battery, wires, and a bulb, will help you understand how to light up a torch. Finally, you will learn that some materials conduct electricity easily, like copper wires, while others, like plastic, do not.

## Earth-chemistry: rocks-atmosphere

In Year 7 Science, you will learn about Earth’s chemistry, including different types of rocks like granite, chalk, and slate, and how they are formed. You will explore the layers of our atmosphere, including the air we breathe, and understand how gases like oxygen and nitrogen keep us safe. The topic also covers how rocks change over time through processes like weathering, where wind and rain break them down. You will discover simple chemical reactions, such as when limestone reacts with acid to produce carbon dioxide bubbles, and how this relates to caves forming underground. Finally, you will learn about the rock cycle, showing how rocks can be melted, cooled, and weathered to form new types of rocks.

## Ecosystems-interdependence

In ecosystems, living things depend on each other and their surroundings to survive. Plants make their own food using sunlight, and animals eat plants or other animals to get energy. All creatures need things like water, air, and shelter from the non-living parts of their habitat. If one part of an ecosystem changes, like if a food source disappears, it can affect other living things too. For example, if bees disappear, flowers might not get pollinated and plants could struggle to grow.
