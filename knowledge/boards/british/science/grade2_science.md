# British (GCSE / A-Level) Grade 2 Science
## Seasons and Weather
Children learn about the four seasons: spring, summer, autumn, and winter. They learn to name typical weather for each season, such as sunny and warm in summer, and cold and frosty in winter. They explore how day length changes, with longer days in summer and shorter days in winter. They learn to describe weather using simple vocabulary like 'windy', 'rainy', 'sunny', and 'cloudy'. They observe the weather daily and record it using simple symbols, like a sun for sunny or a cloud for cloudy. They also learn that the weather can change quickly and sometimes unexpectedly.
## Plants and Growth
Children learn that plants grow from seeds. They identify common plants like daisies, buttercups, and sunflowers. They learn about the basic parts of a plant, such as the roots, stem, leaves, flower, and seeds. They understand that plants need water, light, and soil to grow. They conduct simple experiments, such as planting a seed in a pot and watering it to observe germination and growth. They learn that different plants grow at different speeds, and some plants have flowers while others do not. They also learn about the importance of bees and insects for helping plants to grow.
## Materials and Their Properties
Children explore different materials, such as wood, metal, plastic, glass, rock, and fabric. They learn to name and describe these materials using simple adjectives like hard, soft, shiny, dull, rough, smooth, waterproof, and absorbent. They compare materials by their properties, for example, wood floats but metal sinks. They learn that some materials, like wood, can be bent, while others, like glass, break. They use their senses, sight and touch, to investigate the materials. They sort materials into groups based on their properties, for instance, all the shiny things in one group.
## Living Things and Habitats
Children learn to identify common animals like cats, dogs, birds, and insects. They learn about basic animal habitats, such as a garden, a pond, or a woodland. They understand that animals need food, water, and shelter to survive. They learn to group animals based on what they eat: herbivores (e.g., rabbits) eat plants, carnivores (e.g., foxes) eat other animals, and omnivores (e.g., humans) eat both. They observe small creatures in their local environment, such as worms in the soil or insects on plants, and learn to treat them with care. They understand that different animals live in different places.
## Forces and Movement
Children learn about pushes and pulls as types of forces. They explore how forces can make objects start moving, stop moving, change direction, or change shape. They investigate examples of forces in everyday life, like pushing a swing or pulling a toy. They learn about gravity, the force that pulls things towards the Earth, making things fall downwards. They explore floating and sinking, understanding that some objects float on water while others sink. They use simple equipment, like toy cars and ramps, to investigate how the height of a ramp affects how far a car will travel.
## Senses and the Human Body
Children learn about the five senses: sight, hearing, smell, taste, and touch. They identify which body part is used for each sense: eyes for sight, ears for hearing, nose for smell, tongue for taste, and skin for touch. They conduct simple investigations using their senses, such as identifying objects by touch in a feely box or matching smells. They learn about the basic parts of the human body, such as the head, arms, legs, and torso. They understand the importance of looking after their bodies, for example, washing hands to stay clean and eating healthy food to stay strong. They learn to name simple feelings like happy, sad, and angry.
