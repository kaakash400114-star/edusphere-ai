# British Year 2 Science - National Curriculum/GCSE

## Living things and habitats

Living things need food, water, and air to grow and stay healthy. They live in special places called habitats, such as ponds, forests, or gardens, where all their needs can be met. Plants and animals are living things; plants make their own food using sunlight, while animals get food by eating plants or other animals. Different animals have different features, like sharp teeth for eating meat or wings for flying, which help them survive in their habitats. All living things change as they grow, for example, a seed grows into a big tree or a caterpillar turns into a butterfly.

## Plants: growth-need

Plants grow tall and strong when they have water, sunlight, air, and the right soil. They use their roots to drink water from the ground and their leaves to catch sunlight like a solar panel. Without enough water, a plant wilts and turns brown, and without sunlight, it stays pale and weak. They also need air to make their own food, which helps them grow big and healthy. Good soil gives them all the food they need to stay strong.

## Animals including humans: offspring-survival

Young children learn that animals, including humans, have babies called offspring. Different animals have different ways to help their babies stay safe, such as a mother bird keeping eggs warm in a nest or a kangaroo carrying its joey in a pouch. Human babies need special care, like being fed milk and kept warm, to grow strong and healthy. Offspring look a bit like their parents but may not be identical, like kittens looking like their cat mother. All offspring need food, water, and protection to survive and grow into adults.

## Uses of everyday materials

We explore how different materials are used in everyday objects, like wood for chairs or metal for cutlery. We learn to identify materials by their properties, such as strong, hard, or bendy, and group them into categories like natural (wood, stone) or man-made (plastic, glass). Children discover that materials can change their shape by squashing, bending, or twisting, and that some materials, like water, can flow or melt. We also consider why certain materials are chosen for specific jobs, such as why glass is used for windows (it is transparent) and why we use rubber for tyres (it is bouncy and waterproof).
