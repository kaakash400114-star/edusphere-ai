# British Year 3 Science - National Curriculum/GCSE

## Plants: parts-functions-life cycle

Plants have special parts like roots, which hold them in the ground and drink water, and leaves, which catch sunlight to make the plant’s food. Flowers help plants make seeds, and seeds grow into new plants when they land in soil with water and air. The life cycle of a plant starts as a seed, then it grows into a seedling, and finally into a big plant that can make more seeds. Some plants, like sunflowers, grow tall and have big yellow flowers, while others, like beans, grow on stems with green leaves. All plants need sunlight, water, and air to stay healthy and grow strong.

## Animals: nutrition-skeletons

Animals need food to grow and stay healthy, which gives them energy for moving and living. Different animals eat different foods, like plants or other animals, to get the nutrients they need. Skeletons are the hard frames inside animals that hold them up and protect soft parts like the brain and heart. Some animals have skeletons inside their bodies, like humans and dogs, while others have hard outer shells, like snails and crabs. Skeletons help animals move by allowing their muscles to pull on the bones to walk, run, or jump.

## Rocks-fossils-soils

Children learn to identify and sort different types of rocks, such as granite and sandstone, by looking at their colours and textures. They discover how soil is made from broken-down rock and dead plants, and they can find worms and other living things in healthy soil. Fossils are like pictures of plants or animals that were trapped in rock long ago, showing us what life was like millions of years in the past. Pupils explore how rocks change over time through processes like weathering, where wind and rain slowly break them into smaller pieces. By collecting and comparing soil samples, they can see how dark, rich soil helps plants grow better than sandy or clay soil.

## Light: shadows-reflection

Light travels in straight lines until it hits something. When light is blocked, it creates a shadow that can change size depending on how close the object is to the light source. A smooth, shiny surface, like a mirror, reflects light back, allowing us to see images. The size of a shadow gets bigger when the object is closer to the light and smaller when it is further away. We use our understanding of light and shadows to explain why we see reflections and how shadows are made every day.

## Forces-magnets

Magnets are objects that can push or pull other things without touching them. They have two special ends called poles, a north pole and a south pole. Poles that are the same will push away from each other, but poles that are different will pull together. This pull is called a magnetic force. Magnets can attract things made from iron, like paper clips, and some magnets are even strong enough to lift a car.
