# British Year 7 English - National Curriculum/GCSE

## Reading: fiction-non-fiction analysis

In Year 7, you learn to spot the differences between fiction stories and non-fiction information books. For fiction, you explore characters, settings, and plots, like describing Harry’s bravery or the magical setting of Hogwarts. With non-fiction, you find facts, opinions, and features such as headings and captions in a book about dinosaurs. You practise comparing a story and a report on the same topic, noticing one makes up a tale while the other gives real details. This helps you understand how different types of writing work.

## Writing: imaginative-descriptive

In Grade 7, you learn to write imaginative and descriptive stories using exciting words that help your reader picture the scene, like ‘sparkling’ instead of ‘shining’. You will be taught how to build interesting sentences by using describing words (adjectives) and telling words (verbs), for example, ‘The big, fluffy dog quickly chased the red ball’. Your teacher will show you how to plan your story with a clear beginning, a middle with lots of details, and an exciting end. You will practice using your senses to describe what things look, sound, smell, feel and taste like, helping your writing come alive for your reader.

## Grammar: sentence structures

In Class 7, you will learn to build different kinds of sentences. A simple sentence has one part that tells you who or what and what they are doing, like “The cat sleeps”. You will also learn to join two ideas with words like ‘and’ or ‘but’ to make a compound sentence, such as “The sun was hot and we went to the beach”. Another important structure is the complex sentence, where one idea starts with a word like ‘when’ or ‘because’, for example “I was happy because I won the game”. Mastering these helps you write clearly and share your ideas with others.

## Vocabulary-spelling development

In Year 7, you will learn new words and how to spell them correctly by understanding common patterns, such as prefixes and suffixes. You will explore words with prefixes like 'un-', 'dis-', and 're-', and learn how to add them to root words to change their meaning, like adding 'un-' to 'happy' to make 'unhappy'. Similarly, you will work with suffixes such as '-ly', '-ful', and '-ness', for example, changing 'care' into 'careful' or 'carefulness'. Your teacher will help you use dictionaries to check meanings and spellings, and you will practise using new words in your own sentences to build your vocabulary and writing skills.

## Speaking-listening: discussion skills

In Year 7, you will learn how to share your ideas clearly in a group and listen carefully to what others say. You must speak one at a time, using words like 'I agree with Ben because...' to be polite. You should also ask questions, such as 'Can you tell me more about that?', to help you understand. During discussions, you will practice taking turns and making sure everyone gets a chance to talk. Your teacher might help you by giving you words to use, like 'First, I think...' to start your idea.

## Poetry-introduction analysis

In Year 7, you will learn to read and understand poetry by looking closely at the words the poet uses. You will explore how poets create pictures with their words, known as imagery, and use patterns of rhyme and rhythm to make their poems sound good. You will also discover how poets use special sounds, like alliteration, to make their writing exciting. Finally, you will learn to share your thoughts about a poem by explaining what you think it is about and how it makes you feel.

## Shakespeare-introduction

In Year 7, you will meet William Shakespeare, the famous writer from a very long time ago. You will learn about plays, which are like stories people act out on a stage, and you will read one of his exciting stories, such as 'A Midsummer Night’s Dream'. You will discover characters and the words they say, understanding that old words can sound a bit funny today. You will also learn about the structure of a play, including scenes and acts, and why we still enjoy his stories today. This helps you build skills for understanding books and acting them out.

## Media-texts awareness

Pupils learn to spot different types of media-texts, like TV adverts, posters and websites, and think about who made them and why. They explore how media uses bright colours, exciting pictures and catchy slogans to grab attention and make people feel happy or want something. For example, a cereal advert might show smiling children and big letters saying “YUMMY!” to persuade you to buy it. Children also practise asking questions about media, such as “Who is this for?” and “What is it trying to make me do?”. By the end of this topic, pupils can better understand media messages and make smart choices when reading or watching.

## Creative-transactional writing

In Year 7, you will learn to write creatively by making up exciting stories and poems using your imagination. You will also learn to write transactional pieces, like friendly letters or simple instructions, to share information clearly with others. For creative writing, you can use a story mountain plan to structure your beginning, middle and end, while transactional writing needs a clear layout with headings and bullet points to be easy to read and understand.

## Research-library skills

In Year 7, you will learn how to find books and information in a library using the Dewey Decimal system to number books by their subject. You will practise using the library catalogue to search for titles by author or keyword, just like typing into a computer. You will discover how to check if a book is reliable by looking at the author’s name and the date it was written. For example, you can find a book about dinosaurs by looking at the 567 number. Finally, you will learn to take simple notes by writing down key facts in your own words to help you with your homework.
