# British Year 11 English - National Curriculum/GCSE

## Reading: exam-extracts-timed

In Year 11, students learn to analyse unseen literary extracts under timed exam conditions for GCSE English Language. You will practise identifying and explaining key methods writers use, such as similes, metaphors, and powerful verbs, to create effects like tension or excitement. For example, you might spot a sentence like "the wind howled like a hungry wolf" and discuss how the simile makes the description more intense. You will also focus on the structure of the text, thinking about how a short paragraph can build suspense or reveal a character’s feelings quickly. This skill helps you write structured answers that directly answer the question using evidence from the text.

## Writing: 40-mark-craft

In the 40-mark-craft writing task, you will learn to build a compelling and cohesive piece by carefully structuring your ideas with a clear introduction, main body, and conclusion. You will develop descriptive techniques, such as using vivid sensory details and figurative language, to bring your writing to life, for example, by describing a storm as 'the sky roared like a furious lion'. Effective paragraphing is essential, with each paragraph focusing on a single main point to maintain the reader’s interest and develop your argument or narrative logically. Finally, you will apply precise grammar, punctuation, and ambitious vocabulary to ensure your writing is accurate and engaging, aiming to meet the GCSE criteria for both technical accuracy and creative flair.

## Grammar-punctuation-perfection

In Class 11, you will master advanced punctuation, such as colons and semicolons, to structure complex sentences and clarify lists or explanations. You will learn to use dashes for emphasis and brackets for adding extra information within a sentence, ensuring clarity in your writing. Grammar lessons focus on perfecting the use of subjunctive mood for hypothetical situations and varying sentence structures to create impact, for example, starting sentences with adverbials. You will also revise and apply rules for apostrophes in plural nouns and contractions, avoiding common errors like "its" versus "it's". This rigorous practice ensures your writing is precise, polished, and ready for GCSE success.

## Vocabulary-sophistication

In Year 11, you will learn to choose more sophisticated vocabulary to make your writing more engaging and precise. This involves using precise nouns and powerful verbs instead of common, everyday words, such as replacing ‘walk’ with ‘amble’ or ‘stride’ to create a specific image. You will also practise using advanced adverbs and adjectives to add detail, and learn how to select subject-specific terminology for formal writing. Finally, you will develop an understanding of connotation, exploring the different feelings and associations words can carry, to ensure your word choice is always deliberate and effective.

## Poetry-anthology: mastered-comparison

In Poetry: mastered-comparison, you will learn how to carefully compare two poems, looking at what they have in common and how they are different. You will explore key areas like structure (such as the number of stanzas or lines in a poem), language (the use of powerful words, similes and metaphors), and themes (the main ideas or messages). For example, you might discuss how one poet uses rhyme to create a happy feeling while another uses no rhyme to show sadness. By analysing these elements, you can write a strong comparison essay, explaining your thoughts with clear evidence from the text to support your points.

## Shakespeare-Macbeth-style: essays

In this unit, you will learn how to write essays on *Macbeth* that explore the play's themes, characters, and dramatic effects. You will practice structuring your arguments with a clear introduction that presents your main point, body paragraphs that analyse specific quotations and stage directions, and a conclusion that summarises your findings. For example, you might argue how Macbeth's ambition leads to his downfall, using lines like "I have no spur / To prick the sides of my intent, but only / Vaulting ambition" (Act 1, Scene 7) to support your ideas. Your writing must show you can explain Shakespeare's language and link his techniques to the play's meanings.

## Viewpoint-writing: mastered

In GCSE English Language, you will learn to write powerful viewpoint pieces that clearly argue your position using P-E-E paragraphs (Point, Evidence, Explanation). For example, you might state your point about school starting times later, use evidence like scientific studies on teenage sleep, and then explain how this improves learning. You will structure your writing with an engaging introduction that presents your stance, several developed arguments that build on each other, and a conclusion that summarises your case and reinforces your main message. You must also use persuasive techniques like rhetorical questions and emotive language to convince your reader of your viewpoint.

## Non-fiction-comparison-mastered

In this topic, you will learn how to compare non-fiction texts by analysing their key features, such as language, purpose, and structure. You will identify and explain similarities and differences between two or more texts, for example, comparing a news article and a charity leaflet about the same event. You will also explore how different presentational devices, like headings and images, are used to influence the reader. Your analysis should include specific details from the texts to support your comparisons, using subject terminology like 'bias' and 'audience'. This skill helps you to understand how writers create meaning and how to form a well-supported critical response.

## Speaking-endorsed

In the Speaking-endorsed component of GCSE English Language, you develop skills to speak clearly and confidently in formal and informal settings. You learn how to structure your ideas effectively using a clear introduction, main points, and conclusion, such as when delivering a persuasive speech. You practice using standard English and purposeful vocabulary to engage your audience, for example, by explaining a viewpoint during a group discussion. You also learn to listen actively to others and respond appropriately, building on their ideas. The focus is on demonstrating clear organisation, appropriate language, and effective communication in a range of spoken contexts.

## Exam-technique-revision-planning

In this topic, you will learn how to plan your revision effectively by creating a clear timetable that breaks down subjects into smaller, manageable chunks. You will practise exam techniques like skimming texts to find key information quickly and carefully reading questions to ensure you understand exactly what is being asked. For example, when answering a ‘how’ question, you must focus on the writer’s methods rather than just summarising the content. You will also learn to structure your answers with an introduction, main points supported by evidence, and a conclusion to achieve the highest marks. Finally, you will develop the skill of checking your work carefully to spot and correct any spelling, punctuation, or grammar mistakes before time runs out.
