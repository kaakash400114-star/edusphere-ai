# British Year 1 English - National Curriculum/GCSE

## Phonics phases: digraphs-trigraphs

In Year 1, children learn to recognise and read consonant digraphs, where two letters make one sound, such as ‘sh’ in ship and ‘ch’ in chip. They also explore vowel digraphs like ‘ai’ in rain and ‘ee’ in feet. Additionally, they are introduced to trigraphs, which are three letters that make one sound, such as ‘igh’ in light and ‘air’ in chair. Through daily phonics lessons, children practise blending these sounds together to read simple words and sentences containing digraphs and trigraphs. This skill helps them become more confident and fluent readers as they tackle new texts.

## Tricky words

Children in Year 1 learn to read and write tricky words, which are words that cannot be easily sounded out using their phonics knowledge. These words do not follow the usual letter-sound patterns, so they must be learned by sight. Examples include common words like "the," "was," and "said." Pupils practise recognising these words in reading and spelling them from memory to support their fluency. Mastering tricky words helps children build confidence and become more independent readers and writers.

## Sentence building

In Year 1, children learn to build simple sentences by saying a clear idea and adding a full stop. They discover that a sentence needs a 'doing word', like 'jump' or 'skip', and a 'who' or 'what', like 'the cat' or 'the ball'. Pupils are taught to join two short ideas with 'and' to make a longer sentence, for example, "The dog barked and the cat ran." They also start to use capital letters for the very first word and names of people, like "Anna plays." Children practice writing sentences to tell stories and share information, making sure each one makes sense and is easy to read.

## Capital letters-full stops

Capital letters start a sentence and for names like ‘Ben’ or ‘Luna’. Full stops stop a sentence so it makes sense. We always use them at the end of a sentence, like ‘I can run.’ or ‘The cat sat.’. We learn to find where a sentence starts and ends to read and write clearly. Using them right helps everyone understand our work.

## Nouns-verbs introduced

In Year 1, children learn to spot nouns as naming words for things like cat, table, and book. They also identify verbs as doing words, such as run, jump, and eat. Pupils understand that sentences often start with a noun and follow with a verb to make sense, like "The dog barks." They practise matching nouns to the correct verbs, for example, "The boy kicks the ball." By the end of this topic, young learners can confidently separate these two important word types in simple sentences.

## Handwriting joins

In Year 1, we learn how to join letters neatly when we write, starting with short, simple joins. We learn to connect the letter 'a' to 'b' like this: 'ab', and 'c' to 'd' like this: 'cd'. We also learn that some letters, like 't', have a special flick at the top that helps us join to the next letter, such as 'ta'. These joins make our writing faster and easier to read as we get better at forming all the letters correctly.

## Poetry by heart

In this topic, children learn to recite simple poems from memory by listening carefully and repeating lines. They focus on remembering the words in the correct order and using a clear voice. Poems often have a strong rhythm and rhyme, like “Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star”, which helps children remember them. Teachers might use actions or pictures to support memory. The goal is to build confidence with spoken language and develop an early appreciation for poetry’s sound and structure.

## Fiction-nonfiction basics

Children learn to spot the difference between stories that are made up (fiction) and books that tell us true things (nonfiction). They understand that fiction has characters, like a friendly teddy bear, and that nonfiction gives facts, such as what a cat eats. Pupils use pictures and simple words to tell if a book is real or pretend, like seeing a photo of a dog for nonfiction or a drawing of a dragon for fiction. By looking at covers and titles, they can guess if a book will tell a story or teach them something new. This helps them choose the right book for fun or learning.

## Speaking-listening

In Class 1, children learn to speak clearly, take turns talking, and listen carefully when others are speaking. They follow simple rules like raising a hand to speak and using words to share ideas or ask questions. For example, they might tell a friend about their weekend toy or listen to a story and answer questions about it. Children also practise understanding instructions, such as "Please pass the red crayon," and learn to look at the person speaking to show they are paying attention. They build confidence by speaking in small groups or to the whole class, using simple sentences to describe things that happen to them.

## Spelling patterns

In Year 1, children learn to recognise and apply common spelling patterns, such as the 'ck' sound at the end of short words like 'duck' and 'sock'. They are also taught that the 'ai' sound is often found in words like 'rain' and 'paint'. Another key pattern is 'ee', which makes the long vowel sound in words like 'see' and 'tree'. Pupils explore 'oo' for the sound in 'moon' and 'book', learning that the same letters can make different sounds. Finally, they are introduced to the 'sh' digraph for words like 'ship' and 'shop', understanding that two letters can work together to make one sound.
