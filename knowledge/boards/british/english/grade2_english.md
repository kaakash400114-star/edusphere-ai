# British Year 2 English - National Curriculum/GCSE

## Phonics consolidation-suffixes

In Year 2, children learn to add suffixes like -ed and -ing to root words to change their meaning. They explore the simple rule of adding -ed for past tense, as in 'jump' becomes 'jumped'. They also practice adding -ing for actions happening now, like 'run' becomes 'running'. This helps them build longer words and understand how word endings change verb tenses. For example, 'walk' becomes 'walked' and 'play' becomes 'playing'.

## Comprehension: retrieval

In Year 2, children learn to find specific information directly in a text to answer questions. They practise using clues in the words and pictures to find simple answers, like a name, a number, or a colour. For example, they can answer “What colour is the cat?” by looking at a picture or “Who is the main character?” by reading the first page. Teachers show them how to search carefully for the exact words from the question. This helps them become confident readers who can find facts without guessing.

## Punctuation: ?-!-commas

In Year 2, you will learn to use different punctuation marks to make your writing clear. A question mark goes at the end of a sentence that asks something, like ‘What is your name?’. An exclamation mark shows big feelings or excitement, such as ‘Wow, that is amazing!’. You will also use commas to separate items in a list, for example, ‘I need apples, bananas, and pears’. These punctuation marks help your reader understand exactly what you mean.

## Past-present verb consistency

In Year 2, we learn to use past and present tense verbs correctly in our sentences. This means if we are writing about something happening now, we use present-tense verbs like 'walk', 'jump', or 'sing'. When we write about something that has already happened, we use past-tense verbs, such as 'walked', 'jumped', or 'sang'. We make sure all the words in our sentence match the same time, so we do not mix 'walks' (now) with 'walked' (before). This makes our writing clear and helps others understand when things happened.

## Apostrophes for contraction

We learn about apostrophes to show two words squashed into one, like "do not" becomes "don’t" with an apostrophe. The apostrophe goes where the missing letters are, for example, "I am" is "I’m" and "is not" is "isn’t". We also use them for "we are" to make "we’re" and "you are" to make "you’re". It helps us write shorter and faster when we speak. These are called contractions, and the apostrophe tells us letters are missing.

## Expanded noun phrases

In Year 2, children learn to make their writing more descriptive by adding information to nouns. They use adjectives to describe nouns, like a "big, red bus" or a "soft, fluffy cat". They also learn to add more detail using numbers and types, such as "three shiny buttons" or "a long, wavy road". This helps them build longer, more interesting noun phrases to improve their stories and descriptions. By practising this, they can create clearer and more exciting sentences for their readers.

## Poetry-performance

In Year 2, children learn to recite simple poems aloud, using clear voices and actions to bring the words to life. They explore how poets use patterns, such as rhyming couplets (‘cat’/‘hat’) and repeated phrases, to create musical effects. By memorising short verses, pupils build confidence in performing for others and understand how gestures can enhance the meaning of a poem. They also compare different poems, noticing how rhythm and sound make them fun to listen to and say.

## Story writing

Children learn to write simple stories with a clear beginning, middle, and end, using short sentences and familiar words. They discover how to describe characters, such as ‘The girl had long hair’, and where stories happen, like ‘In the big park’. Pupils are taught to plan their ideas by thinking of ‘What happens first?’, ‘What happens next?’, and ‘How does it end?’. They practise using punctuation, like full stops and capital letters, to make their writing easy to read. For example, a story might be: ‘The cat sat on the mat. It saw a bird. The bird flew away.’

## Non-chronological reports

Children in Year 2 learn to write non-chronological reports, which are factual texts that describe things by grouping information into clear sections. They use headings to show the different topics, like "Habitat" or "Diet", and write in simple sentences using present tense to state true facts. A report might include a title, a short introductory sentence, and several paragraphs with facts about a single subject, such as an animal or a mode of transport. Pupils are encouraged to use adjectives to add descriptive detail, ensuring their writing is informative and easy to read. The key is to present factual information in a logical, organised way without telling a story.

## Handwriting fluency

In Year 2, children learn to form letters and numbers correctly, using consistent size and shape to make writing neat and easy to read. They practise holding their pencil with a comfortable grip to control their movements on the page. Pupils are taught to join some letters together, like ‘o’ and ‘e’ in ‘go’, to make their writing flow more smoothly. They also learn to write on lines, using ascenders (like in ‘h’ or ‘k’) and descenders (like in ‘g’ or ‘p’) correctly. Regular practice helps children build fluency, so they can write words without thinking too hard about each letter.
