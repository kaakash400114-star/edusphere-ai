# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 4 Science

## Living Things and Their Habitats
Living things are plants and animals. They all have basic needs: air, water, food, and shelter. A habitat is the place where a plant or animal naturally lives. For example, the fish in the Niger River lives in an aquatic habitat, while the weaver bird builds its nest in a tree in Lagos. Harmattan season can affect habitats by drying up water sources. Plants make their own food through a process called photosynthesis, using sunlight, water, and air.

## The Human Body and Health
The human body is made up of different parts like the head, chest, and arms. Each part has a specific job. The brain controls our thoughts and actions, while the heart pumps blood all over the body. To stay healthy, we must eat good food like jollof rice and vegetables, drink clean water, and get enough sleep. Washing our hands with soap and water regularly prevents germs from making us sick, which is especially important during the harmattan season.

## Materials and Their Properties
Materials are things that are made from substances. We can group materials by their properties. For example, wood is hard and opaque, while glass is transparent. Materials can be changed by processes like heating, melting, or mixing. Water can be heated to become steam, or cooled to become ice. Some materials, like the naira note, are used for money because they are durable and difficult to tear. Harmattan dust can settle on materials, so we need to keep them clean.

## Forces and Movement
A force is a push or a pull. It can make a stationary object move or make a moving object stop. For example, kicking a football is a force that makes it roll. Gravity is a force that pulls everything down towards the ground. We feel gravity when we drop something. Without friction, a car would slide on the road. Friction is a force that stops things from moving too easily. We can make things move faster by pushing or pulling them harder.

## Light and Sound
Light helps us see things. It travels in a straight line. The sun is a source of light that we see during the day. Sound is a form of energy that we hear. It travels through the air as vibrations. When a school bell rings, it makes a loud sound everyone can hear. Some objects, like a kola nut, can make a sound when you break it. Shadows are formed when an object blocks a source of light.

## The Solar System and Weather
The solar system consists of the sun and all celestial bodies, including eight planets, that orbit it, with Earth being the third planet from the sun. The sun is a massive star at the system's centre, providing essential heat and light that sustain life on Earth through the process of radiation. Weather describes the state of the atmosphere at a specific time, measured by elements such as temperature, humidity, wind speed, and rainfall, and can change from day to day. In Nigeria, the climate is characterised by two primary seasons influenced by the sun's position: the rainy season, marked by heavy downpours and high humidity from April to October, and the dry harmattan season. The harmattan, which typically lasts from November to March, is a dry and dusty trade wind originating from the Sahara Desert, causing cool temperatures and reduced visibility. These seasonal patterns directly influence agricultural activities, such as the planting of crops like maize and yam during the rainy season.
