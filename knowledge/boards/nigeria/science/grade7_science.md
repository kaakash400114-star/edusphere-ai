# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 7 Science
## Classification of Living Things
Living things are grouped into a hierarchy for easier study. The largest group is the Kingdom, followed by Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, and Species. Organisms are classified based on shared characteristics like cell structure, how they obtain energy, and their evolutionary relationships. For example, humans belong to the Animal Kingdom, Phylum Chordata, Class Mammalia, Order Primates, Family Hominidae, Genus Homo, and Species sapiens. Organisms with very similar structures and can reproduce together are in the same species. This system, developed by scientists like Carolus Linnaeus, helps us understand the diversity of life.

## Forces and Motion
A force is a push or a pull that can change the motion or shape of an object. Motion is the change in an object's position over time. The SI unit for force is the Newton (N). Forces can be contact forces, like kicking a football, or non-contact forces, like the magnetic force. The speed of an object is calculated by dividing the distance travelled by the time taken. For instance, if a bus travels 200 kilometres from Lagos to Ibadan in 4 hours, its speed is 50 kilometres per hour. An object remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by an unbalanced force, a principle known as Newton's First Law of Motion.

## Properties of Matter
Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space. It exists in three main states: solid, liquid, and gas. Solids have a definite shape and volume because their particles are tightly packed. Liquids have a definite volume but take the shape of their container because their particles are close but can move. Gases have no definite shape or volume as their particles are far apart and move freely. For example, a piece of kola nut is solid, the palm oil used in jollof rice is a liquid, and the air we breathe during the harmattan season is a gas. The change of state between these forms is called a physical change.

## Energy Forms and Transformation
Energy is the ability to do work. It exists in many forms, including kinetic (energy of motion), potential (stored energy), chemical, heat, light, and sound. Energy cannot be created or destroyed, but it can be transformed from one form to another. For example, the chemical energy stored in the food you eat is transformed into kinetic energy as you walk to school. Similarly, a torch battery contains chemical energy that is transformed into light energy and heat energy. In Nigeria, the chemical energy in coal or natural gas is used in power plants to produce electrical energy, which powers our homes and industries.

## The Human Digestive System
The digestive system breaks down food into simple, soluble substances that can be absorbed into the blood for use by the body. It consists of the alimentary canal and associated organs. The main parts are the mouth (for mechanical digestion and salivary amylase action), the stomach (which churns food and uses acid for digestion), and the small intestine (where most chemical digestion and nutrient absorption occurs). The leftover waste moves to the large intestine and is expelled through the anus. Undigested food, like the fibre from yams or plantain, provides bulk and helps in efficient waste removal. A balanced diet ensures all necessary nutrients are obtained.

## Weather and Climate
Weather is the state of the atmosphere at a specific time and place, including temperature, rainfall, humidity, and wind. It is short-term and can change daily. Climate, on the other hand, is the average weather pattern of a region over a long period, typically 30 years or more. Nigeria experiences different climatic zones; for example, the coastal areas have a rainy season, while the northern parts have a more arid climate. The harmattan is a prominent weather feature, bringing cool, dry dust-laden winds from the Sahara, especially from December to March. Understanding the difference between weather and climate is crucial for agriculture and daily planning.
