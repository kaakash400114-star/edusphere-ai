## Vectors and Scalars in Physics
A scalar has only magnitude, such as mass (50 kg) or temperature (28°C). A vector has both magnitude and direction. Displacement is 10 km north. Velocity is 80 km/h east. Force is a vector; a push of 20 N on a classroom door requires direction. To add vectors, use the head-to-tail method or Pythagoras' theorem for perpendicular vectors. If a canoe travels 5 m/s across a river flowing at 3 m/s, its resultant velocity is √(52 + 32) = 5.83 m/s, directed at an angle to the current.
## The Mole and Stoichiometry in Chemistry
The mole is the unit for amount of substance (mol). One mole of any substance contains 6.02 x 1023 particles (Avogadro's number). To find the number of moles (n), use n = mass (g) / molar mass (g/mol). For example, 88 g of CO₂ has n = 88 / 44 = 2 mol. In a balanced equation like 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, the mole ratio is 2:1:2. This means 2 mol of hydrogen gas reacts with 1 mol of oxygen gas to produce 2 mol of water.
## Electromagnetic Waves and Optics in Physics
Electromagnetic waves are transverse waves that consist of oscillating electric and magnetic fields. They travel at 3.0 x 108 m/s in a vacuum. The spectrum includes radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays. Visible light is refracted when passing from air to water, causing a straw in a glass of water to appear bent. The refractive index of water is 1.33, calculated as the ratio of the speed of light in a vacuum to its speed in the water.
## Energy Transfer and Thermodynamics in Physics
The first law of thermodynamics states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred or transformed. Heat energy (Q) flows from a hotter body to a colder body. Specific heat capacity (c) is the energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1°C. For example, to heat a 2 kg pot of water from 20°C to 100°C, the energy required is Q = mcΔT = 2 kg x 4200 J/kg°C x (100-20)°C = 672,000 J.
## Chemical Bonding and Structure in Chemistry
Chemical bonds hold atoms together. Ionic bonding occurs between metals and non-metals through electron transfer, forming ions (e.g., Na+ and Cl- in table salt, NaCl). Covalent bonding occurs between non-metals through shared electron pairs, forming molecules (e.g., the H-O-H bonds in water). Metallic bonding is a 'sea of delocalized electrons' holding metal cations in a lattice, which explains the high electrical conductivity of a copper wire used in electrical circuits.
## Ecological Concepts and Biodiversity in Biology
An ecosystem is a community of living organisms interacting with their non-living environment. Biotic factors are living components like the maize plant and the weevil in a farm. Abiotic factors are non-living components like the soil pH, sunlight, and the harmattan wind. Biodiversity refers to the variety of life. In Nigeria, high biodiversity is seen in the Cross River National Park, which contains different species of monkeys, birds, and plants, contributing to the ecological stability of the region.
