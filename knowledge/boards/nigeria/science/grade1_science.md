# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 1 Science


## Our Body and the Senses
Children learn the main external body parts. They identify the head, neck, shoulders, arms, hands, chest, waist, legs, and feet. They understand the five senses: sight (eyes), hearing (ears), smell (nose), touch (skin), and taste (tongue). Children observe that eyes help us see objects like a ball, ears help us hear sounds like a bell, skin helps us feel things like rain, a nose helps us smell food like jollof rice, and the tongue helps us taste things like yam.

## Living and Non-Living Things
Children learn to classify things as living or non-living. Living things grow, eat, move, and reproduce. Examples include plants like maize in a garden and animals like goats. Non-living things do not grow, eat, or move. Examples include stones, tables, and a school bag. Children can identify that a kola nut is a living thing that grows on a tree, while a naira coin is a non-living thing used for buying things at the market.

## Our Environment and Weather
Pupils learn to identify key weather conditions: sunny days have clear, blue skies and strong sunlight, while rainy days are characterised by grey clouds, lightning, and heavy rainfall from the sky, such as in Port Harcourt. They also observe the harmattan season, marked by dry, dusty wind and hazy, brown skies, with temperatures cooler than usual. Children associate each weather condition with specific activities and environmental changes, such as playing football outdoors when it is sunny, using umbrellas to stay dry during rain showers, and wearing warmer clothes to protect from the harmattan’s cold and dry air. They are taught to describe the appearance of the sky, noting that dark, low clouds often signal an approaching rainstorm. This learning helps pupils understand how weather affects daily life and the natural environment around them.

## Materials and Their Uses
Pupils classify common materials like wood, metal, plastic, cloth, and glass by observing their observable properties such as hardness, texture, colour, weight, and shininess. They identify that specific properties make materials suitable for certain objects, for instance, glass is transparent and waterproof, making it ideal for windows and drinking cups. Children learn that wood is hard and strong, therefore it is used to make doors, chairs, and tables, while plastic is light and can be moulded into many shapes, making it useful for buckets and plates. They understand that cloth is flexible and soft, hence it is used to make clothes and bags. The concept is reinforced by explaining that metal is a strong conductor of heat, which makes it the right choice for pots used for cooking on a fire.

## Plants Around Us
All plants begin their life cycle from a seed, which germinates with the right conditions. A typical plant consists of five essential parts: roots anchor the plant and absorb water from the soil, the stem supports the plant and transports nutrients, leaves make food through sunlight, and flowers produce seeds for new plants. For a plant to grow healthily, it requires three basic things: sunlight, water, and nutrient-rich soil. Common Nigerian plants include maize, yams, and mango trees, each with a specific growth cycle. Trees provide essential resources such as shade, edible fruits like mangoes, and habitats for animals such as birds.

## Caring for Our Environment
The Nigerian NERDC Science curriculum for Grade 1 requires children to learn the fundamental methods of caring for the environment. Students are taught that rubbish, such as sweet wrappers and plastic bottles, must always be placed in a designated waste bin to prevent pollution. A key concept is that dirty water from rubbish can spread sickness, so waste must never be thrown into rivers or streams. Children practice waste management by sorting different types of refuse, for example, separating paper and plastic into distinct bins. This action directly contributes to maintaining a clean and healthy environment in the school and the wider community.
