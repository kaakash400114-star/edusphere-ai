# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 12 Science
## Electromagnetic Induction and Electrical Power Generation
Electromagnetic induction is the process of generating an electromotive force (EMF) in a conductor when the magnetic flux passing through it changes. Faraday’s law states that the induced EMF is directly proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux. Lenz’s law states that the direction of the induced current opposes the change causing it. This principle is used in generators to convert mechanical energy into electrical energy. For example, a coil of wire rotated in a magnetic field within a power plant in Lagos produces an alternating current (AC) that is stepped up by transformers for transmission across the national grid, supplying power for homes and industries.

## Energetics and Chemical Equilibrium
The enthalpy change (ΔH) of a reaction measures the heat absorbed or released at constant pressure. An exothermic reaction has a negative ΔH, releasing heat like the combustion of kola nuts. An endothermic reaction has a positive ΔH, absorbing heat. Chemical equilibrium is a state where the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction. The equilibrium constant (Kc) is the ratio of the concentrations of products to reactants at equilibrium. The position of equilibrium changes with temperature, pressure, and concentration, according to Le Chatelier’s principle, crucial for optimizing industrial processes like the production of ammonia.

## Genetics and Evolution
Mendel's laws of inheritance state that traits are passed from parents to offspring via discrete units called genes. The law of segregation explains that an organism has two alleles for each trait, which separate during gamete formation. The law of independent assortment describes how alleles for different traits are sorted independently. Variations in a population, such as the different strains of jollof rice across Nigeria, arise from mutation, gene flow, and sexual reproduction. Charles Darwin's theory of evolution by natural selection proposes that organisms with favourable adaptations are more likely to survive, reproduce, and pass on their genes, leading to gradual changes in species over time.

## Waves and Communication
A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy from one point to another without transferring matter. The main types are transverse (e.g., light) and longitudinal (e.g., sound). The frequency (f) is the number of oscillations per second, measured in Hertz (Hz), and the wavelength (λ) is the distance between successive crests. The relationship is given by v = fλ, where v is the wave speed. Communication systems use electromagnetic waves like radio and microwaves to transmit information over long distances. For example, mobile phone networks in Lagos use these waves to allow people to connect, enabling seamless business and social interactions across the nation and beyond.

## Applied Ecology and Environmental Conservation
Ecology is the study of interactions between organisms and their environment. Applied ecology applies this knowledge to solve environmental problems. Environmental factors include biotic (living components) and abiotic (non-living components) factors. Pollution, such as from industrial waste in major cities, harms biodiversity. The harmattan season, with its dusty winds, can exacerbate respiratory problems. Conservation involves managing natural resources, like water and forests, to prevent depletion. Sustainable practices, such as using solar power, are encouraged to reduce carbon footprint and protect Nigeria's unique ecosystems for future generations.

## Organic Chemistry and Industrial Applications
Organic chemistry is the study of carbon-based compounds. Hydrocarbons are compounds made of hydrogen and carbon. Alkanes, alkenes, and alkynes are homologous series with single, double, and triple carbon-carbon bonds, respectively. Aromatic compounds, like benzene, have a stable ring structure. The cracking of heavy crude oil fractions from the Niger Delta produces smaller, more useful molecules like petrol. The addition of bromine water to an alkene decolourizes it, a test for unsaturation. These processes are vital for the Nigerian petrochemical industry, which produces fuels, plastics, and other essential materials for the economy.
