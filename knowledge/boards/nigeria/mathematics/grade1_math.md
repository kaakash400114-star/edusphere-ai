# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 1 Mathematics
## Numbers and Counting
Children learn to count, read, and write numbers from 0 to 50. They use objects like kola nuts and counters to show amounts. They can count out loud and match numbers to groups of items. Pupils practise writing numerals correctly. They learn that numbers represent quantity, like five naira coins. They count forward and backward, starting from different numbers. Activities involve sorting objects into groups and finding out how many are in each. This builds a strong foundation for understanding number value and basic arithmetic in real life.

## Basic Addition and Subtraction
Children learn to add and subtract using real-life objects. They combine groups, like adding two yams and three yams to get five yams. This is addition, using the '+' sign. They also take away items, like starting with ten pieces of jollof rice and eating two, to find the remainder. This is subtraction, using the '-' sign. They work with sums and differences within 10. Simple word problems involve sharing sweets or adding fruits. This practical approach helps them understand that addition increases quantity while subtraction decreases it.

## Shapes and Patterns
Pupils recognise and name common shapes like circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles in the world around them. They see circles in plates and wheels, and rectangles in doors and books. They learn to describe shapes by their sides and corners. They also identify patterns in sequences, like red, blue, red, blue. Creating patterns with beads, colours, or sounds helps them understand predictability. They complete simple patterns and make their own. This develops their observational skills and logical thinking, recognizing order in everyday things.

## Measurement
Children learn to compare and measure using non-standard units. They measure length using their hands or footsteps to see who is taller. They measure weight by feeling objects to see which is heavier, like a bag of rice versus a book. They measure capacity by pouring water between different containers. They use terms like 'long', 'short', 'heavy', 'light', 'full', and 'empty'. They compare objects directly, like which rope is longer. This hands-on approach teaches them about size, weight, and volume in their environment.

## Time and Money
Pupils learn about time by sequencing events in their day, like waking up, eating lunch, and going to bed. They understand morning, afternoon, and night. They use simple vocabulary like 'yesterday', 'today', and 'tomorrow'. For money, they recognise and identify the naira coins (kobo coins are for older students). They learn the value of small coins like the 10 kobo and 25 kobo pieces. They match coins to their values and use them to buy simple items in class play, connecting currency to real-world buying.

## Data Handling
Children collect, sort, and organise information about things they see. They can sort objects by colour, shape, or size, like arranging fruits into red and green groups. They make simple pictographs by drawing or sticking pictures to show how many items are in each category, for example, charting favourite types of food like jollof rice or pounded yam. They learn to interpret these charts to answer questions like 'Which has the most?'. This introduces them to data collection and representation skills in a fun and visual way.
