# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 4 Mathematics
## Addition of Four-Digit Numbers
We learn to add numbers up to 9,999 using the column method. We write numbers in columns, starting with the thousands on the left. We add the digits in each column from right to left. If the sum in a column is 10 or more, we carry the tens to the next column on the left. For example, 2,485 + 1,726 is 4,211. We can use this to find the total cost of buying a textbook for N485 and a school bag for N726 in Lagos.
## Subtraction of Four-Digit Numbers
We subtract numbers up to 9,999 using the column method. We write the larger number on top, ensuring digits are lined up by place value. We subtract from right to left. If the digit on top is smaller, we borrow from the next column on the left. For example, to find how much change you get from N5,000 after buying a carton of eggs for N1,250, you calculate 5,000 - 1,250 = 3,750 naira.
## Multiplication of Two-Digit Numbers
We multiply two-digit numbers using the grid or long multiplication method. For 34 x 12, we first multiply 34 by 10 (the tens digit), which is 340. Then we multiply 34 by 2 (the units digit), which is 68. Finally, we add these two products together: 340 + 68 = 408. We can use this to calculate the total number of grains in 12 bags of rice, if each bag has 34 grains.
## Division of Numbers with Remainders
We divide numbers to share items equally. We find out how many groups we can make and what is left over. For example, to share 85 kola nuts among 4 friends, we divide 85 by 4. We can make 20 groups of 4 (20 x 4 = 80). There are 5 nuts left over, which is the remainder. So, each friend gets 20 nuts with 5 remaining.
## Fraction of a Whole
A fraction shows a part of a whole. The top number is the numerator (the part), and the bottom is the denominator (the total parts). To find one half (1/2) of 50 naira, we divide by 2. 50 ÷ 2 = 25 naira. To find one quarter (1/4) of a plate of jollof rice shared by 4 children, each child gets 1 part out of the 4 whole parts.
## Area in Square Units
Area is the space covered by a flat surface. We measure area in square units. For a rectangle, we count the number of squares inside or calculate length times width. If a school garden is 4 metres long and 3 metres wide, its area is 4 x 3 = 12 square metres. During the harmattan, this helps us know how much tarpaulin is needed to cover a rectangular rooftop in Lagos.
