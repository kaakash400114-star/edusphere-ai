# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 9 Mathematics

## Number Base System
Students understand and convert numbers between base 2 (binary) and base 10 (denary). The process involves dividing the denary number by 2 and listing the remainders. For example, to convert 13₁₀ to binary, we divide: 13 ÷ 2 = 6 remainder 1; 6 ÷ 2 = 3 remainder 0; 3 ÷ 2 = 1 remainder 1; 1 ÷ 2 = 0 remainder 1. Reading the remainders from bottom to top gives 1101₂. This conversion is fundamental for computer science applications and logic circuits.

## Algebraic Expressions and Formulae
Students simplify and evaluate algebraic expressions by substituting given values. For example, evaluating 3p² - 2q + 5 when p = 2 and q = -1 involves calculation: 3(2)² - 2(-1) + 5 = 3(4) + 2 + 5 = 12 + 2 + 5 = 19. Students also derive and apply formulas, such as the area of a trapezium, A = ½h(a+b), to solve practical problems involving measurements of land or plots in Lagos.

## Equations and Inequalities
Students solve linear equations and inequalities in one variable. For the equation 3x - 7 = 14, the solution is found by adding 7 to both sides (3x = 21) and then dividing by 3 (x = 7). For inequalities like 2y + 5 < 15, students solve for y (2y < 10, so y < 5) and represent the solution on a number line, a skill used for analysing budgets or resource limitations in a household.

## Geometry: Plane Shapes and Solid Figures
Students calculate the perimeter and area of plane shapes like triangles, trapeziums, circles, and regular polygons, applying relevant formulas such as Area = ½ × base × height for triangles and Area = πr² for circular farmland. For solid figures, they determine the volume of cuboids, prisms, cylinders, and pyramids using formulas like Volume = base area × height. They also learn to calculate the surface area of these solids. These calculations are essential for practical applications like determining the materials needed for a rectangular building foundation, the fencing for a circular fish pond, or the capacity of a cylindrical storage silo for grain. Mastery of these geometric concepts is vital for fields such as construction, engineering, and farming in Nigeria.

## Trigonometry
In trigonometry, Grade 9 students apply sine (sin), cosine (cos), and tangent (tan) ratios to solve problems involving right-angled triangles, where sinθ = opposite/hypotenuse, cosθ = adjacent/hypotenuse, and tanθ = opposite/adjacent. They use these ratios to calculate unknown lengths of sides, such as finding the height of a building when given the angle of elevation and the distance from the building. Similarly, they apply these principles to determine unknown angles in a triangle, for instance, calculating the angle of a ramp when given its height and horizontal length. Practical applications include using a clinometer to measure angles and then applying trigonometric functions to find real-world distances. Mastery of these concepts provides a foundation for solving more complex problems in geometry and physics.

## Statistics and Probability
Students calculate mean, median, and mode for a set of data. For instance, finding the average (mean) cost of a basket of kola nuts over a week. They also determine the probability of a single event, such as the probability of the harmattan wind blowing on any given day in December. Probability is expressed as a fraction, decimal, or percentage, providing a framework for predicting outcomes and making informed decisions.
