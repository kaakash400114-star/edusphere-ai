# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 6 Mathematics
## Whole Numbers, Fractions, and Decimals
Students will further their understanding of numbers by learning to add and subtract large whole numbers using the column method. They will also multiply and divide by 10, 100, and 1,000. Fractions are a key focus, including finding equivalent fractions, simplifying to their lowest terms, and adding and subtracting fractions with the same denominator. In decimals, students will learn to compare, order, and round decimal numbers to the nearest whole number and one decimal place, using practical examples like calculating the cost of market items in naira.

## Basic Geometry and Shapes
This topic explores the properties of 2D and 3D geometric shapes. Pupils will identify, name, and describe properties such as the number of sides, vertices, angles, faces, and edges for shapes including triangles, quadrilaterals, cubes, and cuboids. They will learn to differentiate between regular and irregular polygons. Using Nigerian examples, they might identify the shapes used in local architectural designs like ancient walls or modern buildings in Lagos, connecting abstract concepts to the physical world around them.

## Measurement and Time
Students will master units of measurement for length, mass, and capacity within the metric system, including converting between kilometres and metres, grammes and kilograms, and litres and millilitres. They will solve practical problems involving the measurement of objects. Time-telling skills are extended, covering reading time from an analogue clock to the minute and calculating simple durations. Examples could include calculating the travel time between Nigerian cities or measuring ingredients for a large pot of jollof rice.

## Data Handling and Probability
Pupils will learn to collect, organise, and present data. They will interpret and construct bar charts, pictograms, and tables from given data sets. From these representations, they will answer simple questions to find information, totals, and comparisons. In probability, students will use words like 'certain', 'likely', 'unlikely', and 'impossible' to describe the likelihood of everyday events, such as the chance of seeing the Harmattan wind during the dry season or finding a kola nut in a bowl of assorted nuts.

## Algebraic Foundations
This introduces basic algebraic thinking. Students will express simple number patterns and sequences using words and symbols. They will understand and use simple formulas, such as finding the perimeter of a rectangle (P = 2(l + w)). The concept of a missing number in a simple equation is introduced, for example, finding the value of 'n' in 15 + n = 30. This prepares them for more abstract mathematical reasoning and problem-solving.

## Problem Solving and Financial Literacy
Students will develop skills to solve one-step and two-step word problems involving the four basic operations. A crucial application is financial literacy, where they will solve problems involving money. This includes adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing amounts in naira (₦) and kobo (k). For instance, calculating the total cost of several items at a market stall or determining how much change to receive after a purchase, grounding mathematical skills in real-world financial contexts.
