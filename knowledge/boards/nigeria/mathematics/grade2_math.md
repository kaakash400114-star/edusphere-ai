# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 2 Mathematics
## Number and Numeration
Pupils identify and read numbers up to 100 in ones and tens. They count objects accurately, matching numerals to quantities. For example, a pupil can count 50 naira notes in a bundle. They understand place value, separating numbers into tens and units (e.g., 78 is 7 tens and 8 units). They write numbers in words, such as writing "seventy" for 70. They compare numbers using greater than (>) and less than (<) symbols, like 45 < 50, connecting this to comparing the price of items at a local market in Lagos.

## Basic Operations
Pupils learn addition and subtraction facts within 20 using concrete objects like counters or kola nuts. They solve simple word problems: "Ada has 12 crayons. She gives 5 to her friend. How many crayons does Ada have left?" They use the minus sign (-) for subtraction. For addition, they combine two groups: "There are 8 mangoes in one basket and 6 in another. How many mangoes are there altogether?" They use the plus sign (+) and find the sum.

## Measurement
Pupils measure lengths in non-standard units like hand spans and foot lengths. They compare the length of their desks using their hands. They identify Nigerian currency coins (kobo) and notes (naira), understanding their values. They use real naira notes to buy items, like paying ₦20 for jollof rice. They tell time to the hour and half-hour on an analogue clock, relating it to daily routines like school start time at 8 o'clock.

## Geometry
Pupils identify and name common plane shapes in their environment: circle, square, rectangle, and triangle. They recognise that a round plate is a circle and a slice of yam is often triangular. They sort shapes based on properties, like counting sides. They understand basic positional words, describing where objects are located: "The book is on the table," "The pencil is under the chair," using this to describe the arrangement of items in the classroom.

## Everyday Life and Data Handling
Pupils solve simple everyday problems involving sharing. For example, "If 4 children share 8 oranges equally, how many oranges does each child get?" They collect simple data by voting for their favourite food from options like jollof rice, rice and beans, or pounded yam. They use tally marks to record the votes. They then interpret the data, identifying which food had the most votes.

## Patterns and Relationships
Pupils recognise, copy, and extend simple patterns using shapes, colours, numbers, or sounds. For instance, they can continue a pattern of red-blue-red-blue beads. They identify number patterns, counting up and down in twos from different starting points, like 0, 2, 4, 6. They also understand and create patterns related to the seasons, such as the harmattan season being followed by the dry season, connecting this to yearly changes in the weather.
