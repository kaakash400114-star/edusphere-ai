# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 3 Mathematics
## Basic Operations with Whole Numbers
In Grade 3, we learn to add, subtract, multiply, and divide whole numbers. Addition means putting numbers together, like 25 + 13 = 38. Subtraction is taking away, such as 40 - 18 = 22. We multiply repeated groups: 3 × 4 means 3 groups of 4, which is 12. Division is sharing equally; 20 ÷ 4 means sharing 20 items into 4 equal groups, giving 5 in each. We use the Naira sign (₦) for money problems. For example, finding the total cost of ₦15 exercise books and ₦25 pencils involves adding 15 + 25 to get ₦40.

## Fractions
We learn about fractions, which are parts of a whole. A fraction has a numerator (top number) and a denominator (bottom number). The denominator shows how many equal parts the whole is divided into, and the numerator shows how many parts we have. For example, ½ means one of two equal parts. We use everyday Nigerian examples: sharing one ripe kola nut between two children is ½ each. A quarter (¼) of a loaf of bread is one of four equal pieces. We identify and name fractions like ½, ¼, and ¾ using pictures of common items like yam or akara.

## Measurement
In measurement, we learn to measure length, weight, and capacity using standard units. For length, we use metres (m) and centimetres (cm). A school desk might be 60 cm long. For weight, we use grams (g) and kilograms (kg). A bag of rice weighs 50 kg. We measure capacity (how much a container holds) in litres (L). A large jug of palm oil is 5 L. We also tell time using clocks. We practice reading the time to the hour, half-hour, and quarter-hour. For example, school starts at 8 o'clock in the morning and ends at 2 o'clock in the afternoon.

## Geometry
Geometry teaches us about shapes and their properties. We identify and name two-dimensional (2D) shapes like squares, rectangles, circles, and triangles. A square has four equal sides and four right angles. A rectangle has four sides with opposite sides equal and four right angles. We look for these shapes in our environment, such as round wheels on cars in Lagos or triangular roofs on houses. We also learn about three-dimensional (3D) shapes like cubes and cylinders, which have faces, edges, and corners. A sugar cube is a cube shape.

## Data Handling
Data handling is about collecting, organizing, and interpreting information. We collect data by counting and recording items. We organize data using simple pictographs and bar charts. For example, we can count the number of students who prefer jollof rice or eba and represent this with pictures or bars. Each picture or bar can stand for a certain number of people, like one picture for 5 students. We read and interpret the information from the charts to answer questions, such as "Which food is more popular?" This helps us understand information from our surroundings.

## Everyday Problem Solving
We solve everyday problems using the four basic operations. These problems are based on real-life situations in Nigeria. For example, "If a plate of jollof rice costs ₦300 and you buy 2 plates, how much do you pay?" This requires multiplication (2 × 300). Another problem: "You have ₦100 and buy harmattan clothes for ₦75. How much money is left?" This needs subtraction (100 - 75). We learn to understand the problem, choose the correct operation, solve it, and check if the answer makes sense.
