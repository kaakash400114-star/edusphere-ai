# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 10 Mathematics
## Algebraic Expressions and Formulae
Students learn to expand and simplify algebraic expressions involving brackets, including the square of a binomial (a+b)² and the difference of two squares (a-b)(a+b). They substitute numerical values into given formulae, such as finding the area of a trapezium using A = ½(a+b)h with values like a=7cm, b=11cm, and h=5cm. They also factorise simple quadratic expressions of the form x² + bx + c, for example, factorising x² + 6x + 8 into (x+2)(x+4), and interpret their results in practical contexts like calculating costs for events in Lagos.

## Equations and Inequalities
Students solve various types of linear equations in one variable, including those with fractional coefficients and those that require simplification first. For instance, they solve 3x/2 - 5 = x/4 + 1 by finding a common denominator. They also solve word problems resulting in linear equations, such as calculating the number of kola nuts each person receives if a total of 36 nuts are shared equally among a group. For inequalities, they solve simple linear inequalities like 2x - 7 < 5 and represent the solution set on a number line, using the harmattan season as a metaphor for a range of conditions.

## Trigonometry
Students apply trigonometric ratios (sine, cosine, and tangent) to solve problems involving right-angled triangles. They learn the definitions: sinθ = opposite/hypotenuse, cosθ = adjacent/hypotenuse, and tanθ = opposite/adjacent. They use these to find unknown sides and angles. For example, they can calculate the height of a building if the angle of elevation from a point 50m away is 35 degrees. They apply this to practical Nigerian scenarios, such as determining the length of a shadow cast by a flagpole in the intense afternoon sun.

## Commercial Arithmetic
Students calculate simple and compound interests using the formulas SI = PRT/100 and A = P(1 + R/100)^n. For example, they find the compound interest on ₦50,000 invested for 3 years at 5% per annum, rounding to the nearest naira. They also solve problems involving hire purchase, calculating the total cost of an item if an initial deposit of ₦10,000 is paid followed by 12 monthly instalments of ₦5,000 each, and comparing it to a cash price.

## Statistics
Students calculate and interpret the mean, median, and mode for both ungrouped and grouped data. For ungrouped data, they find the average daily sales of jollof rice from a food stall over a week. For grouped data, they calculate the estimated mean of students' heights grouped in class intervals (e.g., 150-159 cm). They also interpret these measures in context, using the mean to determine the central value of a data set, such as the average price of a phone in a market survey.

## Logic and Set Theory
Students use symbols like ∈ (element of), ⊂ (subset), and ∩ (intersection) to represent relationships between sets. They solve problems involving Venn diagrams, such as finding the number of students in a Lagos survey who speak Yoruba (Y), Hausa (H), or both, given n(Y)=25, n(H)=20, and n(Y ∩ H)=8. They use logical reasoning to determine the truth value of statements like "If a student is in the football team, then the student is an athlete," and apply set notation to classify groups, like students who eat beans and those who eat rice.
