# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 5 Mathematics
## Whole Numbers and Operations
Pupils identify and write numerals up to one million. They learn to add and subtract numbers with up to six digits, using the column method. For example, 485,230 + 123,547 equals 608,777. They also solve word problems involving money, such as finding the total cost of two items bought at the Lagos market. Multiplication covers 3-digit by 2-digit numbers, like 345 x 12 equals 4,140. Division involves dividing 3-digit numbers by 1 or 2-digit divisors, with and without remainders, using the short division method.

## Fractions
Pupils identify equivalent fractions using common multiples. They learn to add and subtract fractions with the same denominator, e.g., 3/5 + 1/5 = 4/5. They compare and order unit fractions like 1/2, 1/3, and 1/4. Real-life examples include sharing kola nuts equally among friends. They also solve problems involving adding and subtracting fractions of a whole, like calculating the fraction of a bag of rice eaten during a family feast.

## Measurement
Pupils convert between larger and smaller units of length, mass, and capacity. For example, 1,000 grams make 1 kilogram, and 1,000 millilitres make 1 litre. They measure objects in centimetres and metres, and liquids in litres and millilitres. A practical example is measuring the ingredients for jollof rice: 2 kg of rice and 1,500 ml of palm oil. They also solve problems involving time, converting hours to minutes and reading timetables for the harmattan season.

## Geometry
Pupils identify and name plane shapes like squares, rectangles, triangles, and circles. They identify properties, such as the number of sides and corners. They learn about 3-dimensional shapes like cubes, cuboids, and cylinders, identifying their faces and vertices. A Nigerian example is identifying the shapes of local items like a ‘kola nut’ (cuboid) or a ‘calabash’. They also learn to identify lines of symmetry in shapes, like the symmetry in a traditional ‘adire’ cloth pattern.

## Data Handling
Pupils collect, organise, and interpret data using tally marks and frequency tables. They represent data using pictograms and bar graphs, where one picture or square represents a value. For example, a pictogram could show the favourite foods of pupils, with pictures of jollof rice. They interpret these graphs to answer questions, such as ‘Which food is the most popular?’. They also learn to find the mode (most frequent item) from a given set of data.

## Everyday Mathematics and Problem Solving
Pupils apply mathematics to everyday life. They solve problems involving buying and selling goods, calculating change with Nigerian Naira. For example, if a bag of crayfish costs ₦2,450 and you pay with ₦5,000, the change is ₦2,550. They solve problems involving distance, speed, and time, like calculating the travel time from Lagos to Ibadan. They also solve simple problems involving sharing money and items, ensuring the principles of fairness are applied.
