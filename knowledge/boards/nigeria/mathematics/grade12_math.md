# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 12 Mathematics
## Calculus: Differentiation and its Applications
Students learn to find the derivative of a function as the limit of the gradient of a secant line. The basic rules of differentiation are covered: sum, difference, product, quotient, and chain rules. They differentiate expressions like x², sin(x), and eˣ, and apply these to find rates of change. For example, the instantaneous velocity of a car moving from Lagos to Abuja can be modelled as the derivative of its position function. Gradients of tangents to curves and turning points on a graph are found using the first derivative, linking calculus to real-world motion and optimization.

## Calculus: Integration and its Applications
Integration is introduced as the reverse process of differentiation, or finding the area under a curve. Students learn the basic rules for integrating standard functions, including polynomials, trigonometric, and exponential functions. Definite and indefinite integrals are explored. A key application is calculating the area between a curve and the x-axis, which can represent the total volume of water in a reservoir over time. They also use integration to solve kinematics problems, such as finding the distance travelled by a vehicle given its velocity function from Port Harcourt to Enugu.

## Vectors and Scalar Quantities
Students distinguish between scalar quantities (like the price of a bag of rice, measured in Naira) and vector quantities (like the force of the Harmattan wind, which has both magnitude and direction). They represent vectors geometrically using directed line segments and perform operations like addition, subtraction, and multiplication by a scalar. The component form of a vector is used for calculation. For instance, the resultant displacement of a boat travelling across the Niger River can be found by adding vector components representing its engine power and the river's current, demonstrating practical navigation in Nigerian waterways.

## Probability and Statistics
This section covers advanced probability concepts, including conditional probability, independent events, and the use of tree diagrams. Students apply the principles of permutations and combinations to solve problems involving arrangements and selections from a group. For example, calculating the number of possible ways to arrange guests for a traditional wedding. Statistical data analysis involves calculating measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) and dispersion (variance, standard deviation) for ungrouped and grouped data, such as analysing the daily sales of a street food vendor selling jollof rice in Lagos to understand profit patterns.

## Sequence and Series
Students study finite and infinite arithmetic and geometric sequences and series. They learn to find the nth term (Tₙ) and the sum of the first n terms (Sₙ) of an arithmetic sequence, using the formula Sₙ = n/2 (2a + (n-1)d). For a geometric sequence, the sum is Sₙ = a(1-rⁿ)/(1-r). Applications include calculating the total population of a growing city over decades or the total cost of a business investment with compound interest. The concept of a convergent geometric series is introduced, which can model processes like the decay of a currency's value over time.

## Logic and Set Theory
Students explore formal logic, including statements, connectives (AND, OR, NOT, IMPLIES), and the truth tables of compound statements. They identify tautologies and contradictions. In set theory, they study operations on sets such as union (∪), intersection (∩), and complement. Venn diagrams are used to solve problems involving the distribution of items, such as finding the number of students in a Lagos school who prefer either pounded yam or jollof rice for the end-of-year party, demonstrating how set theory organises real-world data effectively.
