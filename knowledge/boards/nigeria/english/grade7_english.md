# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 7 English

## Sentence Structure and Types
A sentence is a group of words expressing a complete thought. In Grade 7, you will learn four types. A declarative sentence makes a statement, for example, "Lagos is a bustling city." An interrogative sentence asks a question, such as, "Do you enjoy jollof rice?" An imperative sentence gives a command or request, like, "Please bring the kola nuts." An exclamatory sentence expresses strong emotion, for instance, "What a beautiful harmattan morning!" Each type begins with a capital letter and ends with a full stop, question mark, or exclamation mark.

## Parts of Speech: Verbs and Adverbs
Verbs are action words that tell what the subject of a sentence does, for example, "Students study for their exams." Adverbs are words that describe verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs, telling how, when, or where an action happens. For instance, "She quickly ran to the market" uses 'quickly' to describe how she ran. "He arrived yesterday" uses 'yesterday' to tell when he arrived. "The money is very old" uses 'very' to describe the adjective 'old'. These words add detail and clarity to your writing.

## Vocabulary Development and Spelling
Building your vocabulary is essential for clear communication. You will learn to identify and spell words correctly, focusing on common errors. For example, the word 'necessary' has one 'c' and two 's's. You will also expand your vocabulary by learning words related to Nigerian life and culture, such as 'naira', our currency, and 'balogun', a traditional title. Understanding word roots, prefixes, and suffixes can help you unlock the meaning of new words, making your reading and writing more precise and expressive.

## Comprehension Skills and Summarising
Comprehension is the ability to understand what you read. In Grade 7, you will learn strategies to identify the main idea and supporting details in a text. For example, in a passage about the harmattan season, the main idea might be its effect on daily life, while details include the dry, dusty air and cooler temperatures. A good summary captures the main points in your own words, without adding new information or opinions. This skill helps you process information effectively and demonstrates your understanding of any text you encounter.

## Writing: Formal Letters and Paragraphs
You will learn to write formal letters, such as job applications or complaints, using the correct structure. A formal letter has your address at the top, followed by the date, the recipient's address, a formal salutation like 'Dear Sir/Madam,' the body with clear paragraphs, and a closing like 'Yours faithfully.' You will also focus on writing well-structured paragraphs, with a topic sentence, supporting sentences with examples (like "The aroma of jollof rice fills the air"), and a concluding sentence. This ensures your writing is organised and easy for others to understand.

## Oral English: Pronunciation and Public Speaking
Clear pronunciation is key to effective oral communication. You will practise distinguishing and producing Nigerian English sounds correctly, paying attention to stress and intonation. For public speaking, you will learn to prepare and deliver short talks on familiar topics, such as "My Favourite Nigerian Dish" or "The Importance of Education". You will use clear voice, appropriate volume, and maintain eye contact with the audience. Good posture and avoiding filler words like 'um' and 'ah' will help you present your ideas confidently and professionally.
