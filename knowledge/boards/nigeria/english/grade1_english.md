# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 1 English

## Letter Sounds and Identification
Children learn to identify and name the 26 letters of the English alphabet. They associate each letter with its primary sound, for example, 'A' as in 'apple', 'B' as in 'ball', and 'C' as in 'cat'. They practice distinguishing between uppercase and lowercase letters. Activities involve pointing to letters when called, singing the alphabet song, and using flashcards. They begin to link letter sounds to simple picture objects found in their environment, like 'D' for 'dog' or 'E' for 'egg'. Mastery involves correctly naming letters and their sounds upon sight.

## Word Families and Simple Blending
Children are introduced to short vowel word families, such as the '-at' family (cat, hat, bat) and the '-an' family (can, man, pan). They learn to blend individual letter sounds to form simple, consonant-vowel-consonant (CVC) words like 'sun', 'pot', and 'pen'. This is done by saying the sounds of each letter in quick succession. They practice with word cards and simple pictures. Examples used include 'fan', 'mat', and 'pin'. The goal is to confidently decode and read these simple, phonetically regular words.

## Nouns: Naming Things Around Us
Children learn that nouns are words that name people, places, and things. They identify and name common nouns from their immediate Nigerian environment. Examples include 'mother', 'Lagos', 'school', 'book', 'yam', and 'naira'. They sort pictures into categories of people, places, and things. They practice using these nouns in simple sentences, such as "I see a market." or "The boy has a ball." They learn to use articles like 'a' and 'an' before singular nouns, for instance, "a car" or "an umbrella."

## Verbs: Action Words in Daily Life
Children are taught that verbs are action words. They learn to identify and use common action verbs from their daily routines. Examples include 'run', 'eat', 'go', 'play', 'read', and 'write'. They connect these verbs to familiar activities, such as 'eat' jollof rice or 'play' with friends. They practice forming simple, present-tense sentences where a subject performs an action, like "I write." or "She runs." The focus is on using the verb correctly to describe an action happening now.

## Speaking and Listening with Friends
Children develop essential speaking and listening skills. They learn to listen carefully to a speaker, such as a teacher telling a story. They practice taking turns to speak in small groups. They learn to speak clearly in full, simple sentences to share their ideas, for example, "I have a toy car." or "I like harmattan season." They are encouraged to ask simple questions like "What is this?". Activities include telling a short personal experience and listening to a partner speak without interruption.

## Reading Simple Sentences with Nigerian Flavour
Children apply their phonics knowledge to read short, decodable sentences. They blend individual sounds to read words like 'dog', 'sun', and 'mum'. They practice reading sentences composed of familiar words, such as "The big pig is in the mud." or "I see a red car." Context is enriched with Nigerian cultural references. They read simple sentences like "Mama buys beans." or "The boy eats rice." This builds confidence and reinforces the connection between spoken and written language.
