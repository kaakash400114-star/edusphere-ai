# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 3 English
## Reading Simple Texts
Students learn to read and understand simple texts using phonics. They identify short and long vowel sounds, such as in "cat" and "cake." They also recognize common consonant blends like "br" and "st." Students practice reading aloud with correct pronunciation and fluency. They answer simple questions about a story, like "Who is the main character?" or "Where did the story happen?" Teachers use familiar Nigerian stories and pictures to make learning easy and enjoyable.

## Writing Simple Sentences
Students learn to write complete sentences that start with a capital letter and end with a full stop. They use common nouns, like "school" and "Lagos," and action verbs, like "run" and "eat." They form simple sentences such as "The boy eats jollof rice." They also learn to write the alphabet clearly. They practice spacing words correctly on a line. This builds a strong foundation for future writing in English and other subjects.

## Spelling Common Words
Students learn to spell common words used daily. They memorize high-frequency sight words like "the," "and," "was," and "said." They learn simple spelling rules, such as adding "s" to make a word plural (e.g., "nut" becomes "kola nuts"). Students practice writing these words correctly in sentences. They use a picture of a "naira" coin to help them spell "naira." Regular practice helps them become better writers and readers.

## Understanding Basic Grammar
Students learn parts of speech like nouns and verbs. A noun is a person, place, or thing, like "teacher" or "harmattan." A verb is an action word, like "read" or "write." Students identify nouns and verbs in simple sentences. They learn to use "is" and "are" correctly, for example, "The soup is hot" or "The children are happy." This helps them build correct sentences that others can understand easily.

## Listening and Speaking Skills
Students practice listening carefully to stories and instructions from their teachers. They learn to follow one-step directions, like "Bring your book." They practice speaking in clear sentences, telling short stories about their family or weekend. They share information about Nigerian things, like "I ate jollof rice on Saturday." This helps them communicate their ideas effectively and respectfully in the classroom and community.

## Vocabulary Building
Students learn new words related to their daily lives in Nigeria. They learn words like "market," "yam," "singing," and "drum." They connect these words to pictures, such as a picture of a market scene. Students also learn words that describe things, like "big," "small," "hot," and "cold." They practice using these new words in simple sentences to talk about their experiences and the world around them.
