# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 3 English

## Reading Simple Texts
Students learn to read and understand simple texts using phonics. They identify short and long vowel sounds, such as in "cat" and "cake." They also recognize common consonant blends like "br" and "st." Students practice reading aloud with correct pronunciation and fluency. They answer simple questions about a story, like "Who is the main character?" or "Where did the story happen?" Teachers use familiar Nigerian stories and pictures to make learning easy and enjoyable.

## Writing Simple Sentences
Students learn to write complete sentences that start with a capital letter and end with a full stop. They use common nouns, like "school" and "Lagos," and action verbs, like "run" and "eat." They form simple sentences such as "The boy eats jollof rice." They also learn to write the alphabet clearly. They practice spacing words correctly on a line. This builds a strong foundation for future writing in English and other subjects.

## Spelling Common Words
Grade 3 students master the spelling of high-frequency words essential for daily communication and foundational literacy. They commit to memory essential sight words such as "with," "they," "have," and "from" through consistent repetition and contextual practice. Pupils apply fundamental spelling rules, including adding "-s" or "-es" to nouns to form plurals, as seen when "leaf" becomes "leaves" and "box" becomes "boxes". They also learn to add "-ed" to verbs to form the past tense, correctly spelling "walked" and "played". Students practice constructing sentences using these words to ensure correct application, integrating key national vocabulary like "naira" to support their development as confident readers and writers.

## Understanding Basic Grammar
Students will identify nouns as naming words for people, places, or things such as "mother," "Lagos," or "pencil," and verbs as action words like "run," "dance," or "teach." They will learn to use singular and plural subjects with the correct verb forms: singular subjects with "is" and plural subjects with "are." For example, in "The boy is tall," "The boy" is singular and uses "is," while in "The girls are tall," "The girls" is plural and uses "are." Students will practice distinguishing these parts of speech within simple, everyday sentences to ensure correct sentence construction and clear communication. This foundational knowledge builds the necessary skills for reading comprehension and effective writing.

## Listening and Speaking Skills
Students develop active listening skills by demonstrating comprehension of oral instructions and narratives through appropriate responses, actions, and summary questions. They practice articulating personal experiences using complete sentences, such as "I visited my grandmother in the village last holiday," to build fluency and confidence. Effective speaking methods include sequencing events logically and using appropriate volume for classroom interaction. Students learn to ask and answer questions to clarify information, for instance, inquiring, "What is the main food of your people?" to gather facts. They apply these skills by engaging in structured discussions about Nigerian cultural practices like naming ceremonies and traditional festivals.

## Vocabulary Building
Students build vocabulary through thematic instruction, identifying and learning new words related to familiar Nigerian contexts, such as "kokoro" and "akara" in the kitchen or "adire" and "gele" at cultural events. They expand their word banks by learning descriptive adjectives to compare and contrast objects, using examples like "tall" and "short" for buildings, or "sweet" and "sour" for fruits. A core method involves matching newly learned words to corresponding images, like linking "igwe" and "ulogu" to pictures of traditional attire, to reinforce meaning. Students practice constructing simple, complete sentences to incorporate these words, such as "The hot sun makes me sweat," to develop practical application skills.
