# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 5 English

## Parts of Speech
A noun is a word that names a person, place, animal, or thing. Examples of nouns include Lagos, Abuja, teacher, pupil, and harmattan. A verb is a word that shows action or a state of being. Action verbs include run, jump, sing, and cook. The verb "to be" (am, is, are) shows a state of being. Adjectives describe nouns. We use adjectives like big, small, red, and delicious. For example, "She bought a big bunch of kola nuts." A pronoun takes the place of a noun. Common pronouns are he, she, it, they, and we.

## Reading Comprehension
To understand what we read, we identify the main idea. The main idea is what the passage is mostly about. We find supporting details that explain the main idea. For example, a passage about Lagos might describe its busy markets and tall buildings as details. We also learn to make inferences. An inference is a conclusion based on the information given. If a character is sweating and it is the harmattan season, we can infer it is unusually hot. Answering "who," "what," "where," "when," "why," and "how" questions helps us understand the passage completely.

## Creative Writing
A story has a beginning, middle, and end. The beginning introduces the characters and the setting. For example, "Ade lived in a small village." The middle tells the problem and the events that happen. For example, "There was no water and Ade had to find some." The end is the solution to the problem. For example, "He found a stream and the village was saved." We use our senses to make our writing interesting. Instead of "The food was good," write "The jollof rice smelled spicy and tasted delicious."

## Spelling and Vocabulary
We learn common spelling rules. A 'y' at the end of a short word often makes a long 'e' sound, like in 'money' and 'happy'. When adding a suffix to a word that ends with a silent 'e', you usually drop the 'e'. For example, 'hope' becomes 'hoping'. We also learn new words. A 'village' is a small community in the countryside. A 'market' is a place where people buy and sell goods. A 'recipe' is a set of instructions for cooking a dish, like a recipe for jollof rice.

## Grammar and Punctuation
Every complete sentence must have a subject (who or what the sentence is about) and a predicate (what the subject is or does). For example, "The chef cooked the jollof rice." We use commas to separate items in a list, like "They bought rice, tomatoes, and pepper." We use capital letters for the names of people, places, and days. For example, "Monday" and "Nigeria". We use full stops (.) to end a statement and question marks (?) to end a question.

## Practical English
We learn to write and addresses correctly. The address is written in this order: Receiver's name, street number and name, town, and state. For example: Amina Garba, 15 Allen Avenue, Ikeja, Lagos. We also learn to use money correctly. The Nigerian Naira has coins and notes. We write '₦' before the amount. For example, "The price of the mango is ₦100." When talking to an elder or someone in authority, we show respect by using polite words like "please" and "thank you".
