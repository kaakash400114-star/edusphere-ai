# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 10 English
## Phonetics and Sound Patterns
Students learn the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) to transcribe Standard Nigerian English (SNE) sounds. They study vowel and consonant phonemes, focusing on features like vowel length and nasalization. They identify minimal pairs, such as /bʌt/ (but) and /bɔːt/ (bought), and practice distinguishing them. Students also analyze syllable structure, including open, closed, and consonant clusters, and practice intonation patterns in statements, questions, and exclamations using common Nigerian words like 'Nigeria' and 'Lagos'.

## Grammar and Sentence Structure
This module covers complex sentence construction, including coordinate, compound, and complex sentences using coordinating (and, but) and subordinating (because, although) conjunctions. Students learn to use non-finite verbs (infinitives, gerunds, and participles) correctly in sentences. They master the formation of passive voice and practice converting active sentences to passive ones, such as changing "The market sells fresh fish" to "Fresh fish is sold in the market". They also study subject-verb agreement with compound subjects.

## Vocabulary Development and Usage
Students focus on expanding their vocabulary through the study of word classes, affixes (prefixes, suffixes), and word formation processes like derivation and compounding. They learn to differentiate between formal and informal registers, and to choose appropriate vocabulary for specific contexts. They study idiomatic expressions and proverbs common in Nigerian culture, for example, "You no go hear word" and "The early bird catches the worm". They practice using contextual clues to infer the meaning of new words in passages.

## Comprehension and Summary Skills
Students develop the ability to read and understand a variety of texts, including excerpts from Nigerian literature, news articles, and speeches. They learn to identify the main idea, supporting details, logical arguments, and author's purpose. They practice answering different types of comprehension questions, such as literal, inferential, and evaluative. Furthermore, they are taught to summarize texts concisely, preserving the key points in their own words, using a passage about the Harmattan season or a local festival as a model.

## Creative Writing and Essay Techniques
This section teaches students the principles of effective essay writing. They learn to structure essays with a clear introduction, body paragraphs with topic sentences and supporting evidence, and a conclusion. They practice writing different genres, including narrative essays (e.g., "My Experience at the Lagos Carnival"), descriptive essays (e.g., "A Harmattan Morning in Sokoto"), and argumentative essays (e.g., "The Importance of Jollof Rice in Nigerian Culture"). They focus on coherence, unity, and appropriate language for each genre.

## Oral Communication and Speech Delivery
Students learn the principles of effective oral communication, including clarity, relevance, and proper articulation. They practice delivering speeches, debates, and oral presentations. They study non-verbal communication, such as eye contact, posture, and gestures. They learn to prepare and deliver impromptu and prepared speeches on topics relevant to Nigerian life, such as "The Economic Potential of Kola Nuts" or "Managing the Effects of the Harmattan". They also master techniques for handling questions and audience interaction.
