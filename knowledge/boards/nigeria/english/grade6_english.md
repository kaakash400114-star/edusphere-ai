# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 6 English
## Sentence Structure and Punctuation
A simple sentence contains one main clause with a subject and a predicate. Compound sentences join two main clauses with a coordinating conjunction like 'and', 'but', or 'so'. For example, "The Lagos market was busy, and we bought fresh fish". A complex sentence has a main clause and a subordinate clause. "Because of the harmattan, the school assembly was cancelled." Punctuation marks include the full stop (.), question mark (?), exclamation mark (!), comma (,), and apostrophe ('). Correct punctuation is essential for clear communication.

## Parts of Speech
A noun is a word for a person, place, thing, or idea, such as 'teacher', 'Abuja', or 'kola nut'. A pronoun takes the place of a noun, like 'he', 'she', or 'it'. A verb is a word showing action ('run') or a state of being ('is'). An adjective describes a noun, for example, a 'hot' jollof rice. An adverb describes a verb, telling how, when, or where an action happens, as in 'They speak loudly'. Adverbs often end in '-ly'. Understanding these parts helps build correct sentences.

## Reading Comprehension
To comprehend a text, students must identify the main idea and supporting details. They can ask questions like 'Who?', 'What?', 'Where?', and 'Why?' to understand the story's events. For a passage about a new Lagos bridge, students can list facts, such as its length and the number of cars it can hold. They should also infer the author's purpose and the character's feelings. Using strategies like predicting outcomes and summarising in their own words improves their ability to understand and remember what they read.

## Writing Skills
Writing clear, coherent paragraphs requires a topic sentence, supporting sentences, and a concluding sentence. The topic sentence introduces the main idea. Supporting sentences give details and examples. For instance, in a paragraph about the Nigerian naira, you could describe its features and value. A concluding sentence summarises the paragraph. Students learn to write letters, stories, and factual accounts, paying attention to correct spelling, grammar, and the logical order of ideas to ensure their writing is easy to follow.

## Vocabulary Development
Students expand their vocabulary by learning the meanings and spellings of new words related to their environment and studies. They understand synonyms (words with similar meanings, like 'big' and 'large') and antonyms (opposites, like 'buy' and 'sell'). They also learn idiomatic expressions used in Nigerian English, such as 'how far?' meaning 'hello'. Students are encouraged to use new words in sentences and to look up unfamiliar words in a dictionary to improve their communication skills in both written and spoken forms.

## Spelling and Phonics
Year 6 students master spelling rules for common vowel and consonant patterns. This includes understanding the 'i before e except after c' rule, as in 'receive' and 'believe'. They learn to spell words with silent letters, like 'knight' and 'know'. Phonics involves recognising the sound-letter correspondences to decode and spell unfamiliar words, for example, the 'sh' sound in 'she' and the 'kn' sound in 'kola nut'. Regular practice with spelling lists and dictation helps build accuracy in their writing.
