# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 2 English
## Listening and Speaking
Students learn to listen attentively for key details and follow simple, multi-step instructions. They will practise identifying the main characters and setting in a short story told aloud. For speaking, children are guided to speak clearly using full sentences to share personal experiences, such as "I ate jollof rice for lunch." They participate in class discussions by taking turns and responding directly to a classmate's comment. The goal is to build confidence in oral communication and respectful listening, using correct pronunciation of common Nigerian words.

## Reading Readiness and Alphabetical Order
Students will master the names and sounds of all 26 letters of the English alphabet. They will learn to put a group of five to seven mixed-up letters into the correct alphabetical order, for example, arranging "e, b, d, a, c" as "a, b, c, d, e." They will also identify the first letter in simple, common Nigerian words like "Lagos," "Naira," and "Harmattan." This skill forms the foundation for using dictionaries and finding information in alphabetical lists, a crucial early literacy tool.

## High-Frequency Word Recognition
Learners will read and spell a list of 30 high-frequency words instantly by sight. These words are not easily decodable using letter sounds, such as "the," "and," "was," "said," "they," and "here." Children will practise these words through flashcards and by locating them in simple, repetitive texts. For example, they will find and read the word "the" in the sentence "The sun is hot during Harmattan." This automatic recognition is essential for reading fluency and comprehension.

## Phonics: Consonant Vowel Consonant (CVC) Words
Students will learn to blend the sounds of letters to read simple three-letter Consonant Vowel Consonant (CVC) words. They will learn the one-to-one sound correspondence for each letter and apply it to words like "cat," "pin," "sun," and "dog." Using pictures of familiar objects like a "nut" or a "pot," children will segment the word into its initial, middle, and final sounds to read and spell it accurately. This skill is fundamental for decoding new, unfamiliar words.

## Basic Handwriting and Letter Formation
Pupils will learn to form all capital and lowercase letters of the alphabet correctly. They will practise tracing and then writing letters within lines, focusing on correct starting points and strokes. For instance, they will learn to write a lowercase 'a' by starting at the top, making a circle, and adding a straight line down. They will write their own name using proper capitalisation. Correct and neat handwriting is developed through guided practice and the use of appropriate writing tools like pencils.

## Storytelling and Creative Expression
Children will learn the basic structure of a simple story: a beginning, a middle, and an end. They will listen to stories featuring Nigerian settings and characters. Using picture prompts of items like kola nuts or a plate of jollof rice, students will work in groups to create a short, sequential story. They will express their ideas by completing simple sentences like "The man bought..." or "The children shared...", encouraging creativity and an early appreciation for narrative structure.
