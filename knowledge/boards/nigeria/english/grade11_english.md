# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 11 English

## Narrative Essay Structure
A narrative essay tells a story, requiring a clear plot structure. It begins with an orientation to establish the setting, characters, and context. The complication introduces a conflict or problem, creating suspense. The crisis is the turning point, the peak of the tension. The resolution shows the outcome and how the conflict is resolved. Finally, the coda provides a concluding comment or reflection, often a moral of the story. For example, a student might write about preparing for a JAMB exam, facing the stress of the harmattan season, and ultimately succeeding.

## Use of Modals in Argumentative Writing
Modals are auxiliary verbs used to express possibility, necessity, or obligation. In arguments, they are crucial for persuading readers. 'Must' is used for strong obligation, such as 'The government must invest in education'. 'Should' expresses advice or recommendation, like 'Students should prioritise their studies'. 'Can' indicates ability or possibility, for instance, 'Technology can bridge the educational gap'. 'May' is used for permission or possibility, for example, 'We may see improvements in our national economy'. Proper use strengthens a writer's position and adds nuance to their claims.

## Idiomatic Expressions in Context
Idiomatic expressions are phrases whose figurative meaning differs from their literal meaning. They add vividness to language. For instance, 'to burn the midnight oil' means to study or work late into the night. To 'go the extra mile' means to make a special effort beyond what is required. If a friend asks for help with a difficult problem, you might say, 'I'll go the extra mile to find the solution'. Using the correct idiom in context demonstrates a high level of language proficiency and cultural understanding in Nigerian English.

## Comprehension of Expository Texts
Expository texts explain or inform using facts and logic. To comprehend them, students identify the main idea, which is the central argument or message. They must distinguish between the main idea and supporting details. They analyse how the author uses facts, statistics, and examples to build their case. For example, an expository text about the challenges of Lagos traffic would present statistics, discuss infrastructure problems, and provide examples of daily commutes, all to explain the complex nature of the issue.

## Business Letter Format and Conventions
A formal business letter follows a strict structure. The writer's address and date are at the top left. The recipient's address follows below. The subject line summarises the letter's purpose. The salutation is formal, using 'Dear Sir/Madam,'. The body is concise, organised into clear paragraphs, with each paragraph developing one point. The closing is 'Yours faithfully,' if the salutation was generic, or 'Yours sincerely,' if a name was used, followed by the sender's full name. A business letter must be clear, professional, and free of slang.

## Literary Appreciation: The Novel
A novel is a long fictional narrative exploring character and theme. Students analyse the protagonist and antagonist, their motivations, and how they drive the plot. They examine the setting and its influence on the story, such as the bustling life of Lagos shaping a character's journey. Themes are the underlying ideas, like justice, love, or the impact of the harmattan on village life. Students use textual evidence to support their interpretations, quoting specific passages to explain how characters develop and themes are revealed throughout the novel.
