# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 12 English
## Introduction to Advanced Composition
Students learn to construct argumentative essays using the Toulmin model, establishing claims with qualified evidence. A typical essay presents a clear thesis statement, followed by three well-developed body paragraphs, each with a topic sentence, supporting details, and analysis. Students must refute counter-arguments to demonstrate critical thinking. Examples drawn from Nigerian socio-economic issues, such as the impact of fuel subsidy removal or the "Not Too Young to Run" Act, are used to illustrate persuasive writing techniques and foster national discourse.
## Advanced Literary Appreciation
In this module, students analyze complex literary devices in post-colonial African prose and drama. The study focuses on narrative techniques like stream of consciousness, symbolism, and dramatic irony. Students learn to interpret themes of cultural conflict, identity, and social justice using texts like "The Interpreters" by Wole Soyinka. Class discussions explore how authors use setting, such as the bustling streets of Lagos or the quiet of an Igbo village, to reflect the Nigerian experience and comment on universal human conditions.
## Nigerian Folklore and Oral Traditions
Students examine the structure and function of Nigerian oral narratives, including myths, legends, and folktales. The module covers the role of the griot or storyteller in preserving cultural history. Students identify recurring archetypes, such as the trickster figure (like the tortoise), and analyze their moral lessons. Practical exercises involve retelling a well-known folktale, such as the story of "Anansi the Spider," and explaining its relevance to contemporary Nigerian society and values.
## Grammar and Lexis for Academic Purposes
This section covers advanced grammatical structures essential for academic writing, including complex sentence types, participle phrases, and conditional sentences. Students learn to use precise academic lexis, distinguishing between formal and informal registers. Exercises involve correcting sentences with common errors in subject-verb agreement and tense usage, particularly in relation to Nigerian English conventions. For example, a student might be asked to refine a sentence describing the harmattan season to use more formal language for a research paper.
## Rhetoric and Public Speaking
Students master the principles of effective public speaking, including structuring a speech, employing rhetorical devices, and managing non-verbal communication. Techniques such as ethos, pathos, and logos are taught with Nigerian examples, like a persuasive speech on the importance of agriculture. The module also covers the art of parliamentary debate, teaching students to propose motions, argue points, and deliver rebuttals with clarity and conviction, often using topics relevant to the Nigerian National Assembly.
## Media and Information Literacy
This critical module teaches students to analyze and evaluate information from various media sources in the Nigerian context. Students learn to identify bias in news reporting, distinguish between fact and opinion, and understand the impact of social media on public perception. They examine case studies, such as the spread of misinformation during elections or about public health crises, and learn to use credible sources like The Punch or Vanguard to verify information before forming an opinion or sharing it.
