# Nigerian (NERDC) Grade 8 English

## Parts of Speech: Introduction and Nouns
A noun is a word that names a person, place, animal, thing, or idea. Common nouns name general items, such as *book*, *market*, or *teacher*. Proper nouns name specific people or places and always begin with a capital letter, like *Lagos* or *Ngozi*. Abstract nouns name ideas or qualities that cannot be seen, such as *freedom* or *honesty*. Collective nouns name a group of individuals, like *committee* or *family*. For example, the *family* attended a wedding in Abuja. Recognizing different types of nouns is essential for constructing clear and grammatically correct sentences.

## Verbs: Tenses and Voice
A verb is a word that expresses an action or a state of being. It is the most important word in a sentence. The English language has twelve tenses, including the simple present, present continuous, simple past, and past continuous. For example, in the simple present, we say "She *sells* kola nuts." In the past continuous, we say "They *were cooking* jollof rice." Verbs also have voice: active voice, where the subject performs the action (The student *wrote* the essay), and passive voice, where the subject receives the action (The essay *was written* by the student).

## Prepositions and Conjunctions
A preposition is a word that shows the relationship between a noun or pronoun and other words in a sentence, often indicating location or time. Common prepositions include *in*, *on*, *at*, *under*, and *near*. For instance, we store the yams *in* the barn. Conjunctions are words that join words, phrases, or clauses. There are three types: coordinating (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so), subordinating (after, although, because, if, when), and correlative (either/or, neither/nor). For example, "She bought rice *and* beans *because* she wanted to cook jollof rice."

## Punctuation and Capitalization
Proper punctuation and capitalization are essential for clear written communication. Commas are used to separate items in a list and to separate introductory clauses. The full stop (.) marks the end of a declarative sentence. Question marks (?) are used for questions, while exclamation points (!) show strong emotion. In Nigerian English, we write the naira symbol as "₦". Capital letters are used at the beginning of sentences, for proper nouns (such as *Lagos* or *Harmattan*), and for the pronoun 'I'. For example, "During the *Harmattan*, the dust from the north covers *Lagos*."

## Reading Comprehension and Analysis
Reading comprehension is the ability to understand, interpret, and analyze written text. Students learn to identify the main idea, supporting details, and author's purpose. When analysing a text, students distinguish between facts and opinions. For instance, a fact is "The official currency is the Nigerian naira," while an opinion is "Jollof rice is the best food." Students are taught to make inferences based on clues within the text and to summarize information in their own words, demonstrating a deep understanding of the material they have read.

## Composition: Writing Paragraphs and Narratives
Effective composition involves structuring ideas logically into paragraphs. A paragraph begins with a topic sentence that introduces the main idea. Supporting sentences provide details and examples, and the concluding sentence summarises the paragraph. For a narrative, students must include characters, setting, and plot, writing in a sequential manner. For example, a story might begin: "Young Chidi walked through the bustling market in Onitsha, his eyes searching for a special kola nut to present to his guest." Clear and well-structured writing ensures that ideas are communicated effectively to the reader.
