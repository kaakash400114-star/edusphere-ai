# French (Éducation nationale) Grade 9 English

## Literary Analysis and Textual Comparison
Students will learn to compare two literary texts, such as an excerpt from *The Little Prince* by Antoine de Saint-Exupéry and a modern poem, focusing on themes of friendship and growing up. They must identify narrative techniques like symbolism (e.g., the rose) and analyse characters' motivations. The comparative approach requires identifying similarities and differences in structure, perspective, and use of literary devices. Students will support their analysis with precise quotations and contextual references, including the author's French background, to demonstrate how cultural context influences theme development in both texts.

## Argumentation and Structured Debate
Pupils will learn to construct a structured argument using the *thèse/antithèse/synthèse* (thesis/antithesis/synthesis) model. They must select a relevant topic, such as the environmental impact of the Tour de France, and formulate a clear thesis statement. Each argument must be supported by evidence, logical reasoning, and counter-arguments. Debates will require formal language, appropriate transition phrases, and respectful rebuttal techniques. Students will evaluate the effectiveness of persuasive strategies used in French media and political discourse, focusing on rhetorical devices and appeals to logic and emotion.

## Grammatical Precision and Complex Structures
The curriculum focuses on mastering complex grammatical structures, including the subjunctive mood, relative clauses, and inversion for questions. Students will practise forming sentences to express hypotheses (*Si j'étais riche, j'achèterais un vélo de compétition*) and wishes (*Il faut que je parte à la boulangerie*). They will learn to correctly use pronouns such as *y* and *en* in varied contexts, for example, *Je pense à cette recette, j'y pense souvent*. Exercises will include correcting errors in sentences related to daily life in France, ensuring agreement in gender and number with precision.

## Media Literacy and Digital Communication
Students will analyse diverse media texts, including a news report about the Paris Métro or a blog post comparing French and English school systems. They will identify the purpose, target audience, and persuasive techniques used. In digital communication, they will learn to write formal emails requesting information and to construct concise social media posts. Emphasis will be placed on adapting tone, register, and format for different platforms. A key activity involves evaluating the reliability of online sources and distinguishing between factual reporting and opinionated commentary.

## Intercultural Understanding and Identity Exploration
This theme involves exploring the concept of identity through French and English cultural lenses. Students will compare traditions, such as the importance of family Sunday meals (*le déjeuner du dimanche*) versus British roast dinners. Using authentic materials, they will analyse how cultural values are reflected in language. A key task is to create a presentation on a French cultural aspect, such as the role of *la boulangerie* in the community, highlighting differences and similarities with local traditions. This fosters an appreciation of cultural diversity and its impact on personal and national identity.

## Functional Writing and Project Management
Students will learn to write functional texts for specific purposes, such as a formal complaint letter about a faulty product purchased in euros or an itinerary for a school trip to Paris. Project management involves planning, drafting, revising, and editing written work. Emphasis is placed on clarity, conciseness, and appropriate formatting. They will apply these skills to group projects, creating a detailed proposal for a school event, demonstrating ability to meet deadlines and collaborate effectively while maintaining a formal tone throughout the documentation process.
