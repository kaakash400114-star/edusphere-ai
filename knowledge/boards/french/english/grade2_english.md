# French (Éducation nationale) Grade 2 English

## Greetings and Polite Expressions
Children learn to use standard greetings for different times of day: 'Hello', 'Good morning', 'Good afternoon', 'Good evening'. They practise polite forms when speaking to adults, using 'Please' (s'il vous plaît) when asking for something and 'Thank you' (merci) when receiving help. They learn to respond to 'How are you?' with 'I'm fine, thank you' and to say 'Excuse me' (excusez-moi) to get attention politely. Simple apologies like 'I'm sorry' (je suis désolé) are also introduced for everyday interactions.

## Classroom Commands and Objects
Pupils understand and use common classroom commands in English, such as 'Listen', 'Look', 'Open your book', and 'Stand up'. They learn the names of essential school objects, including a 'pencil' (un crayon), a 'rubber' (une gomme), a 'ruler' (une règle), and a 'notebook' (un cahier). They respond to 'Where is the...?' by identifying object locations with 'It's on the table' or 'It's under the chair'. These terms are practised through physical activities and games in the classroom.

## Counting to 30 and Simple Maths
Children confidently count from 1 to 30, recognising both numerals (1, 2, 3) and number words (one, two, three). They learn to count objects up to 30, such as ' euros' or ' crayons'. They are introduced to simple addition problems using numbers up to 10, solving problems like '2 + 3 = 5'. They also learn to identify and name basic 2D shapes, including a 'circle' (un cercle), a 'square' (un carré), and a 'triangle' (un triangle), often using examples from everyday life.

## Daily Routines and Time
Pupils learn to talk about their daily routines using phrases like 'I wake up', 'I eat breakfast', and 'I go to school'. They understand and use key time-related vocabulary, such as 'morning', 'afternoon', 'evening', and 'night'. They learn to tell the time to the hour on an analogue clock, responding to questions like 'What time is it?' with answers like 'It's seven o'clock'. The structure of a typical school day, including 'playtime' (la récréation), is a core part of this learning.

## Family and Friends
Children learn to name immediate family members in English: 'mother' (la mère), 'father' (le père), 'sister' (la sœur), and 'brother' (le frère). They use the possessive 's' to state relationships, for example, 'This is my mother'. They learn simple descriptive words for people, such as 'tall', 'short', 'big', and 'small', to describe their friends or family members. They practise asking 'Who is this?' and responding with 'This is my...'.

## French Culture and Traditions
Pupils are introduced to simple elements of French culture to connect their learning to their daily life. They learn about food from the 'boulangerie', such as a 'croissant' or a 'baguette'. They are exposed to the concept of the 'Tour de France' as a famous cycling race. They learn the name of a common mode of transport in France, the 'métro', and understand it is used in cities like Paris. This section uses visuals and simple conversations to make the culture tangible.
