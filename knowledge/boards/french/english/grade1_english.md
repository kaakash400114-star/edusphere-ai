# French (Éducation nationale) Grade 1 English


## Everyday Language
Pupils acquire foundational oral skills to interact in everyday classroom situations, mastering the essential linguistic structures for introductions, object identification, and social etiquette. They learn to formulate simple sentences to introduce themselves using the pattern "My name is..." and to identify others with "This is...". Vocabulary acquisition focuses on common classroom objects, with pupils expected to name items such as "pencil case", "scissors", and "rubber". Core social language is taught through structured practice of greetings like "Hello" and "Good morning", and farewells including "Goodbye". Finally, pupils develop politeness by learning to frame requests with the formula "Can I have..., please?" to obtain classroom materials.

## Colours and Numbers
Pupils learn to name and identify the primary colours: red, blue, yellow, green, and orange in English. They link these to familiar French items, like a "rouge" strawberry or a "bleu" pencil. They count from 1 to 20 in English, using real objects like blocks or counters. They learn to state their age, for example, "I am six years old". They use numbers to identify quantities, such as "I have three books" or "There are ten euros".

## Action Words
Pupils learn to understand and use common English action words (verbs) to describe activities. These include run, jump, walk, sing, dance, draw, and listen. They respond to simple commands like "Stand up", "Sit down", and "Open your book". They use these words in short sentences to describe their own actions, for example, "I like to draw" or "I can jump high". This links to physical play and movement in the classroom.

## Familiar People and Places
Pupils acquire and use specific vocabulary to identify members of their family, such as mother, father, brother, and sister, and key figures in their immediate community like teacher and friend. They learn the English names for familiar locations within their daily environment, including home, school, park, shop, and classroom, alongside specifying functions like the canteen or library. The pedagogical focus is on constructing simple, declarative sentences using the verb "to be" (e.g., "He is my friend") and the verb "to have" (e.g., "I have a brother") to describe these people and places. Students also practice forming basic affirmative sentences with action verbs in the simple present tense, for instance, "I play in the park" or "We go to the library." This thematic approach is consolidated through activities involving labelling drawings of their classroom environment and engaging in short oral dialogues to introduce themselves and others.

## Rhymes and Songs
Pupils acquire vocabulary and sentence structures through exposure to simple English chants, rhymes, and songs featuring repetitive and predictable language patterns. The pedagogical method focuses on call-and-response and echo singing to reinforce oral comprehension and production. Key learning objectives include developing phonological awareness through the identification and manipulation of rhyming words and initial sounds, using songs like "Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star" and "Five Little Ducks" as concrete examples. This musical and kinaesthetic approach involves associating lyrics with physical actions, embedding language learning in a multisensory, playful context. These activities systematically build a foundational repertoire of oral language structures and sounds, which are essential for subsequent literacy acquisition.

## Simple Stories and Narratives
Pupils listen to and understand short, simple stories read by the teacher. The stories are familiar, often involving animals or a daily routine. They learn to answer simple questions about the story, like "What is the dog's name?" or "Where did the boy go?". They can sequence pictures from a story in the correct order. They may begin to use a few English words to retell a familiar story, like "The Tour de France is a big race for cyclists".
