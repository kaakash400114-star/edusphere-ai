# French (Éducation nationale) Grade 3 English


## Oral Communication: Guided Discussions
Students learn to express simple opinions and participate in structured dialogues. They practice asking and answering questions about their daily lives, using phrases like "What is your favourite...?" and "I like... because...". For example, "What is your animal préféré? My préféré is the chien because it is gentil." They learn to listen to their peers and react with basic comments such as "Interesting!" or "I agree," fostering respectful conversation skills in a supportive classroom environment.

## Phonics and Spelling: Consonant Digraphs
Students systematically learn the core French consonant digraphs, each representing a unique sound: 'ch' (/ʃ/) as in 'chaîne', 'sh' (/ʃ/) as in 'show', 'th' (/t/) as in 'thé', and 'ph' (/f/) as in 'photo'. They decode these digraphs as single units, not as individual letters, to master pronunciation and spelling. Instruction involves identifying these digraphs in context, such as in 'chat' (/ʃa/) and 'phare' (/faʁ/). Students practice applying these phonetic rules to decode increasingly complex vocabulary, including loanwords from English like 'shampooing' (/ʃɑ̃.pwɛ̃/) and 'shopping' (/ʃɔ.pɛ̃/), building foundational accuracy in their reading and spelling skills.

## Reading Comprehension: Simple Narratives
Students are taught to systematically identify key narrative elements: the main character, the setting, and the central event. They learn to distinguish between explicit details stated in the text and implied information. A core skill is formulating answers to literal comprehension questions directly supported by the story, such as naming the main character or identifying the story's location. For instance, after reading about a child buying a baguette, a student can extract the main character, "Thomas," the setting, "a boulangerie," and a key event, "buying a baguette," from the illustrations and text. They practice summarizing the story in a simple sentence using these elements.

## Writing: Simple Sentences and Paragraphs
Students learn to construct simple, grammatically correct sentences by identifying a subject and a conjugated verb, such as "Le chat dort" (The cat sleeps). They master the essential rules of capitalisation for sentence beginnings and proper nouns like "Paris," and correctly place periods to mark sentence termination. Building on this, they progress to forming cohesive paragraphs by combining three to four related sentences on familiar themes. They achieve this by using connecting words like 'et' (and) or 'mais' (but), ensuring a logical flow of ideas, for example, describing a school activity. The ultimate goal is to write a short, unified paragraph that presents a complete thought about a personal experience.

## Grammar: Articles and the Verb 'To Be'
Students in Éducation nationale Grade 3 master the definite article 'le', 'la', 'l'' and the indefinite article 'un', 'une', 'des', learning to correctly identify nouns that require them based on specificity and number. They apply articles in simple declarative sentences such as "Elle mange une pomme" or "Le garçon lit un livre". In parallel, they master the conjugation of the verb être in the present tense—je suis, tu es, il/elle est, nous sommes, vous êtes, ils/elles sont—by constructing sentences like "Je suis étudiant" and "Marie est à l'école". Students learn to integrate these grammatical elements into cohesive sentences using proper nouns and vocabulary from their thematic units, ensuring correct subject-verb agreement and article-noun concordance. This reinforces foundational skills for constructing clear and accurate communication in both written and spoken French.

## Cultural Knowledge: French Life and Customs
Students will acquire specific vocabulary to describe daily life in France, focusing on food items such as *croissant* and *pain au chocolat*, and modes of transport like *métro* and *vélo*. They will identify and name significant national landmarks, including the Tour Eiffel and the Louvre Museum. Students will learn about major cultural events, such as the Tour de France bicycle race, to understand their place in French society. They will compare these elements of French life to their own experiences, using simple comparative structures. This factual knowledge provides a foundational understanding of French cultural practices within the national curriculum.
