# French (Éducation nationale) Grade 3 English

## Oral Communication: Guided Discussions
Students learn to express simple opinions and participate in structured dialogues. They practice asking and answering questions about their daily lives, using phrases like "What is your favourite...?" and "I like... because...". For example, "What is your animal préféré? My préféré is the chien because it is gentil." They learn to listen to their peers and react with basic comments such as "Interesting!" or "I agree," fostering respectful conversation skills in a supportive classroom environment.

## Phonics and Spelling: Consonant Digraphs
Students master common consonant digraphs and their corresponding sounds. They learn 'sh' as in 'shop', 'ch' as in 'chat' (cat), 'th' as in 'think', and 'ph' as in 'phone'. For example, the word 'metro' is pronounced /meɪtroʊ/ using the 'm' and 'tr' sounds. They practice these in simple words like 'chat', 'feu', and 'thé', building their ability to decode unfamiliar French loanwords and English vocabulary with increasing accuracy.

## Reading Comprehension: Simple Narratives
Students read short illustrated stories to identify the main character, setting, and a simple event. They focus on answering literal questions about the text, such as "Who is the main character?" or "Where does the story take place?". For example, after reading a story about a child buying a baguette, a student might answer, "The main character is a boy named Thomas. The story takes place in a boulangerie."

## Writing: Simple Sentences and Paragraphs
Students learn to write simple, grammatically correct sentences using a subject and a verb. They practice using capital letters for the beginning of sentences and names, and full stops at the end. For example, "I visit the Louvre museum in Paris." They then progress to writing short, cohesive paragraphs of 3-4 sentences on familiar topics, like describing their favourite meal or outing, using connecting words like 'and' or 'but'.

## Grammar: Articles and the Verb 'To Be'
Students learn the correct use of the definite articles 'the' and indefinite articles 'a' and 'an'. For example, "I eat an apple for my goûter." They also master the conjugation of the verb 'to be' (am, is, are) in simple present tense sentences. For example, "He is happy," "They are at the school," reinforcing this with exercises involving proper nouns like "Marie is from Lyon."

## Cultural Knowledge: French Life and Customs
Students explore aspects of daily life in France. They learn vocabulary related to food, such as 'croissant', 'pain au chocolat', and 'fromage'. They discover French landmarks like the Tour Eiffel and learn about the Tour de France bicycle race. Students also learn about transportation, understanding the words 'métro', 'bus', and 'velo', comparing them to forms of transport they know in their own community.
