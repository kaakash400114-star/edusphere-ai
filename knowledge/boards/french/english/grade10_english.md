# French (Éducation nationale) Grade 10 English
## World Literature and Literary Analysis
Students analyse Anglophone literary works from the 20th and 21st centuries, focusing on thematic development and stylistic devices. They learn to identify and explain literary concepts such as 'point of vue' (point of view), 'symbolisme' (symbolism), and 'ironie' (irony) using specific textual evidence. For example, a student might explain the symbolic use of the 'métro' in a poem by T.S. Eliot to represent urban anonymity. Emphasis is placed on structuring analytical essays with a clear 'thèse' (thesis), 'argumentation' (argumentation), and 'conclusion' (conclusion) supported by precise quotations.

## Advanced Grammatical Structures and Syntax
The curriculum focuses on mastering complex grammatical rules essential for academic writing and formal communication. Students study the conditional and subjunctive moods to express hypotheses, wishes, and contrary-to-fact situations, for instance, 'Si j’avais plus d’argent, j’achèterais une maison près de la boulangerie' (If I had more money, I would buy a house near the bakery). They also practice analysing and using subordinate clauses, reported speech, and passive voice to enhance sentence variety and precision in their expression.

## Research Methodology and Academic Writing
Students are taught a structured approach to academic research and composition, including how to formulate a 'question de recherche' (research question) and develop a 'plan de travail' (work plan). They learn to use primary and secondary sources, correctly citing references using standard formats. The module emphasizes the importance of 'originalité' (originality) and 'rigueur' (rigour) in academic writing. For example, a student would write a research paper on the economic impact of the 'Tour de France', using statistics on the number of 'euros' generated for host cities and synthesising findings from various articles.

## Oral Expression and Debate
This section develops formal speaking skills through structured debates and oral presentations. Students learn to present a structured 'argumentaire' (argumentation) with a clear 'introduction', 'développement', and 'conclusion'. They practice using persuasive language, 'transition logique' (logical transitions), and responding to 'questions du public' (questions from the audience). An activity might involve a class debate on the merits of a 4-day work week, with students using data and clear rhetoric to support their 'proposition' (proposition) or 'opposition' (opposition).

## Media and Cultural Studies
Students critically analyse various forms of media (print, digital, television) and their influence on society. They learn to identify persuasive techniques, such as 'propagande' (propaganda) and 'biais' (bias), in news articles or advertisements. Using French examples, students might compare the media coverage of a 'grève' (strike) in French and English newspapers. The curriculum also explores cultural representations in film and television, analysing how media reflects or shapes societal values in different Francophone and Anglophone contexts.

## Socio-Cultural Themes and Intercultural Communication
This module explores contemporary themes through an intercultural lens, examining concepts such as 'identité' (identity), 'multiculturalisme' (multiculturalism), and 'mondialisation' (globalisation). Students study how social issues are perceived differently across cultures. They engage in projects like comparing the cultural significance of a 'pique-nique' in France with a 'barbecue' in Australia, or analysing consumerism through the lens of French 'salles de cinéma' (cinemas) versus American 'drive-in theatres'. The goal is to foster 'compréhension interculturelle' (intercultural understanding).
