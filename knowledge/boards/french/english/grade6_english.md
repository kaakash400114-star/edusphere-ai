# French (Éducation nationale) Grade 6 English

## Grammar Basics and Sentence Structure
Students review the fundamental components of English sentences, focusing on the roles of subject, verb, and object. They learn to identify simple subjects, both singular and plural, and conjugate common regular verbs in the present tense, such as 'to be' and 'to have'. Lessons cover the correct placement of adjectives, generally following the noun in English (e.g., 'a red car'). Practice involves constructing complete sentences and correcting fragments, using examples from daily life like 'the cat sleeps' or 'they buy bread at the boulangerie'.

## Vocabulary Building: Thematic Units
Vocabulary acquisition is thematic. Students learn terms related to school (teacher, notebook, cafeteria), food (croissant, cheese, pain au chocolat), and local geography (Paris, the Loire Valley). They are introduced to synonyms and antonyms to expand their lexical range, such as 'big' and 'small'. Lessons include common verbs like 'parler' (to speak), 'écouter' (to listen), and 'manger' (to eat), with students required to form simple sentences using these new words in context.

## Reading Comprehension and Analysis
Students read short texts, including fictional stories and simple non-fiction passages about French culture, such as the Tour de France or the Paris Métro system. They practice identifying the main idea, locating specific details, and making inferences. Comprehension questions require students to use evidence from the text, for example, 'Where does the character buy their lunch?'. The focus is on understanding sequence, cause and effect, and the author's purpose, using authentic materials adapted for their level.

## Writing: Descriptive and Narrative Texts
Students learn to write descriptive sentences using sensory details and adjectives, for example, describing the 'beautiful gardens' of a château. They practice structuring simple narrative paragraphs with a clear beginning, middle, and end. Lessons cover the use of past simple tense for completed actions ('I visited the Louvre'). Writing tasks include composing short emails or postcards, incorporating vocabulary from thematic units, such as describing a meal eaten in a Parisian café.

## Oral Communication: Dialogue and Presentation
This unit develops spoken fluency and listening skills. Students engage in simple dialogues, such as ordering food at a 'restaurant' or asking for directions to the 'musée'. They practice clear pronunciation of common sounds and intonation patterns. The module includes a short prepared presentation on a personal topic, like their family or a hobby, using visual aids. They learn to ask and answer basic 'wh' questions, for example, 'How much does a ticket to the Tour de France cost?'.

## Cultural Awareness: French and English Perspectives
Students explore cultural similarities and differences between France and English-speaking countries. Topics include daily routines (e.g., comparing the French school schedule to a typical American day), food habits, and holidays. They learn specific French terms used in English, like 'rendez-vous' or 'cuisine'. Lessons involve comparing prices in euros and dollars or discussing the significance of national events, fostering an understanding of cultural context while expanding their communicative competence.
