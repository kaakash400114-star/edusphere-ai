# French (Éducation nationale) Grade 5 English


## Understanding Simple Stories and Narratives
Students learn to identify the key elements of a story: the characters, the setting, and the plot. They practice distinguishing between the beginning (introduction of characters and situation), the middle (development of events and a small problem or *le pépin*), and the end (the solution). They can retell a story in a logical sequence using connectors like 'first', 'then', 'after that', and 'finally'. For example, they can recount the story of a character who visits a *boulangerie* to buy a croissant for breakfast.

## Asking and Answering Questions About Daily Life
Students learn to formulate questions using interrogative words such as 'What?', 'Where?', 'When?', 'Who?', and 'Why?', applying correct word order, for instance, "What time do you wake up?" or "Where do you buy your baguette?". They practice these skills by conducting interviews with classmates about their daily routines and activities. Methodology involves using question stems to build queries about specific topics like family, school schedules, hobbies, and transportation, such as taking the métro. The objective is for students to understand the function of each interrogative word to elicit particular types of information. Finally, they must provide answers in complete, grammatically correct sentences using relevant vocabulary related to daily life in France.

## Describing People, Places, and Things with Adjectives
Students master descriptive adjectives to provide precise information about people, places, and things. They learn to form the feminine of adjectives, typically adding an -e (e.g., *grand* becomes *grande*), and must agree in gender and number with the noun they modify (e.g., *un garçon grand*, *une fille grande*). The comparative is formed using *plus...que* (e.g., *plus grand que* - taller than) for adjectives with fewer than two syllables, and *plus...que* for longer adjectives (e.g., *plus intéressante que* - more interesting than). They also practice using the superlative, *le/la/plus...de* (e.g., *le plus grand cycliste* - the greatest cyclist), to create detailed comparisons.

## Understanding and Using Basic Time References
Students master the reading of analogue and digital clocks to the minute and use this skill to schedule daily activities. They interpret and employ key temporal markers such as *tous les matins* (every morning), *hier* (yesterday), and *dès que possible* (as soon as possible). They learn to form and understand questions like *« À quelle heure est la réunion ? »* (What time is the meeting?) and responses indicating specific moments in the day. Concretely, this allows them to create simple timetables or sequences of events using phrases like *« Je fais mes devoirs à 16h30 tous les jeudis. »* (I do my homework at 4:30 every Thursday). They also practice calculating elapsed time between two given events, such as determining the duration of a school outing from start to finish times.

## Recognising and Using High-Frequency Verbs
Students focus on common verbs used to describe actions and states of being, such as 'to be', 'to have', 'to go', 'to like', and 'to play'. They learn to conjugate these verbs in the simple present tense (e.g., "I go, you go, he/she goes") and use them to form affirmative, negative, and interrogative sentences. For example, they can state, "I *joue* au football," or ask, "Do you *acheter* des jouets?"

## Learning About French Culture and Symbols
Students discover key elements of French culture through simple texts and pictures. They learn to identify and name important symbols like the *tricolore* flag, the Eiffel Tower, and the national animal, the Gallic rooster. They are introduced to concepts such as *la quinzaine* (a two-week holiday) and discuss how people in France celebrate events like *la Bastille*. They also learn about products like *le fromage* and *le vin* in a simple, descriptive way.
