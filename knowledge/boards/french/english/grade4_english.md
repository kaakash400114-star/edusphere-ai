# French (Éducation nationale) Grade 4 English

## Written Communication: Building Paragraphs
Students learn to structure simple paragraphs around a central idea, often introduced by a topic sentence. They use linking words such as *first*, *next*, *then*, and *finally* to sequence events logically, for example, describing the steps to buy a baguette at a *boulangerie*. Capitalization rules for proper nouns like the *Tour de France* or the *métro* are reinforced. Punctuation, including commas in lists (e.g., "carrots, potatoes, and onions") and periods to end statements, is practiced to ensure clarity in their writing.

## Oral Expression: Describing Experiences and Giving Instructions
Pupils develop the ability to recount personal experiences using past tense verbs, for instance, "I visited the Louvre last weekend." They learn to give clear, sequential instructions using imperative verbs, such as "First, put money in the machine. Next, press the button." Students are encouraged to use simple descriptive language to convey details about a trip or an event, incorporating vocabulary related to French daily life, like the name of a local *boulangerie* or a market.

## Vocabulary: Everyday Life and French Culture
The vocabulary focus is on themes related to the children's environment and French culture. This includes terms for public transport (*métro*, bus, tram), food items from a *boulangerie* (croissant, pain au chocolat), and national events like the *Tour de France*. Students also learn the names of common professions and places in a town (*l'école*, *la banque*, *le parc*), often using flashcards with pictures to aid memorization.

## Grammar: Verb Tenses and Sentence Structure
Students are introduced to the past tense (*passé composé*) with regular verbs, learning to form sentences like "I played in the park" or "She walked to school." They practice using negative sentences with "not" or "don't," for example, "I did not go to the market." The simple present tense for habitual actions is reinforced ("I take the *métro* to school"). Basic sentence structure, subject-verb agreement, and the use of common adjectives (e.g., big, small, happy) are consistently applied.

## Listening Comprehension: Stories and Announcements
Pupils train to understand simple spoken narratives and short announcements. They listen to stories set in familiar contexts, such as a day in Paris or a trip to the countryside, and identify key characters, settings, and events. They practice following instructions related to classroom tasks or games. Authentic materials, like a simplified *métro* announcement or a description of a French festival, are used to develop their ability to extract main ideas and specific details from spoken English.

## Phonics and Spelling: Common Patterns and French-Influenced Words
The course focuses on mastering common English spelling patterns, such as "ai" (rain, pain) and "ee" (see, meet), and identifying silent letters (e.g., "k" in *knife*). Students learn to recognize and produce simple French loanwords used in English, like *café*, *ballet*, and *décor*. They practice writing and spelling words related to their lessons, such as *euro* and *métro*, ensuring correct pronunciation and letter formation through exercises and dictation.
