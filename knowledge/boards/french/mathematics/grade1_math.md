# French (Éducation nationale) Grade 1 Mathematics  

## Numbers and Counting up to 30
Children learn to count forwards and backwards to 30. They write numerals correctly and understand quantities. They use concrete materials like counters or coins to show groups of objects. They compare numbers using words like 'plus grand' (bigger) and 'plus petit' (smaller). For example, they identify that 15 is more than 10. They also learn ordinal numbers, such as 'premier' (first) and 'deuxième' (second), using real-life examples like métro stops or positions in a Tour de France team.

## Addition and Subtraction up to 20
Children explore addition and subtraction using objects and drawings. They learn to write number sentences, like 5 + 3 = 8. They understand addition as combining groups and subtraction as taking away. For example, they might solve 'J'ai 8 euros. J'en dépense 3. Combien me reste-t-il?' (I have 8 euros. I spend 3. How much do I have left?). They use techniques like counting on or back to find answers. They also solve simple word problems with contexts like sharing pastries from a boulangerie.

## Space and Geometry
Children recognize and name common 2D and 3D shapes. They learn terms like 'carré' (square), 'cercle' (circle), 'cube', and 'sphère'. They identify shapes in everyday objects, such as a round métro ticket or a cube-shaped dice. They describe shapes using words like 'grand' (big), 'petit' (small), and 'épais' (thick). They also explore position and orientation, using vocabulary like 'dessus' (on top) and 'dedans' (inside) through play-based activities like building with blocks.

## Measurement and Comparisons
Children learn to compare objects by size, length, and weight. They use direct comparison, such as lining up pencils to see which is 'plus long' (longer). They measure length using non-standard units like paper clips. They also compare capacity, using containers like cups to see which holds 'plus d'eau' (more water). They learn vocabulary like 'lourd' (heavy) and 'léger' (light) by lifting objects. Time is introduced with daily routines, using words like 'matin' (morning) and 'soir' (evening).

## Data Handling
Les élèves apprennent à collecter et à organiser des données simples en utilisant des relevés et des tableaux. Ils représentent ces informations par des pictogrammes, où chaque symbole (comme une icône d’un fruit) correspond à une unité. Par exemple, après avoir interrogé leurs camarades sur leur fruit préféré, ils créent un graphique où chaque dessin de pomme représente un vote. Ils interprètent ensuite ces représentations pour répondre à des questions simples comme « Quel objet est le plus choisi ? » ou « Combien y a-t-il de votes pour l’option A ? ». Cette méthode permet de dénombrer, classer et comparer des données de manière concrète.

## Problem Solving and Reasoning
Children apply mathematical skills to solve practical problems. They use strategies like drawing, acting out, or using objects to find solutions. For example, they might figure out how many chairs are needed for a group of friends at a picnic. They explain their thinking using simple sentences, such as 'J'ai compté 10 enfants, donc j'ai besoin de 10 chaises.' (I counted 10 children, so I need 10 chairs.) They learn to justify their answers with clear reasoning.
