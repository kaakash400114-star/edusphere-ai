# French (Éducation nationale) Grade 5 Mathematics


## Numbers and Calculations
Students master numbers up to 999,999. They learn to write and order them (numération jusqu'à 999 999), including understanding place value (unités, dizaines, centaines, milliers). Calculation techniques are key, with formal methods for adding and subtracting with regroupement (carrying/borrowing). They multiply by a two-digit number using an algorithm, often seen in comparing ticket prices for the Tour de France. Division is introduced with a one-digit divisor (diviseur), using the partage method, such as sharing a bag of croissants equally.

## Geometry
Pupils identify and describe plane figures (figures planes), like squares, rectangles, triangles, and circles. They learn to draw these shapes using a ruler and a compass. The concept of perpendicularity (perpendicularité) and parallelism (parallélisme) is studied using street maps of Paris, like seeing métro lines. Students calculate the perimeter (périmètre) of polygons. They also study solid shapes (solides), recognising prisms and pyramids, and can name their faces (faces), edges (arêtes), and vertices (sommets).

## Measurements
Students understand the metric system and convert units of length (mètre, centimètre), mass (gramme, kilogramme), and capacity (litre). They calculate the perimeter and area (aire) of rectangles in real-world contexts, like finding the size of a piece of land for a new boulangerie. Time is a focus, with pupils reading the time on an analog clock and calculating durations, such as the length of a TV programme. Money calculations use euros (€), including finding the total cost of several items and giving change.

## Fractions and Decimals
Students solidify their understanding of fractions by manipulating numerators and denominators to compare and order them, using common denominators or benchmarks like one-half. They place fractions on a number line and learn to find equivalent fractions by scaling the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number. The study then extends to decimal numbers, focusing on numbers up to two decimal places, which are explicitly linked to monetary and metric units (e.g., €3.25, 2.4kg). Students learn to translate between decimal and fractional forms, understanding that 0.75 is equivalent to 75/100, which can be simplified to 3/4. They are also introduced to the concept of mixed numbers, such as 1 and 1/2, and their decimal equivalents, like 1.5.

## Problem Solving (Résolution de problèmes)
Students solve one-step and two-step word problems, choosing the correct operation. They are encouraged to reason (raisonner) and explain their thinking. Problems involve all areas of maths, such as calculating the total cost of multiple items or determining the distance travelled on a family holiday. Using data from a chart, like a temperature graph over a week, to answer questions is a key skill. Students learn to verify their answers to ensure they are logical.

## Data Handling (Gestion des données)
Pupils learn to collect, organize, and represent data. They interpret and construct bar charts (diagrammes en bâtons) and pictograms (pictogrammes) from simple surveys, for example, favourite pastimes of classmates. They learn to read information from a table, like a class schedule (emploi du temps). This includes identifying the maximum and minimum values in a dataset. These skills help them make sense of information presented in everyday life, such as comparing prices at different bakeries.
