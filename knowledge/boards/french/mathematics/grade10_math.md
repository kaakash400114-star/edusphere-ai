# French (Éducation nationale) Grade 10 Mathematics

## Second-Degree Equations and Functions
Students solve second-degree equations, or *équations du second degré*, of the form ax² + bx + c = 0. They use the discriminant, or *discriminant* (Δ = b² - 4ac), to determine the number of real roots. If Δ > 0, there are two distinct real roots; if Δ = 0, there is one real root; and if Δ < 0, there are no real roots. The roots are found using the formula x = (-b ± √Δ) / 2a. Students also graph quadratic functions, or *fonctions du second degré*, which are parabolas. For example, the function f(x) = x² - 5x + 6 has roots at x = 2 and x = 3.

## Plane Geometry and Trigonometry
This topic covers geometric properties of triangles and circles, including the Pythagorean theorem and trigonometric ratios. Students learn the sine (*sinus*), cosine (*cosinus*), and tangent (*tangente*) of an angle in a right-angled triangle. For instance, in a triangle with an angle of 30°, if the opposite side is 5 cm, the hypotenuse is 10 cm because sin(30°) = 1/2. They solve problems involving distances, such as calculating the height of the Tour de France finish line tower using trigonometry. Key formulas include a/sin(A) = b/sin(B) = c/sin(C).

## Statistics and Probability
Students analyze statistical data using measures of central tendency (mean, median) and dispersion (range, standard deviation). They calculate probabilities for events, including independent and dependent events. For example, the probability of drawing a red metro ticket from a bag of 20 tickets (5 red, 15 blue) is 5/20 = 1/4. They also study conditional probability, such as finding the likelihood of rain in Paris given the forecast. Students use real-world data, like daily temperatures or boulangerie sales, to create and interpret histograms and box plots.

## Algebraic Expressions and Equations
Students simplify and manipulate algebraic expressions, including expanding and factoring. They solve linear and quadratic equations, as well as systems of linear equations. For example, to solve the system x + y = 10 and 2x - y = 5, they can use substitution or elimination. Students also work with rational expressions, such as (x² - 4)/(x - 2), simplifying them to x + 2 when x ≠ 2. They solve inequalities like 3x - 7 < 2, finding the solution set x < 3.

## Proportionality and Functions
This topic covers proportional relationships, or *proportionnalité*, and linear, affine, and inverse functions. Students identify proportional relationships using the formula y = kx. For example, if 3 baguettes cost €6, then 5 baguettes cost €10 because the price per baguette is constant (k = 2). They study linear functions of the form f(x) = mx + b, where m is the slope and b is the y-intercept. Inverse functions, like f(x) = 1/x, are also explored, with applications such as speed and time calculations.

## Geometry in Space
Students study spatial geometry, including volumes and surface areas of prisms, pyramids, cylinders, and cones. They calculate the volume of a cylinder with radius 3 cm and height 10 cm using V = πr²h ≈ 282.74 cm³. They also learn about the geometric properties of spheres, using formulas like V = (4/3)πr³. Students solve problems involving real-world objects, such as finding the volume of a boulangerie display case or the surface area of a cylindrical bread bin. Concepts of similarity and scale are applied to models and maps.
