# French (Éducation nationale) Grade 2 Mathematics
## Numbers and Calculation up to 100
Students learn to read, write, and represent numbers up to 100 using numerals and words. They practice counting forwards and backwards in various steps (by 1, 2, 5, 10). Calculation methods focus on developing mental and written strategies for addition and subtraction within 100. For example, they learn to break down numbers (e.g., 47 + 23 can be seen as 40 + 20 = 60 and 7 + 3 = 10, then 60 + 10 = 70). They also solve simple word problems involving everyday situations, such as calculating the total price of several items in a boulangerie.

## Geometry: Discovering Shapes and Solids
In geometry, students identify, describe, and name common plane shapes (cercle, triangle, carré, rectangle) and common 3D solids (cube, cuboid, sphere). They learn to use specific vocabulary: côté (side), sommet (vertex), face (face), angle (angle). A key activity is to recognize and name shapes in their environment, like the rectangular shape of a métro ticket or the circular shape of a coin. Students also learn to create simple patterns using these shapes and solids, building an understanding of spatial relationships.

## Measurement: Introduction to Length and Time
Students begin measuring lengths using non-standard units before learning standard units like the centimètre (cm). They compare and order objects based on their lengths using words like plus long (longer), plus court (shorter), and de même longueur (of the same length). For time, they learn to read the time to the hour and half-hour on an analog clock, using the terms heure (hour) and demie-heure (half-hour). They also become familiar with the days of the week and months of the year, understanding the concept of a calendar year.

## Handling Data: Organizing and Representing Information
Students learn to collect simple data, such as the favourite fruits of their classmates. They practice organizing this information into categories and represent it using tally charts (comptage par jetons) and pictograms (pictogrammes). For example, they can draw a picture of a strawberry for every three students who chose it as their favourite. The goal is to interpret the information from these charts and graphs, answering simple questions like "Which item was the most popular?" to develop basic analytical skills.

## Problem Solving in Context
Problem solving (résolution de problèmes) is integrated into all areas. Students are presented with simple, one-step problems drawn from real-life French contexts. They learn to identify the necessary information and the question being asked. For instance, a problem might be: "A Tour de France cyclist has ridden 45 km in the morning and 30 km in the afternoon. How many kilometres has he ridden in total?" Students must choose the correct operation (addition or subtraction) and apply their calculation skills to find the solution, showing their working.

## Calculation Strategies and Numerical Relationships
Students deepen their understanding of numbers by exploring relationships. They learn to find a number between two given numbers (e.g., 45 and 47) and to round numbers to the nearest ten. They practice calculation strategies such as adding the same number multiple times to introduce the concept of multiplication (e.g., 4 groups of 5 is 4 x 5). They also explore the composition of numbers up to 100, for example understanding that 58 is composed of 5 tens and 8 units, and using this knowledge to flexibly solve problems.
