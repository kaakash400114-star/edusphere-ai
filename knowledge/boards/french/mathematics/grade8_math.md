# French (Éducation nationale) Grade 8 Mathematics


## Numerical Calculation and Proportionality
Students consolidate their operations with relative numbers, mastering the rules for calculating sums and differences involving positive and negative integers. They learn to solve linear equations with one unknown, such as 4x + 7 = 19, by isolating the variable. The study of proportionality is deepened through applications including percentage calculations, such as determining a 20% discount on a €90 jacket, and the use of rates to solve problems, such as calculating the time taken for a TGV travelling at 300 km/h to cover a distance of 600 km. They apply the rule of three, or coefficient of proportionality, to solve these varied problems.

## Plane Geometry and Right Triangles
Pupils work with the Pythagorean theorem (théorème de Pythagore) to find the hypotenuse or a side of a right-angled triangle (triangle rectangle), for example, calculating the distance from a Métro station to the top of the Eiffel Tower. They study the properties of geometric transformations (transformations géométriques), including translations, rotations, and symmetries, to create and analyse patterns. They also calculate the area of a circle using πr², like finding the area of a circular boulangerie sign.

## Solids and Spatial Geometry
This section focuses on calculating the volumes and surface areas of common solids (solides) such as prisms (prismes), cylinders (cylindres), and cubes (cubes). Students learn to distinguish between volume measured in cubic units (m³) and surface area measured in square units (m²). They solve practical problems, like determining the volume of a rectangular swimming pool or the amount of concrete needed to build a section of a Tour de France support structure.

## Functions and Linear Representations
Students are introduced to the concept of a function (fonction) and its representations. They learn to read and interpret graphs (graphiques), particularly linear functions (fonctions linéaires) drawn as straight lines, connecting an input value to an output. For example, they can plot the cost of a Paris museum visit against the number of tickets purchased. They begin to distinguish between affine (y = ax + b) and linear (y = ax) functions.

## Organizing and Representing Data
Learners will organize raw data into distribution tables (tableaux de distribution) and determine frequencies and cumulative frequencies to understand the proportion of occurrences within defined intervals. They will calculate the mean (moyenne) as the sum of all values divided by the number of values and identify the median (médiane) as the central value in an ordered data set. Using a class's results on a market basket price calculation as an example, they will compare these two measures of central tendency to analyze the data's distribution. They will construct and interpret bar charts (diagrammes en barres) for discrete data and histograms (histogrammes) for grouped continuous data, while pie charts (diagrammes circulaires) will be used to represent relative frequencies for qualitative variables.

## Algorithmic Thinking and Programming
Students acquire fundamental algorithmic logic by designing step-by-step solutions to problems, then translating these into executable programs using block-based or textual languages. They learn to define and manipulate variables to store numerical or textual data, such as the price and quantity of items. Conditional statements ('if...then...else') enable them to make decisions, like applying a discount if the total purchase exceeds a certain amount. Loops ('while', 'repeat') automate repetitive tasks, such as iterating through a list of products to calculate the final grocery cost. This programming approach is applied to various concrete scenarios, including calculating the total cost of groceries in euros or creating an algorithm to find the shortest route in the Paris Métro system.
