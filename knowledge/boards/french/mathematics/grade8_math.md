# French (Éducation nationale) Grade 8 Mathematics

## Numerical Calculation and Proportionality
Students consolidate their skills in operating with relative numbers (nombres relatifs), including calculating the sum and difference of positive and negative integers. They solve linear equations with one unknown (inconnue), such as 3x - 5 = 10. The study of proportionality (proportionnalité) is deepened through problems involving percentages (pourcentages), like calculating a discount on a €45 baguette, and rates of change, such as the speed of a TGV train.

## Plane Geometry and Right Triangles
Pupils work with the Pythagorean theorem (théorème de Pythagore) to find the hypotenuse or a side of a right-angled triangle (triangle rectangle), for example, calculating the distance from a Métro station to the top of the Eiffel Tower. They study the properties of geometric transformations (transformations géométriques), including translations, rotations, and symmetries, to create and analyse patterns. They also calculate the area of a circle using πr², like finding the area of a circular boulangerie sign.

## Solids and Spatial Geometry
This section focuses on calculating the volumes and surface areas of common solids (solides) such as prisms (prismes), cylinders (cylindres), and cubes (cubes). Students learn to distinguish between volume measured in cubic units (m³) and surface area measured in square units (m²). They solve practical problems, like determining the volume of a rectangular swimming pool or the amount of concrete needed to build a section of a Tour de France support structure.

## Functions and Linear Representations
Students are introduced to the concept of a function (fonction) and its representations. They learn to read and interpret graphs (graphiques), particularly linear functions (fonctions linéaires) drawn as straight lines, connecting an input value to an output. For example, they can plot the cost of a Paris museum visit against the number of tickets purchased. They begin to distinguish between affine (y = ax + b) and linear (y = ax) functions.

## Organizing and Representing Data
Learners work with statistical data (données statistiques), including frequency (fréquence) and cumulative frequency (fréquence cumulée). They calculate and interpret the mean (moyenne) and median (médiane) of a data set, such as the average price of items in a French market basket. They construct and analyse different types of graphs (graphiques), including bar charts (diagrammes en barres) and pie charts (diagrammes circulaires) to represent data distributions.

## Algorithmic Thinking and Programming
Students are introduced to algorithmic logic (logique algorithmique) by creating simple algorithms using block-based or textual programming languages. They learn to use variables (variables), conditional statements (conditions), and loops (boucles) to solve problems. An example is writing a program to calculate the total cost of groceries in euros or to design a simple step-by-step plan for navigating the Paris Métro system.
