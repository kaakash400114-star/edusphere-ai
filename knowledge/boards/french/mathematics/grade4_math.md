# French (Éducation nationale) Grade 4 Mathematics
## Numbers and Calculations (Nombres et calculs)
Students master numbers up to 999,999. They read, write, and compare these numbers in digits and words. They learn to round numbers to the nearest ten, hundred, or thousand. Calculation work solidifies mental and written strategies for addition and subtraction of large numbers. Multiplication is extended to two-digit by one-digit numbers using the 'méthode par étapes' (step method). Students begin simple division by finding a quotient and a remainder, using practical examples like dividing a set of 25 into groups of 6.

## Fractions (Fractions)
Students understand fractions as parts of a whole or of a collection. They learn to read and write fractions (e.g., trois quarts, 3/4) and to place them on a number line. They compare and order fractions with the same denominator. A key skill is to find equivalent fractions using simple multiplication and division. Students practice adding and subtracting fractions that share the same denominator, such as calculating two-fifths plus one-fifth (2/5 + 1/5).

## Measurement and Measurement Tools (Mesures et instruments)
This unit covers measuring length in metres and centimetres, mass in grams and kilograms, and capacity in litres and millilitres. Students learn to choose the appropriate unit for a task, like measuring a baguette in centimetres or the weight of a melon in kilograms. They work with time, including reading a 24-hour clock and calculating durations. They solve problems involving perimeter by measuring the sides of shapes, like finding the perimeter of a classroom table.

## Space and Geometry (Espace et géométrie)
Students learn the properties of 2D and 3D shapes. They name and describe triangles, quadrilaterals, circles, cubes, prisms, and pyramids. They learn to draw these shapes accurately using a ruler, set square (équerre), and compass. The concept of symmetry is introduced, and students identify lines of symmetry in shapes. They practice recognising and drawing right angles (angles droits) and use grid paper to plot points and create simple geometric patterns, like a mosaic tile design.

## Organising and Representing Data (Organisation et représentation de données)
Students learn to collect data through surveys or observations. They create tables and charts to present this information. The focus is on bar charts (diagrammes en barres), where they interpret data and answer questions about it, for example, comparing the number of students who prefer croissants to pain au chocolat. They also learn to make simple pie charts (diagrammes circulaires) for categorical data, understanding the representation of parts of a whole using fractions.

## Problem Solving (Résolution de problèmes)
Throughout the year, students apply their knowledge to solve multi-step word problems. These problems are set in realistic French contexts, such as calculating the cost of items from a boulangerie, determining the total distance for stages of the Tour de France, or figuring out how many metro tickets are needed for a family trip. The emphasis is on identifying the necessary calculations, choosing the correct operations, writing down the steps of their work, and checking if their answer is logical.
