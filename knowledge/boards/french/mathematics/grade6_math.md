# French (Éducation nationale) Grade 6 Mathematics
## Numbers and Calculations (Nombres et calculs)
Students build upon their understanding of whole numbers by working with integers (nombres entiers positifs et négatifs) on a number line (droite numérique), using terminology such as positive (positif), negative (négatif), and zero (zéro). They master the four operations (addition, soustraction, multiplication, division) with positive integers, including long division (division euclidienne). The order of operations (priorité des opérations) is rigorously applied: first parentheses (parenthèses), then multiplication and division (de la gauche vers la droite), and finally addition and subtraction. For example, calculating 3 + 4 × 2 requires first calculating 4 × 2.

## Fractions and Decimals (Fractions et nombres décimaux)
Students deepen their knowledge of fractions (fractions) by learning to write them as decimal numbers (nombres décimaux), such as converting ½ to 0.5. They perform calculations with decimals (addition, soustraction, multiplication, division) by placing the decimal point (virgule décimale) correctly. This skill is applied to practical French contexts like calculating the total cost of two baguettes at €1.35 each or determining the change from a €10 note after purchasing a book costing €7.80. Emphasis is placed on estimation (estimation) to check the reasonableness of results.

## Geometry in Space (Géometrie dans l'espace)
Pupils explore two- and three-dimensional shapes. They study properties of solids (solides) such as prisms (prismes) and pyramids (pyramides), identifying and naming their faces (faces), edges (arêtes), and vertices (sommets). In two dimensions, they consolidate their knowledge of plane figures (figures planes) like triangles, quadrilaterals, and regular polygons (polygones réguliers). The concept of symmetry (symétrie axiale) is reinforced, and students learn to recognize axes of symmetry (axes de symétrie) in geometric figures and everyday objects, like the lines of symmetry on the Eiffel Tower's silhouette.

## Proportionality and Percentages (Proportionnalité et pourcentages)
Students are introduced to the concept of proportionality (proportionnalité) and learn to recognize situations where quantities vary in proportion. They solve problems using proportions, often involving everyday French life, such as determining the number of baguettes needed for a school picnic based on a ratio of one baguette per student. They are also taught to calculate percentages (pourcentages), for instance, calculating a 20% reduction (réduction) on a €50 item at a sale, finding the discount amount and the final price.

## Management of Data (Gestion de données)
Students learn to collect, organize, and represent data. They interpret different types of graphs (graphiques), including bar charts (diagrammes en barres), pie charts (diagrammes circulaires), and line graphs (graphiques linéaires). For example, they might analyze a line graph showing the attendance figures for the Tour de France stages over a week. They calculate statistical measures such as the mean (moyenne) of a data set, like finding the average daily temperature in Paris for a given week.

## Introduction to Algebra (Introduction à l'algèbre)
Students begin their study of algebra by using simple equations (équations) to represent problems. They solve first-degree equations in one variable, such as 2x + 5 = 11. The goal is to find the unknown value (la valeur inconnue) that makes the equation true. They also explore writing expressions (expressions) to describe real-world situations, like using the expression `c = 2.50 × n` to calculate the total cost (`c`) in euros for `n` croissants at a boulangerie. This introduces the power of abstraction.
