# French (Éducation nationale) Grade 2 Science

## The Five Senses
Children learn to name the five senses: la vue (sight), l'ouïe (hearing), l'odorat (smell), le goût (taste), and le toucher (touch). They identify which sense is used for different actions, such as using la vue to read a book, l'ouïe to hear a metro, and le goût to taste pain (bread) from the boulangerie. Activities involve describing objects using descriptive words related to each sense, for example, feeling the rough texture of a cobblestone or smelling a flower in a park. They understand that each sense provides unique information about their environment.

## Observing Materials
Students learn to identify and classify everyday materials based on their properties, such as metal, wood, plastic, and fabric. They observe simple objects like a euro coin (metal), a wooden toy, and a plastic bottle. The lesson focuses on describing materials using words like dur (hard), souple (flexible), lisse (smooth), and rugueux (rough). Children conduct sorting activities, grouping objects by material type and understanding that an object's material determines its characteristics, like a glass window being transparent and fragile.

## Day and Night Cycle
Children explore the patterns of the sky to understand the difference between day and night. They learn that the Sun appears during the day to provide light and heat, allowing activities like playing outside. At night, the stars and the Moon become visible. They observe the changes in the sky, such as the Sun rising in the morning and setting in the evening. A simple discussion connects this to their daily routine, linking the cycle of light and darkness to times for meals and sleep, using examples like waking up for school when it is light.

## Living Things and Their Needs
Students identify common living things, including plants, animals, and people, and their basic needs. They learn that all living things need air, water, food, and a suitable place to live. Examples include a dog needing water to drink, a plant needing sunlight to grow, and humans needing a home for shelter. Children draw or label pictures of living things and match them to their basic requirements, understanding that living things depend on their environment to survive.

## Forces and Movement
Children discover the concept of force as a push or pull that makes an object move or stop. They learn to identify forces in everyday actions, such as pushing a swing or pulling a toy. Simple experiments involve observing how different forces affect objects; for example, blowing on a feather to make it move or stopping a rolling ball. They understand that a stronger force makes an object move faster or farther, using real-world examples like pedalling a bicycle harder to go faster or a cyclist pushing hard during the Tour de France.

## Weather and Seasons
Students learn to observe and describe different types of weather, such as sunny, rainy, windy, and cloudy. They associate weather with clothing, for example, wearing a coat when it is cold and raining. The four seasons—printemps (spring), été (summer), automne (autumn), and hiver (winter)—are introduced with their typical weather and activities. Children discuss how trees change with the seasons, like losing leaves in autumn and growing new ones in spring, connecting seasonal changes to their own experiences, such as eating ice cream in summer or playing in the snow in winter.
