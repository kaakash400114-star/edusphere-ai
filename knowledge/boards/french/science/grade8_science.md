# French (Éducation nationale) Grade 8 Science
## Forces and Motion
Students study the relationship between forces and motion. They learn that force is a push or pull that can change an object's state of movement or shape, measured in Newtons (N). A balanced force (sum is zero) results in uniform motion or rest, while an unbalanced force causes acceleration. Newton’s second law is explored: the acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass (F = ma). An example: pushing a shopping cart with a force of 50 N, which has a mass of 20 kg, results in an acceleration of 2.5 m/s².

## Energy Transformations
The theme is the conservation and transformation of energy. Students identify different forms of energy, such as kinetic (movement), potential (height or position), thermal (heat), and chemical (in food or batteries). The law of conservation of energy states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted from one form to another. For instance, a cyclist pedaling converts chemical energy from food into kinetic energy to move the vélo, with some thermal energy also produced. Efficiency is calculated as (useful energy output / total energy input) × 100%, a key concept in evaluating systems like a boulangerie's oven.

## Electric Circuits
Students learn to assemble and interpret electric circuits. They differentiate between series and parallel connections. In a series circuit, the same electric current flows through all components, and the voltage is divided. In a parallel circuit, the voltage is the same across all branches, and the current divides. The relationship between voltage (V), current (I), and resistance (R) is defined by Ohm's law (V = I × R). For example, a circuit with a 12V battery and a 60 Ω resistor will have a current of 0.2 A. Safety rules regarding electricity are paramount.

## Chemical Reactions and Equations
This topic covers the fundamentals of chemical reactions and their symbolic representation. Students learn that a chemical reaction involves the transformation of reactants into products. A chemical equation must be balanced, meaning the number of atoms of each element is the same on both sides. The equation for the combustion of propane, a fuel used in some homes, is C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O. Students also distinguish between different types of reactions, such as synthesis and decomposition, using examples like the formation of rust from iron and oxygen.

## The Diversity of Living Things
Students explore the classification of living organisms. The main levels of classification are domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, and species. They learn about the five kingdoms: animals, plants, fungi, protists, and monera. Students identify key characteristics of each, for example, animals are heterotrophic and eukaryotic, while plants are autotrophic and usually immobile. A study of French biodiversity might include classifying local birds like the canard colvert (mallard) or the perdrix (partridge) and noting their place in the animal kingdom.

## Geological Time and Plate Tectonics
Students understand the immense scale of geological time and the theory of plate tectonics. They learn that the Earth's crust is divided into large plates that move slowly, causing phenomena like earthquakes and mountain formation. The theory explains the existence of fossils of similar marine creatures on different continents, now separated by the Atlantic Ocean. The theory is applied to explain the formation of the Alps and the seismic activity in regions like the Provence. A timeline is constructed to place major events, like the extinction of the dinosaurs, in the context of Earth's 4.6-billion-year history.
