# French (Éducation nationale) Grade 6 Science

## The Diversity of Living Organisms
In France, students explore the classification of living organisms. They learn the five kingdoms: Animalia, Plantae, Fungi, Protista, and Monera. The focus is on identifying key characteristics, such as vertebrates having a spine and invertebrates lacking one. A common example classifying animals like a dog (*Canis lupus familiaris*) as a mammal versus a frog (*Anura order*) as an amphibian is used. Students practice using identification keys based on observable features like the presence of feathers or scales.

## Matter and its Transformations
Students study the properties of matter in its three states: solid, liquid, and gas. They learn to distinguish between physical changes, such as melting butter (*beurre*) from a solid to a liquid, and chemical changes, like burning a piece of paper (*papier*). Key concepts include mass and volume, which are measured in grams (g) and milliliters (mL) respectively. An experiment involves mixing baking soda (*bicarbonate de soude*) with vinegar (*vinaigre*) to observe a gas production, demonstrating a chemical reaction.

## The Sun and Earth's Movements
The curriculum covers the geocentric model where the Earth appears to move around the Sun. Students learn about the rotation of the Earth on its axis causing day and night, and its revolution around the Sun creating a year. This cycle is linked to the seasons experienced in France. For instance, the longest day is the summer solstice, often around June 21st, and the shortest is the winter solstice, around December 21st. Shadows cast by objects like a sundial (*cadran solaire*) change throughout the day due to the Earth's rotation.

## Electricity and Circuits
Pupils construct simple electrical circuits using components like a battery (*pile*), wires (*fils*), a light bulb (*ampoule*), and a switch (*interrupteur*). They learn that a closed circuit allows current to flow, lighting the bulb, while an open circuit does not. The concept of conductors, such as copper, and insulators, such as plastic, is introduced using real-world examples. Students draw schematic diagrams using standard symbols for each component to represent their circuits accurately.

## Forces and Motion
Students explore forces as pushes or pulls that can change an object's motion. They identify contact forces, like friction slowing a cyclist during the Tour de France, and non-contact forces, like gravity causing an apple (*pomme*) to fall. The unit introduces the idea that forces can be balanced, resulting in no movement, or unbalanced, causing acceleration. A simple activity involves measuring the force needed to slide a book across a desk, demonstrating the effect of different surfaces on friction.

## Data and Representations in Science
This topic teaches students how to collect and present scientific data. They learn to create and interpret tables and graphs, such as a bar chart showing the number of students who travel to school by *métro*, bus, or car. Students practice measuring objects with a ruler (*règle*) in centimeters (cm) and recording masses using a scale. For instance, they might measure the mass of a baguette from a local *boulangerie* and record the data in a table before creating a visual representation.
