# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 3 Science
## Living Things and Their Habitats
Learners investigate the basic needs of plants and animals for survival, including water, air, food, and shelter. They observe how living things are suited to their specific environments, such as cacti in deserts with thick stems for storing water. Learners classify animals as herbivores, carnivores, or omnivores based on their diets. They explore simple food chains, identifying producers, consumers, and decomposers. For example, they might trace a food chain from grass (producer) to a rabbit (primary consumer) to a fox (secondary consumer).

## Materials and Their Properties
Learners explore the properties of different materials, such as hardness, flexibility, and absorbency. They conduct simple tests to compare materials, for instance, checking which fabric is stretchiest or which paper towel absorbs the most water. Learners group materials into categories like natural (wood, cotton) and human-made (plastic, glass). They discover how properties make materials suitable for specific uses, like glass being transparent for windows. This develops vocabulary to describe materials scientifically based on observable characteristics.

## Forces and Motion
Learners investigate different forces, including pushes, pulls, gravity, and friction. They explore how forces can change an object’s speed or direction through hands-on activities, like pushing toy cars on different surfaces to see the effect of friction. Learners identify gravity as the force that pulls objects towards the Earth, causing leaves to fall. They observe and record how the strength and direction of a force affect motion, such as how a harder push makes a ball roll faster and farther.

## Weather and Climate
Learners investigate daily weather patterns and seasonal changes. They observe and record temperature, precipitation, wind, and cloud cover using simple tools like a thermometer and wind vane. Learners identify weather symbols used in forecasts and connect them to real conditions, such as a sun symbol indicating clear skies. They explore how climate varies around the world, comparing a hot desert to a cold polar region, and discuss how living things adapt to these different climates, such as camels in deserts.

## Earth’s Resources
Learners investigate Earth’s natural resources, including water, soil, rocks, and minerals. They explore how these resources are used in everyday life, such as water for drinking and soil for growing plants. Learners distinguish between renewable resources, like solar energy, and non-renewable resources, like coal. They discuss the importance of conserving resources by reducing waste and recycling materials, linking this to global efforts like protecting freshwater rivers for communities in different countries.

## Light and Sound
Learners investigate how light travels and how sound is made. They explore how light reflects off shiny surfaces like mirrors and bends when passing through water, creating simple rainbows. For sound, learners discover that vibrations create noise, such as plucking a guitar string to produce sound. They compare how sound travels through different materials, noticing it is louder through a solid than air. Learners also identify natural sources of light and sound, like the sun and birdsong, connecting them to the environment.
