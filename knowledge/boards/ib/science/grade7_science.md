# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 7 Science

## Structure and Properties of Matter
Learners investigate the classification of matter into elements, compounds, and mixtures through physical and chemical properties. They learn about atoms, the basic units of matter, and their subatomic particles: protons, neutrons, and electrons. Using the periodic table, they identify atomic number, mass, and symbols for common elements. They explore chemical formulas, such as H₂O for water, and distinguish between physical changes, like melting ice, and chemical changes, like rusting iron, by observing changes in substance composition.

## Energy Transfer and Transformations
Learners investigate the different forms of energy, including kinetic, potential, thermal, and electrical, and their transformations. They apply the law of conservation of energy, understanding that energy cannot be created or destroyed. They explore methods of heat transfer through conduction, convection, and radiation, using real-world examples like a metal spoon heating in a pot. Learners also examine electrical circuits, calculating current, voltage, and resistance using Ohm's Law (V = IR), and differentiate between series and parallel circuits.

## Forces and Motion
Learners investigate Newton's Laws of Motion to describe the relationship between forces and movement. They define force as a push or pull and measure it in newtons. Learners calculate speed using the formula speed = distance/time, for example, determining the speed of a car travelling 100 km in 2 hours. They explore friction and air resistance as forces that oppose motion and investigate gravity as a force that attracts objects towards the Earth's centre, affecting their weight and falling speed.

## Ecosystem Dynamics and Interactions
Learners investigate the components of ecosystems, including biotic and abiotic factors, and the relationships between organisms. They classify organisms into trophic levels: producers, consumers, and decomposers. Learners study food webs and chains, such as the grass → rabbit → fox example, to understand energy flow. They explore the concept of carrying capacity and limiting factors, like food availability, and investigate human impacts on ecosystems, including deforestation and pollution, on a global scale.

## Earth's Systems and Resources
Learners investigate the structure of the Earth, including the crust, mantle, and core, and the theory of plate tectonics. They explore the rock cycle, distinguishing between igneous, sedimentary, and metamorphic rocks, and learn about their formation. Learners study the water cycle, understanding processes like evaporation, condensation, and precipitation. They investigate renewable and non-renewable resources, such as solar energy versus fossil fuels, and analyse the sustainable management of these resources for international communities.

## Scientific Inquiry and Research
Learners investigate the scientific method to design and conduct controlled experiments. They formulate testable questions and hypotheses, such as "If a plant receives more light, then it will grow taller." They learn to identify variables: independent, dependent, and controlled. Learners collect and record data using appropriate tools like thermometers or rulers, and represent data through graphs and charts. They analyse results to draw evidence-based conclusions and communicate findings, demonstrating international-mindedness by considering the global applications of their scientific discoveries.
