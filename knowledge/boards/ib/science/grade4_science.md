# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 4 Science
## States of Matter
Learners investigate the three main states of matter: solid, liquid, and gas. They learn that matter is made up of tiny particles. Solids have a fixed shape and volume, like a wooden block. Liquids have a fixed volume but take the shape of their container, like water in a glass. Gases have neither a fixed shape nor volume and expand to fill any space, like the air in a football. They can observe the changes in state, such as ice melting into water, using real-world examples.

## Forces and Motion
Learners investigate how pushes and pulls create movement and change an object's motion. They explore forces like gravity, friction, and magnetism. Gravity is the force that pulls objects toward the Earth's center, making a ball fall to the ground. Friction is a force that opposes motion, like the resistance felt when rubbing hands together. Magnets can attract or repel certain materials without touching them, demonstrating non-contact forces. They use simple tools to measure and compare the strength of different forces.

## Plant Life Cycles
Learners investigate the life cycles of flowering plants. They learn that plants grow from seeds, develop roots, stems, and leaves, produce flowers for reproduction, and create seeds for the next generation. They can identify the key stages: seed, germination, seedling, mature plant, and flower. For example, they can observe the life cycle of a bean plant, planting a seed and tracking its growth into a plant that produces more seeds, understanding the cyclical nature of plant life in an ecosystem.

## Habitats and Adaptations
Learners investigate different habitats around the world, such as rainforests, deserts, and oceans. They explore how living things (plants and animals) are adapted to survive in their specific environments. For instance, a cactus has thick stems to store water in a desert, while a camel has long eyelashes to protect its eyes from sand in a desert. They learn that these adaptations help organisms meet their basic needs for survival in their particular habitat.

## Light and Sound
Learners investigate the properties of light and sound. They learn that light travels in straight lines and can be reflected, like seeing their face in a mirror, or refracted, making a straw look bent in a glass of water. They explore sound as a form of energy produced by vibrations; when a guitar string vibrates, it makes a sound. Sound needs a medium, like air or water, to travel and can be blocked by materials, demonstrating how we can hear less with our hands over our ears.

## Weather Patterns
Learners investigate how weather is measured and the patterns associated with it. They learn about tools like thermometers (for temperature), rain gauges (for precipitation), and wind vanes (for wind direction). They explore the water cycle, where water evaporates from oceans, forms clouds through condensation, and falls back to Earth as precipitation. They can use simple data to describe daily and seasonal weather changes in their local area and compare them with weather patterns in different parts of the world, such as a monsoon in Southeast Asia.
