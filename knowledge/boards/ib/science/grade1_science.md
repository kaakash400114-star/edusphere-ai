# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 1 Science
## Living Things
Living things, such as plants, animals, and people, need air, water, and food to survive. Plants make their own food through a process called photosynthesis, using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide. Animals eat plants or other animals for energy. All living things grow and change over their lifespans. For example, a seed sprouts into a seedling that grows into a tall tree, and a caterpillar transforms into a butterfly through a process called metamorphosis. These observable changes are key signs of life.

## Materials and Their Properties
Materials have different properties that make them useful. We can describe materials by their properties like being hard or soft, smooth or rough, and transparent or opaque. For instance, a wooden block is hard and opaque, while a plastic cup might be smooth and transparent. We test materials by observing them with our senses: seeing, touching, hearing, and smelling. This investigation helps us choose the right material for a job, such as using a metal fork because it is hard and strong.

## Forces and Motion
Forces are pushes or pulls that make objects move or change shape. When you kick a ball, you apply a force that makes it roll. Gravity is a force that pulls everything down towards the Earth. Magnets can push or pull other objects without touching them, demonstrating a non-contact force. We can change how an object moves by using more or less force. For example, pushing a toy car gently makes it roll slowly, while pushing it hard makes it go faster.

## Light and Sound
Light helps us see by bouncing off objects into our eyes. Sunlight and a torch are sources of light that can create shadows. Sound is caused by vibrations, which are quick back-and-forth movements. When you pluck a guitar string, it vibrates and makes a sound. Different objects make different sounds because they vibrate in different ways. A small bell makes a high-pitched sound, while a large drum makes a low-pitched sound because the drumhead vibrates slowly.

## Weather and Seasons
Weather describes what the sky and air are like outside, such as sunny, cloudy, rainy, or windy. We use tools like a thermometer to measure temperature and a rain gauge to measure rainfall. Seasons are changes in weather that happen over a long time: summer (hot), autumn (cool and leaves fall), winter (cold), and spring (warmer and plants grow). These patterns are influenced by how much sunlight different parts of the Earth receive.

## Our Senses
Humans use five main senses to explore the world: sight, hearing, touch, taste, and smell. Our eyes help us see colours and shapes, our ears hear sounds, our skin feels texture and temperature, our tongue tastes sweet, salty, sour, or bitter, and our nose smells things like flowers or rain. Our senses work together to give us information about our environment. For example, the smell of rain and the sound of thunder tell us a storm is coming.
