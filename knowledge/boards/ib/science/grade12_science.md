# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 12 Science
## Topic: Advanced Quantum Physics and Nuclear Chemistry
Learners investigate the wave-particle duality of matter through de Broglie’s hypothesis, λ = h/p, and confirm it with electron diffraction experiments using crystals. They study the quantum model of the atom, focusing on atomic orbitals defined by quantum numbers (n, l, m_l, m_s) and the Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle, ΔxΔp ≥ ħ/2. Nuclear chemistry encompasses radioactive decay laws, calculating half-life (t_½) using N = N_0e^(-λt), and balancing nuclear equations, including examples of carbon-14 decay in archaeological dating and uranium-235 fission in nuclear power reactors.
## Topic: Energetics and Chemical Kinetics
Learners investigate thermodynamic principles, calculating enthalpy changes (ΔH) using Hess's Law and standard enthalpies of formation from data tables like ΔH_f° (CO₂, g) = -393.5 kJ/mol. They apply the Gibbs free energy equation (ΔG = ΔH - TΔS) to predict reaction spontaneity. For chemical kinetics, they determine reaction orders using initial rate data, derive the integrated rate law for first-order reactions, ln[A]_t = -kt + ln[A]_0, and analyze the Arrhenius equation, k = A e^(-Ea/RT), to understand temperature's effect on the rate constant, k.
## Topic: Evolution and Biodiversity
Learners investigate evolutionary mechanisms through population genetics, calculating allele frequencies using the Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium equation, p² + 2pq + q² = 1. They apply this to study genetic drift, gene flow, and natural selection in real-world cases, such as peppered moth populations during the Industrial Revolution. The study of phylogenetics involves constructing and interpreting cladograms based on molecular data like DNA sequences to trace evolutionary relationships and assess biodiversity patterns, such as the higher species richness in tropical rainforests compared to temperate zones.
## Topic: Human Physiology and Homeostasis
Learners investigate the endocrine system, focusing on the hypothalamic-pituitary axis and its regulation of thyroid hormones through negative feedback. They study glucose homeostasis by analyzing the hormonal control of insulin and glucagon, understanding the feedback loop involved in Type 1 diabetes. In neuroscience, they explore the action potential using the Nernst equation for ion equilibrium and analyze synaptic transmission by examining the role of neurotransmitters like acetylcholine and dopamine in conditions such as Parkinson's disease, connecting molecular mechanisms to global health impacts.
## Topic: Electromagnetic Induction and Energy Transmission
Learners investigate Faraday's law of induction, calculating the induced electromotive force (emf) using ε = -N(ΔΦ/Δt), where a change in magnetic flux within a coil of 100 turns induces a voltage. They apply Lenz’s law to determine the direction of the induced current. For power transmission, they analyze transformer equations (V_p/V_s = N_p/N_s) to understand how voltage is stepped up for efficient long-distance energy distribution from power plants, comparing energy losses in alternating current (AC) systems versus direct current (DC) systems across different national grids.
## Topic: Ecology and Climate Change Science
Learners investigate biogeochemical cycles, modeling the carbon cycle by calculating fluxes between reservoirs like the atmosphere and biosphere using data from the Mauna Loa Observatory. They analyze climate forcing by quantifying the radiative forcing (W/m²) of greenhouse gases like CO₂ and CH₄. Population ecology studies use the logistic growth equation, dN/dt = rN(1 - N/K), to model carrying capacity (K) in environments like the Serengeti, linking these concepts to global issues such as ocean acidification and its impact on coral reef ecosystems worldwide.
