# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 2 Science

## Properties of Materials
Learners investigate the characteristics of different materials. They explore properties such as hardness, flexibility, and absorbency by testing objects. For example, a wooden block is hard, while a rubber band is flexible. They use real-world examples, like observing that a cotton towel absorbs water, but a plastic sheet does not. This hands-on investigation helps them classify and understand why certain materials are used for specific purposes, like using metal for pans because it conducts heat well.

## Life Cycles of Plants
Learners observe and document the life cycle of a common plant, such as a bean or sunflower. They plant a seed, monitor its growth, and record stages: germination, sprouting, growing leaves, and flowering. Using simple diagrams and words, they describe how the plant needs water, sunlight, and soil to live. This investigation connects to global examples, like comparing a bean plant in their classroom to a rice paddy farmer’s crop in another country, highlighting universal needs for life.

## Forces and Movement
Learners investigate pushes and pulls as forces that make objects move. They conduct experiments by using toy cars, balls, and ramps to explore how the strength and direction of a force affect speed and distance. For example, a gentle push makes a ball roll slowly, while a strong push makes it roll quickly. They explore gravity by observing how objects fall and how different materials, like a feather and a stone, fall at different rates.

## Habitats and Needs of Living Things
Learners explore a local habitat, such as a school garden or park, to identify living things and their basic needs: food, water, shelter, and space. They draw or list animals like worms, birds, and insects, describing what each one needs to survive. They connect this to international-mindedness by discussing similar habitats in other countries, like a coral reef, and how the needs of its creatures are the same, fostering an appreciation for global biodiversity and interdependence.

## Weather and Daily Observations
Learners observe and record daily weather patterns using simple tools like a thermometer, a wind sock, and a rain gauge. They describe weather using vocabulary like sunny, cloudy, windy, or rainy. They create a simple chart to track changes over a week and compare their local weather with examples from around the world, such as the snowy conditions in Canada or the dry heat in Australia, to understand global climate variations.

## Light and Shadows
Learners investigate how light travels and how shadows are formed. They use a light source, like a torch, and objects of different shapes and sizes to create and observe shadows. They discover that blocking light creates a shadow and that changing the object's distance or angle changes the shadow's size and shape. This inquiry connects to real-life examples, like the changing position and length of a shadow from a tree throughout the day.
