# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 9 Science  
## Atomic Structure and the Periodic Table  
Learners investigate the fundamental building blocks of matter, focusing on the structure of atoms, including protons, neutrons, and electrons. They explore electron configurations and how they determine an element’s position and properties in the periodic table. Key concepts include atomic number, mass number, and isotopes. For example, carbon-12 and carbon-14 are isotopes with different neutron counts. Students analyze trends such as atomic radius and electronegativity across periods and down groups, using data from the periodic table to predict chemical behavior and reactivity.  

## Chemical Bonding and Reactions  
Learners explore how atoms form compounds through ionic, covalent, and metallic bonding. They investigate the role of valence electrons and the octet rule in determining bond types. For instance, sodium chloride forms via ionic bonding, where electrons are transferred, while methane forms via covalent bonding, where electrons are shared. Students balance chemical equations and analyze reaction types, such as synthesis, decomposition, and combustion. They apply these principles to real-world examples, like the formation of water or rusting of iron, to understand energy changes and conservation in reactions.  

## Energy Transfer and Thermodynamics  
Learners investigate different forms of energy—kinetic, potential, thermal, and chemical—and how they transfer between systems. They explore the laws of thermodynamics, focusing on energy conservation and entropy. For example, a falling object converts potential energy to kinetic energy. Students calculate energy efficiency in systems like car engines or power plants, analyzing heat loss and work output. They examine global examples, such as solar energy conversion, to understand sustainable energy solutions and the impact of human activities on energy resources and climate change.  

## Forces and Motion  
Learners investigate the relationship between forces, motion, and Newton’s laws of motion. They analyze concepts like acceleration, velocity, and inertia using real-world examples, such as braking in a vehicle or planetary orbits. Students apply the equation F = ma to calculate net forces in scenarios like a rocket launch. They explore friction, gravity, and air resistance, examining their effects on motion. Through international examples, such as the design of high-speed trains or the physics of bungee jumping, learners understand how principles of mechanics shape technology and engineering solutions globally.  

## Waves and Sound  
Learners investigate the properties of waves, including amplitude, frequency, wavelength, and speed. They differentiate between transverse and longitudinal waves, using examples like light waves and sound waves. Students explore the behavior of waves, including reflection, refraction, and diffraction, applying them to real-world situations like fiber optics or ultrasound imaging. They analyze the Doppler effect in contexts such as radar speed detection or the changing pitch of an ambulance siren. By studying global examples, like seismic wave monitoring for earthquakes, learners understand the role of wave physics in communication, medicine, and disaster prediction.  

## Biodiversity and Ecology  
Learners investigate the interdependence of living organisms and their environments. They explore biodiversity, ecosystem services, and human impacts on ecosystems, such as deforestation and pollution. Students analyze food chains, webs, and energy pyramids to understand energy flow and nutrient cycling. For example, they might study the coral reef ecosystem in Australia or the Amazon rainforest. They examine concepts like sustainability and conservation, evaluating strategies to protect biodiversity. Through international case studies, learners develop an understanding of global ecological challenges and the importance of international cooperation in addressing environmental issues.
