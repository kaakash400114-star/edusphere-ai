# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 11 Science

## Nuclear Physics
Learners investigate the structure of the atomic nucleus and the forces that hold it together. They explore radioactive decay, including alpha, beta, and gamma emissions, and learn to balance nuclear equations. Key concepts include half-life, with calculations using the formula N = N₀(1/2)^(t/T), where N is the remaining quantity, N₀ is the initial quantity, t is elapsed time, and T is the half-life. For example, a 10 g sample of Carbon-14 (T = 5730 years) decays to approximately 5 g after 5730 years.

## Energetics and Thermochemistry
Learners investigate the energy changes associated with chemical reactions, focusing on enthalpy changes. They apply Hess's Law to determine the enthalpy of a reaction from a series of steps. The standard enthalpy change of formation, ΔHf, is calculated using the formula ΔH_reaction = ΣΔHf(products) - ΣΔHf(reactants). For instance, the combustion of methane (CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O) has a ΔH of -890 kJ/mol, indicating a highly exothermic process with significant energy release.

## Redox Reactions and Electrochemistry
Learners investigate oxidation-reduction reactions, identifying oxidizing and reducing agents through changes in oxidation states. They apply the activity series to predict the feasibility of reactions. Key calculations involve standard electrode potentials (E°) to determine cell potential using E°_cell = E°_cathode - E°_anode. For example, a zinc-copper cell (Zn²⁺/Zn, E° = -0.76 V; Cu²⁺/Cu, E° = +0.34 V) has a E°_cell of +1.10 V, confirming it is a spontaneous electrochemical process.

## Chemical Bonding and Molecular Geometry
Learners investigate the types of chemical bonds, including ionic, covalent, and metallic, using electronegativity differences. They apply Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion (VSEPR) theory to predict molecular shapes based on electron domain geometry. For example, carbon dioxide (CO₂) has two double bonds, resulting in a linear electron domain geometry and a bond angle of 180°, while water (H₂O) has two lone pairs and two bonding pairs, resulting in a bent shape with a bond angle of 104.5°.

## Human Physiology and Homeostasis
Learners investigate the mechanisms of homeostasis in the human body, focusing on the endocrine and nervous systems. They explore the regulation of blood glucose levels by insulin and glucagon. Key concepts include negative feedback mechanisms that counteract deviations from a set point. For example, after a meal, blood glucose rises, stimulating pancreatic beta cells to release insulin, which promotes glucose uptake by cells, lowering blood glucose back to its normal range of 4.0-6.0 mmol/L.

## Biodiversity and Evolution
Learners investigate the process of evolution by natural selection, using evidence from fossil records, comparative anatomy, and molecular biology. They analyze how adaptations, such as the beak variations in Darwin's finches on the Galápagos Islands, provide a survival advantage in specific environments. Learners examine the Hardy-Weinberg principle (p² + 2pq + q² = 1) to understand genetic equilibrium in populations and calculate allele frequencies, providing a mathematical model for studying evolutionary change.
