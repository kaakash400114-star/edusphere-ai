# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 5 Science  
## The Structure and Function of Living Things  
Learners investigate the key systems within plants and animals, focusing on how they support life. In plants, they identify the roots, stem, leaves, and flowers, understanding that roots absorb water and nutrients, the stem transports them, and leaves use sunlight for photosynthesis. For animals, they explore the roles of the skeletal, muscular, and circulatory systems, learning that bones provide support, muscles enable movement, and the heart pumps blood to deliver oxygen, for instance, a rabbit's heart beats 120-150 times per minute to sustain its active life.  

## States of Matter and Their Changes  
Learners explore the properties of solids, liquids, and gases and the processes that change one state to another. They learn that solids have a fixed shape and volume, liquids flow and take the shape of their container, and gases expand to fill any space. They investigate processes like melting (ice at 0°C turning to water), freezing, evaporation (puddles disappearing on a hot day), and condensation (water droplets forming on a cold window), recognizing how temperature and energy drive these changes in everyday materials like water.  

## Forces and Simple Machines  
Learners investigate forces as pushes and pulls that can change an object's motion. They explore contact forces like friction, which slows a sliding block, and non-contact forces like gravity, which pulls a ball toward the Earth. They identify and work with simple machines: levers (seesaws), pulleys (flagpoles), and inclined planes (ramps). Learners calculate that using a ramp to lift a 10 kg box requires less force than lifting it straight up, demonstrating how machines make work easier by changing the direction or amount of force needed.  

## Energy Sources and Their Uses  
Learners investigate different forms of energy and their sources, focusing on renewable and non-renewable resources. They learn that sunlight powers solar panels, wind turns turbines, and water dams generate hydroelectricity. They compare these to non-renewable sources like coal and oil, which release greenhouse gases when burned. Learners explore energy transformations, such as how a light bulb converts electrical energy into light and heat, and why conserving energy, like turning off unused lights, is important for global sustainability.  

## Earth's Systems and Their Interactions  
Learners investigate how Earth's systems—geosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere, and biosphere—interact. They learn about the rock cycle, where igneous, sedimentary, and metamorphic rocks form and change over time. They explore the water cycle, where evaporated water from oceans forms clouds and returns as rain, and how the atmosphere protects life by absorbing harmful UV radiation. Learners analyze how human actions, like deforestation, disrupt these balances, linking local examples to global environmental challenges.  

## Scientific Inquiry and Evidence  
Learners develop skills to ask questions, design fair tests, and collect evidence. They learn to form a hypothesis, such as "Plants grow taller with sunlight," and test it by growing bean plants in light and dark conditions. They measure and record data, like plant height in centimeters each day, and use tools like magnifying glasses or thermometers. Learners communicate findings through drawings, charts, and simple reports, emphasizing that scientific ideas are based on observable evidence, and that sharing results helps build collective knowledge.
