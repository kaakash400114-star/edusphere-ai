# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 6 English

## Literary Analysis and Textual Interpretation
Learners investigate how authors use literary devices to create meaning and evoke emotion. Students identify and analyze the function of simile, metaphor, personification, and hyperbole within various texts. For instance, they can explain how the metaphor 'her voice was music to his ears' conveys deep appreciation. Learners examine how a character's motivations are revealed through dialogue and action, and they support their interpretations with direct textual evidence to build a substantiated analysis.

## Structured Writing and Expository Communication
Learners develop skills in organizing information and constructing coherent arguments. Students learn to structure multi-paragraph expository essays with a clear thesis statement, supporting points, and a conclusion. They practice using transitional phrases (e.g., 'furthermore', 'in contrast') to ensure logical flow. For example, a student might write an essay arguing that renewable energy is crucial for the future, providing specific examples like solar and wind power to support their claim in a structured format.

## Narrative Structure and Creative Expression
Learners investigate the foundational elements of storytelling, including plot, setting, character, and conflict. Students study the narrative arc of exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, and resolution. They apply this knowledge by crafting their own short stories, ensuring a clear problem and resolution. For example, a learner might write a story where the protagonist must overcome a fear, using descriptive language to bring the setting and characters to life.

## Media Literacy and Critical Viewing
Learners critically analyze the construction and purpose of media messages. Students deconstruct advertisements and news articles to identify the target audience, persuasive techniques, and underlying biases. They compare how the same event is reported in different international news sources to understand varying perspectives. For example, learners can evaluate an advertisement for a smartphone, identifying its persuasive language and imagery and discussing its intended impact on a global youth audience.

## Language Conventions and Grammatical Precision
Learners investigate and apply complex grammatical structures to enhance clarity and sophistication in their writing. Students focus on the correct use of commas in complex sentences, the distinction between 'affect' and 'effect', and the proper application of subject-verb agreement with compound subjects. For example, they can identify and correct the error in the sentence: 'The dog and the cats is playing outside.'

## Vocabulary Development and Academic Diction
Learners expand their vocabulary to include more precise and academic language. Students are introduced to thematic word lists (e.g., words related to sustainability, diversity, and innovation) and practice using them in context. They learn strategies for determining the meaning of unfamiliar words using root words, prefixes, suffixes, and context clues. For example, a learner can infer that the word 'transportable' means 'able to be moved' by recognizing the root 'port' (to carry).
