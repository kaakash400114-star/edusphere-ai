# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 6 English


## Literary Analysis and Textual Interpretation
Learners investigate how authors use literary devices to create meaning and evoke emotion. Students identify and analyze the function of simile, metaphor, personification, and hyperbole within various texts. For instance, they can explain how the metaphor 'her voice was music to his ears' conveys deep appreciation. Learners examine how a character's motivations are revealed through dialogue and action, and they support their interpretations with direct textual evidence to build a substantiated analysis.

## Structured Writing and Expository Communication
Learners develop skills in organizing information and constructing coherent arguments. Students learn to structure multi-paragraph expository essays with a clear thesis statement, supporting points, and a conclusion. They practice using transitional phrases (e.g., 'furthermore', 'in contrast') to ensure logical flow. For example, a student might write an essay arguing that renewable energy is crucial for the future, providing specific examples like solar and wind power to support their claim in a structured format.

## Narrative Structure and Creative Expression
Students will analyze the five-stage narrative arc: exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, and resolution, identifying these structures in literary texts. They will examine the four core elements of fiction—plot, setting, character, and conflict—to understand how they drive a narrative forward. Learners will apply this framework to construct original short stories, explicitly developing a central conflict and its resolution. For instance, a student might compose a story using the "hero's journey" model, where the protagonist must defeat a monster (conflict), employing sensory details to vividly describe a dark forest (setting). This process ensures a cohesive narrative structure and demonstrates mastery of creative expression.

## Media Literacy and Critical Viewing
Students apply systematic frameworks to deconstruct media messages, analyzing their construction techniques and persuasive intent. They evaluate advertising by identifying rhetorical devices like loaded language and emotional appeals to assess intended impact on specific demographics. When analyzing news, learners apply source criticism to evaluate authorial bias by examining word choice, omission, and frame of reference. Comparing the textual and visual presentation of a single event across multiple international news outlets reveals how diverse perspectives shape public understanding. For instance, they might deconstruct a sports beverage advertisement to identify its visual and linguistic targeting of adolescent athletes.

## Language Conventions and Grammatical Precision
In the International Baccalaureate Middle Years Programme, students master grammatical rules to ensure precision and style. They learn to correctly punctuate complex sentences using commas after introductory clauses and before coordinating conjunctions to separate independent clauses. A key focus is distinguishing between 'affect' as a verb meaning 'to influence' and 'effect' as a noun meaning 'a result.' For compound subjects joined by 'and,' students apply the rule that a plural verb is required, such as correcting 'The dog and the cats is playing outside' to 'The dog and the cats are playing outside.' These conventions enable students to construct grammatically sound and rhetorically effective written communications.

## Vocabulary Development and Academic Diction
Learners expand their vocabulary to include more precise and academic language. Students are introduced to thematic word lists (e.g., words related to sustainability, diversity, and innovation) and practice using them in context. They learn strategies for determining the meaning of unfamiliar words using root words, prefixes, suffixes, and context clues. For example, a learner can infer that the word 'transportable' means 'able to be moved' by recognizing the root 'port' (to carry).
