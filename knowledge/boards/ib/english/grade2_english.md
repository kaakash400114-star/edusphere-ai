# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 2 English

## Understanding Characters and Settings
Learners investigate how characters' feelings and actions are revealed through simple dialogue and descriptions. They identify the main character in a story and describe the setting using concrete details from the text. Students use sentence starters like "The character felt ____ because ____" and "The setting was a ____ where ____." They explore how settings influence events, such as a storm at sea in a story like 'The Snail and the Whale', connecting to global environments.

## Building Vocabulary with Word Families
Students are introduced to the concept of word families by recognizing common rimes, such as the 'at' family to generate words like 'cat', 'hat', and 'bat'. They sort and match word and picture cards into these phonetic groups to build foundational decoding skills. In addition to phonetic word families, instruction extends to expanding vocabulary by identifying and using synonyms for common words. For instance, students learn alternatives for 'happy' such as 'joyful' and 'cheerful'. They practice applying these new synonyms in context to write more precise sentences. This systematic approach enhances both phonetic understanding and expressive language skills.

## Structuring a Simple Narrative
Learners investigate the basic structure of a story: beginning, middle, and end. They create a sequence of three pictures to show a simple event, such as planting a seed. Students use time-order words like 'first,' 'next,' and 'last' to describe the sequence aloud. For example, "First, we put the seed in the soil. Next, we watered it. Last, it grew a sprout." This organizes thoughts to tell a clear story with a logical flow.

## Non-Fiction Features and Facts
Learners will systematically analyze non-fiction texts to locate and utilize key features including headings, table of contents, indexes, glossaries, labels, captions, and maps. They will distinguish between factual statements and fictional content by identifying signal words like "can," "have," and "are" in simple sentences. Students will employ the "I see... I learn..." method to directly extract and record verifiable information from the text, such as "I see a label for a root. I learn a root takes in water." This process builds foundational information literacy, enabling students to use text features to navigate texts efficiently and locate knowledge independently.

## Expressing Opinions with Reasons
Students learn to formulate and articulate personal opinions using a clear statement and a supporting reason. They employ sentence structures to connect an opinion, such as "My favorite part was...", with justification, for example, "...because the illustrations told a story without words." Key methods include identifying preferences, evaluating information, and selecting appropriate evidence to support a claim. A conceptual understanding of reasoning is developed, where one belief logically leads to another. For instance, a student might state, "I think the playground is the best place for recess because it gives us space to run and exercise.

## Collaborative Storytelling Through Role-Play
Learners engage in collaborative storytelling by collectively developing a narrative premise and assigning character roles. They use simple props and costumes to embody these characters, enacting scenes that move a shared plot forward. The core method involves structured improvisation where students use spoken dialogue and descriptive language to advance the story, exemplified by phrases like "The dark cave is too scary, but I have a torch!" This process reinforces narrative concepts such as beginning, middle, and end while requiring students to listen, respond, and build upon each other's contributions. Through this method, they learn to co-construct coherent narratives while practicing cooperation, creative problem-solving, and clear verbal communication skills.
