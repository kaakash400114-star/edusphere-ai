# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 2 English
## Understanding Characters and Settings
Learners investigate how characters' feelings and actions are revealed through simple dialogue and descriptions. They identify the main character in a story and describe the setting using concrete details from the text. Students use sentence starters like "The character felt ____ because ____" and "The setting was a ____ where ____." They explore how settings influence events, such as a storm at sea in a story like 'The Snail and the Whale', connecting to global environments.

## Building Vocabulary with Word Families
Learners investigate common word families with shared endings, such as 'at' (cat, hat, bat) and 'ight' (light, night, sight). They sort picture and word cards into these families to build phonetic awareness. Students also learn synonyms for common words like 'happy' (joyful, cheerful) and apply them in simple sentences. For example, "The girl was cheerful when she played." This supports expanding language for expressing ideas clearly and precisely.

## Structuring a Simple Narrative
Learners investigate the basic structure of a story: beginning, middle, and end. They create a sequence of three pictures to show a simple event, such as planting a seed. Students use time-order words like 'first,' 'next,' and 'last' to describe the sequence aloud. For example, "First, we put the seed in the soil. Next, we watered it. Last, it grew a sprout." This organizes thoughts to tell a clear story with a logical flow.

## Non-Fiction Features and Facts
Learners investigate non-fiction books to identify features like headings, labels, and captions. They find and share simple facts from texts, such as "A butterfly has four wings." Students use a "I see... I learn..." chart to record observations, for example, "I see a map of a garden. I learn where plants grow." This builds information literacy by distinguishing fact from fiction and using text features to locate knowledge.

## Expressing Opinions with Reasons
Learners investigate how to share opinions and provide simple reasons to support them. They use sentence frames like "I enjoyed the book because ____" and "I think ____ is a better choice because ____." For example, "I think the character was brave because he helped his friend." This encourages critical thinking and respectful dialogue, acknowledging different perspectives, which is central to international-mindedness.

## Collaborative Storytelling Through Role-Play
Learners investigate how to build a story together by taking on character roles. They use simple props to act out scenarios, such as a journey or a problem to solve. Students practice using dialogue to advance the plot, like "Let's cross the bridge together!" This activity develops cooperation, creativity, and spoken language skills, allowing them to create narratives that reflect diverse experiences and global issues.
