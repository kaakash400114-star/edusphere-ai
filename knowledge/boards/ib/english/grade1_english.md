# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 1 English

## Narrative Structures
Learners investigate the basic components of a story. They learn that stories have a character, a setting, and a sequence of events. Students identify the beginning (what happens first), the middle (what happens next), and the end (what happens last). They can sequence picture cards to tell a simple story, such as a frog jumping from lily pad to lily pad in a pond. Learners use this structure to create and orally recount their own short narratives.

## Phonemic Awareness
Learners investigate the sounds that make up words. They learn to identify and produce initial sounds (phonemes) in words. For example, they learn that the word 'sun' starts with the /s/ sound. Students play games like 'I Spy' ("I spy with my little eye something beginning with /b/"). They practice blending sounds to read simple CVC (consonant-vowel-consonant) words like 'cat', 'dog', and 'pin'.

## High-Frequency Sight Words
Learners investigate and memorize common words that appear frequently in texts. These words often cannot be easily sounded out and must be recognized by sight. The list includes words like 'the', 'is', 'was', 'to', and 'and'. Students practice reading these words in isolation and in simple, decodable sentences. For example, they read "The cat is big." They build fluency through repeated exposure and daily practice.

## Listening and Responding
Learners investigate how to be active listeners. They learn to look at the speaker, keep quiet, and think about what is being said. Students listen to short stories, poems, and songs from diverse cultures. After listening, they respond to simple, direct questions like "Who was the main character?" or "What was your favourite part?" This practice helps them understand information, follow instructions, and show respect for the speaker.

## Language Conventions: Punctuation
Learners investigate the basic rules of punctuation to understand the meaning of sentences. They are introduced to the period (.) and the question mark (?). Students learn that a period marks the end of a statement ("I like red.") and a question mark ends a question ("What is your name?"). They practice identifying and using these correctly when writing and reading simple sentences.

## Non-Fiction: Identifying Facts
Learners investigate the difference between real (non-fiction) and make-believe (fiction) texts. They learn that non-fiction books provide facts and information. Students explore simple non-fiction texts about animals from around the world, such as "An elephant has a long trunk." They can point to a picture of an animal and name a fact they learned, connecting information to the real world.
