# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 3 English

## Reading for Understanding and Enjoyment
Learners read a variety of engaging texts, including stories, poems, and simple non-fiction books, to identify the main idea and supporting details. They use pictures and context to understand new vocabulary, learning to decode words by breaking them into syllables (e.g., 'hap-py', 'el-e-phant'). Learners make predictions about what will happen next and ask questions to clarify their understanding, supported by prompts like, "What do you think will happen to the character?" and "Can you find the part that tells us this?".

## Writing to Communicate Ideas
Learners write simple, coherent sentences using capital letters, full stops, and question marks correctly to express their thoughts. They organize their writing with a clear beginning, middle, and end, often using a planning template or graphic organizer. For example, they might write a short, structured paragraph describing a family member, ensuring each sentence gives one specific detail. Learners also begin to use descriptive words (adjectives) to make their writing more interesting, such as 'large' instead of just 'big'.

## Speaking and Listening Skills
Learners practice active listening by making eye contact, nodding, and asking relevant questions when others speak. They present short, prepared talks on familiar topics, such as 'My Favourite Book' or 'Our School Rules', speaking clearly and using simple gestures for emphasis. During group discussions, learners express their own ideas respectfully and consider the opinions of others, using phrases like, 'I agree with...' or 'Can I add something?'. They learn to speak at an appropriate volume and pace for their audience.

## Language Structure and Conventions
Learners investigate the building blocks of language by identifying and using common nouns (e.g., 'dog', 'house') and proper nouns (e.g., 'London', 'Maria'). They learn to recognize and use simple past-tense verbs to describe events that have already happened, like 'walked' or 'played'. Learners also begin to understand the function of conjunctions like 'and', 'but', and 'because' to join two simple ideas into one sentence, for example: "I wanted to play outside, but it started to rain."

## Developing Foundational Literacy
Learners develop phonemic awareness by identifying initial and final sounds in words (e.g., the 's' in 'sun' and the 't' in 'cat'). They practice common spelling patterns and high-frequency sight words to improve reading fluency. Learners engage with reading by making connections between texts and their own lives, asking, "Have you ever felt like that character?" They also learn to retell a story in sequence, using first, then, and finally to demonstrate comprehension of the plot.

## Exploring Different Text Types
Learners are introduced to different purposes for writing and reading. They can identify features of a simple information report, such as headings and facts. They practice writing a basic procedural text, like 'How to Make a Sandwich,' by listing the steps in order. Learners also examine poetry, focusing on rhythm and rhyme, and try to write their own short, simple poems using patterns they have studied, reinforcing their understanding of language as a creative tool.
