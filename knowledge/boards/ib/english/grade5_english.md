# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 5 English

## Reading Comprehension
Learners investigate a variety of global texts, including folktales from Japan, non-fiction articles about marine life, and poetry from different cultures. They identify the main idea, supporting details, and author's purpose. Students learn to infer meaning using context clues and distinguish between fact and opinion. They also explore how different perspectives influence a story's retelling by comparing versions of the same folktale from various countries.

## Narrative Writing
Learners investigate the structure of stories by reading and writing their own narratives. They learn to develop a plot with a clear beginning, middle, and end, focusing on creating detailed settings and compelling characters. Students practice using descriptive language to show, not tell, emotions and events. They explore stories from around the world to understand how different cultures use narrative techniques to convey universal themes, such as courage and kindness.

## Grammar and Mechanics
Learners investigate the rules of English grammar to improve their communication. They study parts of speech, including more complex adverbs and prepositional phrases. Students practice using commas correctly in dates, addresses, and introductory clauses. They learn to correctly punctuate dialogue and identify subjects and predicates in longer sentences. This knowledge is applied to their own writing to ensure clarity and correctness.

## Speaking and Listening
Learners investigate effective communication through group discussions, oral presentations, and listening to diverse speakers. They practice speaking clearly and concisely to share ideas about global topics like conservation or cultural traditions. Students learn to be active listeners, asking clarifying questions and summarizing what others have said. They present their research to peers, using visual aids like posters or digital slides to support their message.

## Research Skills
Learners investigate how to find and use information from various sources. They learn to formulate specific research questions to guide their inquiry. Students practice taking notes from non-fiction texts and websites, paraphrasing information in their own words to avoid plagiarism. They understand the importance of citing sources and organize their findings into a structured report or presentation on an international issue.

## Media Literacy
Learners investigate how media messages are constructed and their purpose. They analyze different forms of media, such as news websites, advertisements, and educational videos. Students learn to identify the target audience of a media text and recognize persuasive techniques like slogans and imagery. They compare how a single event is reported by different news sources from various countries to understand the role of perspective.
