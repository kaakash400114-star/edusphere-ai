# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 5 English


## Reading Comprehension
Learners apply specific comprehension strategies to determine a text's central idea by distinguishing between key supporting details and minor information. They analyze an author's purpose by examining text features such as tone, word choice, and structure in genres like a non-fiction article on coral reefs or a Japanese folktale. Students practice making inferences about unstated meanings by synthesizing explicit textual evidence with background knowledge. They differentiate between objective statements of fact and subjective expressions of opinion by evaluating the use of verifiable language in sources like a poem about cultural identity. Finally, learners compare and contrast multiple versions of a single folktale from different countries to analyze how unique cultural perspectives influence a narrative's plot, characters, and underlying message.

## Narrative Writing
Learners investigate the structure of stories by reading and writing their own narratives. They learn to develop a plot with a clear beginning, middle, and end, focusing on creating detailed settings and compelling characters. Students practice using descriptive language to show, not tell, emotions and events. They explore stories from around the world to understand how different cultures use narrative techniques to convey universal themes, such as courage and kindness.

## Grammar and Mechanics
Students analyze sentence structure by identifying subjects and predicates, including compound subjects and predicates with two or more elements. They distinguish between adverbs that modify verbs, adjectives, and other adverbs, and correctly form prepositional phrases to function as adjectives or adverbs. Punctuation rules include using commas after introductory clauses (e.g., "After the rain stopped, we went outside") and in addresses (e.g., "123 Maple Street, Springfield, IL"). Learners also apply standard conventions for punctuating dialogue, such as placing commas and periods inside quotation marks and using capital letters for the first word of spoken dialogue. Finally, students apply this grammatical knowledge to edit their own writing, focusing on sentence structure for clarity and correctness.

## Speaking and Listening
Students learn to structure collaborative discussions using agreed-upon protocols, such as establishing speaking turns and summarizing main points, to ensure productive conversations on global issues like climate change or migration. They apply a clear formula for oral presentations: an introduction to state the topic and purpose, a body section with evidence, and a conclusion to summarize key takeaways, often incorporating digital slides or posters as visual support. A core concept for active listening involves paraphrasing what a speaker has said and preparing specific, clarifying questions to demonstrate comprehension. Students practice these methods through role-playing scenarios, such as organizing a community garden or planning a cultural celebration, to develop clear, concise, and persuasive communication skills. They also learn to critique peer presentations constructively by focusing on strengths and offering respectful suggestions for improvement.

## Research Skills
Students learn to develop specific, researchable questions using models like the 5 Ws (Who, What, Where, When, Why) to guide their inquiry. They practice two-column note-taking from diverse sources, such as encyclopedia entries and news articles, to separate key facts from personal observations. To ensure academic integrity, students are taught to paraphrase information by restating it in their own words and acknowledging all sources using a simple citation format, like author and date. Finally, they organize their researched findings into a multi-paragraph report using a clear structure: introduction, body paragraphs with supporting evidence, and a conclusion.

## Media Literacy
Learners will apply a structured framework to deconstruct media messages, identifying key elements including the producer, intended audience, and persuasive techniques such as emotional appeals and bandwagon effects. Students will analyze the construction of advertisements by examining the deliberate use of imagery, colour, and slogan to create a specific brand image. They will also evaluate the reporting of a single global event, such as a natural disaster, by comparing and contrasting the headlines, chosen vocabulary, and supporting visuals from two distinct international news outlets. This comparison enables students to critically assess the influence of perspective and bias in news reporting. Through this analysis, learners develop a robust understanding of how media is intentionally shaped to inform and persuade audiences.
