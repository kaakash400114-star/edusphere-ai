# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 7 English
## Narrative Structures and Short Story Elements
Learners investigate the foundational elements of narrative fiction, including plot structure (exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, resolution), character development (protagonist, antagonist, motivation), and setting. They analyse how authors use dialogue and descriptive language to advance the plot and reveal character, examining examples from various cultural traditions to understand how these elements create engaging and meaningful stories. Students apply this understanding to construct their own short stories, ensuring a coherent plot and well-developed characters.

## Shakespearean Language and Themes
Learners investigate the language and enduring themes in Shakespearean works through adapted scripts or abridged versions suitable for middle school, such as *A Midsummer Night's Dream* or *Romeo and Juliet*. They explore Shakespearean vocabulary, iambic pentameter, and dramatic irony, connecting these literary devices to universal themes of love, conflict, and identity. This study fosters an appreciation for the Bard's influence on the English language and his exploration of human emotions relevant across different cultures and eras.

## Informational Text Analysis
Learners investigate how to analyse non-fiction texts by identifying the author's purpose, main arguments, and supporting evidence. They develop skills in differentiating between fact and opinion and assessing the reliability of sources, a key component of international-mindedness. Students learn to employ strategies such as skimming, scanning, and summarising to extract information from a variety of global texts, including news articles, biographies, and informational websites, preparing them for academic research.

## Poetry: Form and Figurative Language
Learners investigate the structure and figurative language used in diverse poetic forms, such as the sonnet, haiku, and free verse. They explore literary devices, including simile, metaphor, personification, and imagery, analysing how poets use them to convey emotion and create vivid mental images. Students study examples of poetry from different cultural traditions, examining how form and figurative language reflect and shape human experience across borders and foster appreciation for diverse poetic expression.

## Persuasive Writing and Rhetorical Devices
Learners investigate the techniques of persuasive writing, focusing on the construction of logical arguments and the use of rhetorical devices to influence an audience. They learn to identify and employ ethos, pathos, and logos to structure their own persuasive texts, such as letters to the editor or opinion pieces. Students analyse examples of persuasive communication from global leaders and activists, considering how language can be used to promote social change and advocate for different viewpoints in a respectful, internationally-minded manner.

## Digital Literacy and Communication
Learners investigate how to effectively and ethically communicate in a digital world. They develop skills in analysing digital media for bias and intent, evaluating the credibility of online sources, and using digital tools to collaborate and share information respectfully. This includes understanding cyber safety, digital footprints, and the responsible use of social media, preparing students to be critical and compassionate digital citizens in an interconnected global community.
