# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 8 English

## Literary Devices and Figurative Language
Learners investigate how authors use language to create meaning. They are taught to identify, analyze, and evaluate the effect of key devices, including simile (e.g., 'her voice was as clear as a bell'), metaphor (e.g., 'time is a thief'), personification (e.g., 'the wind whispered secrets'), and hyperbole (e.g., 'I'm so hungry I could eat a horse'). Students apply this knowledge to dissect the word and imagery in a variety of texts, understanding how figurative language enhances emotional impact and creates vivid mental pictures for the reader.

## Structuring Argumentative and Persuasive Writing
Learners develop skills in constructing coherent and convincing arguments. They master the formal structure of a persuasive essay, which includes a clear thesis statement, logically sequenced body paragraphs each with a topic sentence and supporting evidence (facts, statistics, expert quotes), and a concluding paragraph that reinforces the main claim. Students learn to anticipate and counter opposing viewpoints, employing rhetorical strategies such as ethos, pathos, and logos to strengthen their position on global issues like environmental protection or social equity.

## Narrative Structure and Plot Development
Learners investigate the fundamental elements that drive a compelling story. They analyze plot structures, including the traditional five-stage arc (exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, resolution) and more modern variations. Students learn how authors use techniques such as foreshadowing, flashback, and suspense to build tension and engage an audience. They apply this knowledge by planning and drafting their own narratives, focusing on crafting a well-defined conflict, dynamic character development, and a satisfying resolution that resonates with a global reader.

## Non-Fiction Text Analysis and Summarization
Learners develop critical skills for engaging with diverse non-fiction texts. They are taught to identify the author's purpose, main argument, and key supporting details within articles, essays, and reports. Students learn to differentiate between fact, opinion, and bias. They practice summarizing complex information objectively, extracting only the essential points without adding personal interpretation. This skill is applied to real-world texts, such as news articles about international events or scientific papers on climate change, fostering information literacy.

## Shakespearean Language and Themes
Learners are introduced to the enduring influence of Shakespeare through accessible works like *A Midsummer Night's Dream* or *Romeo and Juliet*. They investigate the unique language of Early Modern English, learning to decode unfamiliar vocabulary and grammatical structures. Students explore universal themes such as love, conflict, and identity, making connections to their own lives and contemporary global society. Activities include close reading of key scenes, paraphrasing dialogue into modern English, and analyzing character motivations in a socio-historical context.

## Research Skills and Academic Integrity
Learners investigate the process of conducting research responsibly. They are taught to formulate effective inquiry questions, evaluate the credibility and relevance of sources (books, websites, databases), and synthesize information to support a thesis. A key focus is on academic integrity; students learn and practice the correct methods for paraphrasing, summarizing, and quoting sources to avoid plagiarism. They learn to create a formal bibliography, using a standardized citation style, ensuring their work is ethical and respects intellectual property from a global perspective.
