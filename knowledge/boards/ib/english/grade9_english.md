# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 9 English

## Literary Analysis: Character Motivation and Development
Learners investigate how authors use direct and indirect characterization to construct multifaceted characters. Students identify and analyse methods such as dialogue, thoughts, actions, and appearance to understand character motivation. They apply textual evidence to explain how a character evolves, perhaps from innocence to experience, using examples like the protagonist’s transition in a novel such as *To Kill a Mockingbird*. This involves evaluating the impact of a character’s decisions on the plot and exploring the global context of social pressures and moral dilemmas that shape their journey.

## Comparative Textual Analysis: Theme and Perspective
Learners investigate how diverse cultural perspectives shape the exploration of universal themes. Students compare two texts, such as a poem from a post-colonial author and a short story from a Western tradition, to analyse how each writer addresses themes like identity or conflict. They employ a structured comparative framework to discuss similarities and differences in authorial purpose, narrative style, and cultural context, fostering an understanding of how local stories contribute to a global conversation about shared human experiences.

## Non-Fiction and Media Literacy: Argument and Persuasion
Learners investigate the construction of arguments in non-fiction and media texts. Students identify and evaluate rhetorical devices such as ethos, pathos, and logos in sources like opinion articles or documentary transcripts. They distinguish between fact and opinion, analyse the use of selective evidence, and assess the intended audience and purpose. This involves crafting a written response that synthesises information from multiple sources to form a reasoned viewpoint on a contemporary global issue, such as climate change or digital privacy.

## The Writing Process: Crafting Academic Arguments
Learners investigate the conventions of formal academic writing. Students learn to structure a persuasive essay with a clear thesis statement, logically sequenced paragraphs, and relevant evidence. They practice integrating source material through effective paraphrasing and summarisation, using in-text citations. The process includes peer review to provide constructive feedback on argumentative coherence, language accuracy, and the development of ideas, adhering to global academic standards such as the MLA or Chicago style guides.

## Language and Structure: Figurative Language and Narrative Devices
Learners investigate how authors use figurative language and narrative structures to create meaning and effect. Students analyse devices such as symbolism, metaphor, and irony, and examine how structural choices like foreshadowing and flashbacks build suspense. For example, they might explore the use of a central motif in a play like *The Crucible* to understand thematic development. This analysis develops precise terminology to discuss authorial craft and its impact on the reader’s interpretation.

## Global Literature and Cultural Context
Learners investigate a range of texts from different cultures to explore how literature reflects and shapes societal values. Students study a novel or play from a non-Anglophone context, such as *Things Fall Apart* by Chinua Achebe, analysing its themes of tradition and change. They consider the historical and social context that informs the text, fostering an appreciation for diverse literary voices and perspectives. This inquiry develops empathy and an understanding of the complex interplay between local culture and global narratives.
