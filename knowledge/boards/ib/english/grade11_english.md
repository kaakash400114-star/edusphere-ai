# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 11 English

## Language and Identity
Students analyze how language constructs and reflects personal and group identities. They examine how dialects, sociolects, and registers signal belonging or otherness. Learners investigate the linguistic power of texts, from political speeches to social media posts, and their impact on perception. They critically assess how authors use narrative voice and characterization to explore themes of self, heritage, and displacement. The unit emphasizes how language choices in different genres (e.g., autobiography, essay) shape the reader's understanding of identity and culture, fostering an appreciation for diverse linguistic expressions across global contexts.

## Literary Analysis of Modernism
Students engage with the formal innovations and thematic concerns of modernist literature. They analyze fragmented narratives, stream of consciousness, and unreliable narration as responses to the early 20th-century world. Learners investigate how authors like Virginia Woolf and T.S. Eliot employed symbolism and allusion to express alienation and the search for meaning. They dissect the use of interior monologue and non-linear plot structures, understanding their role in conveying the psyche of the modern individual. This study connects historical anxieties to literary form, highlighting the break from Victorian traditions.

## The Rhetoric of Persuasion
Learners investigate the classical rhetorical appeals (ethos, pathos, logos) as applied in contemporary and historical texts. They deconstruct advertising campaigns, political manifestos, and journalistic articles to identify persuasive strategies and their intended effects. Students analyze how language and visual elements combine to create arguments and shape public opinion. They practice constructing their own persuasive arguments, employing specific rhetorical devices and logical fallacies, while evaluating the ethical implications of persuasive language in a globalized media landscape. The focus is on critical consumption and production of rhetoric.

## World Literature: Post-Colonial Perspectives
Students study a range of post-colonial literary texts from regions such as Africa, the Caribbean, and South Asia. They examine how authors use literature to challenge imperial narratives and reclaim cultural voices. Learners investigate themes of hybridity, mimicry, and the 'other' as depicted in works by authors like Chinua Achebe or Jamaica Kincaid. They analyze the linguistic choices, such as the incorporation of native languages or dialects, that signify resistance and cultural identity. This study fosters an understanding of the complex legacy of colonialism through diverse literary perspectives.

## Research and Academic Writing
Students develop skills for extended academic inquiry and formal writing. They learn to formulate research questions, evaluate primary and secondary sources for credibility and bias, and integrate evidence effectively using proper citation methods. Learners investigate the structure and conventions of academic essays, literature reviews, and the IB's own Extended Essay. They practice paraphrasing, summarizing, and synthesizing information to construct coherent arguments supported by scholarly analysis. This module emphasizes academic integrity and the development of a systematic research process for in-depth investigation.

## Digital and Multimodal Texts
Learners investigate how meaning is created in digital environments through the interplay of text, image, sound, and video. They analyze the rhetoric of websites, podcasts, social media feeds, and documentary films. Students examine how the medium influences the message, exploring concepts like virality, digital storytelling, and the formation of online communities. They critique the construction of reality in multimodal texts and analyze their impact on global communication. This unit equips students to be critical consumers and creators of content in an increasingly digital and interconnected world.
