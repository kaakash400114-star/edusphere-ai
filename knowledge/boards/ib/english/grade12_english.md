# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 12 English
## World Literature: Comparative Analysis
Learners investigate seminal texts from diverse cultural and linguistic backgrounds through the lens of comparative analysis. They employ a structured approach to evaluate literary works, identifying and contrasting thematic concerns, narrative techniques, and stylistic choices. This involves a close reading of texts such as *One Hundred Years of Solitude* and *The God of Small Things*, examining how magical realism and post-colonial narratives critique power and history. Learners formulate precise arguments supported by textual evidence and critical theory, demonstrating an understanding of how context shapes interpretation.
## Advanced Composition and Rhetoric
Learners develop sophisticated persuasive and expository writing skills required for academic and professional contexts. They master advanced rhetorical devices—including anaphora, polysyndeton, and antimetabole—to construct nuanced and compelling arguments. Using authentic models from The Economist and academic journals, learners analyse and apply techniques for constructing a robust thesis, integrating research with proper citation methods, and structuring complex essays. They practice writing for various audiences and purposes, ensuring their prose is clear, concise, and stylistically mature, while adhering to formal academic conventions.
## Critical Media Studies
Learners investigate the construction and dissemination of meaning across a range of contemporary media forms. They deconstruct news articles, documentaries, and social media campaigns to identify underlying biases, ideological frameworks, and persuasive strategies. This involves analysing how digital platforms like TikTok and Instagram shape public discourse and cultural narratives. Learners apply critical frameworks to evaluate the impact of media ownership, algorithmic curation, and visual rhetoric on audiences, fostering an understanding of their role as critical consumers and producers in a globally interconnected information ecosystem.
## Shakespearean Tragedy: Power and Performance
Learners engage critically with Shakespearean tragedy through a dual focus on text and performance. They analyse the dramatic and linguistic complexity of plays such as *Hamlet* and *Othello*, exploring universal themes of ambition, jealousy, and moral ambiguity. Learners investigate how directors, from Laurence Olivier to Ivo van Hove, reinterpret these plays for modern audiences, examining the role of performance in mediating meaning. This involves analysing textual evidence alongside staging choices, character motivations, and historical context to produce a nuanced appreciation of the plays' enduring relevance.
## Language and Identity
Learners explore the dynamic and complex relationship between language, identity, and social power. They investigate how dialect, register, and discourse shape individual and group identities within a globalized world. This includes analysing examples from literature, political speeches, and everyday conversation to observe code-switching, linguistic prejudice, and the creation of hybrid identities. Learners examine the socio-political implications of language policies and consider how English functions as both a lingua franca and a vehicle for cultural imperialism, fostering a deep appreciation for the power and plasticity of communication.
## The Extended Essay (EE) Research Methodology
Learners investigate and apply the core principles of academic research to develop a focused, argument-based Extended Essay. They master the process of formulating a viable research question, conducting a comprehensive literature review using academic databases like JSTOR, and gathering credible sources. This includes a critical evaluation of source materials and the synthesis of information to construct a coherent argument. Learners develop proficiency in academic writing conventions, including precise citation using MLA or Chicago styles, and the structuring of a formal research document with an annotated bibliography.
