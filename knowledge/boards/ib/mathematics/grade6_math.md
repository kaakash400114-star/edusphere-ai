# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 6 Mathematics

## Number Systems and Operations
Learners investigate the properties of integers and rational numbers, focusing on operations with decimals and fractions. They apply the order of operations (PEMDAS/BODMAS) to solve multi-step problems involving positive and negative numbers. Students explore percentages in real-world contexts, such as calculating discounts and population growth. They use estimation to check reasonableness and employ mental math strategies for fluency. For example, they convert between fractions, decimals, and percentages and apply proportional reasoning to scale recipes or divide resources fairly.

## Algebraic Thinking
Learners investigate patterns and relationships using variables to represent unknown values. They write and evaluate simple algebraic expressions, substituting given numbers for letters. Students explore the concept of a linear equation and solve one-step equations using inverse operations. They represent real-life situations, like calculating the cost of multiple items, with simple formulas. For example, they might express the relationship between the number of hours worked (h) and total pay (p) as p = 10h, then calculate pay for 5 hours of work.

## Geometry and Spatial Reasoning
Learners investigate the properties of two- and three-dimensional shapes, including angles, lines, and symmetry. They classify polygons based on their attributes and calculate the perimeter and area of rectangles and triangles. Students learn to plot points in the Cartesian plane to create simple geometric figures. For example, they might design a school garden by calculating the area needed for different sections or determine the missing angle in a triangle formed by a bridge's support beams using the fact that angles sum to 180 degrees.

## Data Handling and Probability
Learners investigate methods for collecting, organizing, and interpreting data. They construct and analyze bar graphs, line graphs, and pie charts to represent information from international contexts, such as global temperature changes or favorite sports in different countries. Students calculate basic statistics, including the mean, median, and mode, to understand data sets. They also explore simple probability through experiments, like determining the likelihood of selecting a colored marble from a bag, connecting mathematical concepts to everyday decision-making.

## Measurement
Learners investigate the concepts of length, mass, volume, and time within both the metric and customary systems. They convert between different units of measurement to solve problems, such as calculating travel time between cities or determining the amount of water needed for a swimming pool. Students apply their knowledge to calculate area and volume in practical situations. For example, they might find the volume of a rectangular prism to determine how much sand is required for a sandbox, fostering an understanding of spatial relationships in real-world construction projects.

## Financial Mathematics
Learners investigate financial concepts relevant to personal and global contexts. They calculate simple and compound interest to understand savings and debt growth over time. Students learn to create and interpret budgets, managing expenses for a hypothetical trip or community project. They explore concepts of currency exchange, calculating costs for goods in different countries to appreciate international trade. For example, they might determine the total cost of a mobile phone plan or compare the price of an imported item using current exchange rates to develop responsible financial habits.
