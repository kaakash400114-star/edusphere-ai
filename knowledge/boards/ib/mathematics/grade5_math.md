# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 5 Mathematics

## Number Operations and Place Value
Learners build on their understanding of place value to numbers up to 1,000,000. They explore number patterns and sequences, using them to make predictions. Students practice efficient strategies for mental calculations and develop fluency with standard written algorithms for addition, subtraction, and multiplication of whole numbers. For example, they multiply 248 by 15, breaking it down into (248 x 10) + (248 x 5), and divide 624 by 12 to find the quotient. They use rounding and estimating to check the reasonableness of their answers.

## Fractions, Decimals, and Percentages
Learners investigate the relationship between fractions, decimals, and percentages. They learn to find equivalent fractions, simplify them, and compare their size using common denominators. Students add and subtract fractions with the same denominator, and solve simple problems involving fractions. For instance, they calculate 1/2 of 120 or 3/4 of 200. They convert fractions like 3/4 into decimals (0.75) and percentages (75%), using real-world data such as a class survey where 75% of students preferred football.

## Geometry and Shapes
Learners investigate the properties of two-dimensional and three-dimensional shapes. They identify, describe, and compare polygons, including quadrilaterals and triangles, focusing on their angles and sides. Students learn the properties of circles, such as radius and diameter. They explore nets that form 3D shapes like cubes and pyramids. They also apply concepts of symmetry and reflection, creating patterns and identifying lines of symmetry in shapes from different cultures, such as Islamic geometric art or Indigenous Australian dot paintings.

## Measurement and Units
Learners explore measurement with a focus on accuracy and the relationships between different units. They solve problems involving perimeter and area of rectangles, using the formula A = l x w. They solve problems involving volume and capacity, using cubic centimetres and litres. They also work with time, calculating elapsed time and converting between 12-hour and 24-hour clocks. An example includes measuring the volume of a water tank and calculating how long it would take to fill it at a given rate.

## Data Handling
Learners design and conduct surveys to collect data. They create and interpret a variety of graphs to represent their findings, including bar graphs, line graphs, and pie charts. Students calculate and analyze data using the mean (average), median, and mode. They learn to compare different data sets and draw conclusions. For instance, they might graph the average temperature in different countries and discuss why these variations exist, fostering an understanding of global climate patterns.

## Problem Solving and Algebraic Thinking
Learners use their mathematical skills to solve problems in real-life contexts. They learn to identify the relevant information in a word problem and choose the appropriate operation to find a solution. Students explore number sentences and simple algebraic equations with missing numbers, using symbols like a letter or a shape to represent an unknown. For example, they solve for 'n' in the equation 4 x n = 36, and create their own problems for classmates to solve, encouraging collaborative inquiry.
