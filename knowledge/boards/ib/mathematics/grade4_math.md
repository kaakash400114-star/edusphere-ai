# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 4 Mathematics  
## Number and Operations  
Learners investigate whole numbers up to 10,000, understanding place value to thousands. They practise efficient methods for addition and subtraction of four-digit numbers, including regrouping. For multiplication, they learn facts up to 10x10 and use arrays or area models for two-digit by one-digit problems. Division is introduced as sharing or grouping, solving problems like 48 ÷ 6 = 8. Learners use concrete materials like base-10 blocks to visualize operations and solve real-world problems, such as calculating the total cost of multiple items or sharing resources equally in a community project.  

## Patterns and Algebra  
Learners identify, extend, and create repeating and growing patterns using shapes, numbers, and objects. They explore patterns in number sequences, such as counting by 25s or 100s. Learners use symbols (e.g., △, ○) to represent unknown numbers in simple equations like 15 + △ = 22. Through investigations, they connect patterns to real-world contexts, such as recognizing patterns in nature (e.g., leaf arrangements) or schedules. These activities develop logical reasoning and problem-solving skills, encouraging them to generalize rules and predict subsequent terms in a sequence.  

## Measurement  
Learners measure length, mass, and capacity using standard units (e.g., metres, grams, litres) and compare estimates with actual measurements. They calculate perimeter by adding side lengths of polygons and area by counting square units in rectangles. Time is explored through reading clocks to the nearest minute and calculating elapsed time (e.g., duration of a school day). Capacity investigations involve comparing containers and solving problems like, "How many litres of water are needed to fill a tank?" Global examples, such as comparing weights of traditional foods from different cultures, enhance understanding and international-mindedness.  

## Geometry and Spatial Sense  
Learners identify, classify, and draw 2D shapes (triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons) and 3D solids (cubes, pyramids, prisms), noting properties like sides, vertices, and faces. They explore symmetry by creating designs and identifying lines of symmetry in objects from their environment. Position and movement are learned through grids and maps, using directional language (e.g., north, south, left, right). Investigations include analyzing architecture from different countries to observe geometric shapes, fostering appreciation for diverse cultural expressions while developing spatial reasoning skills.  

## Data Handling  
Learners collect, organize, and display data using tallies, pictographs, and bar graphs. They interpret graphs to answer questions, such as "Which is the most common pet?" and compare data sets. Probability is introduced through simple chance experiments, like spinning a spinner with four colors, to determine likelihood (e.g., "Which color is most likely to occur?"). Real-world contexts, such as surveying classmates about favorite games or global festivals, encourage critical thinking and responsible data representation, aligning with IB's inquiry-based approach.  

## Problem Solving and Reasoning  
Learners apply mathematical skills to solve complex, real-world problems using multiple strategies (e.g., drawing diagrams, making lists, working backward). They explain their reasoning clearly, justifying solutions with mathematical evidence. Investigations involve open-ended tasks, like planning a class event within a budget, requiring estimation, addition, and subtraction. These tasks develop perseverance and creative thinking while encouraging learners to consider diverse perspectives, such as equitable resource distribution in a global community, reflecting IB's commitment to international-mindedness.
