# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 11 Mathematics  
## Functions and Equations  
Learners investigate quadratic, power, polynomial, and rational functions, understanding their properties through algebraic manipulation and graphical analysis. They explore function notation, domain, range, and transformations, including reflections, translations, and stretches. Key methods include completing the square to find vertices, factoring to determine intercepts, and using the discriminant to analyse roots. Students solve equations and inequalities involving these functions, applying them to real-world scenarios like projectile motion or population growth, emphasizing precise mathematical language and logical reasoning.  

## Trigonometry and Circular Functions  
Learners investigate trigonometric functions, extending definitions from right-angled triangles to the unit circle. They study radian measure, arc length, and sector area, deriving key identities such as Pythagorean, double-angle, and sum/difference formulas. Techniques include solving trigonometric equations, analysing periodic behavior, and modelling phenomena like sound waves or tides. Students explore inverse functions and apply the sine and cosine rules to non-right-angled triangles, emphasizing exact values and general solutions, while connecting to global contexts like architecture or astronomy.  

## Vectors  
Learners investigate vectors in two and three dimensions, focusing on operations like addition, subtraction, and scalar multiplication. They explore the dot product to calculate angles and projections, and the cross product to find perpendicular vectors. Methods include representing vector equations of lines and planes, and solving problems involving forces, navigation, or 3D geometry. Students apply vector principles to real-world scenarios, such as calculating resultant forces in physics or optimizing routes in logistics, emphasizing precision in notation and spatial reasoning.  

## Calculus: Differentiation  
Learners investigate differential calculus, starting with the limit definition of the derivative. They master differentiation rules for power, exponential, logarithmic, and trigonometric functions, including the chain, product, and quotient rules. Techniques involve finding equations of tangents and normals, analysing rates of change, and optimising functions using first and second derivatives. Students apply these concepts to model real-world phenomena like instantaneous velocity or economic cost functions, emphasizing graphical interpretation and algebraic fluency.  

## Calculus: Integration  
Learners investigate integral calculus, beginning with the concept of area under a curve. They master techniques of integration, including substitution and integration by parts, and apply them to find definite and indefinite integrals. Applications involve calculating areas between curves, volumes of revolution, and solving differential equations. Students explore the connection between differentiation and integration via the Fundamental Theorem, applying these methods to model real-world scenarios like population growth or work done in physics, emphasizing precision and problem-solving.  

## Statistics and Probability  
Learners investigate descriptive and inferential statistics, analysing data using measures of central tendency and dispersion. They study discrete and continuous probability distributions, including binomial, normal, and Poisson distributions, applying them to solve problems involving hypothesis testing and confidence intervals. Techniques include calculating conditional probability and expected values, and using linear regression to model relationships. Students analyse real-world datasets, such as economic trends or environmental data, emphasizing ethical data handling and critical interpretation.
