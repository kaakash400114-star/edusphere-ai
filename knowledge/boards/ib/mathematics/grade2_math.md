# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 2 Mathematics  

## Number Sense and Place Value  
Learners investigate numbers up to 100, understanding place value (tens and ones). They read, write, and compare numbers using symbols (>, <, =). Students practise skip-counting by 2s, 5s, and 10s, and identify odd/even numbers. They use concrete materials like base-10 blocks to represent two-digit numbers. For example, 47 is 4 tens and 7 ones. Learners also explore number patterns in daily contexts, such as counting steps or groups of objects, building a foundation for arithmetic operations.  

## Addition and Subtraction  
Learners investigate addition and subtraction facts within 20 using concrete objects, drawings, and mental strategies. They practise solving word problems, e.g., "If I have 8 apples and buy 5 more, how many do I have?" Students use number bonds and fact families to understand inverse relationships. They explore different methods, like counting on, making ten, or using a number line. For example, 15 - 7 can be solved by counting up from 7 to 15. Learners apply these skills in real-life scenarios, sharing toys or calculating remaining items.  

## Measurement  
Learners investigate length, mass, and capacity using non-standard and standard units (e.g., centimeters, grams, liters). They compare and order objects by measurement, e.g., "The book is longer than the pencil." Students estimate measurements before verifying them, developing a sense of accuracy. For example, they might fill containers to compare volumes or weigh objects using a balance scale. Learners explore measurement in global contexts, such as comparing the heights of landmarks or using recipes from different cultures, linking math to daily life and international diversity.  

## Shapes and Spatial Awareness  
Learners investigate 2D and 3D shapes, identifying and describing properties (e.g., sides, vertices, faces). They recognize shapes in real-world objects, like a cylinder in a can or a rectangle in a door. Students create patterns and designs using shapes, exploring symmetry. For example, they fold paper to find lines of symmetry. Learners also use positional language (e.g., "above," "next to") to describe locations. Activities include building with blocks or tangrams, fostering spatial reasoning and an appreciation for geometric patterns in nature and architecture worldwide.  

## Data Handling  
Learners investigate how to collect, organize, and represent data using tally charts, pictographs, and bar graphs. They sort objects into categories, e.g., "Which fruits do we like?" and tally responses. Students interpret simple graphs to answer questions, such as "Which has the most votes?" They explore data in authentic contexts, like tracking the weather or sharing preferences from classmates. Learners develop an understanding of how data helps us make decisions, connecting to global themes like environmental awareness or cultural diversity through class surveys.  

## Patterns and Algebra  
Learners investigate patterns in numbers, shapes, and objects, identifying and extending sequences (e.g., ABAB or 2, 4, 6, 8). They create their own patterns using materials like beads or blocks. Students explore growing patterns, e.g., 1, 3, 5, 7, by predicting the next number. They also solve simple addition or subtraction problems with missing numbers (e.g., 5 + __ = 12). Through patterns, learners recognize relationships and make predictions, fostering logical thinking and an appreciation for mathematical structures in art, music, and nature across cultures.
