# International Baccalaureate (IB) Grade 3 Mathematics  
## Number and Operations  
Grade 3 learners investigate the composition of numbers up to 1,000. They understand place value (hundreds, tens, ones) and use expanded form (e.g., 547 = 500 + 40 + 7). Students practice addition and subtraction with regrouping using standard algorithms, such as 342 - 178. They explore multiplication as repeated addition (e.g., 4 × 3 = 4 + 4 + 4) and learn multiplication facts up to 10 × 10. Division is introduced as sharing or grouping (e.g., 20 ÷ 4 = 5). Learners use manipulatives and visual models to solve real-world problems, like distributing 36 pencils equally among 9 friends.  

## Measurement  
Learners measure length, mass, and capacity using standard units (meters, grams, liters). They compare objects using comparative language (e.g., "longer than," "heavier than") and estimate measurements (e.g., "This book is about 25 cm long"). Students explore perimeter by calculating the distance around simple shapes (e.g., a 4 cm by 3 cm rectangle has a perimeter of 14 cm). Time is taught to the nearest minute, with students reading clocks and calculating durations (e.g., "From 3:15 PM to 4:45 PM is 2 hours and 30 minutes"). Real-world contexts, like planning a picnic or measuring ingredients for a recipe, connect learning to daily life.  

## Geometry  
Grade 3 learners identify, describe, and classify 2D shapes (triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons, hexagons) and 3D figures (cubes, spheres, cylinders, pyramids). They explore properties like sides, vertices, and faces, comparing similarities and differences. Symmetry is introduced through line symmetry, where learners find and draw lines of symmetry in shapes (e.g., a butterfly). Students create patterns and designs using shapes to develop spatial reasoning. Examples from diverse cultures, such as geometric patterns in Islamic art or African textiles, highlight global perspectives and international-mindedness.  

## Data Handling  
Learners collect, organize, and represent data using tally charts, bar graphs, and pictographs. They pose questions (e.g., "What is our favorite fruit?"), gather responses, and display findings using scaled axes. Students interpret data by answering questions like "Which category has the most?" or "How many more in one group than another?". They compare data sets and make simple predictions (e.g., "If we surveyed another class, which fruit might be most popular?"). Real-world examples, such as tracking class attendance or favorite sports, encourage critical thinking about information.  

## Patterns and Algebra  
Students recognize, describe, and extend number patterns (e.g., 2, 4, 6, 8, ...) and shape patterns. They identify rules governing patterns (e.g., "add 3 each time") and use them to find missing terms. Algebra is introduced through simple equations with unknowns, such as 5 + __ = 12, solved using inverse operations. Learners use variables (e.g., "n + 7 = 15") to represent relationships in word problems. Examples like calculating the number of legs on a group of animals (e.g., 4 chickens + 2 ducks = ?) link patterns to everyday scenarios.  

## Problem Solving and Reasoning  
Learners apply mathematical knowledge to solve one- and two-step problems in authentic contexts (e.g., "If 3 pencils cost $6, how much do 7 pencils cost?"). They use strategies like drawing diagrams, making lists, or working backward. Students explain their reasoning orally or in writing, justifying answers with evidence. They collaborate to solve problems, considering different approaches (e.g., multiplication vs. repeated addition). Global issues, like calculating the cost of sharing resources fairly, foster international-mindedness and ethical reasoning, encouraging learners to see math as a tool for understanding the world.
