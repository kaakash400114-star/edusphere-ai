# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 7 English
## Language: Expressing and Developing Ideas
Students analyse how the structure of a sentence creates meaning. They learn to identify and use different sentence types, such as simple, compound, and complex sentences, to vary their writing for effect. Students examine how clauses, phrases, and conjunctions connect ideas within and between sentences. They apply their understanding by constructing grammatically correct and cohesive sentences, ensuring subject-verb agreement and consistent tense. For example, they might combine two simple sentences, 'The kookaburra laughed loudly' and 'It was dawn', into a complex one to show cause and effect.

## Literature: Responding to Literature
Students interpret, evaluate, and discuss texts that explore Australian identities, histories, and cultures, including texts featuring Indigenous Australian perspectives. They learn to identify the themes and messages conveyed through character, setting, and plot. Students analyse how authors use literary devices, such as symbolism, imagery, and metaphor, to create meaning. For instance, when reading a story set in the outback, they might discuss how the harsh landscape reflects a character's internal struggles. They support their interpretations with specific evidence from the text to build persuasive arguments.

## Literacy: Creating Texts
Students create a range of imaginative, informative, and persuasive texts for different audiences and purposes. They plan, draft, edit, and publish their work, using digital tools to enhance presentation. In writing, students develop their voice and style, employing techniques like figurative language and vivid descriptions. For a persuasive text, they might argue for the conservation of the Great Barrier Reef, using facts and statistics. They learn to structure their writing logically, with an introduction, body paragraphs that contain elaborated points, and a conclusion.

## Language: Text Structure and Organisation
Students learn how texts are structured to convey meaning. They examine the organisational features of different text types, such as the chronological sequence in narratives, the headings and subheadings in reports, and the syllogistic structure of arguments. Students identify how these features guide the reader and help achieve the text's purpose. They apply this knowledge by organising their own writing using appropriate structures. For example, in an informative report on Australian animals, they might use headings like 'Habitat' and 'Diet' to present information clearly.

## Literature: Examining Literature
Students compare and evaluate how stories are told in different literary forms, such as novels, plays, and poems. They analyse the relationship between the text and its social and historical context. Students explore how characterisation is developed through dialogue, action, and description. They consider how texts reflect the values and beliefs of the time in which they were created, for example, how a story about early settlement in Australia portrays different perspectives on colonisation. This develops their critical thinking and analytical skills.

## Literacy: Interacting with Others
Students develop their speaking and listening skills to communicate effectively in a range of formal and informal situations. They learn to listen actively to others, asking clarifying questions to build understanding. Students present ideas and information orally, structuring their talk logically and using appropriate language and non-verbal communication for their audience. In a formal debate about a school issue, for instance, they would state their position clearly, provide evidence to support their claims, and respond respectfully to opposing viewpoints. They also learn to interpret the meaning and intent of others' spoken language.
