# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 4 English


## Language: Text Structure
Students learn that narrative texts follow a specific structure, consisting of an orientation that introduces characters and setting, a complication that presents the main problem or conflict, and a resolution that resolves the conflict. For example, in a story about a lost dog, the orientation describes the family and pet, the complication is the dog's disappearance, and the resolution is its safe return. Information reports are structured with a title, a general classification that defines the subject, and a description that elaborates on its key features, such as using a heading "Habitat" for a section explaining an animal's environment. To sequence events, students identify the beginning, middle, and end of a story, for example, listing 'planting a seed', 'watering it daily', and 'watching it grow' to correctly order the process of plant cultivation.

## Language: Punctuation and Grammar
Students learn to apply specific punctuation rules, such as using commas to separate items in a list (e.g., fruits, vegetables, grains) and to join two independent clauses with a conjunction (e.g., The sun was hot, so we went swimming). They master the correct use of apostrophes for singular possession (the cat's toy) and plural possession (the cats' toys). Students understand the grammatical function of nouns (person, place, thing), verbs (action), adjectives (describing), and adverbs (how/when/where an action happens). To construct more detailed sentences, they connect ideas using subordinating conjunctions like 'because' and 'although' (e.g., Although it was raining, we played outside). This knowledge enables students to write clear, well-structured simple and compound sentences.

## Literature: Responding to Texts
Students analyse how authors use descriptive language and narrative techniques to portray characters, settings, and events in literary texts. They learn to compare the experiences and perspectives of characters with their own, developing a deeper understanding of diverse viewpoints. A key skill is identifying and explaining characters’ emotions and motivations, and connecting these to the development of the plot and conflict. Students infer the central message or theme by analysing key events and character actions throughout the text. They formulate a personal opinion on the text, using specific evidence such as dialogue, character decisions, and plot events to support their reasoning.

## Literature: Creating Texts
Students apply narrative structure by planning stories that include developed characters, a clear setting, a central problem, and a logical resolution, using techniques such as descriptive language and dialogue. They compose poems that employ figurative language to create vivid imagery, drawing on concrete examples from the Australian outback or a coral reef as settings. Students draft short scripts for presentations or plays, structuring dialogue and stage directions to convey character and plot, before performing with purposeful vocal expression and movement. To enhance the communication of ideas, they integrate illustrations and multimodal elements into their written work to support meaning and clarify narrative events. This process involves drafting, reviewing, and refining texts for clarity, impact, and grammatical accuracy according to curriculum standards.

## Literacy: Reading Comprehension
Students identify the literal meaning of texts by locating key facts through explicit skimming for main ideas and scanning for specific details. To infer deeper meaning, students analyse a character's actions and dialogue to determine their unspoken feelings, such as inferring a character is anxious from fidgeting and avoiding eye contact. They determine the meaning of unknown words using context clues, such as identifying 'benevolent' as kind from an example sentence about a generous act. Students synthesise information by summarising the main idea in their own words, capturing the central message of an information report about recycling. They retell the sequence of events in a narrative story, ensuring the plot's key moments are preserved in a logical order.

## Literacy: Writing and Spelling
Students plan and write texts for different purposes, such as to inform, persuade or entertain. They use Australian English spelling, correctly spelling words like 'colour', 'realise' and 'centre'. Students learn common letter patterns and word families to spell new words. They use a dictionary or digital tool to check their spelling. Students edit their work to improve clarity, correct errors and add more descriptive vocabulary to make their writing more engaging.
