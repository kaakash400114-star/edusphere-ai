# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 4 English

## Language: Text Structure
Students learn that narratives have a structure including an orientation, complication and resolution. They identify the main problem in a story and how it is solved. In information reports, students locate the title, general classification and description sections. They understand that each section provides a specific type of information, using headings to guide their reading. Students can distinguish between a story's beginning, middle and end, sequencing events correctly.

## Language: Punctuation and Grammar
Students use a range of punctuation correctly, including commas in lists and to separate clauses, as well as apostrophes for possession (e.g., the koala's habitat). They learn to join sentences using conjunctions like 'but', 'so', 'because' and 'or'. Students understand the functions of nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs, and can identify them in sentences. They use simple and compound sentences to express ideas clearly in their writing.

## Literature: Responding to Texts
Students discuss how characters, settings and events in stories are portrayed. They compare their own experiences with those of characters in texts. They identify the emotions and motivations of characters and how these influence the plot. Students can explain the main messages or themes in texts they have read or viewed. They learn to express a personal opinion about a text, supported by details from the story.

## Literature: Creating Texts
Students create their own narratives by planning a story with characters, a setting, a problem and a resolution. They write poems using descriptive language to create images, including examples from the Australian outback or a coral reef. Students plan and perform short scripts for plays or presentations, using their voice and expression to convey meaning. They use illustrations to support their written work and make their ideas clearer.

## Literacy: Reading Comprehension
Students read a variety of texts, including stories, information reports and poems. They locate specific information by using skimming and scanning techniques. Students infer a character's feelings from their actions and dialogue. They use context clues to work out the meaning of unknown words. Students can summarise the main idea of a text in a few sentences, retelling the key events or facts in their own words.

## Literacy: Writing and Spelling
Students plan and write texts for different purposes, such as to inform, persuade or entertain. They use Australian English spelling, correctly spelling words like 'colour', 'realise' and 'centre'. Students learn common letter patterns and word families to spell new words. They use a dictionary or digital tool to check their spelling. Students edit their work to improve clarity, correct errors and add more descriptive vocabulary to make their writing more engaging.
