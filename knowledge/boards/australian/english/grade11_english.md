# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 11 English

## Analysing and Argumenting Persuasive Texts
Students learn to identify and evaluate the persuasive techniques used in texts, including ethos, pathos, and logos. They analyse how authors position an audience, examining language choices such as loaded vocabulary, inclusive pronouns, and rhetorical questions. Students deconstruct the structure of arguments, identifying the main claim, supporting evidence, and counter-arguments. They compare how different media platforms, such as a political speech and a newspaper editorial, achieve their persuasive goals. Students critique the use of fallacies and the appeal to shared cultural values, such as mateship or fairness, to understand the construction of a convincing case.

## Exploring Australian Literature and Identity
This unit focuses on how Australian literature explores national identity and experiences. Students analyse texts, such as Tim Winton's short stories, that deal with themes of the outback, urban life, and Indigenous perspectives. They learn to identify distinctively Australian settings, colloquialisms, and cultural references, like footy or a barbie. Students examine how authors use character and narrative to challenge or reinforce stereotypes about the Australian way of life. They compare representations of Australia across different time periods, from colonial literature to contemporary works, to trace the evolution of national identity.

## Understanding and Creating Persuasive Multimodal Texts
Students learn to create and critique persuasive texts that combine multiple modes, such as image, text, and sound. They study how a charity advertisement uses a powerful image with a short, emotive caption to evoke sympathy. Students learn to design their own multimodal presentations, for instance, a website advocating for coral reef conservation that includes infographics, statistics on the Great Barrier Reef, and a call to action. They must consider the intended audience and purpose, selecting appropriate fonts, colours, and layout to maximise persuasive impact across different platforms.

## Developing Critical Language and Grammar Skills
Students refine their understanding of grammar and syntax to enhance clarity and persuasion. They study the function of complex sentences, subordinate clauses, and punctuation to create sophisticated arguments. A key focus is on metalanguage, allowing them to discuss language choices precisely. Students analyse the impact of lexical fields—such as words related to 'protection' in a debate on environmental policy—to build a consistent tone. They also examine nominalisation, where verbs are turned into nouns (e.g., 'the government's decision to fund' instead of 'the government decided to fund'), to create a more objective and formal academic style.

## Close Study of a Text
Students undertake an intensive study of a substantial literary work, such as a novel, film, or play. They analyse the text's narrative structure, character development, and thematic concerns in detail. Students learn to use specific evidence from the text to support their interpretations, examining quotes, symbols, and motifs. They explore the context in which the text was produced and how this influences its meaning. This culminates in the ability to construct a sustained, detailed analytical response that offers a personal and well-argued interpretation of the text's significance.

## Crafting and Refining Persuasive Writing
Students learn to structure and craft different forms of persuasive writing, including expositions, discursive essays, and speeches. They master the use of topic sentences, signposting language (furthermore, however, consequently), and paragraphs to ensure a logical and coherent argument. A key skill is tailoring writing for different audiences and purposes, such as a formal submission to a government inquiry versus a passionate letter to a local newspaper. Students learn to effectively integrate evidence, such as quotes from an expert or data from a report, to substantiate their claims and build a convincing and authoritative case.
