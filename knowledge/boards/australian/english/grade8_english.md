# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 8 English

## Language: Grammar and Style
Students analyse how grammatical choices shape meaning, including the use of passive voice for objectivity and active voice for directness. They learn to use more complex sentence structures, such as compound-complex sentences, to link ideas. They expand their knowledge of clauses, identifying main and subordinate clauses. Students apply conventions of Australian English spelling and punctuation, correctly using hyphens, colons, and semicolons. They learn to use metalanguage to discuss language choices, distinguishing between denotation and connotation in words like 'footy' versus 'Australian Rules football'.

## Literature: Analysing and Interpreting
Students interpret texts by analysing how narrative devices like foreshadowing and character development create meaning and influence responses. They examine texts from diverse Australian cultures, including those by Indigenous authors. Students learn to make connections between texts and their social contexts, discussing how a story about the 'outback' reflects Australian identity. They use evidence from the text to support interpretations of themes and values. They compare and contrast representations of similar ideas across different texts, such as comparing a novel to its film adaptation.

## Literacy: Composing and Creating
Students create a range of imaginative and informative texts for different audiences. For a persuasive piece, they structure a logical argument using techniques like rhetorical questions and emotive language. For informative writing, they synthesise information from multiple sources, correctly paraphrasing and quoting. They plan and draft texts using digital tools, revising for coherence and impact. Students learn to select vocabulary for precision and effect, using Australian-specific terms like 'arvo' or 'barbie' where appropriate to create authentic tone. They edit their work for spelling, punctuation, and grammatical accuracy in Australian English.

## Literacy: Critical Reading and Viewing
Students develop critical reading skills by questioning a text's purpose, perspective, and potential bias. They evaluate the credibility of sources, distinguishing between fact and opinion. When viewing media, such as advertisements for AUD-priced products, they analyse how visual and language choices are used to persuade. Students identify the values and attitudes embedded in texts, considering how they reflect the time and culture in which they were made. They compare different perspectives on a single issue, using graphic organisers to map out arguments. Students learn to interpret figurative language, identifying its effect on the reader.

## Literature: Texts and Contexts
Students explore the relationship between texts, contexts, and the reader. They analyse how context influences the creation and interpretation of texts, for example, reading a historical novel set during the Great Barrier Reef's early tourism boom. They learn to identify and describe the social, cultural, and historical contexts that shape a text. They investigate how themes and ideas can be represented differently across time and cultures. Students make connections between their own experiences and those represented in texts. They study how texts like poems about the Australian landscape reflect a national identity.

## Language: Vocabulary and Word Knowledge
Students extend their vocabulary knowledge through explicit instruction, focusing on word origins, roots, and affixes. They learn to determine the meaning of unfamiliar words using contextual clues and etymological analysis. Students develop the ability to understand and use precise subject-specific vocabulary in different learning areas. They explore connotative meaning, identifying the feelings and ideas associated with words like 'freedom' or 'home'. Students use word maps and thesauri to find synonyms and antonyms, enriching their own writing. They analyse how word choice can alter the tone and formality of a text.
