# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 12 English

## Analysing Complex Texts
Students deconstruct sophisticated texts, including classic and contemporary literature, drama, and non-fiction. They analyse how composers use language, perspective, and form to convey complex ideas about the human condition. For instance, a student might examine how Tim Winton’s *Cloudstreet* utilises narrative structure and dual perspectives to explore themes of community and spirituality. This involves identifying textual evidence, evaluating stylistic choices, and constructing reasoned interpretations that reflect a deep understanding of context and purpose.

## Crafting Sophisticated Arguments
Students develop and evaluate logical arguments using evidence, reasoning, and rhetorical devices. They formulate clear theses and structure them coherently in extended written pieces. An example includes composing a persuasive essay arguing for the preservation of the Great Barrier Reef, using scientific reports, economic data, and Indigenous perspectives as supporting evidence. The focus is on precision in language, logical sequencing, and anticipating counter-arguments to build a robust and convincing case.

## Interpreting and Creating Persuasive Texts
Students examine how language shapes meaning and influence in a range of persuasive texts. They analyse the construction of arguments in opinion pieces, speeches, and advertisements, identifying persuasive techniques such as loaded language, appeals to authority, and logical fallacies. Students then create their own persuasive texts, such as a campaign speech for a school election or a letter to a local MP about a community issue, purposefully selecting techniques to achieve a specific effect on their target audience.

## Understanding Language and Identity
Students investigate the dynamic relationship between language and identity, focusing on Australian English. They explore how dialect, slang (e.g., 'footy', 'arvo'), and accent signal belonging, social groups, and regional identity. They analyse texts like those by Indigenous or multicultural Australian authors to examine how language negotiates cultural identity. Students also examine the Australian Broadcasting Corporation's (ABC) use of Australian English and discuss the influence of global media on local linguistic practices.

## Evaluating Literature and Critical Perspectives
Students engage with critical frameworks to analyse literary texts. They apply theories such as post-colonialism, feminism, or psychoanalysis to deepen their interpretation of works. For example, a student might apply a feminist lens to a classic text like *Pride and Prejudice* or an Australian novel like *Picnic at Hanging Rock*. This involves understanding the theoretical perspective’s core concepts and using this specialised vocabulary to construct a nuanced analysis of character, theme, and context.

## Navigating Media and Digital Texts
Students critically evaluate the impact of digital and media platforms on communication and information. They examine how social media algorithms, digital advertising, and online news outlets shape public discourse. A practical activity involves deconstructing the way a major Australian news network (like the ABC or News Corp) reports on a current event, comparing its online and print coverage. Students develop advanced digital literacy skills to identify bias, distinguish fact from opinion, and understand the ethical implications of digital environments.
