# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 1 English
## Reading
Students read aloud simple decodable texts with accuracy and phrasing. They learn to use letter-sound relationships, including consonant clusters and short vowel sounds, to decode unfamiliar words. Students read common high-frequency words such as 'the', 'was', and 'they' on sight. They understand that texts have a purpose and can be about real or imagined situations. Students discuss the main events in a story and identify key characters using details from the text, like the animals featured in an Australian bush tale.

## Writing
Students write using unjoined, upper- and lower-case letters. They form letters correctly, ensuring consistency in size and shape. Students learn to compose simple sentences using a subject and a predicate. They use full stops and capital letters correctly to mark the end of a sentence and for proper nouns like 'Sydney' or 'Aussie'. Spelling is based on sound and letter patterns; students attempt to write high-frequency words correctly, including 'colour' and 'realise', and use familiar words to label drawings and write short messages.

## Speaking and Listening
Students listen to and respond to others' ideas in class discussions and activities. They learn to speak clearly in complete sentences to share information or ask questions. Students use appropriate volume and tone of voice. They can retell a familiar story or recount a personal experience with a beginning, middle, and end, using descriptive language like 'hot' for the outback or 'big' for a cricket match. They engage in role-play using a simple script to explore characters and situations.

## Language
Students learn to identify nouns (people, places, things) and verbs (actions, states of being) in simple sentences. They understand that adjectives are describing words, such as 'red', 'fast', or 'big', and use them to make their writing and speech more interesting. Students are introduced to the different types of sentences: statements, questions, and commands. They understand that spelling conventions in Australian English can differ, for example, 'colour' instead of 'color'.

## Literature
Students listen to, read, and view a range of literature, including stories, poetry, and plays. They identify the characters and setting in a familiar story, like one set at the Great Barrier Reef. Students make connections between the characters and their own feelings or experiences. They recognise that texts can have rhyme and rhythm and enjoy listening to poems read aloud. They can express a simple preference about a text and give a reason, such as 'I liked the funny dog'.

## Literacy and Knowledge
Students understand that different types of texts have different features and purposes. They learn to identify the front and back cover, title, and author of a book. Students understand the concept of a sentence and that spaces are used between words. They begin to create simple texts for a purpose, like writing a list for a shopping trip or a card for a friend. They use digital tools, with guidance, to create texts and can use the AUD symbol in simple classroom-based money activities.
