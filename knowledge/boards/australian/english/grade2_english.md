# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 2 English

## Expressing and Developing Ideas
Students understand that texts have different structures and features depending on their purpose. They learn to use adjectives to describe nouns, such as 'a red kangaroo' or 'a crunchy apple', making their writing more detailed. Students explore the meaning of words and how words can have more than one meaning, like 'bat' (the animal) and 'bat' (used in cricket). They use these skills to create simple texts, such as a short description of a favourite Australian animal or a plan for a story about the outback.

## Language for Interaction
Students learn that language changes depending on the context and the people they are talking to. They practise using polite language when asking for something, such as 'Could I please borrow your pencil?'. They understand that speaking and listening are used for different purposes, like sharing news with a friend or giving instructions for a simple game. Students learn to listen carefully to others to understand what is being said and to take turns when speaking in small group discussions.

## Texts and Contexts
Students explore a range of texts, including stories, poetry, and informative texts about Australia. They identify characters, settings, and events in stories they read. Students learn that texts can be about real events or made-up stories. They engage with texts that reflect Australian life, such as stories about going to the beach or a game of footy. Students discuss their own experiences and relate them to the themes and ideas they encounter in different texts.

## Interpreting, Analysing and Evaluating
Students learn to identify the main idea or message in a text. They make connections between the text and their own life, such as linking a story about camping to a family holiday. Students ask questions to help them understand, like 'Why did the character do that?'. They begin to think about how the author makes the story interesting. Students can identify the purpose of a text, such as to inform them about the Great Barrier Reef or to entertain them with a funny poem.

## Creating Texts
Students create simple texts using their knowledge of sounds, letters, and words. They write short sentences to express their ideas, ensuring they start with a capital letter and end with a full stop. Students learn to spell common words correctly, using Australian English spelling like 'colour' and 'realise'. They create simple recounts of their day or imaginative stories about a magical bush animal. Students also learn to plan their writing by thinking about what they want to say before they start.

## Spelling
Students develop strategies for learning new words. They learn that some letter patterns make certain sounds, such as the 'sh' sound in 'ship' and the 'ai' pattern in 'rain'. They learn common prefixes like 'un-' to change a word's meaning, as in 'unhappy'. Students explore how plurals are formed, adding 's' or 'es' to nouns like 'kangaroo' to 'kangaroos'. They are introduced to homophones, words that sound the same but have different meanings and spellings, like 'to', 'too', and 'two'.
