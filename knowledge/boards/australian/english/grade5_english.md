# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 5 English
## Expressing Ideas Through Texts
Students create narratives, persuasive texts, and information reports using clear structures. They learn to write a story with a setting, characters, and a problem, using descriptive words. For a persuasive text, they state an opinion (e.g., "School uniforms should be optional") and give reasons supported by facts. In reports, they use headings and subheadings to organise information about Australian topics like the Great Barrier Reef. Students use paragraphs to group related ideas and correct punctuation, including commas in lists. Australian spelling, like 'colour' and 'realise', is consistently used.

## Analysing and Interpreting Literature
Students read a variety of fiction and non-fiction texts to understand meaning, characters, and settings. They learn to infer a character's feelings from their actions and dialogue, for example, why a character in a story might be described as 'grumpy'. Students compare different versions of the same story, identifying similarities and differences in the plot. They use text features like headings and illustrations to locate information and answer questions. Students discuss their personal response to a text, explaining what they liked or disliked using examples from the story.

## Building Language and Vocabulary
Students expand their vocabulary by learning new words related to other subjects, like 'photosynthesis' in science or 'democracy' in humanities. They learn to understand and use words with multiple meanings, such as 'bat' (the animal) or 'bat' (the equipment). Students explore word origins, including how some words come from Aboriginal languages like 'kangaroo' or 'boomerang'. They use a dictionary or glossary to check the meaning of unfamiliar words. Students learn to use synonyms (words with similar meanings) to make their writing more interesting.

## Communicating and Interacting
Students practise speaking and listening skills in formal and informal situations. They learn to express their opinions clearly in class discussions and listen actively to others' points of view. Students deliver short oral presentations on topics like 'My Favourite Australian Animal', using visual aids like pictures or posters to support their message. They learn to use formal language when speaking to an adult and informal language when talking to peers. Students participate in role-plays to practise skills like negotiating a plan for a school project or asking for directions to the local library.

## Grammar and Punctuation
Students learn to use more complex grammatical structures in their writing. They are explicitly taught the difference between common and proper nouns, for example, 'river' versus 'Murray River'. Students learn to correctly use pronouns (he, she, it, they) to avoid repetition. They are introduced to verb tenses, including past, present, and future, ensuring correct usage (e.g., 'I will go' instead of 'I will goes'). Students learn to use apostrophes for possession (the dog's bone) and for contractions (it's, didn't). They learn to use commas correctly after introductory phrases (e.g., 'Yes, I can help you').

## Language Conventions and Spelling
Students focus on consolidating their spelling knowledge with an emphasis on Australian English conventions. They learn spelling rules for adding suffixes, like doubling the final consonant in words such as 'stop' becoming 'stopped'. Students explore word families to find patterns, for example, light, bright, and night. They learn to correctly spell common homophones like 'their', 'there', and 'they're'. Students are introduced to words with silent letters, such as 'knight' and 'write'. They use dictionaries to check and confirm spelling of both everyday words and topic-specific vocabulary.
