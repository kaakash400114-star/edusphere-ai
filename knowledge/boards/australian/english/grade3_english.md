# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 3 English
## Language: Expressing and Developing Ideas
Students learn how different words, such as adjectives and adverbs, add detail and interest to their writing. They explore how language can be used to express feelings and create images, using descriptions like 'the sun was a hot, red orb' or 'the kangaroo hopped quickly'. They also learn to use figurative language, such as similes ('as brave as a soldier'), to make their writing more colourful and engaging for the reader. They understand that choosing the right words can change how a story sounds and feels.

## Language: Text Structure and Organisation
Students learn that texts have different structures to help them make sense of information. For stories, they identify the beginning (orientation), middle (complication) and end (resolution). For information reports, they learn to find the general statement at the start and details in the following paragraphs. They understand that headings and sub-headings in a report about the Great Barrier Reef organise information into topics like 'animals' and 'plants', making it easier to find facts. They can sequence events in a story using words like 'first', 'then' and 'finally'.

## Literature: Responding to Literature
Students learn to discuss and share their ideas about stories, poems, and plays they read or watch. They identify the characters, settings, and main events in a text, such as the characters in a Dreaming story. They express how a story makes them feel and whether they enjoyed it or not, using examples from the text to support their opinion. They can make connections between the text and their own lives, like comparing a character's adventure to their own trip to the outback.

## Literacy: Texts in Context
Students learn that texts can be created for different purposes and for different people. They can identify if a text is meant to entertain (like a story about a footy game), inform (like a fact file on koalas), or persuade (like a poster to save the Great Barrier Reef). They understand that the language and images used change depending on the purpose and audience. For example, a recipe will use clear, simple instructions, while a story will use descriptive language to create excitement.

## Literacy: Interpreting, Analysing, Evaluating
Students learn to ask questions about texts to find information and understand ideas better. They can find the main idea in a paragraph, such as the main reason people should visit the Great Barrier Reef. They can identify the difference between facts (like 'the reef is 2,300 km long') and opinions (like 'the reef is the most beautiful place'). They can describe how characters are feeling by looking at what they say and do in a story. They can also talk about the author's choices, like why certain pictures were included in a book.

## Literacy: Creating Texts
Students learn to plan and create their own writing for different purposes. They can draft a simple narrative story with a clear beginning, middle, and end, using characters and a problem. They can write an information report on a topic like Australian animals, using headings and factual sentences. They learn to write simple and compound sentences correctly, such as 'The kookaburra laughed. It laughed loudly.' They can edit their own writing to correct spelling mistakes, like making sure they use Australian English spelling (colour, realise) and full stops.
