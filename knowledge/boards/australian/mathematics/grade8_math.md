# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 8 Mathematics

## Understanding Integers and the Number Line
Students learn to operate with integers, including positive and negative whole numbers. They add and subtract integers using rules such as 'two negatives make a positive' and use a number line to visualise the process, for instance, calculating a temperature drop from 5°C to -3°C. They also multiply and divide integers, applying the rule that the product of a positive and a negative is negative, such as working out a loss of $15 over 4 weeks. This builds fluency with the four operations across all rational numbers.

## Solving Linear Equations with One Variable
Students develop skills in solving linear equations with one variable, such as 2x + 7 = 13. They learn to apply inverse operations to isolate the variable, like subtracting 7 from both sides and then dividing by 2. They also construct and solve equations from word problems, for example, finding the cost of a concert ticket if two tickets plus a $15 booking fee total $95. This provides a foundation for algebraic reasoning and problem-solving.

## Exploring Geometric Properties and Congruence
Students explore the properties of triangles and quadrilaterals, focusing on congruence as the geometric property of having exactly the same size and shape. They formally prove two triangles are congruent using established criteria such as Side-Side-Side (SSS), Side-Angle-Side (SAS), and Angle-Side-Angle (ASA). Students apply these principles to solve practical problems, for instance, by using congruent triangles formed by parallel lines to calculate the width of a riverbed from measured on-land distances. This includes the critical application that Angle-Angle-Angle (AAA) is a condition for similarity, not congruence. They learn to analyse geometric diagrams, identify corresponding parts, and construct formal logical arguments to demonstrate that two figures are congruent.

## Analysing Data and Representing it with Graphs
Students in Year 8 expand their statistical capabilities by analysing both univariate and bivariate datasets using specific graphical methods. For single-variable numerical data, such as student heights, they apply the rule-based methods of constructing and interpreting stem-and-leaf plots and histograms to display distributions. When investigating the relationship between two variables, such as weekly rainfall and crop yield, they construct scatter plots to identify and comment on the strength of any correlation observed. The key concepts involve analysing the spread and shape of data for one variable, and for two variables, focusing on the trend and strength of the linear or non-linear association. This practical application of methods provides the foundational skills for drawing meaningful conclusions from collected data.

## Calculating with Percentages and Ratios
Students become proficient in calculating percentages and solving related problems. They find a percentage of a quantity, such as calculating 15% GST on a new computer. They also use percentages to compare quantities, like determining what percentage of a class prefers AFL footy. Furthermore, they simplify ratios, such as the ratio of kangaroos to emus in a sanctuary, and use them to solve problems involving scale, such as converting a map distance to an actual distance in kilometres.

## Understanding and Applying the Pythagorean Theorem
Students are formally introduced to the Pythagorean Theorem to solve problems involving right-angled triangles. They learn the formula a² + b² = c², where 'c' is the hypotenuse. They apply the theorem to calculate an unknown side length, for example, finding the distance between two points on a grid. They also use it in real-world contexts, such as determining the length of a diagonal ladder to reach a 3m-high window if it must be placed 2.1m from the wall.
