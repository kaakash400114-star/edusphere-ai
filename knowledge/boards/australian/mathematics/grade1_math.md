# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 1 Mathematics

## Number and Algebra: Representing Whole Numbers
Children learn to represent numbers up to 100 using a range of materials like Bundled Sticks, MAB blocks, and number charts. They read, write, and order numbers correctly, using terms like 'first', 'second', and 'last'. Students partition numbers into tens and ones, understanding that 34 is 3 tens and 4 ones. They recognise, model, and order numbers to at least 100, locating them on a number line. For example, they can show the number 75 using seven ten-blocks and five one-blocks and find the number that comes just after 99.

## Number and Algebra: Addition and Subtraction
Students develop strategies for adding and subtracting numbers up to 20 using concrete materials like counters, number lines, and drawings. They learn to use the language of addition (plus, add, total) and subtraction (minus, take away, difference). Real-world problems are explored, such as having 15 footy cards and giving 3 away, leaving 12. They find unknown numbers in problems like 7 + ? = 15. A key strategy is using 'counting on' for addition, such as starting at 8 and counting on 3 fingers to find 8 + 3.

## Number and Algebra: Money and Financial Mathematics
Children learn to identify and describe Australian coins and notes, including the 5-cent, 10-cent, 20-cent, 50-cent, $1, and $2 coins and the $5, $10, $20, and $50 notes. They understand that coins and notes have different values and can be used to make amounts. Students count collections of coins and notes to make up a total value, for example, working out that two 20-cent coins and one 10-cent coin make 50 cents. This builds a foundation for understanding money in everyday transactions.

## Measurement and Geometry: Length and Area
Students measure and compare lengths using informal units like paper clips, pop sticks, or their hand spans. They learn to place units end-to-end without gaps to get an accurate measurement. For example, a student might find that their desk is 15 pop sticks long. Children also compare objects directly, using terms like 'longer', 'shorter', and 'taller'. They explore area by covering surfaces with tiles or blocks and compare the area of different shapes, understanding that a larger area requires more tiles to cover it completely.

## Measurement and Geometry: Time and Mass
Children learn to tell the time to the half-hour on both analogue and digital clocks. They name and order the days of the week and the months of the year. In mass, students use informal units like balance scales to compare and order the mass of objects. They use terms such as 'heavier', 'lighter', and 'as heavy as'. A common activity is comparing the mass of a small rock and a large feather to understand that size does not always determine mass.

## Statistics and Probability: Data and Chance
Students collect data through simple surveys, like asking their classmates about their favourite colour or animal. They represent this data using drawings, tally marks, and concrete materials. Information is then organised into picture graphs and simple bar graphs, with one symbol representing one data point. Students also learn to describe and interpret these graphs. They also discuss everyday chance events, using terms like 'will happen', 'might happen', and 'won't happen', such as 'It might rain tomorrow'.
