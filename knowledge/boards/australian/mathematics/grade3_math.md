# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 3 Mathematics  
## Number and Algebra: Place Value  
Students recognise, represent and order numbers up to 10,000. They partition numbers using materials and diagrams, understanding that 1000 is 10 hundreds. For example, they show 3784 as 3 thousands, 7 hundreds, 8 tens, and 4 ones. Students round numbers to the nearest 10, 100, or 1000, such as rounding 2431 to the nearest 100 is 2400. They use expanded notation (2000 + 400 + 30 + 1) and solve problems involving money in Australian dollars (AUD), like calculating the total cost of items priced at $12.50 and $5.75.  

## Number and Algebra: Addition and Subtraction  
Students solve problems involving addition and subtraction using efficient mental and written strategies. They recall addition facts to 20 and related subtraction facts, and use algorithms for four-digit numbers. For example, they calculate 2356 + 1874 by aligning place values. Students estimate answers to check reasonableness, like estimating 567 – 299 is near 300. They solve word problems involving money, such as finding the change from a $50 note after spending $32.40. Real-life examples include calculating the total cost of footy tickets or saving for a toy.  

## Number and Algebra: Multiplication and Division  
Students develop fluency in recall of multiplication facts up to 10 × 10 and related division facts. They represent multiplication as repeated addition and groups, such as 3 × 4 is 3 groups of 4. Students model division as sharing or grouping, like sharing 12 lollies equally among 4 friends. They solve problems involving multiplication and division, including using equal groups and arrays, for example, arranging 24 chairs in rows of 6. Real-world contexts include calculating the number of weeks in 21 days or sharing 20 sausage sizers among family members.  

## Measurement and Geometry: Length, Area, and Volume  
Students measure, order, and compare objects using familiar metric units. They measure length in millimetres, centimetres, and metres, such as a ruler is 30 cm long. Students estimate and calculate the area of shapes using grids, like covering a rectangle with 24 squares. They compare and order the volume of containers using informal units, such as how many cups fill a bucket. Examples include measuring the length of a cricket bat or comparing the size of different swimming pools at a local beach.  

## Measurement and Geometry: Time and Location  
Students tell time to the minute using digital and analogue clocks, reading times like 3:45. They understand duration, such as calculating how long a movie lasts from 7:00 pm to 9:30 pm. Students interpret simple maps and plans, using grid references or directional language (left, right, north). They explore calendars to identify dates and days, like planning a school excursion. Real-life examples include timetabling a footy match or locating a town on a map of the outback.  

## Statistics and Probability: Data and Chance  
Students collect, represent, and interpret data. They use tally marks, lists, and column graphs to display information, such as favourite animals. Students interpret graphs to answer questions, like "How many students prefer apples?" They describe chance events using everyday language, such as "impossible," "unlikely," and "certain." For example, they might predict whether it will rain tomorrow based on the weather. Real-world contexts include surveying class preferences or observing the chance of seeing a kangaroo in the bush.
