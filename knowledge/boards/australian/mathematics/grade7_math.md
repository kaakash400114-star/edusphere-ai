# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 7 Mathematics
## Number and Algebra: Integers and the Four Operations
Students learn to operate with positive and negative whole numbers (integers). They develop proficiency in addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division of integers, applying the sign rules for these operations. For example, subtracting a negative number is equivalent to adding its positive counterpart (e.g., 7 - (-3) = 10). Students solve problems involving integers in real-world contexts, such as temperature changes or financial accounts (e.g., a bank withdrawal of $50 is represented as -$50). They also explore the concept of squared and cubed integers.

## Number and Algebra: Fractions, Decimals, and Percentages
Students extend their understanding to all rational numbers, including fractions and decimals. They learn to compare and order fractions and decimals by finding a common denominator or converting to a decimal form. Students practise the four operations with fractions and decimals, using efficient strategies for calculations. A key skill is converting between fractions, decimals, and percentages to solve problems, such as calculating a 15% discount on an item costing $120. They also express one quantity as a percentage of another, using examples like determining what percentage of a class plays footy.

## Measurement and Geometry: Using Formulas for Area and Volume
Students use formulas to calculate the area and perimeter of two-dimensional shapes, including squares, rectangles, triangles, and parallelograms. They learn the formula for the area of a circle (A = πr²), using 3.14 for π and measuring radii in centimetres or metres. For three-dimensional objects, students calculate volumes of prisms (both right and oblique) and cylinders. A common Australian example involves calculating the volume of a water tank to determine how much rainwater it can collect from the roof, applying the formula V = area of base × height.

## Measurement and Geometry: Angle Relationships and Geometric Reasoning
Students identify and calculate angles formed by the intersection of lines and by parallel lines crossed by a transversal. They use properties such as corresponding angles, alternate angles, and co-interior angles being equal to find unknown angles. Students classify triangles and quadrilaterals based on their side and angle properties. They apply this knowledge in geometric proofs and to solve practical problems, such as determining the angle of a sunshade required to provide shade at a specific time at a location near the Great Barrier Reef.

## Statistics and Probability: Data Representation and Interpretation
Students learn to construct and interpret a range of data displays, including dot plots, column graphs, and pie charts. They calculate and interpret measures of central tendency (mean, median, and mode) and spread (range). Students analyse data sets to identify trends and make comparisons. A key activity involves collecting data, such as surveying peers about their preferred AFL team, and then presenting the findings in a meaningful way. They also distinguish between categorical and numerical data to ensure they select the appropriate display for their information.

## Statistics and Probability: Chance and Probability
Students describe the likelihood of events using language such as 'impossible', 'unlikely', 'even chance', 'likely', and 'certain'. They assign probabilities to simple events as values between 0 and 1 (e.g., the probability of rolling a 3 on a standard die is 1/6). Students conduct experiments and simulations to determine relative frequencies and compare them to theoretical probabilities. For example, they might flip a coin 50 times to see if the relative frequency of heads is close to the theoretical probability of 0.5.
