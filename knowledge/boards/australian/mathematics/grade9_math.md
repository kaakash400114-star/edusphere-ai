# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 9 Mathematics

## Algebraic Techniques
Students expand binomial products and factorise monic quadratic expressions using techniques such as grouping and finding a common factor. They apply the distributive law to simplify algebraic fractions and solve linear equations involving variables on both sides, including equations with brackets. Students use substitution to evaluate expressions, with examples like calculating the area of a rectangle with a length of (x + 3) metres and a width of (x - 2) metres, or finding the value of 3a² - 4b when a = 5 and b = -2.

## Linear and Non-linear Relationships
Students graph linear equations using the gradient-intercept form (y = mx + c) and find the equation of a line given its gradient and a point on the line. They solve simultaneous linear equations using both the substitution and elimination methods, using real-world contexts such as comparing the cost of two different mobile phone plans. Students sketch parabolas, identifying features like the turning point and axis of symmetry for quadratics in the form y = ax² + bx + c.

## Indices and Surds
Students apply the index laws to simplify expressions involving positive integral indices, including the product, quotient, and power of a power laws. They convert between fractional indices and surds, and perform operations with surds like addition, subtraction, and simplification. For example, they would simplify 5√3 + 2√2 - √3 or rationalise the denominator of an expression like (2 + √5)/2. Students also use scientific notation to represent very large or small numbers, such as the population of Australia (approximately 26 million) or the wavelength of visible light.

## Financial Mathematics
Students calculate simple and compound interest using formulas, with compound interest calculated annually or for other compounding periods. They solve problems involving depreciation, such as calculating the value of a new car after three years. Students compare different investment options and calculate the effective interest rate. They apply concepts to real-life scenarios, such as calculating the total cost of a loan of AUD 25,000 at an annual interest rate of 5% over 5 years, or determining the future value of an AUD 500 deposit into a savings account earning 3.5% p.a. interest.

## Measurement and Pythagoras' Theorem
Students calculate the areas of composite shapes, including combinations of triangles, quadrilaterals, and circles. They apply Pythagoras' theorem to solve problems involving right-angled triangles in two and three dimensions, such as finding the diagonal of a rectangular prism or the height of a flagpole. Students convert between units of measurement, including metric units for area and volume, and solve problems involving the surface area and volume of prisms, cylinders, and spheres, using real-world objects like a can of soft drink.

## Data Representation and Probability
Students construct and interpret a variety of data displays, including back-to-back stem-and-leaf plots, box plots, and histograms. They calculate and compare measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) and spread (range, interquartile range). Students represent events using two-way tables and Venn diagrams, and calculate relative frequencies to estimate probabilities. For example, they might find the probability of drawing a red card from a standard deck of 52 cards or analyse the results of a survey of 200 students about their favourite footy team to determine the most popular team.
