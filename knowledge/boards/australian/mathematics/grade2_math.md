# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 2 Mathematics
## Number and Algebra: Counting and Place Value
Students count to and from at least 1000. They group, partition and rearrange collections up to 1000 in hundreds, tens and ones to facilitate more efficient counting. Students recognise, model, represent and order numbers to at least 1000, using physical materials, diagrams and number lines. They apply place value to understand the effects of adding or subtracting 10, 100 or 1000. For example, they calculate the cost of two toys priced at $45 and $55 by adding to find the total $100.

## Number and Algebra: Addition and Subtraction
Students develop efficient mental and written strategies for addition and subtraction. They recall addition facts for single-digit numbers and related subtraction facts, and extend these to three-digit numbers. Students choose between strategies such as counting on, partitioning, and jump counting on a number line to solve problems involving money, like working out the change from a $20 note after buying a book for $8. They model problems with materials and record solutions using equations, ensuring answers are sensible in the context of the problem.

## Number and Algebra: Multiplication and Division
Students represent multiplication as repeated addition and arrays. They explore the connection between multiplication and division, using concrete materials to model sharing into equal groups. Students recall multiplication facts of 2, 3, 5 and 10, and use these to solve simple problems. For example, they can find the total number of seats in three rows of ten, or share 15 biscuits equally among five friends, understanding that each friend gets three biscuits. They use efficient counting strategies to support their calculations.

## Measurement and Geometry: Units and Shape
Students order objects based on length, area, volume, capacity and mass using uniform informal units. They compare masses using a balance scale, such as a pineapple versus a bag of apples. Students name and describe common 2D shapes (e.g., triangle, square, hexagon) and 3D objects (e.g., cube, sphere, cylinder). They create and describe patterns with shapes, and identify shapes in their local environment, like the circular wheels on a ute or the rectangular shape of a cricket pitch in the outback.

## Measurement and Geometry: Location and Time
Students give and follow directions to move people and objects using positional language such as 'next to', 'between', 'forwards' and 'clockwise'. They interpret simple maps of familiar locations, like their classroom or a local park. Students tell time to the quarter hour, including using the language of 'past' and 'to', and connect time to daily activities, such as lunch starting at half past twelve. They describe duration using terms like 'longer' and 'shorter', comparing the length of different footy matches.

## Statistics and Probability: Data and Chance
Students collect data by asking questions, like 'What is your favourite colour?'. They record and sort data using tally marks and lists, then represent it with objects, drawings and column graphs. They interpret simple graphs to answer questions, such as finding the most popular pet in the class. Students describe outcomes of familiar events as 'likely' or 'unlikely', including everyday situations like it being 'likely' to be sunny at the Great Barrier Reef in summer. They distinguish between questions that are answerable from data and those that are not.
