# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 5 Mathematics

## Number and Algebra: Fractions and Decimals
Students compare and order common fractions and locate them on a number line. They add and subtract fractions with the same or related denominators, such as eighths and quarters. For decimals, they read, write, and order numbers to three decimal places, connecting them to money amounts like $12.75. They multiply and divide decimals by whole numbers using strategies like partitioning, for example, calculating $4.20 divided by 7 for sharing the cost of footy tickets. Students also find equivalent fractions and decimals, such as 0.5 being the same as one-half.

## Number and Algebra: Patterns and Algebra
Students create, identify, and describe number patterns involving addition, subtraction, and multiplication. They explore the rule for a pattern, like adding 6 each time to get 4, 10, 16, 22. Students use symbols to represent unknown values in simple equations, for instance, working out that a box plus 15 equals 45 means the box holds 30. They also use the language of 'if...then' to describe relationships, such as, 'If you have 5 kangaroos, then you have 20 legs', reinforcing the concept of multiplication as repeated addition.

## Measurement and Geometry: Using Units of Measurement
Students choose and use appropriate metric units to measure length, mass, and capacity, using tools like rulers, scales, and measuring jugs. They convert units, such as 1.5 kilograms being 1500 grams or 2500 millilitres being 2.5 litres. They calculate perimeter and area of rectangles, using grid paper or real objects like a paddock. To connect with their surroundings, they might find the area of a classroom in square metres or estimate the volume of a water tank, applying their knowledge to solve practical problems in the outback.

## Measurement and Geometry: Shape
Students identify and describe 2D shapes, including quadrilaterals, and classify them based on their properties like the number of sides and angles. They name 3D objects such as prisms and pyramids and describe their features, including the shape of their faces. Students learn about nets and can visualise how a flat shape folds to form a cube or pyramid. They connect these shapes to real-world objects, like recognising a triangular prism as the shape of a tent or a prism as the shape of the Great Barrier Reef's underwater structures.

## Statistics and Probability: Data Representation and Interpretation
Students pose questions and collect data through observation or surveys. They construct and interpret a range of data displays, including tables, column graphs, and dot plots. They use data to answer questions, for instance, by analysing a graph of favourite AFL teams to see which team is most popular. Students also calculate and interpret the mean (average) for a small set of data, such as finding the average number of pets in their class to understand a typical result.

## Statistics and Probability: Chance
Students list outcomes of familiar chance events, like rolling a dice or drawing a card from a deck. They use the terms 'certain', 'likely', 'unlikely', and 'impossible' to describe the likelihood of an event. For example, it is 'certain' a coin will land on heads or tails, and 'impossible' to draw a green marble from a bag containing only red ones. Students also design simple chance experiments, such as flipping a coin 20 times and recording the results to see if it is a fair activity.
