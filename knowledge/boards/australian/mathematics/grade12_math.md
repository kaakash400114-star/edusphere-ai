# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 12 Mathematics
## Complex Numbers and the Complex Plane
Students explore the system of complex numbers as a two-dimensional number plane. They represent numbers in the form a + bi, where a and b are real numbers and i is the imaginary unit with the property i² = -1. Learners perform algebraic operations, including addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division, of complex numbers. They learn to plot these numbers on the complex plane, with the real component on the horizontal axis and the imaginary component on the vertical axis. This includes understanding the modulus, |z|, and the argument, Arg(z), of a complex number.

## Trigonometry and Circular Functions
Students extend their knowledge of trigonometry to the unit circle, defining sine and cosine for all real angles, including those beyond the 0° to 360° range. They master the use of radians as a measure of angle. The curriculum covers the application of the sine, cosine, and tangent functions to model periodic phenomena in the real world, such as the height of a tide near the Great Barrier Reef. Key identities, such as Pythagorean and angle sum formulas, are used to simplify and solve trigonometric equations and prove related statements.

## Introduction to Differential Calculus
Students are introduced to the concept of the derivative as an instantaneous rate of change. They learn to differentiate from first principles for simple functions, and subsequently master the application of differentiation rules, including the power, product, quotient, and chain rules. These rules are used to find the derivatives of polynomial, exponential, logarithmic, and trigonometric functions. This foundational knowledge allows students to analyse and sketch the graphs of functions, finding gradients, turning points, and points of inflection to describe motion, for example, the path of a footy kicked in a parabolic arc.

## Further Differential Calculus and Applications
Building on the fundamentals, students apply calculus techniques to solve practical and theoretical problems. This includes using derivatives to determine the rate of change of related quantities, a concept crucial in physics and economics. They investigate optimisation problems, such as finding the maximum area of a paddock with a fixed amount of fencing or minimising the cost of producing a gadget. Integration is introduced as the inverse process of differentiation, with students learning to find the area under a curve, such as calculating the distance travelled from a velocity function given in metres per second.

## Statistical Inference
Students develop skills in statistical inference, allowing them to make judgements about populations based on samples. This includes the use of random sampling techniques to ensure data is representative. They study the key distribution, the normal distribution, and calculate probabilities corresponding to given z-scores. Students are introduced to the concept of a confidence interval for a population mean, interpreting it as a range of values that is likely to contain the true mean. This knowledge could be applied to, for instance, estimating the average weekly income in Australian dollars (AUD) for small business owners.

## Matrices and their Applications
Students learn to define, represent, and manipulate matrices to store and solve systems of linear equations. This includes performing operations such as addition, subtraction, scalar multiplication, and matrix multiplication. Students find the determinant of a 2x2 matrix and use it to determine if a matrix is invertible. They explore the application of matrices in real-world contexts, such as analysing network flows (like data across the internet), cryptography, and performing transformations in computer graphics, such as resizing a digital image of the outback.
