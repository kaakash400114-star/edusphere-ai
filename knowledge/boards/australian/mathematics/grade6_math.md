# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 6 Mathematics

## Number and Algebra: Whole Numbers and Integers
Students recognise, represent and order positive and negative integers to solve problems. They extend their understanding of place value to the billions, using numerals, words and expanded notation. Students learn to use all four operations with integers, applying the order of operations (BODMAS/PEMDAS) to solve multistep problems with real-world contexts, such as calculating temperature changes or tracking financial gains and losses in AUD. Mental and written strategies are practised for fluency, with an emphasis on checking the reasonableness of solutions.

## Number and Algebra: Fractions, Decimals and Percentages
Students multiply and divide fractions by simple fractions and whole numbers, using efficient strategies. They connect equivalent fractions, decimals and percentages, recognising that a percentage is a special fraction with a denominator of 100. Students solve problems involving finding a percentage of a quantity, such as calculating a 10% Goods and Services Tax (GST) on an item. They order common fractions and terminating decimals on a number line, developing a strong sense of proportional reasoning for financial calculations, like discounts at the shops.

## Number and Algebra: Financial Mathematics
Students solve financial problems involving purchases, making change, and calculating profit and loss. They are introduced to the concept of a budget, calculating income and simple expenses, such as the weekly cost of a footy team's equipment. Students explore the impact of saving and interest, using percentages to model growth in a savings account. They critically evaluate financial decisions, such as comparing the cost of a new item versus a second-hand one, and learn to represent financial data in simple tables and charts for clear communication.

## Measurement and Geometry: Length, Area and Volume
Students convert between common metric units of length, area, volume and capacity, using efficient multiplication or division strategies. They calculate the area of triangles and parallelograms using the formulas A = ½bh and A = bh, and apply these to find the area of composite shapes. Volume is explored using the formula V = lwh, with students finding the volume of rectangular prisms. Problems relate to real-world contexts, like calculating the area of a backyard for turf or the volume of a water tank for a remote outback property.

## Measurement and Geometry: Time and Angles
Students use the 12- and 24-hour time systems to solve problems involving duration and timetables, such as planning a trip to see the Great Barrier Reef. They estimate and measure angles using a protractor, classifying them as acute, obtuse, straight, reflex or a revolution. Students apply their knowledge of angles to solve problems involving shapes and parallel lines, using geometric reasoning to find unknown angles. This builds spatial awareness for interpreting maps and navigating familiar and unfamiliar environments.

## Statistics and Probability: Data Representation and Interpretation
Students interpret and compare data sets from a variety of sources, including surveys and digital media. They construct, label and interpret a range of graphs, including side-by-side column graphs for comparing data and line graphs for showing changes over time, such as tracking the monthly attendance at a sports oval. Students calculate the mean (average), median, mode and range of data sets to describe and compare them. This develops skills for analysing information and drawing evidence-based conclusions.
