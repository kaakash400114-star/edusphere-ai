# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 4 Science
## Forces and Movement
Students explore how pushes and pulls affect the movement of objects. They learn that a force is a push or a pull and that it can change an object’s speed, direction, or shape. Students identify contact forces, like kicking a football, and non-contact forces, like the Earth’s gravity pulling a ball down. They conduct experiments to observe how friction, a force that opposes motion, can slow things down, such as a toy car on carpet versus a smooth bench top.
## Heat and Temperature
Students learn that heat is a form of energy that can move from a hotter object to a colder one. They use thermometers to measure temperature in degrees Celsius (°C) and observe how heat sources like the sun or a radiator warm things up. They understand that materials can be thermal conductors, like metal, which heat up quickly, or thermal insulators, like wood, which stay cooler. This helps explain why a metal spoon left in a hot cup of tea becomes hot to touch.
## Living Things and Their Habitats
Students classify living things based on observable features, such as how they move, what they eat, and where they live. They learn about different habitats in Australia, including the arid outback, the coral reefs of the Great Barrier Reef, and eucalypt forests. They explore how living things are suited to their environment, like how a kangaroo’s long legs help it travel long distances to find food and water in the dry outback.
## Earth’s Resources
Students identify Earth’s natural resources, such as fresh water, soil, rocks, and air. They learn that some resources are renewable, like sunlight, and others are non-renewable, like coal. They understand the importance of sustainability and how we can reduce waste by reusing items like plastic bottles and recycling paper. Students also explore how these resources are used in everyday life, such as clean water for drinking and soil for growing food.
## Earth and Space
Students describe the daily and seasonal changes caused by the Earth’s rotation on its axis and its orbit around the Sun. They observe the Sun’s movement across the sky and learn that the length of daylight changes with the seasons. Students identify the main features of the solar system, including the Sun, planets, and Earth’s Moon. They use this knowledge to understand why our seasons in Australia are the opposite of those in the Northern Hemisphere.
## Materials and Properties
Students investigate the properties of everyday materials, such as whether they are transparent (like clear glass), opaque (like a wooden block), or absorbent (like a paper towel). They explore how materials can be changed physically, like by cutting or melting ice, or chemically, like when wood burns to form ash. They also learn how these properties make materials suitable for different uses, such as using metal for saucepans because it is a good conductor of heat.
