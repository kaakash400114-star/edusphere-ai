# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 3 Science
## Heat and Temperature
Students learn that heat is a form of energy that moves from warmer objects to cooler ones until their temperatures become equal. This transfer can happen through conduction (direct contact, like a hot spoon warming cold soup), convection (through liquids and gases, like warm air rising), and radiation (without direct contact, like heat from the sun). Temperature is a measure of the heat energy in an object. An oven temperature of 180 degrees Celsius is much hotter than the inside of a refrigerator, which is around 4 degrees Celsius.
## Features of Living Things
Students identify the basic characteristics that all living things share. These include growth, where a seedling can grow into a tall tree over many years; movement, even if it is very slow like a plant growing towards the sun; the need for food or energy, such as a kangaroo grazing on grass; and reproduction, where animals have babies or plants produce seeds. A magpie building a nest and laying eggs is an example of this last feature, demonstrating how living things create new life.
## Earth's Resources
Students explore how materials from the Earth are used in everyday life. Natural resources can be solid, like the iron ore used to make cars in Australia; liquid, such as the crude oil refined into petrol; or gas. Water is a critical resource, managed carefully in Australia due to its importance for life and farming. Farmers in the outback rely on bores to access groundwater for their livestock and crops, showing how we use the Earth's resources to support communities.
## Materials and their Properties
Students investigate how materials are suited to their uses based on their observable properties. For example, a metal spoon is good for stirring hot soup because it is a good conductor of heat, while a wooden spoon is a poor conductor and won't get as hot. Glass is transparent, so we use it for windows, and rubber is flexible, making it perfect for shoe soles. These properties—conductivity, transparency, flexibility—determine the best material for a specific job.
## Earth's Place in Space
Students learn that the Earth is a sphere that spins on an axis, which causes day and night. When our part of the Earth faces the Sun, it is daytime. As the Earth rotates, our part turns away, and it becomes night. The Earth also orbits, or travels around, the Sun once every year. This orbit is why we experience the changing seasons. In summer in Australia, the Southern Hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun, giving us longer days and more direct sunlight.
## Forces and Motion
Students discover that a push or a pull is a force that can make an object start moving, stop moving, change direction, or change shape. A force can be strong, like kicking a football (footy), or gentle, like blowing a dandelion seed. The heavier an object is, the more force is needed to move it. A netball is lighter than a basketball, so it requires less force to throw it the same distance. Forces are all around us and affect how things move every single day.
