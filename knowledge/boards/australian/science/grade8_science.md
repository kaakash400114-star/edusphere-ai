# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 8 Science
## Physical Sciences: Energy Transfer
Students learn that energy is transferred and transformed, but never created or destroyed. They explore different forms of energy, including kinetic, potential, thermal, and electrical, and how they convert from one to another. For example, a swinging pendulum continuously transforms gravitational potential energy (at its highest point) into kinetic energy (at its lowest point) and back, with small amounts of thermal energy generated by air resistance. This principle is fundamental to understanding everyday devices like a battery-powered torch, which converts chemical energy into light and thermal energy.

## Physical Sciences: Forces and Motion
Students investigate how forces can change an object’s motion. They learn Newton’s First Law (inertia), explaining that an object will stay at rest or in constant motion unless an unbalanced force acts upon it. A common example is a seatbelt stopping a person from moving forward when a car brakes suddenly. They also learn about the relationship between force, mass, and acceleration, expressed by the formula F = ma. This helps them understand why kicking a heavy rugby ball requires more force than kicking a light tennis ball to achieve the same acceleration.

## Chemical Sciences: Atoms and Elements
Students develop the atomic model to understand that all substances are made of atoms. They learn about the structure of atoms, including a nucleus containing protons and neutrons, surrounded by electrons. They distinguish between elements, which are pure substances made of only one type of atom, and compounds, which are formed when two or more different elements chemically bond. For instance, water (H₂O) is a compound made of hydrogen and oxygen atoms, while gold (Au) is a single element. This knowledge underpins the classification of all matter.

## Chemical Sciences: Chemical Reactions
Students explore how substances react to form new substances with different properties. They learn to identify evidence of a chemical reaction, such as a colour change, the production of a gas (e.g., bubbles when vinegar is added to baking soda), or a change in temperature. They also understand the law of conservation of mass, stating that the total mass of reactants equals the total mass of products in a closed system. For example, burning wood transforms it into ash and gases, but the total mass remains the same.

## Earth and Space Sciences: Plate Tectonics
Students learn about the dynamic Earth, focusing on the theory of plate tectonics. They discover that Earth's crust is divided into massive plates that move slowly on the semi-fluid mantle below. This movement can cause earthquakes, volcanoes, and mountain ranges. A key Australian example is the Pacific Plate colliding with the Indo-Australian Plate, forming the Great Dividing Range and leading to seismic activity in regions like Tasmania and Victoria. This process also explains the existence of the continental landmass.

## Biological Sciences: Ecosystems and Interactions
Students understand that ecosystems consist of living and non-living components interacting in a balance. They learn about food chains and webs, which illustrate the transfer of energy from producers (like grass) to consumers (like kangaroos) and decomposers. They explore how changes to one part of an ecosystem, such as the introduction of a feral animal or a bushfire, can have significant flow-on effects. A local example is the impact of drought on outback food webs, where the decline in certain plants affects herbivores like the emu, which in turn impacts predators.
