# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 5 Science
## Earth and Space Sciences
Students learn about the Earth's place in the solar system, focusing on the sun and planets. They describe the features of the sun, including that it is a star that provides light and heat. They model the relative sizes of planets using everyday materials and understand that Earth rotates every 24 hours, causing day and night. Students learn that Earth orbits the sun once a year. They also identify and describe seasonal changes in Australia, linking these to the Earth's tilt and orbit.

## Physical Sciences
Students explore forces and their effects on the movement of objects. They learn that a push or a pull is a force and identify contact forces, like friction. Students conduct experiments to see how forces change the speed or direction of a toy car. They learn that gravity is a force that pulls objects towards the Earth. Students use simple tools like springs to measure force and understand that different surfaces create different amounts of friction, affecting how far a ball will roll.

## Biological Sciences
Students examine the life cycles of different living things, particularly focusing on those found in Australian environments. They compare the life cycles of a frog and a mammal like a kangaroo. Students learn about the stages of growth and reproduction in flowering plants, from seed to germination and pollination. They classify plants and animals based on observable features, such as whether they have fur, feathers, or scales. They also investigate how living things in an ecosystem, such as those in the Great Barrier Reef, depend on each other for survival.

## Chemical Sciences
Students investigate the properties of everyday materials and how they can change. They distinguish between reversible changes, like melting ice into water, and irreversible changes, like burning a piece of wood. Students learn that mixing materials can cause a chemical reaction by observing examples like baking soda and vinegar. They use their senses to safely observe and describe materials, noting their colour, texture, and state of matter, and understand that changes can be caused by heat, light, or mixing.

## Science Inquiry Skills
Students develop skills to plan and conduct their own investigations. They ask questions, such as 'Which material is the best insulator?', and design a fair test to find an answer. They learn to make predictions based on observations. Students record data in tables and use their senses to make careful observations. They analyse their results to identify patterns or relationships. Students share their findings using simple graphs, models, or written reports, explaining what they discovered and why their results might have turned out that way.

## Science as a Human Endeavour
Students learn that science is used in everyday life and by people in their community. They explore examples of Australian technology, like the design of a surfboard or a solar-powered irrigation system used in the outback. They investigate the work of scientists, such as marine biologists studying the Great Barrier Reef. Students understand that science has led to important discoveries, like developing new medicines, and that it helps us solve problems, such as protecting unique Australian animals like the koala.
