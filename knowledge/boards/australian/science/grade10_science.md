# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 10 Science

## The Structure and Properties of Matter
Students explore the atomic model and how it explains the properties of materials. They apply the Periodic Table to predict the properties of elements and compounds. The focus is on the classification of substances into metals, non-metals, and metalloids, and how atomic structure influences conductivity and reactivity. Students investigate chemical bonding, including ionic bonding between sodium (Na) and chlorine (Cl) to form table salt, and covalent bonding in water molecules. The physical properties of materials like diamond (carbon) and silicon are compared to understand applications in modern technology, such as microchips.

## Energy Transformations and Efficiency
Students learn about the laws of thermodynamics, focusing on energy conservation and transformations. They calculate the efficiency of appliances using the formula: Efficiency (%) = (Useful Energy Out / Total Energy In) × 100. Examples include analysing a car engine's efficiency or comparing the energy use of incandescent versus LED globes. The concept of energy dissipation, such as waste heat from machinery or a campfire, is central. Students also study renewable energy sources like solar panels converting sunlight into electrical energy, and wind turbines on properties near rural towns, calculating their power output in kilowatts (kW).

## Genetic Inheritance and Variation
This topic covers Mendelian genetics, where students use Punnett squares to predict the probability of inherited traits. They learn about dominant and recessive alleles, using examples like pea plant colour or human traits such as widow's peak. Students explore how genetic variation arises from mutations, gene shuffling during meiosis, and environmental influences. The role of DNA as the genetic blueprint is examined, alongside the ethical implications of technologies like CRISPR. A local example could be analysing the genetic diversity of the Tasmanian devil population and its impact on resistance to facial tumour disease.

## Evolution and Natural Selection
Students examine the theory of evolution by natural selection as proposed by Charles Darwin. They analyse how variation within a species and selective pressures lead to adaptation. Case studies include the development of antibiotic resistance in bacteria or the evolution of the thylacine. The fossil record, comparative anatomy, and DNA evidence are presented as supporting evidence. Students investigate how geographical isolation, such as the formation of Bass Strait, can lead to speciation, using Australian examples like the diversification of kangaroo species across the continent to suit different arid and coastal environments.

## Our Dynamic Earth
Students investigate the theory of plate tectonics and its role in shaping Earth's surface. They learn about the three main plate boundaries: convergent, divergent, and transform, and their associated landforms, like deep-sea trenches and mountain ranges. The focus includes analysing geological hazards such as earthquakes and volcanic eruptions, and the development of early warning systems. An Australian context is provided by studying the tectonic activity that formed the Great Dividing Range and the 1989 Newcastle earthquake, and understanding how plate movements created the unique geography of the continent over millions of years.

## Scientific Investigation and Analysis
Students develop skills in planning and conducting experiments. They learn to formulate testable hypotheses, identify variables, and design controlled procedures. Data analysis is a key component, involving the use of tables, graphs, and statistical measures like mean and range to identify trends. Students practice writing formal scientific reports, ensuring clarity and precision. A practical example could involve testing the pH of water samples from different local sources, such as a billabong in the outback and a river near a town, to assess water quality and report on potential environmental impacts from local industry or agriculture.
