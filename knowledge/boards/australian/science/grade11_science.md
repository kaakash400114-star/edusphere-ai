# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 11 Science
## Newton's Laws of Motion and their Applications
Students explore the principles of classical mechanics, focusing on Newton's three laws. The first law, or the law of inertia, states that an object remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by an unbalanced force. The second law, F = ma, is used to calculate force, mass, and acceleration; for example, a 10 kg parcel accelerating at 2 m/s² requires a net force of 20 N. The third law explains that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction, a principle fundamental to understanding rocket propulsion and the forces in a game of footy.
## Energy Transformation and Efficiency in Systems
This topic investigates the different forms of energy and their conversions within systems. Students learn to distinguish between kinetic, potential, thermal, and chemical energy, applying the principle of conservation of energy to analyse closed systems. The efficiency of an energy transfer is calculated as the useful output energy divided by the total input energy, often expressed as a percentage. For instance, a coal-fired power plant might have an efficiency of around 35%, meaning 65% of the chemical energy is lost primarily as waste heat.
## Atomic Structure and Periodic Table Trends
Students delve into the Rutherford-Bohr atomic model, learning about the composition of atoms: protons, neutrons, and electrons. They understand how the arrangement of electrons in shells determines an element's chemical properties. Key periodic trends are analysed, including atomic radius, ionisation energy, and electronegativity. For example, moving across a period from left to right (e.g., from Sodium to Chlorine), atomic radius decreases and ionisation energy increases due to the increasing nuclear charge.
## Chemical Bonding and Intermolecular Forces
This topic covers the formation of chemical bonds through the transfer or sharing of valence electrons. Students differentiate between ionic bonding (e.g., Sodium Chloride, NaCl), where electrons are transferred creating ions, and covalent bonding (e.g., Methane, CH₄), where electrons are shared. They also learn about weaker intermolecular forces such as hydrogen bonding, which is crucial for the high boiling point of water and the structure of DNA. These forces explain the properties of substances ranging from the hardness of diamond to the fluidity of petrol.
## Ecosystem Dynamics and Biodiversity Conservation
Students examine the interactions within ecosystems, focusing on energy flow through food chains and webs. They learn to use trophic levels to describe an organism's position, from producers to apex predators like the saltwater crocodile. The concept of biodiversity is explored, including species, genetic, and ecosystem diversity. Case studies of conservation efforts in Australia are used, such as managing the health of the Great Barrier Reef against threats like crown-of-thorns starfish and climate change.
## Wave Properties and the Electromagnetic Spectrum
This topic involves the study of transverse and longitudinal waves, defining properties like wavelength, frequency, and amplitude. Students learn the wave equation, v = fλ, and apply it to calculate these variables. They explore the electromagnetic spectrum, from low-energy radio waves to high-energy gamma rays, understanding how different wavelengths are used in technologies like MRI scans in medicine and Wi-Fi communication. The dual nature of light, as both a wave and a particle, is introduced.
