# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 12 Science

## Unit 1: Dynamics and Thermodynamics
This unit examines Newton's laws of motion and the concepts of energy and momentum. Students apply the formula F = ma to analyse forces acting on objects, including those on Earth and in space. Conservation of momentum, p = mv, is used to solve collision problems. In thermodynamics, the first law, ΔU = Q - W, relates change in internal energy to heat and work. Practical experiments involve measuring energy transfer in systems like a piston-cylinder, using SI units joules and watts, with examples drawn from Australian mining machinery operations.

## Unit 2: Electromagnetism and Quantum Theory
Students explore electric fields using Coulomb's law, F = kq₁q₂/r², to calculate forces between point charges, such as in atmospheric electricity. Magnetic fields are studied in relation to current-carrying wires and motors, applying the right-hand rule. The photoelectric effect introduces quantum theory, demonstrating light as quanta with E = hf, where h is Planck's constant and f is frequency. Australian examples include the operation of the Sydney Opera House's ventilation system and the principles behind solar technology used in the outback.

## Unit 3: Chemical Equilibrium and Reaction Kinetics
This unit focuses on the dynamic nature of chemical equilibrium, quantified by the equilibrium constant Kc. Students write expressions for Kc for reactions like the Haber process for ammonia synthesis. Le Chatelier's principle is used to predict how systems respond to changes in concentration, pressure, and temperature. Reaction rates are determined using rate laws and the rate equation, Rate = k[A]ᵐ[B]ⁿ. Experiments involve investigating factors affecting the rate of a reaction, such as the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide, a common classroom reagent.

## Unit 4: Human Biology and Health
Students investigate the structure and function of the human circulatory system, focusing on the cardiac cycle and blood pressure regulation. The renal system is examined in detail, exploring kidney function in maintaining homeostasis through filtration, reabsorption, and secretion. Health topics include the pathophysiology of diseases such as type 2 diabetes and the impact of lifestyle factors. Australian contexts include the high rates of kidney disease in Indigenous communities and the public health campaign to reduce smoking rates, measured in percentages per 100,000 people.

## Unit 5: Genetics and Evolution
This unit covers the principles of Mendelian genetics, using Punnett squares to predict inheritance patterns of traits like seed shape or flower colour. Modern genetic technologies, such as PCR and DNA sequencing, are discussed in the context of medical diagnostics. Evolution by natural selection is explained using the fossil record and comparative anatomy of Australian species, such as the platypus and kangaroo. The unit also addresses genetic diversity and the concept of a gene pool, with examples from managing endangered species like the Tasmanian devil.

## Unit 6: Earth and Environmental Science
Students analyse plate tectonics and their role in forming Australian geographical features, including the Great Dividing Range and the Bass Strait. Seismic data is used to locate earthquake epicentres. Environmental science examines the carbon cycle and climate change, focusing on Australia's carbon emissions measured in megatonnes. The impact of human activity on ecosystems is assessed through case studies like the management of the Great Barrier Reef, including threats from coral bleaching and strategies to improve water quality.
