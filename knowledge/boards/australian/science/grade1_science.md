# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 1 Science

## Living Things and Their Needs
Students identify the basic needs of living things, including air, water, food and shelter. They observe and describe how plants and animals in their local environment, such as a schoolyard or local park, meet these needs. Children learn that different animals, like a kangaroo in the outback or a possum in a suburban garden, have different shelters. They explore how a tree provides shelter and foraging for birds. This understanding forms the foundation of recognising that all living things depend on their environment to survive.

## Weather and Seasons
Students observe daily and seasonal weather changes in their local area. They learn to describe and measure weather using simple tools like a rain gauge and a thermometer. They describe the weather as being sunny, rainy, windy or cloudy. Children link observable changes, such as leaves falling in autumn or wearing a jumper in winter, to the seasons. They identify examples of seasonal changes in Australia, like the dry and wet seasons in the tropical north or the arrival of cyclones.

## Materials and Their Properties
Students explore a variety of everyday materials, such as wood, metal, plastic and paper. They describe these materials based on observable properties like colour, texture (rough or smooth), strength and flexibility. Children conduct simple tests to find out which materials are waterproof by pouring a small amount of water on them. They learn that materials can be changed by processes like squashing, bending or twisting. They identify common objects made from these materials, like a metal spoon or a plastic bucket.

## Light and Sound
Students investigate sources of light, including the sun and torches, and identify that light allows us to see. They learn that sound is produced by vibrations and can be made by plucking a rubber band or tapping a drum. Children describe how sounds can be loud or soft and high or low pitch. They explore how sound travels through materials, noticing that when they cover their ears, a bell sounds muffled. They link these concepts to everyday experiences, like the sound of a footy siren or a campfire crackling.

## Pushes and Pulls
Students explore the effects of forces, particularly pushes and pulls, on objects. They identify that forces can make objects start moving, stop moving, change speed or change direction. Children conduct simple investigations, like pushing a toy car or pulling a sled, to observe these effects. They learn that a heavy object like a backpack requires a bigger force to move than a light object. They also identify contact forces, like kicking a football, and non-contact forces, like the pull of a magnet on a paperclip.

## Earth and Its Place in Space
Students observe and describe the features of the Earth, including land and water. They use models and globes to identify the major continents and oceans. Children learn that the sun appears to move across the sky during the day and that the moon is sometimes visible at night. They identify Earth’s natural features, such as the Great Barrier Reef, beaches and mountains. They begin to understand that Earth is part of a larger system of objects in space.
