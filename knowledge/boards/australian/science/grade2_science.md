# Australian Curriculum (ACARA) Grade 2 Science

## Living Things and Their Needs
Living things, including plants and animals, have basic needs to stay alive and grow. They need food for energy, water to stay hydrated, air to breathe, and a safe place to live, such as a burrow or a tree. Animals can be grouped by their features, like birds with feathers and mammals with fur. A pet dog needs water from its bowl, dry kibble for food, and a comfortable kennel. Plants, like a geranium on a windowsill, need sunlight, potting mix, and regular watering to make their own food and thrive.

## Materials and Their Properties
Every object is made from materials that have special properties. We can describe materials by how they look, feel, and behave. For example, a metal spoon is hard and smooth, while a woollen jumper is soft and warm. Some materials, like plastic or glass, are waterproof, which makes them good for drink bottles. Wood is a strong material used for making chairs. By testing these properties, we can choose the best material for a job, such as using a strong metal like steel for a bridge.

## Observing Day and Night
The Earth’s movement causes day and night. As our planet spins on its axis, different parts face the Sun. When we face the Sun, it is daytime; we can see clearly and feel its warmth. When we are on the side facing away, it is nighttime; the sky becomes dark, and we see stars and the Moon. A cycle of one full spin takes 24 hours. During the day in Australia, you might play outside with a footy, while at night you might see the Southern Cross constellation in the outback sky.

## Weather and Our Clothes
Weather changes over time, and we can describe it using words like sunny, rainy, windy, or cold. The weather affects our choices, like what clothes to wear. On a windy day at the beach, we might wear a beanie and a jacket. On a hot, sunny day, we need a hat, sunscreen, and t-shirt to protect our skin. We can use a rain gauge to measure rainfall in millimetres. By observing the weather patterns, we can predict if we need an umbrella for our walk to school.

## Pushes and Pulls
A push is a force that moves an object away, and a pull is a force that moves an object closer. Forces can make things start moving, stop moving, change direction, or change shape. When you kick a football, you are applying a push force. When you lift your schoolbag, you are applying a pull force. Some forces, like the wind pushing a sailing boat or the water pushing a surfboard, are not touched by our hands but are still very powerful and make objects move.

## Caring for Our Environment
We all have a responsibility to care for our local environment and special places. The Great Barrier Reef is a unique Australian environment that needs protection. We can reduce waste by using reusable containers instead of plastic wrap. We can save water by turning off the tap when we brush our teeth. By not littering, we help keep parks and beaches clean for animals and people. Making these small habits helps protect our country for everyone to enjoy.
