# ICSE Class 3 Mathematics - CISCE approach

## Numbers to 10,000: place value and rounding
In Class 3, we learn to write, read, and count numbers up to 10,000, understanding the place value of each digit—ones, tens, hundreds, and thousands. For example, in the number 4,563, '4' is in the thousands place, '5' in the hundreds, '6' in the tens, and '3' in the ones. We also learn to round numbers to the nearest ten or hundred using simple rules: if the next digit is 5 or more, we round up; if it’s 4 or less, we round down. For instance, 78 rounds to 80 (nearest ten), and 342 rounds to 300 (nearest hundred). This helps us make quick estimates and work with bigger numbers easily.

Ten-thousands; compare-order; round to nearest 10 and 100; Roman numerals I-L.

## Four operations with word problems
In Class 3 Mathematics, children learn to add, subtract, multiply, and solve simple word problems using numbers up to 1000. They understand addition as putting things together and subtraction as taking away, using methods like regrouping for larger numbers. Multiplication is introduced as repeated addition, such as 3 × 4 means adding 3 four times (3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12). Word problems help them apply these operations to real-life situations, like sharing sweets or counting toys, making learning fun and practical.

Add-subtract to 5 digits; multiply 2-3 digit by 1-digit; divide by 1-digit with remainder; choose-the-operation stories.

## Fractions on the number line
In Class 3, we learn to show fractions on a number line by dividing the space between two whole numbers into equal parts. For example, to show 1/4, we divide the gap between 0 and 1 into four small equal parts and mark the first point. The number line helps us see that fractions are numbers between whole numbers and shows their order, like 1/4 is smaller than 3/4. We practice drawing number lines for fractions like 1/2, 1/3, and 2/3 to understand their positions clearly. This teaches us that fractions represent equal parts of a whole and can be placed just like whole numbers.

Unit fractions; compare with pictures; equivalent pairs (1/2 = 2/4); fraction of a set.

## Money: bills, change and simple budgets
In Class 3, you learn about money by identifying Indian currency notes and coins, and understanding their values. You practice adding and subtracting amounts to find the total cost of items or the change you get back after paying. You also make simple budgets for things like a school trip, adding up costs for snacks and drinks to stay within a given amount. This helps you count money correctly and use it in everyday situations like buying pencils or sharing with friends.

Add prices; change from ₹500; saving-goal mini projects.

## Measurement: length, mass, capacity with conversions
In Class 3, children learn to measure length using centimetres and metres, comparing short and long objects like a pencil (15 cm) and a door (2 m). They measure mass in grams and kilograms, understanding that a heavy book is 1 kg while a small apple is 100 g. Capacity is explored using litres and millilitres, where a water bottle holds 500 mL and a big bucket holds 10 L. Kids practice converting smaller units to bigger ones, like 100 cm = 1 m and 1000 mL = 1 L. They solve simple problems, such as finding the total length of two ropes or adding the weight of fruits.

m↔cm, kg↔g, l↔ml; measure-and-estimate trails.

## Time and calendar
In Class 3, we learn to read time on clocks with both the hour and minute hands, like 3:00 or half past 5. We understand that one hour has 60 minutes and one minute has 60 seconds. The calendar helps us know the days of the week, months of the year, and how to find dates. For example, we can count how many days are in January or find which day comes after Tuesday. We also learn to write dates in the correct order, like 15th January 2024, and find simple time differences, like how many hours are between 2 PM and 5 PM.

Minutes-past-to; duration; leap-year idea; reading timetables.

## Geometry and data
Children learn to identify and name basic shapes like circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles in their surroundings. They explore the properties of these shapes, such as the number of sides and corners, using simple examples like a round clock or a square book. Pupils also learn to sort and group objects based on shapes and colours, understanding how to organise things. In data handling, they collect information through simple surveys, like counting friends who like fruits or vegetables, and represent it using tallies or basic pictographs. This helps them compare small groups and understand basic information organisation.

Lines-segments; angles as turns; triangles-quadrilaterals; tally-pictograph-bar basics.
