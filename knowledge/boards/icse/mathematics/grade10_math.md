# ICSE Class 10 Mathematics - CISCE

## Commercial arithmetic
In Commercial Mathematics, you learn to calculate simple interest using the formula I = P × R × T / 100, where P is the principal amount, R is the rate of interest, and T is the time in years. You also study compound interest, where interest is added to the principal at each time period, making the amount grow faster. For example, if you invest ₹1000 at 5% per year, after two years, the compound amount will be more than the simple interest amount. You practice problems involving discounts, marked prices, and profit or loss to understand how shops calculate earnings. Additionally, you learn to compute sales tax (GST) and solve real-life problems like finding the cost of items including taxes or comparing prices to get the best deal.

GST; banking (recurring deposits); shares-dividends; compound interest as repeated percentage.

## Algebra
In Algebra, students learn to use letters like *x* and *y* to stand for unknown numbers and solve simple equations. They practice balancing both sides of an equation to find the missing number, such as in *2x + 3 = 11*, where *x* equals 4. Children also explore how to write expressions using variables, like *3y + 5*, and learn to simplify them by combining like terms. They study linear equations in one or two variables and plot them on graphs to see straight lines. For example, the equation *y = 2x + 1* shows a line where each step of *x* changes *y* by twice that plus one.

Linear inequations; quadratic equations (discriminant-nature); factor theorem-remainder theorem; ratio-proportion (componendo-dividendo); matrices: order, addition, subtraction, multiplication; arithmetic-geometric progressions (nth term, sums); coordinate geometry: section formula, equation of lines, slope conditions.

## Geometry
In Geometry, you learn about triangles and their properties, like the Pythagoras theorem which says that in a right-angled triangle, the square of the longest side equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides. You also study circles, learning to find the area and circumference using formulas like πr² and 2πr, where 'r' is the radius. The topic covers quadrilaterals too, where you identify shapes like squares and rectangles and calculate their areas and perimeters. You will also explore coordinate geometry, learning to plot points on a graph and understand their positions using numbers. All these ideas help you solve problems involving shapes, distances, and positions in real life.

Similarity: criteria and areas-ratios; circles: chord theorems, cyclic quadrilaterals, tangents-angle properties; constructions: tangents, similar triangles; loci basic; symmetry.

## Mensuration
In mensuration, you learn to find the area and perimeter of 2D shapes like rectangles, squares, triangles, and circles. For 3D shapes, you calculate volume and surface area of cuboids, cylinders, and cones using formulas. For example, the area of a rectangle is length times breadth, while the volume of a cylinder is πr²h. You also solve problems involving these concepts, like finding the cost of painting a wall or the amount of water in a tank. These formulas help you understand real-life measurements and solve practical math problems.

Cylinder-cone-sphere combinations; conversion of solids; perimeter-area of circle-related figures.

## Trigonometry
In Trigonometry, you learn about right-angled triangles and the relationship between their sides and angles using three main ratios: sine (sin), cosine (cos), and tangent (tan). You will use these ratios to find missing sides or angles, for example, if you know the length of one side and one angle, you can calculate the other sides. The Pythagoras theorem is also used alongside trigonometry to solve problems involving right-angled triangles. You will study the values of sin, cos, and tan for specific angles like 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, and 90° to make calculations easier. These concepts help in solving real-life problems, like finding the height of a building or the distance of a ship from the shore.

Identities; trigonometric tables use; heights-distances with angles elevation-depression.

## Statistics
In Class 10 Mathematics, you learn to collect, organise, and show data using tally marks and frequency tables. You will find the mean, median, and mode of ungrouped data—like finding the average marks of your class, the middle mark when arranged in order, or the mark that appears most often. For grouped data, you learn to calculate the mean using the step-deviation method and the median and mode from given class intervals. You also draw different graphs like bar graphs, histograms, and frequency polygons to show data clearly, helping you understand information better.

Mean (all methods), median, mode of grouped data; ogives; histogram; quartiles idea.

## Probability
In Class 10, you learn about probability as the chance of something happening, written as a number between 0 and 1. You find probability by dividing favourable outcomes by total possible outcomes, like rolling a die: the chance of getting a 3 is 1/6. You also study equally likely events, where every outcome has the same chance, such as picking a coloured marble from a bag. For example, if a bag has 3 red and 2 blue marbles, the probability of picking red is 3/5. You also learn to calculate the probability of an event not happening by subtracting its probability from 1, like the chance of not getting a tail when tossing a coin is 1/2.

Random experiments; single-event theoretical probability; complementary events; simple card-dice problems.
