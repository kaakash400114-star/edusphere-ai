# ICSE Class 1 Mathematics - CISCE approach

## Numbers 0-100 with place value intro
In Class 1, children learn to count, read, and write numbers from 0 to 100. They understand numbers as groups of tens and ones, like 25 being 2 tens and 5 ones. Using objects like beads or blocks, they build and break numbers into tens and units. Children also practice comparing numbers using bigger than, smaller than, and equal to. For example, 45 is bigger than 38 because it has more tens. They learn to write numbers in both figures (45) and words (forty-five).

Count, read, write 0-100; before-after-between; compare with < > =; tens and ones with bundles.

## Addition and subtraction to 50 (with carrying idea)
In Class 1, children learn to add and subtract numbers up to 50, using simple carrying over when adding two-digit numbers. For example, when adding 18 and 5, they write 8+5=13, carry over the 1 to the tens place, and then add 1+1=2 to get 23. In subtraction, they learn to take away smaller numbers from bigger ones, like 25 minus 3 equals 22. They use number lines and objects to understand these sums better. The focus is on building a clear understanding of place value and simple arithmetic rules.

Number-line jumps; two-digit plus one-digit with carry; take-away stories with objects.

## Tables of 2, 5, 10 and skip counting
Children learn the multiplication tables of 2, 5, and 10, which are numbers we count by skip counting. For example, counting by 2 gives us 2, 4, 6, 8, and so on, while counting by 5 gives us 5, 10, 15, 20, and so on. The table of 10 helps us count in tens, like 10, 20, 30, 40, etc., making it easy to add groups of the same number. Skip counting helps us find answers quickly when we add the same number repeatedly. These tables and counting methods make learning multiplication simple and fun for young learners.

Group counting by 2s (pairs of shoes), 5s (fingers), 10s (bundles).

## Shapes: plane and solid hunt
In Class 1, children learn to identify and name simple plane shapes like circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles, and solid shapes such as balls, cubes, and cylinders. They observe real-world objects, like a round clock (circle) or a book (rectangle), to match them to these shapes. The topic helps children understand the difference between flat plane shapes and solid shapes that can be held. They also sort and group shapes based on their characteristics, like counting sides or corners in plane shapes. By playing games like a shape hunt, children practice recognising shapes in their surroundings, building a foundation for geometry.

Circle-triangle-square-rectangle; cube-cylinder-cone-sphere found at home and class.

## Measurement and money
Children learn to measure short lengths using non-standard units like hand spans or blocks, such as finding how many blocks long a pencil is. They compare objects using words like "longer," "shorter," "taller," and "heavier," like saying a book is heavier than a feather. With money, they recognise and name common Indian coins like ₹1, ₹2, ₹5, and ₹10, and count small amounts of coins to find the total, such as adding two ₹1 coins to make ₹2. They also learn to identify currency notes by their colours and values, like the ₹50 note being green. Simple everyday examples help them understand these ideas clearly.

Longer-shorter, heavier-lighter; Indian coins and ₹10-₹50 notes; simple shopping play with change.

## Time, patterns and data
In Class 1, children learn to tell time using the hour hand on a clock, recognising o’clock times like 3 o’clock or 7 o’clock. They identify simple patterns such as red, blue, red, blue or big, small, big, small. Students sort and classify objects based on one attribute, like grouping fruits by colour or shape. They also learn to collect and represent data using tally marks or simple picture graphs, counting items like toys or animals. These activities help them understand basic sequencing and organisation in daily life.

O'clock and half-past; days-weeks; repeating patterns; picture collection charts.
