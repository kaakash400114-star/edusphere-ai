# ICSE Class 5 Mathematics - CISCE approach

## Numbers: Indian-international systems and estimation
In Class 5, children learn to write big numbers using the Indian and international systems, like 1,00,000 (one lakh) and 100,000 (one hundred thousand). They practice reading and writing numbers up to crores and millions by grouping digits in threes from the right. Kids also learn to round numbers to the nearest ten, hundred, or thousand to make estimation easy, like 342 becomes 340 when rounded to the nearest ten. They use estimation to quickly guess the answer, such as 28 + 41 ≈ 30 + 40 = 70. This helps them check if their calculations are correct and makes math simpler and faster.

Upto crores-millions; round and estimate products-quotients; Roman numerals recap.

## Operations and problem solving
In Grade 5 Mathematics, children learn to solve problems using addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. They use place value to add and subtract numbers up to five digits, like 23546 + 12345. Multiplication is done with two-digit numbers, such as 25 × 12, and division involves dividing three-digit numbers by one-digit numbers, like 144 ÷ 12. They also solve simple word problems using these operations, like sharing 60 sweets equally among 5 children. Children learn to check their answers using inverse operations to ensure they are correct.

All four operations on large numbers and decimals (tenths-hundredths); BODMAS order; multi-step real-life problems.

## Fractions deep
In Class 5, children learn about fractions as parts of a whole, like cutting a chocolate into equal pieces and taking some. They compare fractions to see which is bigger or smaller, such as 1/2 versus 1/4, by looking at how many parts make the whole. They also add and subtract fractions with the same denominator, like 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/8, because the parts are the same size. To make fractions easier, they simplify them by dividing the top and bottom numbers by the same number, like 4/8 becoming 1/2. Finally, they learn about mixed numbers, which are whole numbers and fractions together, like 1 1/2, meaning one whole and half of another.

Unlike add-subtract via LCM; multiply fractions; divide by fractions (reciprocal idea); word problems.

## Decimals and percentages bridge
In Class 5, children learn to connect decimals and percentages by understanding that decimals can be changed into percentages by multiplying by 100, like turning 0.75 into 75%. They also learn to write percentages as decimals by dividing by 100, such as 20% becoming 0.20. Using place value, they compare decimals like 0.5 and 0.25 to see which is bigger, and they solve simple problems like finding 10% of 50 by dividing 50 into 10 equal parts. They practice real-life examples, such as calculating discounts or sharing sweets, to see how decimals and percentages work around them. This helps them build confidence in solving everyday maths problems using both decimals and percentages.

Decimal operations; fraction-decimal-percent conversions; simple percentage of quantities.

## HCF-LCM and tests
In Class 5, students learn to find the HCF and LCM of two numbers using the listing method. The HCF is the biggest number that divides both numbers without leaving a remainder, like for 12 and 18, the HCF is 6. The LCM is the smallest number that both numbers can divide into without leaving a remainder, such as for 4 and 6, the LCM is 12. They also solve simple word problems to decide whether to use HCF or LCM, like sharing pencils equally or finding the earliest time two bells ring together. These help children understand grouping and sharing in real life.

Prime factorisation method; applications: smallest number divisible by set; tile-and-run problems.

## Ratio and average
In Class 5, children learn about ratios, which compare two amounts using numbers, like the ratio of apples to bananas in a basket is 2:3. They also study averages, finding the middle number by adding all numbers and dividing by how many there are, such as finding the average of 5, 10, and 15 to get 10. Ratios are written in simplest form, like 4:8 becoming 1:2, while averages help understand a group of numbers together. They practice real-life examples, like sharing sweets in a ratio or calculating the average temperature of a week. These ideas help solve everyday problems with numbers.

Ratio as comparison; share in a ratio (simple); mean of a data set.

## Geometry and measurement
In Class 5, students learn to recognise and draw different shapes like squares, rectangles, circles, and triangles, and count their sides and corners. They measure lengths using centimetres and metres, weights using grams and kilograms, and volumes using litres. The class covers calculating the area of squares and rectangles by counting square units or multiplying sides, and the perimeter by adding all sides together. They also learn to tell time accurately using clocks and understand the relationship between hours, minutes, and seconds. Simple concepts of symmetry are introduced, where children find lines that divide shapes into two matching halves.

Angle pairs and constructions (copy, bisect); triangles-quadrilaterals properties; circle parts; perimeter-area of rectangles-compound shapes; volume by unit cubes.

## Data handling and patterns
In Class 5, children learn to collect information and arrange it in tables, lists, or tally marks. They study simple bar graphs to show data using pictures or blocks, making it easy to compare numbers. Children also spot patterns in numbers, shapes, or events, like repeating colours or growing sequences. They use these ideas to solve fun problems, such as finding the most popular fruit from a survey or completing number puzzles. The topic helps them read and understand information in a simple and organised way.

Pictograph-bar-line reading; number patterns and rules; magic triangles.
