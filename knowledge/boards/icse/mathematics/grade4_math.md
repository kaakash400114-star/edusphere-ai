# ICSE Class 4 Mathematics - CISCE approach

## Numbers to 1,00,000 and Roman numerals to 100
In this topic, children learn to read, write, and compare numbers up to one lakh (1,00,000). They understand place values, identifying the ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten thousands, and lakhs places with examples like 23,456. They also learn to form numbers using expanded form, such as 50,000 + 3,000 + 400 + 50 + 6 for 53,456. Additionally, children are introduced to Roman numerals up to 100, learning symbols like I (1), V (5), X (10), L (50), and C (100), and simple rules like repeating a numeral to add values (II = 2) or placing a smaller numeral before a larger one to subtract (IV = 4).

Place value; compare-order-round; Roman numerals with rules (no repeat beyond three).

## Four operations mastery
In Class 4, children learn to add, subtract, multiply, and divide big numbers using place value. They use the column method for addition and subtraction, carrying over or borrowing when needed, like solving 45 + 27 = 72. For multiplication, they multiply two-digit numbers by one-digit numbers, such as 12 × 3 = 36, using tables. Division introduces dividing two-digit numbers by one-digit, like 24 ÷ 3 = 8, with or without remainders. They also solve simple word problems using all four operations to find answers.

Large-number add-subtract; 3-digit × 2-digit; long division by 2-digits; multi-step mixed problems; estimation before solving.

## Factors, multiples, tests of divisibility
In Class 4, we learn about factors and multiples of numbers. Factors are numbers that divide another number exactly, like 1, 2, 4, and 8 are factors of 8. Multiples are numbers you get when you multiply a number by counting numbers, such as 3, 6, 9, and 12 are multiples of 3. We also learn simple divisibility rules: a number is divisible by 2 if it ends in 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8, and by 5 if it ends in 0 or 5. For example, 20 is divisible by 2 and 5 because it ends in 0. These rules help us solve problems quickly and easily.

Prime-composite; HCF-LCM by listing; divisibility by 2, 3, 5, 9, 10; factor trees.

## Fractions and decimals side by side
In Class 4, children learn to compare and write fractions like ½ and ¼, understanding that smaller pieces mean bigger fractions. They also see decimals as another way to write parts of a whole, like 0.5 for half. Kids practice converting simple fractions to decimals, such as turning ¼ into 0.25, and learn to arrange both fractions and decimals in order, like ½, 0.3, and ¼. They use everyday examples, such as dividing a chocolate bar into equal parts, to make learning easy and fun. The focus is on helping children see how fractions and decimals are both used to show smaller numbers.

Like-unlike compare; add-subtract like fractions; tenths as decimals; money-length decimals.

## Metric system and money
Children learn about the metric system for measuring length, weight, and capacity. They use units like metres, centimetres, kilograms, grams, litres, and millilitres. They learn to convert between these units, like 100 centimetres make 1 metre, and 1000 grams make 1 kilogram. For money, they learn about Indian currency coins and notes, like ₹1, ₹5, ₹10, ₹20, ₹50, and ₹100. They practice adding and subtracting different amounts of money, like ₹25 and ₹10 to make ₹35.

Unit conversions across length-mass-capacity; unit-price shopping problems.

## Time, speed, distance (intro)
In Class 4, students learn about time, speed, and distance through simple, everyday situations. They understand that speed tells us how fast something moves, like a car going 60 kilometres in one hour. The basic rule is that when we know how fast something is going and for how long, we can find out how far it has travelled. For example, if a train moves at 40 km per hour for 2 hours, it covers 80 kilometres. They also practice reading time on clocks and calculating how long it takes to go from one place to another using these ideas.

Read 24-hour time; distance-time simple cases; timetables.

## Geometry: angles and symmetry
In Grade 4, children learn about angles and symmetry. An angle is formed where two lines meet, like the corner of a book. They identify right angles, which look like the letter ‘L’, and compare angles to see if they are bigger or smaller. Symmetry means one side looks like a mirror image of the other, like a butterfly’s wings or a square folded in half. Children find lines of symmetry in shapes, such as cutting a rectangle into two equal halves. They also complete symmetrical pictures by drawing the missing part to make both sides match.

Measure-draw angles with protractor; types; lines of symmetry; grid pictures.

## Perimeter-area-data
In Grade 4 Mathematics, children learn about perimeter by finding the total distance around shapes like squares and rectangles, using simple addition or the formula 2 × (length + breadth) for rectangles. For area, they count squares inside a shape or use the formula length × breadth for rectangles to measure the space covered. They also practice reading and interpreting data from pictographs and bar graphs, where each picture or bar represents a number. For example, a pictograph of fruits might show 3 apples with each apple picture standing for 2 real apples. These lessons help children understand shapes, measurement, and how to organise information clearly.

Rectilinear perimeter; area by squares; bar graphs with scales.
