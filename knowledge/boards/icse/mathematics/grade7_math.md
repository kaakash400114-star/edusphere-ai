# ICSE Class 7 Mathematics - CISCE

## Number system
In the Number System topic, students learn about different types of numbers like natural numbers, whole numbers, integers, and fractions. They understand how to compare and order these numbers using symbols like greater than (>), less than (<), and equals (=). For example, they learn that 5 is greater than 3, but -2 is less than 1. Students also practice adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing these numbers using simple rules, like carrying over in addition or borrowing in subtraction. They explore place value up to crores to read and write large numbers correctly, such as 12,34,56,789. Fractions are introduced by learning to find equivalent fractions and simplify them, like turning 4/8 into 1/2.

Integers-rational operations; recurring decimals; powers-exponents laws; estimation-percent applications (profit-loss-simple interest).

## Algebra
In Grade 7, we learn to use letters like *a*, *b*, and *x* to stand for numbers in sums. We write simple algebraic expressions, such as *3x + 5* or *2(a - 1)*, which help us solve problems easily. We also find the value of these expressions when we know what the letters stand for—for example, if *x = 2*, then *3x + 5 = 11*. Finally, we solve simple equations like *x + 3 = 7* by finding the number that makes the equation true. These rules help us think logically and solve real-life problems with numbers.

Algebraic expressions add-subtract-multiply (binomials); identities (a±b)^2, a^2−b^2; linear equations two-step; setting-up from words.

## Ratio-proportion-unitary
In this topic, children learn about ratios, which compare two quantities using the same units, like saying the ratio of apples to oranges is 3:2. They understand proportions, where two ratios are equal, for example, 2:3 is the same as 4:6. Students solve problems using the unitary method, finding the value of one unit first to then calculate the value of more units, like finding the cost of 5 pencils if 2 pencils cost ₹20. They also apply these concepts to real-life situations, such as dividing money or sharing sweets in a given ratio. By practicing these methods, children build strong skills for comparing and solving everyday math problems.

Direct-inverse proportion cases; speed-time-distance; percentage rise-fall problems.

## Geometry: triangles and constructions
In Class 7, you learn about triangles and their parts, like sides, angles, and vertices. You study how to classify triangles based on their sides (scalene, isosceles, equilateral) and angles (acute, obtuse, right-angled). You also learn important rules, such as the sum of angles in a triangle always being 180 degrees. Using this, you can find the missing angle in a triangle, like if two angles are 60° and 50°, the third angle must be 70°. Finally, you practice drawing triangles using a ruler, compass, and protractor, like making a right-angled triangle with sides 3 cm, 4 cm, and 5 cm.

Angle-sum-exterior; congruence criteria with proofs (SSS-SAS-ASA-RHS); isosceles-equilateral properties; constructions: triangles with SSS-SAS-ASA data.

## Mensuration
In mensuration, you learn to find the area and perimeter of simple shapes like rectangles and squares. The perimeter is the total length around a shape, found by adding all the sides. The area is the space inside a shape, calculated by multiplying length and breadth for rectangles. You also find the area of triangles by using the formula half the base times the height. For circles, you learn to calculate the circumference using the formula 2 times pi times the radius. These help you measure real things like playgrounds and classroom floors.

Circumference-area of circles; area of parallelogram-triangle; surface area-volume of cubes-cuboids; unit conversions.

## Statistics and data
In Class 7, you learn to collect and arrange numbers using tally marks and tables to show information clearly. You will make simple bar graphs where each bar's height matches the number it represents, like showing how many students like different fruits. The average or mean is found by adding all numbers together and dividing by how many there are, such as finding the average marks in a test. You also learn about the mode, which is the number that appears most often in a group, like the most common shoe size in your class. All these help us understand and share data easily.

Mean-median-mode from data; bar-double bar graphs; chance experiments-fairness talk.
