## Continuity and Differentiability
A function is continuous at a point if its limit exists at that point and is equal to its function value. Differentiability implies continuity. A function is differentiable if its derivative exists. The derivative of a function f(x) at x=a is given by the limit as h approaches zero of [f(a+h)-f(a)]/h. For example, the derivative of f(x) = 3x² + 2x is f'(x) = 6x + 2. The chain rule is used to differentiate composite functions, such as sin(2x), whose derivative is 2cos(2x).

## Applications of Derivatives
Derivatives are used to determine the rate of change of quantities. The first derivative test finds maxima and minima by checking the sign change of f'(x). The second derivative test uses f''(x): if negative, the point is a maximum; if positive, it is a minimum. For instance, for the function f(x) = x³ - 6x² + 9x + 15, setting f'(x) = 3x² - 12x + 9 = 0 gives critical points at x=1 and x=3, which can be classified as a local maximum and minimum respectively.

## Integrals
Integration is the inverse process of differentiation. The indefinite integral of a function f(x) is ∫f(x)dx = F(x) + C, where F'(x) = f(x) and C is the constant of integration. Basic integrals include ∫xⁿdx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1) and ∫sin(ax)dx = -cos(ax)/a. Definite integrals, ∫ₐᵇ f(x)dx, calculate the net area under the curve f(x) from x=a to x=b. The value is F(b) - F(a), where F is the antiderivative. For example, ∫₀² x dx = [x²/2]₀² = 2.

## Differential Equations
A differential equation relates a function with its derivatives. The order is the highest derivative present. A first-order equation is of the form dy/dx = f(x,y). Variables are separable if the equation can be written as g(y)dy = f(x)dx. For example, to solve dy/dx = 2x/y, we separate to y dy = 2x dx, integrate both sides to get y²/2 = x² + C, leading to the solution y² = 2x² + k.

## Probability
Probability is the measure of the likelihood of an event, from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain). For two mutually exclusive events A and B, P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B). For independent events A and B, P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). The conditional probability of A given B is P(A|B) = P(A and B) / P(B). A standard deck of 52 cards can be used to find the probability of drawing a red card, which is 26/52 = 1/2.

## Three-Dimensional Geometry
In 3D space, a point is defined by coordinates (x, y, z). The distance between two points (x₁, y₁, z₁) and (x₂, y₂, z₂) is √[(x₂-x₁)² + (y₂-y₁)² + (z₂-z₁)²]. The direction ratios (a, b, c) of a line define its direction. The equation of a plane can be written as ax + by + cz + d = 0. For example, the plane passing through (1,0,0), (0,1,0), and (0,0,1) has the equation x + y + z = 1.
