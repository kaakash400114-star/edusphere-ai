# ICSE Class 12 Mathematics - CISCE

## Algebra and calculus core
In Class 12, students master advanced algebra by learning to solve complex equations using matrices and determinants, including properties and their application to systems of linear equations. They explore calculus by differentiating and integrating functions, using rules like the product, quotient, and chain rule, while studying applications such as finding maxima and minima. Key concepts include limits, continuity, and differentiability, with examples like determining the slope of a curve or calculating areas under curves using definite integrals. Students also work with logarithmic and exponential functions, applying them to solve problems related to growth and decay. The integration part covers standard formulas and methods like substitution and partial fractions.

Matrices-determinants and systems; inverse trigonometric functions; continuity-differentiability; applications of derivatives: maxima-minima-tangents; integrals: indefinite-definite and properties; differential equations: variable separable-linear.

## Vectors-3D-probability
In this topic, you learn to solve 3D geometry problems using vectors, such as finding the angle between two lines or the equation of a plane. You study the scalar triple product to check if three vectors are coplanar and use the vector equation of a line to find points of intersection. For probability, you revise basic concepts like independent and dependent events, and learn to calculate probabilities using combinations and permutations. For example, you might find the probability of drawing two kings from a deck without replacement. The key formulas you use include the dot product, cross product, and the combination formula \( \binom{n}{r} \).

Vector algebra: dot-cross, applications; lines-planes in 3D: angles-distances; probability: conditional-Bayes; distributions: binomial idea; linear regression-correlation (statements).

## Applications
Students learn how to apply mathematical concepts to solve real-life problems, such as finding the area under a curve to calculate distance travelled or determining the volume of complex shapes like spheres and cones. They explore applications of trigonometry, like using the sine rule to find unknown sides of triangles in navigation problems, and derive formulas for curved surface areas and volumes of cylinders and frustums. The topic also includes probability, where they calculate chances of events like drawing coloured balls from a bag, and statistics, where they interpret data using measures of central tendency such as mean, median, and mode. Practical examples include calculating profit and loss, compound interest, and analysing graphs to understand trends in everyday situations.

Linear programming problems: feasible regions, corner-point optimisation; word-problem modelling across all chapters.
