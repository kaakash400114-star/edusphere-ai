# ICSE Class 6 Mathematics - CISCE

## Numbers and numeration
Students learn to read, write, and compare numbers up to 10 lakhs, understanding place value from ones to ten lakhs. They study Indian and international number systems, like writing 5,00,000 as five lakhs in Indian form and 500,000 in the international system. Children learn to form numbers using expanded notation, such as 3,45,020 = 3 lakhs + 4 ten thousands + 5 thousands + 0 hundreds + 2 tens + 0 ones. They also practice rounding numbers to the nearest ten, hundred, or thousand, like rounding 45,678 to the nearest hundred to get 45,700. Simple rules for comparing numbers—greater than, less than, and equal to—are reinforced using real-life examples like money or quantities.

Integers on number line; prime factorisation; powers-indices; estimation; rounding.

## Arithmetic operations and applications
In Grade 6 Mathematics, you learn to add, subtract, multiply, and divide whole numbers and simple fractions with correct steps. You also understand place value to solve big sums and use words like 'sum', 'difference', and 'product' when talking about answers. For example, 45 + 23 is a sum, and 100 ÷ 5 gives a quotient of 20. You practice solving word problems using these operations in real-life situations like sharing sweets or counting money. Learning these skills helps you build a strong base for more complex maths later.

Four operations on integers-fractions-decimals; simplification with brackets (BODMAS); unitary method; percentage as fraction of 100.

## Ratio, proportion, speed
In Grade 6 Mathematics, students learn about ratios to compare two quantities, like saying there are 3 apples for every 2 oranges. They understand that a ratio can be written as 3:2 or as a fraction 3/2. Proportion is taught as the equality of two ratios, for example, if 2 pens cost ₹10, then 4 pens will cost ₹20. Speed is introduced as the distance covered in a given time, using the formula speed = distance/time, such as a car travelling 60 km in 2 hours has a speed of 30 km/h. Students practice solving simple problems to find missing values in ratios and proportions.

Simplify ratios; direct proportion; speed-distance-time basics; average (mean).

## Algebra fundamentals
In Class 6, we learn about algebra fundamentals by using letters like a, b, c to stand for numbers. We understand simple expressions such as a + 5 or 3 × b, where letters can be any number. We also solve easy equations like x + 2 = 8 to find the value of x by doing the opposite operation. We use the idea of balancing both sides of an equation to keep it equal, just like a seesaw. For example, in 4y = 12, we divide both sides by 4 to find y = 3.

Constants-variables-terms; like-unlike; add-subtract expressions; simple equations one-step.

## Geometry fundamentals
In Class 6 Geometry, children learn about points, lines, and basic shapes like circles, triangles, and squares. They understand how to measure the perimeter of shapes by adding up all their sides, for example, finding the perimeter of a square by adding its four equal sides. Students also study the area of rectangles by multiplying their length and breadth, like counting how many small squares fit inside. They learn to draw and recognise angles, such as right angles, which look like the corner of a book. Basic geometry helps us understand shapes all around us, like a round pizza or a triangular sandwich.

Lines-angles pairs; triangles: angle-sum, types; congruence idea (SSS-SAS-ASA-RHS recognition); constructions: copy-bisect-60°-90°.

## Mensuration and data
In Class 6, children learn to measure the area and perimeter of simple shapes like squares and rectangles using formulas—add all sides for perimeter and multiply length by breadth for area. They also explore data by reading and creating tally marks, bar graphs, and pictographs to count and show information, like favourite fruits or pets. These skills help them understand real-life measurements and organise numbers into clear charts. For example, finding the area of a book cover or recording weather data in a graph makes learning fun and useful.

Perimeter-area of rectilinear figures; volume of cuboids; collection-organisation of data; bar graphs.
