# ICSE Class 11 Mathematics - CISCE

## Sets, relations, functions
In this topic, you will learn about sets, which are well-defined collections of objects like numbers or letters, and how to represent them using roster or set-builder methods. You will study relations, which show how elements from two sets are connected, and functions, which are special relations where every input has exactly one output, like a machine that changes a number. For example, the function f(x) = 2x takes a number x and gives back twice its value. You will also explore different types of functions, such as one-to-one, onto, and constant functions, and practice solving problems using Venn diagrams to understand how sets overlap. The key is to recognise patterns and rules that govern how elements interact within these structures.

Venn problems; domain-range; function types and composition; inverse functions (ideas).

## Trigonometry
In trigonometry, students learn about the three main ratios—sine, cosine, and tangent—which are used to find the unknown sides or angles of a right-angled triangle. They study these ratios for angles of 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, and 90° and use them to solve simple problems, like finding the height of a building using shadow lengths. They also learn the trigonometric identities, such as sin²θ + cos²θ = 1, which help in simplifying expressions. Later, they explore the concepts of trigonometric functions and their graphs, like the sine wave, and how these functions repeat every 360°. Finally, they practice solving real-life problems using these rules, such as calculating distances or heights in everyday situations.

Identities and equations; multiple-substitution angles; inverse trigonometric functions properties; sine-cosine rule applications.

## Algebra essentials
In algebra essentials, you learn the rules for solving linear equations with one variable, like finding the value of x in 2x + 5 = 15. You work with algebraic expressions, including adding, subtracting, and multiplying terms, such as simplifying 3x + 2x - 4. Factoring simple quadratic expressions, like x² + 5x + 6 into (x + 2)(x + 3), is also covered. The topic includes using formulas to solve problems, such as the area of a rectangle, length × breadth. You practise verifying solutions by substituting values back into the equation.

Quadratic theory (discriminant-roots relations); complex numbers-argand; sequences-series AP-GP-AM-GM; permutations-combinations-binomial theorem; logarithms.

## Coordinate geometry, calculus entry
In this part, you learn how to find the distance between two points on a graph and the middle point, like finding the middle of a line joining (1,2) and (3,4). You also study straight lines, learning how to write their equations using different forms, such as the slope form (y = mx + c), to understand their steepness and direction. Next, you start calculus by learning what a derivative is, which is the slope of a curve at any point, and how to find it for simple functions like x² or 3x. For example, the derivative of x² is 2x. You practise finding slopes and rates of change using basic rules of differentiation.

Straight lines-circle equations; limits-intuitive continuity; derivatives: rules, applications to tangents-rates.

## Statistics-probability
In Class 11, you will learn about statistics and probability to understand data and chance. For statistics, you will study how to find the mean, median, and mode of data, and also how to calculate the range to see how spread out the numbers are. For probability, you will learn simple rules like the probability of an event happening is always between 0 and 1, and you can find it by dividing the number of good outcomes by the total number of possible outcomes. For example, the chance of picking a red marble from a box with 2 red and 3 blue marbles is 2 out of 5. You will also learn about mutually exclusive events, where two events cannot happen at the same time.

Measures of central tendency-dispersion; probability: addition-multiplication laws, conditional probability intro.
