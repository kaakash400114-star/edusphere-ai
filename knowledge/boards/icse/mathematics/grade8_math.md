# ICSE Class 8 Mathematics - CISCE

## Rational and irrational numbers
In Class 8, you learn about rational numbers, which are numbers that can be written as fractions like ½ or -¾, and their decimal form either ends or repeats, such as 0.5 or 0.333. Irrational numbers, on the other hand, cannot be written as fractions and have decimal digits that never end or repeat, like the square root of 2 or π. You will practice identifying whether a number is rational or irrational and understand that rational numbers can be placed on a number line, while irrational numbers fill in the gaps between them. The rules for adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing rational numbers are the same as for fractions, and you will solve problems using these operations. For example, ⅓ + ⅖ = 11/15, but you cannot simplify √2 + √3 into a single fraction.

Rational ops recap; irrational idea and π; real numbers; exponents including negative; standard form.

## Arithmetic: commercial maths
In this topic, children learn to calculate simple interest using the formula I = P × R × T ÷ 100, where P is the principal amount, R is the rate of interest, and T is the time in years. They practice finding profit and loss by calculating the difference between the selling price and cost price of items, such as a toy bought for ₹150 and sold for ₹200, making a profit of ₹50. Students also understand how to compute discounts on marked prices, like a 10% discount on a ₹500 shirt reducing the price to ₹450. Additionally, they learn to prepare bills for purchases, including the cost of goods and any applicable taxes. These exercises help them apply maths to real-life shopping and money management situations.

Profit-loss-discount percent; simple-compound interest (formula use); GST idea; time-work and pipes (simple cases).

## Algebra and factorisation
In Class 8, you learn to work with algebraic expressions and their rules, like adding and subtracting terms, and finding their products. You will practise factorisation, which means breaking down big expressions into smaller, simpler parts, like writing 2x + 4 as 2(x + 2). You also learn about special formulas, such as (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b², and use them to solve problems. For example, you will learn to factorise expressions like x² - 9 into (x + 3)(x - 3) using the difference of squares. These skills help you solve equations and simplify complex numbers and expressions.

Polynomials; multiplication identities extended; factorise: common-grouping-identities-quadratic-trinomials; algebraic division by monomial-binomial.

## Equations and graphs
In this topic, students learn to draw graphs of linear equations like x + y = 5 on a graph sheet. They practice finding points by choosing values for x and solving for y, like when x=2, y=3. They also learn to find the solution of two equations by seeing where their graphs cross, like x=1 and y=2 at point (1,2). Simple word problems about ages or numbers are solved by making equations first. Finally, they learn to match real situations, like buying fruits, with their correct graph lines.

Linear equations in one variable (fractional); simultaneous linear equations (substitution-elimination); graphical solution; linear graph reading.

## Geometry: polygons, quadrilaterals, constructions
In this topic, children learn about different shapes like triangles, squares, and rectangles, which are all polygons. They understand the properties of quadrilaterals, such as rectangles having four right angles and opposite sides equal, while squares have all sides equal and four right angles. Students also learn to draw these shapes using a compass and ruler, like making a perfect square or bisecting an angle to find its middle. They explore the sum of angles in triangles (180 degrees) and quadrilaterals (360 degrees), helping them solve problems like finding missing angles. For example, if a triangle has angles 60° and 50°, the third angle must be 70°.

Angle-sum of polygons; parallelogram properties with reasons; constructions: quadrilaterals from data; symmetry recap.

## Mensuration III
In Class 8 Mensuration III, you learn to find the volume and surface area of important 3D shapes like cuboids, cubes, cylinders, and cones. The formulas are simple: for a cuboid, volume is length × breadth × height, and surface area is 2(lb + bh + hl). For a cylinder, volume is πr²h and curved surface area is 2πrh. You also study cones, where volume is (1/3)πr²h and slant height helps find the total surface area. These formulas help you solve real-life problems, like finding the amount of water a tank can hold or the paint needed to cover a box.

Area of trapezium-polygons (splitting); circle area-circumference problems; surface area-volume: cylinder-cone-sphere (formula use).

## Data handling and probability intro
In this topic, you learn to collect information, like your friends' favourite fruits, and group it using tally marks to make charts. You will also understand bar graphs, where bars show numbers, and learn to read the scale to find the correct value. The mean, or average, is found by adding all numbers and dividing by how many there are, for example, finding the average of 5, 10, and 15. You will also get introduced to chance with words like 'impossible', 'sure', and 'maybe', such as guessing if a coin will land on heads. These ideas help us understand information and make simple predictions.

Grouped data mean; histograms-frequency polygons; empirical probability from experiments.
