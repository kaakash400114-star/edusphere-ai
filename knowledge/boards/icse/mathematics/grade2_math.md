# ICSE Class 2 Mathematics - CISCE approach

## Numbers to 999: place value and comparison
In Class 2, children learn to read and write numbers up to 999 by understanding their place value. They identify hundreds, tens, and ones in a number, like 345 having 3 hundreds, 4 tens, and 5 ones. Children compare numbers to find which is bigger or smaller using the symbols > (greater than), < (less than), and = (equal to). For example, 500 is greater than 400, and 234 is less than 345. They also learn to arrange numbers in order from smallest to biggest or biggest to smallest.

Hundreds-tens-ones; expanded form (345 = 300 + 40 + 5); greater-smaller; number names.

## Addition-subtraction with carrying-borrowing to 999
In Class 2, children learn to add and subtract numbers up to 999, using carrying over for addition and borrowing for subtraction. When adding two-digit or three-digit numbers, they carry over the extra tens to the next place value. For example, in 45 + 37, they add 5 + 7 to get 12, write 2 in the units place, and carry over 1 to the tens place. Similarly, in subtraction, if the top number is smaller, they borrow from the next place value, like in 72 – 38, where they borrow 1 from the tens to make 12 in the units place. These methods help them solve problems accurately with bigger numbers.

Column algorithms with verification by reverse operation; missing-number puzzles; two-step stories.

## Multiplication as repeated addition; tables 2-10
In Class 2, children learn that multiplication is like adding the same number many times. For example, 3 × 4 means adding 3 four times (3 + 3 + 3 + 3) to get 12. They practice the multiplication tables from 2 to 10, memorising facts like 2 × 5 = 10 or 7 × 3 = 21. Using pictures or objects, they see how groups of equal amounts work, like 4 groups of 2 apples each (4 × 2 = 8). This helps them solve simple problems quickly without counting one by one.

Arrays and jumps; multiply 2-digit by 1-digit; word stories.

## Division as equal sharing and grouping
Children learn to divide by sharing things equally into groups or making equal groups. They understand division as the opposite of multiplication, splitting objects fairly. For example, 12 sweets shared equally among 3 children means each gets 4 sweets. They also learn to group items, like putting 12 pencils into groups of 4, which gives 3 groups. No objects are left over in fair division at this level. They use simple division facts like 8 ÷ 2 = 4 or 10 ÷ 5 = 2 to solve problems.

Share equally; relate to multiplication facts; remainders noticed informally.

## Fractions: half, quarter, three-quarters
In Class 2, children learn about fractions like half (½), quarter (¼), and three-quarters (¾). They understand that a fraction divides a whole into equal parts, where half means one of two equal parts, quarter means one of four equal parts, and three-quarters means three of four equal parts. For example, a chocolate bar split into two equal pieces shows two halves, while a pizza divided into four equal pieces has four quarters. Children practise colouring or circling these fractions in shapes like circles or rectangles to see the parts clearly. They also learn to write these fractions as numbers and use them in simple problems, like sharing fruits or toys equally.

Fold-and-colour paper halves/quarters; fraction of a collection (1/2 of 8 candles).

## Money, measurement, time
Children learn to identify and count Indian coins like ₹1, ₹2, ₹5, and ₹10, and add them to find the total amount. They measure length using non-standard units such as hand spans or paper clips and compare which object is longer or shorter. Time is introduced by reading the hour and minute hands on a clock face, like 3:00 or 5:30, and understanding morning, afternoon, and evening. They also learn to count days of the week and months of the year in order, such as Monday to Sunday and January to December. Simple word problems involve buying items with coins and finding the remaining money.

Rupee-paise sums to ₹100; metre-centimetre with tape; kilogram with balance; calendar reading, quarter-hour clock.

## Geometry, patterns, data
In Class 2, children learn to identify and name simple 2D shapes like circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles. They explore patterns by repeating shapes, colours, or numbers, such as circle-square-circle-square. Using tally marks, children collect and count small objects to understand basic data handling. They sort objects based on one attribute, like size or colour, and create simple picture graphs. Through hands-on activities, they compare quantities and recognise basic spatial relationships between shapes.

2D-3D sorting; symmetry with mirrors; growing patterns; simple pictograms.
