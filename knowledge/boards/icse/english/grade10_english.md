# ICSE Class 10 English - CISCE (Language + Literature)

## Language paper: grammar precision
In Class 10, students learn to use grammar rules for precise speaking and writing, including correct subject-verb agreement and tense consistency. They master the use of conjunctions like 'because', 'although', and 'unless' to join ideas smoothly and avoid run-on sentences. Students also learn to identify and use active and passive voice correctly, such as changing "The boy kicks the ball" to "The ball is kicked by the boy". Additionally, they practise using modal verbs like 'must', 'should', and 'can' to express necessity, advice, or ability in their writing. These skills help them express their thoughts clearly and accurately.

All tenses in complex narration; transformation of sentences (all boards-style patterns); phrasal verbs; structures (too-to, so-that, no sooner).

## Language paper: comprehension and summary
In the ICSE English Language Arts Class 10 comprehension and summary section, students learn to read passages carefully and answer factual, inferential, and vocabulary-based questions to show their understanding. For writing a summary, they must identify the main idea, remove unimportant details, and express it in their own words in about one-third of the original length. They follow rules like using simple language, keeping the facts right, and not adding new information, for example, turning a long story about a farmer into a short, clear paragraph that tells only the key events. This helps them become better readers and writers who can pick out important information and write it neatly.

Unseen prose: inference-vocabulary; precise summary-writing to word limit; note-taking.

## Writing: composition mastery
Students learn to write well-structured compositions like stories, essays, and reports with clear beginnings, middles, and endings. They practice using correct grammar, punctuation, and varied sentence structures to make their writing interesting and easy to read. For example, in a story, they build suspense with short sentences and describe places with rich details. They also learn to plan their work first by jotting down ideas in order before writing the full piece. Spelling and neat handwriting remain important to show their work clearly.

Argumentative-descriptive-narrative-reflective essays (400-450 words); picture composition; beginning-middle-end craft.

## Writing: letters
In Class 10, students learn to write formal and informal letters with correct structure and purpose, including addresses, dates, and appropriate salutations. Formal letters use respectful language for official requests, such as a letter to the principal seeking permission, while informal letters are friendly and casual, like writing to a cousin about holidays. Letters must have a clear subject line, a well-organised body with 3-4 short paragraphs, and a polite closing with a signature. Pupils practice writing both business letters, such as a complaint to a shop, and personal letters, ensuring correct punctuation and grammar throughout. The focus is on clarity, coherence, and adapting the tone to the audience and situation.

Formal: editor-principal-officials; informal: friendly registers; layouts marked per ICSE convention.

## Literature: prose-drama
In Class 10 ICSE English, students learn to analyse prose and drama texts by understanding their structures, like plot, character, setting and theme. They study how writers use language devices such as similes, metaphors and dialogue to build stories and develop characters. For example, in a drama, they examine how scenes are organised to show conflict and emotion, while in prose, they trace how a plot builds from introduction to climax and resolution. Students also practise writing summaries and answering questions on character development and themes. These skills help them appreciate literature and express their ideas clearly.

Text-based extracts; character sketches with evidence; themes-plot-conflict; author-technique questions.

## Literature: poetry
In Class 10, students learn to analyse poetry by understanding its key elements like rhyme, rhythm, and imagery, which help them grasp the poet’s message. They explore different poetic structures such as sonnets, haikus, and ballads, and how each form shapes the meaning of a poem. For example, they might study a sonnet’s 14-line structure with a rhyming scheme to see how it builds emotion. Students also identify poetic devices like metaphors, similes, and personification, which make poems vivid and engaging. By practising these concepts, they learn to appreciate the beauty and craft behind the words of a poem.

Central idea; devices: metaphor-simile-personification-irony-alliteration; tone-mood shifts; title significance; compare two poems.

## Examination craft
In this topic, students learn to plan their time wisely before exams, reading questions carefully and underlining keywords. They understand how to structure answers properly, using full sentences with correct grammar and punctuation, even in shorter questions. For longer answers like essays or letters, they learn to organise their thoughts with a clear introduction, middle part, and conclusion. They also practice writing neatly and leaving space between answers for the teacher to mark easily. Students are taught to check their answers before finishing to catch any spelling mistakes or missing parts.

Time management across Language-Literature papers; presentation: neat paragraphing, quotes selection; revision cycles.
