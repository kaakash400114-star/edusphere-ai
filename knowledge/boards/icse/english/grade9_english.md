# ICSE Class 9 English - CISCE (Language + Literature)

## Grammar precision set
In Class 9, you learn to use correct grammar when speaking and writing. You study tenses like present perfect and past continuous to show when things happen. You also learn about modals such as can, should, and must to show ability or advice. Sentence structure includes identifying subjects and verbs and using correct punctuation like commas and semicolons. For example, you learn to write, "Rani has been studying since morning" instead of "Rani is studying since morning." This helps you express ideas clearly and correctly.

Tenses; sequence of tenses; active-passive across forms; reported speech all types; non-finites; modals; conditionals 1-2-3.

## Transformation and phrasal mastery
In Class 9, you will learn about transformation sentences, changing the form of a sentence without changing its meaning. For example, making a statement like “The boy is playing” into a question, “Is the boy playing?” You will also master phrasal verbs, which are a verb plus a word like “on” or “up” that change the verb’s meaning, like “look up” meaning to search for information. You will practise using phrasal verbs correctly in sentences, such as “She switched off the light.” This helps you understand how words work together to build clear sentences.

Degree transformations; combine-simple-compound-complex; phrasal verbs academic sets; error correction format.

## Comprehension skills
In Class 9 ICSE English, students learn to read carefully and understand the main idea and small details in a passage. They practise finding answers directly from the text and also think about what the writer means. Children learn to put ideas in order and see how the beginning and end of a story are connected. For example, they can explain why a character feels sad by looking at what happened in the story. They also learn new words from the text and use them in their own sentences.

Unseen prose-poetry; inference-tone-style; précis to one-third; vocabulary contextual.

## Composition: essays and letters
In Class 9, students learn to write formal and informal letters, understanding their correct formats like addresses, dates, and salutations. They practice writing clear essays with proper structure—introduction, body paragraphs, and conclusion—using simple and descriptive language. Students also focus on grammar, punctuation, and spelling to make their writing neat and easy to read. For example, they write a letter to their principal or a descriptive essay about their favourite place. The goal is to help students express their ideas confidently and correctly in different situations.

Expository-argumentative-descriptive-narrative essays (400-450 words); formal-informal letters with correct layout.

## Literature study method
Students learn to study literature by reading stories and poems carefully to understand the characters, feelings, and important messages. They learn to identify the beginning, middle, and end of a story, and to notice interesting words and phrases that writers use. They also practice answering questions about what they have read, explaining their own thoughts and ideas clearly. For example, after reading a poem, a student might write about what they think it is about and why they like it. This helps them become better readers and writers.

Prose-drama-poetry: theme-plot-character-style; note-making; quote-based answers; compare-contrast short pieces.

## Creative writing
In Class 9, students learn to write different types of creative pieces such as stories, poems, and descriptive paragraphs using their imagination. They study how to build interesting characters for stories, create vivid settings with details, and develop a clear beginning, middle, and end. For poems, they explore simple rhyming patterns and rhythm to make their words sound musical. Students also practice describing people, places, or things using strong words like ‘shimmering’ instead of ‘shining’ to make their writing lively. They learn to plan their ideas before writing to ensure their work makes sense and keeps the reader engaged.

Short story with strong opening; diary-journal voice; review-writing framework.
