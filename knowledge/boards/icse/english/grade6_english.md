# ICSE Class 6 English - CISCE

## Grammar core
In Class 6, we learn to use different types of words like nouns, verbs, and adjectives to build sentences. We also study how to make sentences more interesting by using adverbs and conjunctions like 'and' or 'but'. We practice changing sentences from singular to plural, for example, changing 'cat' to 'cats'. Another important rule is identifying subjects and predicates in sentences, such as in 'The dog barks loudly', where 'The dog' is the subject and 'barks loudly' is the predicate. We also learn to use correct punctuation like full stops, commas, and question marks to make our writing clear.

Tenses all simple-continuous; subject-verb agreement traps; irregular verb drills.

## Syntax building
In Class 6, students learn to build clear sentences by understanding how words fit together. They practice arranging subjects, verbs, and objects correctly, like writing "The dog chases the ball" instead of "The dog the ball chases". Children also learn to join two sentences using joining words like 'and' or 'but', for example, "Rita likes apples and oranges". They are introduced to describing words called adjectives, such as "the big blue kite", to make sentences more interesting. By following these simple rules, they can write sentences that make sense and are easy to read.

Phrases vs clauses; simple-compound sentences; punctuation: semicolon-intro, dash-intro.

## Vocabulary development
In Grade 6, students learn to identify and use new words from their reading materials and classroom discussions. They understand how to find word meanings using dictionaries and by looking for clues in the sentences around a difficult word, like guessing 'delicious' means very tasty from a sentence about food. Children practice grouping words into categories, such as fruits (mango, banana, orange) or actions (run, jump, play), to organise their knowledge. They also learn to form new words by adding prefixes and suffixes, like turning 'happy' into 'unhappy' or 'kind' into 'kindly'. Through these activities, students expand their vocabulary to express their ideas more clearly and confidently.

Word-formation: prefixes-suffixes; homophones-homonyms; one-word substitutions starters.

## Comprehension and response
In this topic, students learn to read stories and passages carefully, finding the main idea and important details. They answer simple questions about what they read using their own words. Children also practice identifying characters and the main events in a story. For example, after reading a short story, a student can tell the name of the main character and what happened at the beginning and end. They learn to write short answers that are clear and directly respond to the question asked. This helps them understand and talk about what they read.

Prose-poetry extracts; MCQ + short answers; justify with evidence lines.

## Composition
In Class 6, students learn to write clear and organised paragraphs on simple topics like their favourite game or a pet. They are taught to begin with a main idea sentence, followed by supporting details to explain it. Punctuation rules like using full stops, capital letters, and question marks are practised in sentences. For example, a student might write: "I like to play cricket. It is fun with my friends. We bat and ball together." Simple compositions also include writing friendly letters using proper salutations and closings such as "Dear Priya" and "From Rohan.

Guided essays (person-place-event); formal applications; story from hints; dialogue writing.

## Speaking-reading fluency
In Class 6, children learn to speak and read aloud smoothly, without long breaks or pauses, so their words flow like a river. They practice reading sentences with clear pronunciation, ensuring each word is spoken correctly, like saying "book" not "buk". They also learn to read at a steady pace, not too fast or too slow, so everyone can understand, for example, reading "The cat sat on the mat" slowly and clearly. Children are taught to use proper pauses at full stops and commas, like taking a small breath after "sat". This helps them share stories and ideas confidently, making their speech easy and enjoyable for others to listen to.

Read-aloud with pause-stress; recite poems; retell plots in own words.
