# ICSE Class 8 English - CISCE

## Grammar precision
In Class 8, you will learn about grammar precision, which means using words and sentences correctly. You will study parts of speech like nouns, verbs, and adjectives, and how to use them in sentences. You will also learn about tenses, such as simple present, past, and future, with examples like "She plays football" or "They played yesterday". Another important concept is subject-verb agreement, where the subject and verb match in number, for example, "The dog barks" and "The dogs bark". This helps you write and speak clearly and correctly.

All tenses in narration; conditionals type 1-2; non-finites: gerund-participle-infinitive uses; error-spotting drills.

## Transformation toolkit
In the Transformation toolkit, students learn to change words from one form to another, like turning nouns into verbs or adjectives into adverbs. For example, the noun “sun” becomes the verb “shine,” and the adjective “quick” turns into the adverb “quickly.” They also practice changing sentences from active to passive voice, such as “The dog chased the cat” becoming “The cat was chased by the dog.” Another important rule is adding prefixes and suffixes to change meanings, like turning “happy” into “unhappy” or “happiness.” These tools help students make their writing more interesting and clear.

Active-passive all forms; reported speech commands-questions-exclamations; degree changes; sentence combining.

## Vocabulary enrichment
In Grade 8, students learn to enhance their vocabulary by understanding word roots, prefixes, and suffixes, which helps them decode new words. For example, knowing that 'bio' means life helps understand 'biology' and 'biography'. They also explore synonyms and antonyms to build more precise language, learning words like 'happy' and 'sad' are opposites. Students are taught to identify and use collective nouns correctly, such as a 'team' of players or a 'flock' of birds. They also learn to differentiate between homophones, words that sound alike but have different meanings and spellings, like 'sea' and 'see'.

Figurative language: metaphor-simile-personification-hyperbole; word pairs and confusing sets (affect-effect); spelling rules.

## Comprehension: discursive-literary
Students learn to read and understand different types of texts like stories and descriptive passages. They will find the main idea of a paragraph and answer questions about details like who, what, where, and when. They will also learn to sequence events in the correct order and match meanings of words with their opposites. For example, in a story, they will put sentences in the right order and find words that mean the opposite of 'happy'. This helps them understand better and remember what they read.

Main idea-structure; tone-mood-emotive language; evaluation questions with justification.

## Composition excellence
In this topic, students learn to write clear and organised compositions like paragraphs, letters, and short stories. They practice using proper sentence structure, subject-verb agreement, and punctuation like full stops and commas. A key rule is the five-sentence paragraph structure: a topic sentence, three detail sentences, and a concluding sentence. For example, they write a friendly letter to a cousin with a greeting, main message, and closing. Students also focus on using descriptive words to make their writing interesting, like saying "the sun shone brightly" instead of "the sun was out".

Essays: expository-argumentative-descriptive outlines; formal letter-email packs; story crafting: plot-characters-climax; dialogue writing.

## Literature appreciation
Students learn to appreciate literature by reading stories and poems to understand feelings and ideas. They identify main characters, settings, and plots, and describe how events make them feel. For example, they can explain why a character in a story is brave or sad. They also find important messages or lessons in the text, like being kind or honest. Students use simple words to share what they like about a story or poem, and why it is interesting.

Prose-poetry-charts of devices; theme-character webs; compare texts; review a book-film.
