# ICSE Class 2 English - CISCE approach

## Phonics to fluency
Children learn to link letters to their sounds, such as 'a' for apple and 's' for sun. They blend these sounds to read simple words like 'cat' and 'dog'. They also learn to break down words into their sounds, helping with spelling. Rules like the silent 'e' making vowels long are introduced, as in 'cake'. Children practice reading short, simple sentences to build fluency and confidence.

Long vowels (cake, meet, kite); consonant blends and digraphs (br, pl, ch, th); word families.

## Nouns: naming people, places, things
In Class 2, we learn that nouns are special words that name people, places, and things around us. We can spot a noun by asking: "Who?" for people like *Mona* or *doctor*, "Where?" for places like *school* or *park*, and "What?" for things like *book* or *bench*. Nouns are the building blocks of sentences, helping us talk about our world clearly. We practice naming our family members like *father* and *sister*, favourite spots like *garden* and *market*, and everyday objects like *pencil* and *water bottle*. By understanding nouns, we become better storytellers and writers.

Common and special names with capitals; one-many with -s, -es.

## Verbs and simple sentences
In Class 2, we learn about action words called verbs. Verbs tell us what someone or something is doing, like "run", "jump", "eat", or "sleep". We make simple sentences by putting a subject (who or what) and a verb together. For example, "The dog barks" or "She reads a book". Sentences must make sense and have a capital letter at the start and a full stop at the end. We practice writing our own short sentences using these rules.

Present actions; am-is-are; has-have; make 5-word sentences correctly.

## Describing words and opposites
Children learn to use describing words, called adjectives, to tell us more about things. For example, we say “big ball” or “small doll” to show size. They also learn opposite words, like “hot” and “cold” or “tall” and “short”, to show differences. Adjectives help us make our sentences more clear and interesting, like saying “a red car” instead of just “a car”. By knowing opposites, children can compare things better, like a “heavy box” and a “light box”. This makes their speaking and writing more fun and easier to understand.

Colour-size-quality adjectives; pairs: tall-short, wet-dry, loud-quiet.

## Punctuation and sentence order
In Class 2, we learn to use full stops, question marks, and capital letters correctly. A full stop (.) ends a sentence that tells us something, like “The cat is black.” A question mark (?) is used for questions, such as “What is your name?” We always start a sentence with a capital letter, even small words like “The” or “And.” We also learn to arrange words in the right order to make a meaningful sentence, like “I play with my ball.” By practicing these rules, we can write clearly and read better.

Capital letter, full stop, question mark; jumbled-sentence repair.

## Reading and picture composition
In Class 2, children learn to read simple sentences with short words and full stops. They practice reading aloud with clear sounds and understand that pictures match words to tell a story. For picture composition, they describe what they see in a picture using words like ‘big’, ‘small’, ‘happy’, and ‘sad’, and write short sentences about it. They learn to arrange their thoughts in a beginning, middle, and end, like writing about a playing park. For example, a child might write, “I see a swing. It is red. I like it.” This helps them connect pictures to words and form basic sentences.

Read a 10-line story; answer in full sentences; write 4 sentences about a picture.
