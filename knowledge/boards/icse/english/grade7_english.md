# ICSE Class 7 English - CISCE

## Grammar mastery track
Students learn to use correct parts of speech like nouns, pronouns, verbs, and adjectives in sentences. They practice changing singular nouns to plural nouns and forming comparative and superlative adjectives, such as "tall" to "taller" or "tallest". Punctuation rules like using full stops, commas, and question marks are reinforced with simple examples. Sentence structures include writing simple and compound sentences, and correcting common errors like subject-verb agreement. For instance, they learn to say "She runs fast" instead of "She run fast".

Perfect tenses; passive voice across tenses; reported speech statements-questions; modals deduction (must have, might have).

## Clause work
In Class 7, we learn to find and identify different kinds of clauses in sentences. A clause is a group of words that has a subject and a verb. We learn to spot main clauses, which can make a complete sentence on their own, like "The dog barked." We also learn to find subordinate clauses, which cannot stand alone and depend on the main clause, like "when the bell rang." We practise making our sentences longer and more interesting by joining main and subordinate clauses together using words like 'because,' 'if,' and 'that.'

Noun-adjective-adverb clauses identified and used; complex sentences with correct punctuation.

## Vocabulary and usage
In Class 7, students learn new words from their textbooks and stories to improve their vocabulary. They understand how to use synonyms and antonyms to make their writing more interesting, for example, using "happy" instead of "glad" or "sad" instead of "unhappy". Children also learn about compound words like "butterfly" and "sunscreen", which are made by joining two words. They practice using words in the right context, like knowing when to use "there" or "their". Finally, students are taught the meanings of prefixes such as "un-" and "re-" to help them understand new words, like "unlock" or "return".

Phrasal verbs in context; idiomatic expressions; collocations; formal-informal register shifts.

## Comprehension: literature and language
In Class 7, students learn to read and understand stories, poems, and plays by finding the main idea and details. They also identify characters, settings, and how feelings are shown through words. Students practise using new words in sentences and learn simple grammar rules like parts of speech and tenses. For example, they can find the verb in a sentence or change a word from present to past tense. They learn to answer questions about a text by looking for clues in the words and pictures.

Poetry: imagery-tone; prose: theme-character with quotes; comparison of two short texts.

## Composition suite
In Composition suite for Class 7, you learn to write different types of letters, such as formal letters to the principal and friendly letters to cousins, using correct formats like dates, addresses, and salutations. You practice writing descriptive paragraphs about places or people using sensory details like what you see, hear, and smell. You also learn to structure short stories with a clear beginning, middle, and end, including a problem and a simple solution. For example, you might write a story about finding a lost puppy and returning it to its happy owner. Finally, you learn to frame simple questions and answers for interviews, like asking a friend about their favourite hobby and writing down their replies.

Argumentative paragraph (for-against); story with twist ending; formal letter (complaint); picture composition.

## Listening-speaking
In Class 7, students learn to listen carefully to stories, poems, and instructions, paying attention to the main points and important details. They practice speaking in clear, complete sentences, sharing their ideas and opinions in class discussions. Children also learn to take turns talking and listening politely, like raising their hands before speaking. For example, they might describe their favourite book or give a short talk about their holiday. They practice using polite words such as “please” and “thank you” when talking to teachers and friends.

Note from a spoken passage; structured class debate turns; pronunciation of academic vocabulary.
