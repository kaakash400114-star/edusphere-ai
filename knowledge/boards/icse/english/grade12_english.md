# ICSE Grade 12 English

## The Victorian Novel: Realism and Social Critique
Students study the socio-economic conditions and moral codes of Victorian England through selected novels. They learn to identify realism, a literary approach depicting everyday life with meticulous detail and psychological depth. Key topics include the critique of class stratification, the role of women, and the impact of industrialisation. The focus is on how authors used the novel form as a vehicle for social commentary, employing complex characterisation and intricate plots to mirror the anxieties and values of the era.

## Indian Writing in English: Postcolonial Perspectives
This module explores the literary response to the colonial experience and its aftermath. Students analyse themes of identity, cultural hybridity, and the search for a national voice in works by authors like R.K. Narayan, Salman Rushdie, or Arundhati Roy. They learn to identify literary devices such as magic realism, allegory, and satire used to deconstruct colonial narratives and articulate the complex socio-political landscape of modern India, fostering an understanding of its rich and diverse literary heritage.

## The Elizabethan and Jacobean Drama
Students engage with the dramatic works of William Shakespeare and his contemporaries, analysing their enduring thematic and artistic significance. The curriculum covers the conventions of the Elizabethan theatre, including the soliloquy, dramatic irony, and blank verse. Students examine plays as reflections of Jacobean society, exploring themes of power, ambition, revenge, and human nature. They learn to interpret character motivation and the playwright’s use of language to create complex tragic and comic figures.

## The Romantic Poetry of William Wordsworth
This section focuses on the poetry of William Wordsworth as a cornerstone of the Romantic movement. Students learn the core tenets of Romanticism: a focus on nature as a source of spiritual and moral insight, the importance of emotion over reason, and the celebration of the 'common man'. They analyse specific poems to understand Wordsworth's concept of the 'spontaneous overflow of powerful feelings' and his use of simple language to convey profound meditations on beauty, memory, and the sublime.

## Modern Indian Poetry in English
Students explore the thematic and stylistic evolution of Indian poetry in English from the mid-20th century onwards. They analyse the works of poets like Nissim Ezekiel, A.K. Ramanujan, and Kamala Das, who grappled with issues of alienation, cultural dislocation, and the personal versus the political. The curriculum teaches the identification of modernist techniques, such as fragmentation and symbolism, and how they are employed to articulate a contemporary Indian experience, distinct from both its indigenous and Western literary traditions.

## The Short Story: Modern and Contemporary
This module analyses the concise yet powerful narrative form of the short story. Students study its structural elements—plot, characterisation, setting, point of view, and theme—in a selection of modern and contemporary stories. They learn to interpret the use of irony, symbolism, and ambiguity to create layered meaning and explore complex human situations. The focus is on understanding how brevity allows for a concentrated impact and a deep exploration of specific emotional or social truths.
