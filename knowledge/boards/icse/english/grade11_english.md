# ICSE Grade 11 English

## Drama: The Play within the Play
Students study Shakespeare's *A Midsummer Night's Dream* as a comedy of errors. They analyse the structure of the play-within-the-play, "Pyramus and Thisbe," to understand themes of illusion and reality. Key learning includes identifying dramatic irony and character foils. They must memorize and perform key soliloquies to grasp character motivation and iambic pentameter. The assessment involves a critical essay comparing the mechanics of the inner play to the main plot, focusing on how Shakespeare uses theatricality to explore human folly and the fragility of perception.

## Prose: The Art of the Essay
This section focuses on George Orwell's essays, "Politics and the English Language" and "Shooting an Elephant." Students learn to identify and employ rhetorical devices such as polysyllabic diction, parataxis, and the rhetorical question. They are taught to structure persuasive arguments by formulating a clear thesis and supporting it with logical evidence and personal anecdote. The core skill is deconstructing Orwell’s argument to understand how he uses stylistic simplicity to critique complex socio-political issues, a technique they must replicate in their own critical writing.

## Poetry: The Sonnet's Structure
The curriculum requires a detailed study of the Elizabethan sonnet, particularly Shakespeare's Sonnet 18 ("Shall I compare thee to a summer's day?"). Students learn the formal rules: 14 lines, an iambic pentameter, and a specific rhyme scheme (ABAB CDCD EFEF GG). They analyse the volta or turn at line 9, which shifts the poem's focus from the lover's impermanence to the poem's eternal power. They practice writing their own sonnets to understand the constraints of form and how they shape thematic expression, demonstrating mastery of this classical poetic structure.

## Functional Grammar: Tenses and Concord
Students master complex tenses for precise academic writing, including the Present Perfect Continuous ("She has been studying since morning") and the Future Perfect ("They will have completed the project by June"). They learn subject-verb concord, especially with collective nouns ("The committee has/have agreed") and uncountable nouns ("The information is/is not sufficient"). They practice identifying and correcting errors in tense consistency within narrative paragraphs and ensuring proper subject-verb agreement in complex sentences, a skill essential for exam success.

## Comprehension and Summary Techniques
Students learn a three-step method for comprehension passages. First, they skim for the main idea, identifying the author's central argument. Second, they scan for specific information, locating keywords and data. Finally, they infer unstated meanings using contextual clues. For summary writing, they are taught to identify key topic sentences in each paragraph and condense them into a single, coherent passage of 80 words. They must learn to eliminate examples, illustrations, and redundant details while preserving the factual core of the original text.

## Writing Skills: The Formal Report
Students are trained to write a formal report based on a given set of data, such as the decline of a traditional craft. They must structure it with a title, introduction (stating the purpose), body (divided into sections with clear headings, presenting facts and figures), and conclusion (offering a brief solution). They learn to use formal, impersonal language and objective analysis, avoiding personal opinions. Emphasis is placed on organizing information logically, using statistics correctly, and presenting a balanced, well-structured argument as required by the ICSE examination pattern.
