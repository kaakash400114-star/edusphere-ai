# ICSE Class 4 English - CISCE approach

## Nouns and pronouns expanded
In Class 4, children learn about different kinds of nouns like common nouns (e.g., book, dog) and proper nouns (e.g., Ramesh, Mumbai). They also understand collective nouns for groups of things, such as a team of players or a bunch of grapes. For pronouns, they practise using words like he, she, it, and they to replace nouns in sentences, making their writing shorter and clearer. The children learn to identify and use singular and plural forms correctly, for example, changing child to children. They also begin to see how pronouns help avoid repeating the same noun again and again.

Abstract nouns (honesty, joy); gender sets; pronoun-antecedent agreement.

## Verbs: tense pairs
In Grade 4, we learn about verb tenses and how they tell us when an action happens. We study pairs of tenses: Present and Past, to show if an action is happening now or happened before. We learn to change verbs from their present form to their past form by adding ‘ed’ or changing their spelling. For example, we change ‘walk’ to ‘walked’, ‘play’ to ‘played’, and ‘jump’ to ‘jumped’. We also use these tenses in simple sentences, like “I play football” for present tense and “I played football” for past tense, to understand the difference clearly.

Simple vs continuous in present and past; future with will; irregular verb bank (bring-brought, teach-taught).

## Adjectives: degrees; adverbs in place
In Class 4, we learn about adjectives and how to change them to show if something is small, smaller, or smallest. We also use adverbs to tell us how things are done, like quickly, slowly, or softly. For example, we say “The boy is tall,” “The boy is taller,” or “The boy is tallest” to compare. Adverbs help us describe actions, such as “She runs fast” or “He speaks softly.” These words make our sentences more interesting and clear.

Big-bigger-biggest; more/most for long words; adverbs of time-place-manner.

## Prepositions and conjunctions
Children learn to use prepositions to show where something is, like "in the box," "under the chair," or "near the door." They also use prepositions of time, such as "at school," "in the morning," or "on Monday." For conjunctions, they learn to join small sentences with "and," "but," and "or," like "I like ice cream, but I do not like cake." They understand that "and" adds ideas, "but" shows contrast, and "or" gives choices. These words help make sentences longer and clearer when speaking and writing.

Direction prepositions (towards, across); because-although-so joins.

## Punctuation: dialogue and commas
In Class 4, we learn to write dialogues correctly using quotation marks to show exactly what a person says, like "Can I play with you?" asked Riya. We also use commas to separate words in a list, such as buying apples, bananas, and oranges from the market. When someone speaks to another person, we put a comma before the quotation marks, like Rohan said, "Let's go to the park." Commas help us pause a little and make our sentences clear and easy to read. We practice using these in stories and sentences to make our writing neat and correct.

Speech marks with said-asked; comma lists and clauses; apostrophe possession review.

## Comprehension and vocabulary
In Grade 4, children learn to read and understand short stories and poems. They find answers to questions like "Who, What, Where" by reading the text carefully. They also learn new words and their meanings. For example, if they read "The happy cat played with a ball", they understand "happy" means feeling good. Children learn to use these new words in their own sentences, like "I am happy to play". This helps them become better readers and talkers.

Unseen passage answers; synonyms-antonyms; idioms in sentences (raining cats and dogs).

## Composition: letters and stories
In Class 4, children learn to write friendly letters by using the format – date, salutation like ‘Dear Mum’, body of the message in short sentences, and a closing such as ‘Your loving child, [Name]’. They also compose short stories with a clear beginning, middle, and end, using simple words and describing characters like ‘a brave lion’ or ‘a clever rabbit’. Teachers guide them to use full stops and commas correctly while writing these stories. For letters, they include small details like ‘I ate ice cream yesterday’ to make it interesting. They practice writing both friendly letters and imaginative stories to build their skills.

Informal letter format; story from outlines with moral; diary entry practice.
