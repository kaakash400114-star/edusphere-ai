# ICSE Class 5 English - CISCE approach

## Grammar: tenses full set
In Class 5, we learn about tenses to show when something happens. The present tense tells about things we do now, like "I play football every day." The past tense talks about things that already happened, for example, "She painted a picture yesterday." The future tense is for things that will come next, such as "We will visit the zoo tomorrow." We also learn to change verbs correctly to fit the tense, like adding "-ed" for past actions or using "will" before the verb for future plans. These rules help us speak and write clearly about time.

All simple-continuous-perfect forms; timeline diagrams; consistent narration.

## Voice and reported speech (intro)
In Class 5, we learn about voice and reported speech. Direct speech uses quotation marks to show exactly what someone says, like Ravi said, “I am playing football.” Reported speech tells what someone said without using their exact words, like Ravi said that he was playing football. When we change from direct to reported speech, we often change the pronouns and the tense of the verb, as in “I am” becoming “he was.” We also learn that reported speech usually does not use commas or quotation marks. For example, “She is happy” becomes “She said that she was happy.”

Active-passive simple transforms; said-told-asked reporting with shifts.

## Clauses and complex sentences
In Class 5, you learn to find small groups of words called clauses that can make sense on their own or need other words to be complete. A main clause is a full sentence by itself, like "The cat sat on the mat," and a subordinate clause adds extra information, like "when it was sunny." You will make complex sentences by joining a main clause with a subordinate clause using words like "because," "if," or "when," for example, "We went home because it started to rain." This helps you write longer and more interesting stories to share with your friends and teachers.

Main-subordinate; because-although-when-if-while; combine and punctuate.

## Modals and conditionals (intro)
In class 5, children learn about modal verbs like can, could, should, and must to show ability or permission. They also study simple conditional sentences using "if" to talk about possible situations, such as "If it rains, we will play inside." Modal verbs are used before the main verb, like "She can sing well," while conditionals connect two ideas. For example, "If you study, you will pass" explains what happens in a given case. These words help children express ideas clearly about what might happen or what is possible.

can-could-may-might-must-should; if-type 1 sentences (real conditions).

## Vocabulary: idioms, proverbs, word building
In Class 5, children learn to understand and use common idioms and proverbs in sentences. They study how words are built using prefixes and suffixes, like adding 'un-' to 'happy' to make it 'unhappy'. They also explore compound words, where two words join to form a new word, such as 'sun' + 'flower' = 'sunflower'. Pupils are taught to match idioms to their meanings, like 'raining cats and dogs' meaning heavy rain. Proverbs such as 'actions speak louder than words' help them learn simple life lessons through language.

Prefix-suffix families; idioms in context; proverbs with meanings.

## Comprehension and précis
In Class 5, students learn to read and understand stories and passages by answering questions about what they have read. They also learn to write a short summary, or précis, by picking only the most important ideas from a long text and putting them into their own simple words. For example, if a story is about a boy helping a lost dog find its home, the précis might be, "A boy helped a lost dog find its way back to its house." Students must always use their own words and not copy from the original text. They also learn to leave out small details and focus only on the main points.

Inference and vocabulary-in-context; précis a passage in 5 lines.

## Composition: formal-informal letters, story, description
In Grade 5, children learn to write formal and informal letters using correct formats, like writing a letter to a grandparent for their birthday or a formal letter to the school principal. They practice writing short stories with a clear beginning, middle, and end, such as a story about a lost puppy. For descriptions, they learn to use simple words to paint a picture, like describing their favourite toy or a rainy day. They focus on using full sentences, proper punctuation, and arranging their ideas neatly. These activities help them share their thoughts and feelings in different ways.

Letter formats with layout marks; story with dialogues; describe a festival with senses.
