# ICSE Class 1 English - CISCE approach

## Phonics foundation and handwriting
Children learn to match letter sounds, like 'a' for apple and 'b' for ball, with their written symbols. They practice writing small letters and capital letters in the correct order and shape, like tracing 'c', 'o', and 'd'. They blend simple sounds to read two-letter words, such as 'at', 'in', and 'on'. They also learn to listen carefully for the first sound in words, like 's' for sun. These activities help them connect sounds with letters and build strong reading and writing skills.

Letter-sound mastery (a-z), blends sh-ch-th; capital-small formation; three-letter word building (cat, pin, sun).

## Sight words and simple sentences
Children learn to recognise and read common sight words like ‘the’, ‘and’, ‘is’, and ‘I’ without sounding them out phonetically. They practice forming simple sentences by joining a naming word (noun) like ‘cat’ and an action word (verb) like ‘runs’ to make short sentences such as ‘The cat runs.’ Punctuation like a full stop (.) is used at the end of each sentence. They also learn to write simple sentences by themselves, using capital letters for the first word and leaving spaces between words. For example, ‘I am happy’ is a sentence they read and write.

I, am, the, to, in, is, it; read and say: "I am in the red tent."

## Naming and action words
In Class 1, children learn to name words and action words. Naming words tell us the names of persons, animals, places, or things, like "Rita", "cat", "school", and "book". Action words tell us what a person or thing does, such as "run", "jump", "read", and "write". We use naming words to talk about someone or something and action words to describe what they are doing. For example, "Rita reads her book" uses a naming word ("Rita") and an action word ("reads").

Naming: ball, bird, mummy; action: hop, sing, clap; combine: "The bird sings."

## One-many, a-an, opposites
Students learn to use 'a' before words that start with a consonant sound, like 'a cat' or 'a bag', and 'an' before words that start with a vowel sound, like 'an apple' or 'an egg'. They understand that 'many' is used for things that are not one, such as 'many books' or 'many pencils'. Children also discover opposites, which are words with opposite meanings, for example, 'big' and 'small', 'happy' and 'sad', or 'hot' and 'cold'. They practice matching and using these words in simple sentences to show difference. This helps them build clear sentences and describe things better.

One sun-two stars; an ant, a dog; big-small, open-shut.

## Rhymes, picture talk and storytelling
In Class 1, children learn to enjoy and repeat simple rhymes and poems, recognising the rhythm and sound patterns. They practice identifying rhyming words, like 'cat' and 'hat', to build phonetic awareness. Through picture talk, they describe what they see in images, using new words to name objects and actions. Storytelling helps them follow simple plots, recall the main idea, and understand characters. For example, after hearing a story about a rabbit, they can name the rabbit and where it lived. These activities develop speaking, listening, and early comprehension skills in a fun way.

Recite 4-line rhymes; describe a picture in three sentences; retell a short story with props.

## Capital letters and full stop
In Class 1, we learn to use capital letters for the first letter of our names and the word ‘I’. We also use capital letters to start a sentence, like ‘Ravi has a ball’. A full stop comes at the end of a sentence to show it is complete, for example, ‘The cat is big.’. When we write a sentence, we begin with a capital letter and end with a full stop. This helps us read and write words correctly.

Sentence beginnings, names; end with a full stop; copy and continue a model sentence.
