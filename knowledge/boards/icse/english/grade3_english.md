# ICSE Class 3 English - CISCE approach

## Grammar: nouns, pronouns, adjectives
In Class 3, we learn that nouns are the names of people, animals, places, and things, like “Riya,” “dog,” “school,” and “book.” Pronouns are words that take the place of nouns to avoid repetition, such as “I,” “you,” “he,” “she,” and “they.” Adjectives are words that describe nouns by telling us more about them, like “big” elephant, “red” apple, or “happy” child. We also practice using them to make our sentences clearer and more interesting. For example, we say “The tall boy” instead of just “The boy.”

Collective nouns (swarm, bouquet); pronouns replace neatly; comparative adjectives with -er-than.

## Verbs: tenses begin
Verbs are action words that tell us what someone or something does. We learn to use the present tense to show actions happening now, like "Ravi runs" or "I eat my lunch". The past tense tells us about actions that are already over, such as "She jumped" or "We played yesterday". To form the past tense, we often add "ed" to the verb, like "walk" becomes "walked". Some verbs change their spelling, like "run" becomes "ran". We also learn the future tense to talk about actions that will happen later, using words like "will" before the verb, for example, "I will read a book".

Simple present-past-future; regular -ed forms; am-is-are + -ing.

## Articles, prepositions, conjunctions
In Grade 3, we learn about small helper words called articles, prepositions, and conjunctions to make our sentences better. We learn the three articles: 'a' (before a single thing that starts with a consonant, like "a book"), 'an' (before a single thing that starts with a vowel, like "an apple"), and 'the' (to talk about something special, like "the book on the table"). We also learn about prepositions, which tell us where something is, like "in the box", "under the chair", or "near the door". Finally, we learn about conjunctions, which join words or sentences together, like "and", "but", and "or", so we can say "I like apples and bananas".

a-an-the placement; in-on-under-between; and-but-or-because.

## Punctuation: comma, apostrophe, exclamation
In Class 3, we learn to use commas, apostrophes, and exclamation marks in sentences. We use a comma to separate items in a list, like "I bought apples, bananas, and oranges." We use an apostrophe to show something belongs to someone, like "Riya’s doll is beautiful." We use an exclamation mark at the end of a sentence that shows strong feeling, like "Wow! What a big cake!" These punctuation marks help us write clearly and share our ideas better.

Lists and addresses; belonging ('s); strong feelings with !

## Vocabulary and spelling
In Grade 3, children learn to spell common words by remembering their sound and letter patterns. They understand that some words have double letters, like "little" and "happy", and that some end with 'e' which makes the vowel say its name, like "cake" and "time". They learn new words for describing people, places, and things, such as "brave", "huge", and "colourful". Children also practice putting words in the right order to make clear sentences, like "The big dog runs fast". By reading stories and poems, they find new words and add them to their vocabulary.

Synonyms-antonyms; homophones (hear-here); plurals puzzles (-ies, -ves).

## Composition: guided paragraph and story
Children learn to write a short guided paragraph by describing a picture or a simple topic in four to five sentences. They use a formula: one topic sentence, two detail sentences, and one closing sentence, like "The sun is bright. It has golden rays. I like to play in its light. It makes me happy." They also learn to write a very short story with a beginning, middle, and end, such as "A cat saw a mouse. It chased the mouse. The mouse ran into a hole." Children use full stops to end sentences and try to write in a clear order.

5-6 sentence paragraphs with topic sentence; complete-a-story with picture clues; read aloud with expression.
