# ICSE Grade 11 Science
## Kinematics
The study of motion in terms of displacement, velocity, and acceleration. Velocity is the rate of change of displacement, calculated as v = Δx/Δt. Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity, a = Δv/Δt. For uniform acceleration from rest, displacement s = ½at² and final velocity v² = u² + 2as. The velocity-time graph for uniformly accelerated motion is a straight line whose slope gives acceleration. For an object thrown vertically upwards, its velocity decreases at 9.8 m/s² due to gravity.

## Laws of Motion
Newton’s laws govern the relationship between force and motion. The first law states that an object remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by a net external force. The second law, F = ma, quantifies this relationship. The third law states that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. Frictional force, which opposes motion, is calculated as f = μN, where μ is the coefficient of friction and N is the normal reaction. On an inclined plane, the component of gravity parallel to the plane is mg sinθ.

## Work, Energy, and Power
Work is done when a force causes displacement, calculated as W = Fd cosθ. Energy is the capacity to do work; kinetic energy is ½mv² and potential energy is mgh. The work-energy theorem states that the net work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy. Power is the rate of doing work, P = W/t. In SI units, work is in joules and power in watts. A horse pulling a plough across a field does work against friction, converting chemical energy from food into kinetic energy of the plough.

## Atomic Structure
An atom consists of a central nucleus with protons and neutrons, surrounded by electrons. Rutherford’s gold foil experiment established this nuclear model. Atomic number (Z) is the number of protons, and mass number (A) is the sum of protons and neutrons. Isotopes are atoms of the same element with different mass numbers, like carbon-12 and carbon-14. Bohr’s model explains electron energy levels in a hydrogen atom; electrons absorb or emit photons to jump between these discrete levels, E = hν.

## Chemical Bonding
Atoms bond to achieve stable electron configurations. Ionic bonding involves the complete transfer of electrons, forming cations and anions (e.g., Na⁺Cl⁻). Covalent bonding involves the sharing of electron pairs between non-metals (e.g., H₂O). The electronegativity difference determines bond polarity. A metallic bond is a 'sea of electrons' holding metal cations in a lattice. The octet rule governs the stability of these bonds. The strength of ionic bonds is reflected in high melting points.

## Cell Biology
The cell is the basic unit of life. Prokaryotic cells lack a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, while eukaryotic cells have them. The endomembrane system includes the nucleus, ER, Golgi apparatus, and lysosomes, which work together for protein synthesis, modification, and transport. Mitochondria are the powerhouses, producing ATP through cellular respiration. The cell membrane, a phospholipid bilayer, regulates the passage of substances via passive and active transport. DNA in the nucleus carries genetic information.
