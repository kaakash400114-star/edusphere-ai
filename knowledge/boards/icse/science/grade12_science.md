# ICSE Grade 12 Science

## Electrostatics
Coulomb's law states that the electrostatic force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of their magnitudes and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them, F = k * (q1 * q2) / r², where k is 9 x 10⁹ Nm²/C². The electric field E at a point is the force experienced by a unit positive test charge, E = F/q. For a point charge, E = k * q / r². Gauss's law relates the flux through a closed surface to the charge enclosed, Φ = q/ε₀, and is used to calculate electric fields for symmetric charge distributions like spherical shells and infinite planes.

## Current Electricity
Ohm's law defines the relationship between current I, potential difference V, and resistance R as V = IR. The resistance R of a conductor is R = ρL/A, where ρ is resistivity, L is length, and A is cross-sectional area. Kirchhoff's laws are fundamental: the junction law states the sum of currents at a node is zero, and the loop law states the sum of potential changes in any closed loop is zero. The power dissipated in a resistor is P = VI = I²R = V²/R. Cells in series have total EMF E_total = E1 + E2, and in parallel, E_total is equal to the EMF of one cell if identical.

## Magnetic Effects of Current and Magnetism
The magnetic field B at a distance r from a long straight conductor carrying current I is B = (μ₀ * I) / (2πr), directed by the right-hand thumb rule. For a circular loop, the central field is B = (μ₀ * I) / (2r). The Lorentz force on a moving charge is F = q(v x B). An ammeter is connected in series to measure current and has low resistance, while a voltmeter is connected in parallel and has high resistance. The magnitude of the magnetic field inside a solenoid is B = μ₀nI, where n is the number of turns per unit length.

## Electromagnetic Induction
Faraday's law of induction states that the induced electromotive force (EMF) in a closed loop is equal to the negative rate of change of magnetic flux through the loop, ε = -N dΦ/dt. Lenz's law states that the direction of the induced current is such that it opposes the change in magnetic flux that produced it. The induced EMF in a conductor of length l moving with velocity v perpendicular to a magnetic field B is ε = Blv. The self-inductance L of a coil relates induced EMF to the rate of change of current, ε = -L dI/dt, and the energy stored in an inductor is (1/2)LI².

## Optics
Snell's law describes refraction: n₁sinθ₁ = n₂sinθ₂. The lens formula is 1/f = 1/v - 1/u, where f is focal length, v is image distance, and u is object distance, with sign convention based on the Cartesian system. For a thin lens, the magnification m = v/u. The resolving power of a microscope is given by R = 2nsinθ/λ. In the single-slit diffraction pattern, the condition for minima is asinθ = nλ. The photoelectric effect equation is K_max = hf - φ, where K_max is the maximum kinetic energy of emitted electrons, h is Planck's constant, f is the frequency of incident light, and φ is the work function of the material.

## Modern Physics
Radioactive decay follows the law N = N₀e^(-λt), where N is the number of undecayed nuclei at time t, N₀ is the initial number, and λ is the decay constant. The half-life T₁/₂ is related to λ by T₁/₂ = 0.693/λ. In nuclear fission, a heavy nucleus like ²³⁵U splits into lighter nuclei, releasing energy. The mass defect Δm is the difference between the total mass of individual nucleons and the mass of the nucleus, and it is converted to energy via Einstein's equation E = Δmc². De Broglie proposed that matter has wave-like properties, with wavelength λ = h/p, where p is the momentum of the particle.
