# ICSE Grade 10 Science

## The Periodic Table and Periodicity
The Periodic Table classifies elements based on their atomic number, electron configuration, and recurring chemical properties. Elements are arranged in periods (rows) and groups (columns). Atomic radius decreases across a period and increases down a group due to the increasing number of electron shells. Electronegativity, the ability of an atom to attract electrons, increases across a period and decreases down a group. For example, Fluorine (F) is the most electronegative element, while Cesium (Cs) has the lowest electronegativity.

## Chemical Bonding
Chemical bonding involves the formation of compounds through the transfer or sharing of valence electrons. Ionic bonds form when one atom transfers electrons to another, creating ions like Sodium Chloride (NaCl), where Na⁺ and Cl⁻ are held together by electrostatic forces. Covalent bonds involve sharing of electrons, as in a water molecule (H₂O), where oxygen shares one electron with each hydrogen atom. Metallic bonding occurs in metals, where delocalized electrons are shared among a lattice of positive metal ions, explaining properties like electrical conductivity.

## Acids, Bases, and Salts
Acids are proton donors (e.g., Hydrochloric acid, HCl), bases are proton acceptors (e.g., Sodium hydroxide, NaOH), and salts are ionic compounds formed by neutralization reactions. The pH scale measures acidity or alkalinity, ranging from 0 to 14. A pH of 7 is neutral, below 7 is acidic, and above 7 is alkaline. Phenolphthalein is an indicator that turns pink in alkaline solutions and remains colourless in acidic ones. For example, the reaction between HCl and NaOH produces salt (NaCl) and water.

## Electricity and Magnetism
Electricity is the flow of electric charge, governed by Ohm's Law (V = I × R), where V is voltage, I is current, and R is resistance. Current (I) flows from positive to negative terminal in a circuit. A magnetic field is produced around a current-carrying conductor; the right-hand rule determines its direction. Electromagnets are created by coiling wire around a ferromagnetic core, with their strength depending on the current and number of turns. For example, a simple electric bell uses electromagnets to strike a gong.

## Light and Optics
Light travels in straight lines and can be reflected or refracted. The Law of Reflection states that the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection. Refraction is the bending of light when it passes from one medium to another, described by Snell's Law (n₁sinθ₁ = n₂sinθ₂). The lens formula is 1/f = 1/v - 1/u, where f is focal length, v is image distance, and u is object distance. For example, a convex lens forms a real, inverted image when the object is placed beyond its focal point.

## Biology: Cell Structure and Functions
Cells are the basic structural and functional units of life. Plant cells have a rigid cell wall, large vacuoles, and chloroplasts for photosynthesis, while animal cells lack these. The nucleus contains genetic material (DNA), mitochondria produce energy, and the endoplasmic reticulum aids in protein synthesis. Diffusion is the passive movement of molecules from high to low concentration, e.g., oxygen entering cells during respiration. Osmosis is the diffusion of water across a semi-permeable membrane, crucial for water balance in plant roots.
