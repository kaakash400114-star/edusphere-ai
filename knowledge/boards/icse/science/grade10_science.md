# ICSE Class 10 Science - CISCE (Physics-Chemistry-Biology)

## Physics: force-motion-energy
In this topic, students learn about force, motion, and energy, which are all around us. A force is a push or pull that can make a stationary object move or stop a moving object, like pushing a book on a table. Motion is when an object changes its position over time, and we measure its speed by how fast it covers distance. Energy is the ability to do work; kinetic energy is in moving objects, like a rolling ball, and potential energy is stored energy, like water held high in a dam. Newton's laws of motion explain how forces affect objects: the first law says objects stay at rest or in motion unless a force acts on them.

Turning forces-moments-equilibrium; uniform circular motion; work-power-energy-machines (levers, efficiency); refraction at plane surfaces-refraction through lens; spectrum; sound: echo-vibration-natural frequency-forced vibrations-resonance-loudness-pitch-quality; current electricity: Ohm's law, power, household; electromagnetism: Oersted, motor-rule, transformer idea.

## Chemistry: periodic properties and inorganic
In Class 10, we learn about the periodic table and how elements change properties in a pattern, called periodic trends. We study atomic size, which gets bigger when you go down a group and smaller when you move left to right in a period. Electronegativity, an atom’s power to hold electrons, increases from left to right, like how fluorine is very strong. We also learn about valency, the number of electrons an atom needs to gain or lose to become stable, like sodium having a valency of +1. These rules help us understand why elements react differently.

Periodic table trends; chemical bonding: ionic-covalent-metallic; acids-bases-salts; analytical chemistry: identification of cations-anions (wet tests); mole concept-stoichiometry; electrolysis-electrolytic conduction; metallurgy of aluminium-iron; study of compounds: ammonia-sulphuric acid-hydrogen chloride.

## Chemistry: organic
In Class 10 Chemistry, we learn about organic compounds, which are mainly made of carbon atoms bonded to hydrogen. We study simple compounds like methane (CH₄), ethane (C₂H₆), and ethene (C₂H₄), and how their structures determine their properties. We also explore the functional groups, such as the hydroxyl (-OH) group in alcohols like ethanol (C₂H₅OH), which gives them special uses. The nomenclature rules help us name compounds based on the number of carbon atoms and their bonds. Practical experiments teach us to test for these compounds using simple reagents.

Organic chemistry basics: homologous series; alkanes-alkenes-alkynes; alcohols-carboxylic acids; isomerism; IUPAC nomenclature; substitution-addition-reactions.

## Biology: cell-cycle and genetics
In Class 10, students learn about the cell cycle, which is the process by which a cell grows and divides into two new cells. They study the different phases: interphase (when the cell prepares to divide) and mitosis (when the cell divides into two identical daughter cells). In genetics, they explore how traits are passed from parents to children through genes, which are tiny units on chromosomes. For example, a child may inherit their mother’s eye colour or father’s height. They also learn about Mendel’s laws of inheritance, which explain how dominant and recessive traits combine in offspring.

Cell division: mitosis-meiosis; structure of chromosomes; genetics: Mendel's laws, sex determination; plant physiology: absorption-transpiration-photosynthesis-respiration (experiments); human: circulatory-excretory-nervous-reproductive systems; population-health-diseases; pollution-conservation awareness.
