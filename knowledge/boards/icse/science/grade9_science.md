# ICSE Class 9 Science - CISCE (Physics-Chemistry-Biology)

## Physics: measurements-motion-laws
In this topic, children learn about measurement using standard units like metre and second, and how to measure length, time, and speed accurately. They study motion by understanding different types, such as uniform and non-uniform, and how to calculate speed using the formula speed = distance/time, like a car moving 60 km in 1 hour having a speed of 60 km/h. Newton’s laws of motion teach us that objects stay at rest or in motion unless a force acts on them, like a ball stopping due to friction, and that force equals mass times acceleration, like pushing a heavy box harder to make it move faster.

Units-estimation-error; motion equations with graphs; Newton's laws applications; gravitation-flotation; work-power-energy-machine efficiency.

## Physics: heat-light-sound-electricity-current-magnetism
In Class 9, students learn about heat and how it travels through solids, liquids, and gases by conduction, convection, and radiation. They study light by understanding how it reflects off mirrors, refracts through lenses, and forms images. For sound, they explore how it needs a medium to travel and learn about pitch and loudness. The electricity chapter teaches about electric current, circuits, and the important law Ohm’s law, which states that current equals voltage divided by resistance. Finally, in magnetism, they discover what makes a material magnetic, the differences between temporary and permanent magnets, and how like poles repel while unlike poles attract.

Temperature scales-specific heat; refraction-lens-ray optics; wave-sound characteristics; current-Ohm's law-potential; household circuits-magnetism effects.

## Chemistry: language of chemistry
In Class 9, students learn the basic rules of chemical symbols and formulas, using the first letter or first two letters of an element’s English name to represent it. They understand that molecules are built from atoms, shown using subscripts in formulas like H₂O for water. Students also study chemical equations to describe reactions, like how magnesium (Mg) reacts with oxygen (O₂) to form magnesium oxide (MgO). They learn about the structure of atoms, with protons and neutrons in the nucleus and electrons moving around it, using simple diagrams to show these concepts. The topic helps them read and write chemical language accurately.

Symbols-valencies-formulae; equations-balancing; mole concept-stoichiometry intro; atomic structure (Bohr); periodic table trends.

## Chemistry: physical-chemical principles
Students learn about matter and its changes, like when ice melts into water or wood burns into smoke. They study atoms, the tiny building blocks of everything, and how they join together to form molecules, such as water made of two hydrogen and one oxygen atom. The topic explains the difference between physical changes, like boiling water, and chemical changes, like rusting iron, using simple examples. Students also discover the periodic table, a chart that organises all known elements based on their properties, and learn about symbols like H for hydrogen or O for oxygen. Finally, they explore how elements combine to form compounds, like salt made from sodium and chlorine, following specific rules and formulas.

Water: hardness-purification; gases: preparation-properties (H2, O2, CO2, NH3, HCl); acids-bases-salts-pH; analytical uses of NaOH-NH4OH (qualitative intro).

## Biology: cell-tissues-flowers
In this topic, students learn that all living things are made of tiny building blocks called cells, which group together to form tissues like muscle or skin in plants and animals. They study the different parts of a flower, such as the petals, sepals, stamens, and pistil, and understand how these parts help in the process of reproduction. For example, the stamen makes pollen, which travels to the pistil to help the plant make seeds. Students also learn about the simple tissues in plants, like the ones that carry water from the roots to the leaves, just like pipes carry water in a house.

Cell organelles-ultrastructure; tissues: plant-animal; flower structure-pollination-fertilisation; vegetative propagation.

## Biology: human systems and health
Students learn about the human body's important systems like the digestive system, which breaks down food to give us energy, and the respiratory system, which helps us breathe in oxygen and breathe out carbon dioxide. They also study the circulatory system, where the heart acts like a pump to send blood carrying nutrients and oxygen to all parts of the body. The topic also explains how our body protects itself from germs with the help of the immune system, and how we should eat healthy foods like fruits and vegetables to stay strong and avoid getting sick.

Digestive-respiratory-circulatory-excretory systems in depth; skin-skeleton; health-hygiene-diseases; waste management-seed structure experiments.
