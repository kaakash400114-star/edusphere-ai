# ICSE Class 3 Science - CISCE approach

## Living things: needs and life processes
Living things need air, water, food, and shelter to stay alive and grow. Plants make their own food using sunlight, water, and air, while animals eat plants or other animals to get energy. All living things move, grow, and have babies to make more of their kind, like a seed growing into a plant or a cat having kittens. They also respond to changes around them, such as plants bending towards sunlight or animals running away from danger. These important activities are called life processes that help every living thing stay healthy and alive.

Food-air-water-growth-reproduction in plants and animals; life cycles: butterfly, frog, bean.

## Skeletal and muscular system
Our body has a strong frame called the skeleton, which helps us stand and move. The skeleton is made of many small bones, like the skull that protects our brain and the thigh bone in our legs. Muscles are soft parts that pull on our bones to help us run, jump, and even smile. Strong muscles, like the ones in our arms, let us lift things easily. Together, the bones and muscles make our body move and keep us active every day.

Bones give shape-protection; major bones; joints move with muscles; posture care.

## Food chains in nature
In nature, plants make their own food using sunlight. Animals eat plants or other animals to get energy. A food chain shows who eats whom in a line, starting from a plant. For example, grass is eaten by a rabbit, and the rabbit is eaten by a fox. Every food chain must have a plant, a plant-eater, and a meat-eater. If one animal disappears, it affects all the animals in the chain. This helps us understand how living things depend on each other.

Producers and eaters: grass→deer→lion; make simple chains; interdependence in a pond.

## Air, water, weather deep dive
Air is all around us, filling every space and helping us breathe. Water exists in three forms: solid like ice cubes, liquid like in our water bottles, and gas like steam from a hot kettle. Weather changes every day; we see it when the sun shines brightly, it rains heavily, or the wind blows leaves off trees. We learn that air can make things move, like when we blow air through a straw. The water cycle shows us how water from rain or rivers goes into the air and comes back down as rain, helping plants grow.

Air composition (oxygen for burning demo); water cycle stages; clouds and rain; keep air-water clean.

## Solids, liquids, gases
Solids have a fixed shape and take up their own space, like a book on your table or a hard stone. Liquids flow and take the shape of their container, such as water in a glass or milk in a bottle. Gases are all around us and have no fixed shape; they fill up any space, like the air we breathe from our lungs. Ice is a solid that can turn into liquid water when it gets warm, and water can turn into a gas called steam when it gets very hot. Everything around us is either a solid, liquid, or gas, and we can see how they change by heating or cooling them.

Shapes and flow; changing states by heating-cooling (ice, water, steam); reversible changes.

## Light, sound and force fun
Light helps us see things by traveling in straight lines, like when sunlight comes through a window to brighten a room. Sound makes noise by vibrating, such as when you ring a bell and it moves back and forth to make a loud clang. Force is a push or pull that moves things, like when you kick a football and it rolls away because of your strength. We learn that some things, like a torch, give out light, while others, like a mirror, only reflect it. Sounds can be loud or soft, like a whisper being quiet and a drum being noisy, and force can be strong or gentle, like blowing air to move a feather.

Shadows change with position; sources of sound, pitch loud-soft; push-pull makes things move-stop.
