# German (Lehrplan) Grade 5 English

## Everyday Communication and Social Interaction
Pupils learn to engage in simple, structured conversations about daily routines, school activities, and hobbies. They practice asking and answering questions using present simple tense ("What time do you get up?" / "I get up at 7 o'clock"). They use common phrases for politeness like "Could you...?" and "Thank you very much." Lessons use role-plays for scenarios such as ordering food in a German café or asking for directions to a Christmas market, incorporating euros for price discussions. The focus is on clear pronunciation and responding directly to questions.

## Understanding Simple Texts
Students develop skills to understand short, factual texts. They learn to identify the main topic and find specific information, like the date of a Bundesliga match or the price of a ticket. They practice using contextual clues to understand unfamiliar words, for example, recognising that "Autobahn" relates to driving. Texts include simple posters, event schedules, and short descriptions of German cultural events. Pupils answer multiple-choice and true/false questions to demonstrate comprehension, ensuring they can distinguish between main ideas and supporting details.

## Describing People and Places
Pupils learn to describe physical appearance, clothing, and character using basic adjectives ("She has blonde hair," "He is friendly"). They are taught to use simple present tense to describe locations and buildings ("The market square is big," "The museum has old paintings"). They use German examples, such as describing the layout of a Christmas market. The vocabulary focuses on colour, size, and simple shapes. Students write short descriptions of familiar people or places, structuring sentences with "is/are" and basic connectors like "and."

## Writing Structured Short Texts
Students practice writing short, structured texts such as simple postcards, emails, or diary entries. They learn to organise their writing with a greeting, main body, and closing. The main body consists of 3-5 simple sentences using the present tense to describe an event or experience. They incorporate learned vocabulary and simple connectors like "then" and "after that." Topics include a visit to a Bundesliga game or a family trip. Spelling and basic punctuation are a primary focus, ensuring correct use of capital letters and full stops.

## Language and Word Knowledge
Pupils explore basic word formation rules, including adding suffixes like "-er" and "-ing" to verbs ("walk," "walker," "walking"). They learn about simple compound nouns, such as "football" and "snowman." Lessons focus on expanding vocabulary through thematic units, including words related to weather, food, and German culture (e.g., "pretzel," "glühwein"). They practice categorising words into groups like nouns, verbs, and adjectives. Students are introduced to the singular and plural forms of common nouns, noting irregularities like "foot/feet."

## Sounds and Pronunciation
This section covers the relationship between letters and their corresponding sounds in English. Pupils learn to distinguish and produce vowel sounds (short and long, e.g., "ship" vs. "sheep") and key consonant clusters like "str" and "ng." They practice pronouncing words with German cognates, such as "Autobahn," paying attention to the "th" sound. Lessons use minimal pair exercises to improve listening discrimination. Students practice reading aloud with attention to sentence intonation, using simple phrases to convey basic emotions like happiness or surprise.
