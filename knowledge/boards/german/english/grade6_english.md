# German (Lehrplan) Grade 6 English
## Grammar: Tenses and Sentence Structure
Students master the use of the simple past tense (Präteritum) and present perfect tense (Perfekt) for narrating events. They learn to form regular and irregular past participles (e.g., *arbeitete/ gearbeitet, ging/ gegangen*). Complex sentence structures are introduced, including subordinate clauses with conjunctions like *weil* (because), *dass* (that), and *wenn* (if). Students practice forming compound and complex sentences, ensuring correct subject-verb agreement and word order. Examples include writing a diary entry about a weekend trip to a Christmas market or summarising a Bundesliga match report using the tenses correctly.

## Vocabulary: Thematic Expansion and Collocations
Vocabulary instruction focuses on thematic units such as "Daily Life and Routines," "Travel and Transport," and "Hobbies and Free Time." Students learn collocations and fixed phrases to enhance fluency, such as *sich auf etwas freuen* (to look forward to) or *eine Fahrkarte kaufen* (to buy a ticket). They expand their lexical knowledge with words related to German culture, like *Glühwein* (mulled wine), *Apfelpfannkuchen* (apple pancake), and *der Fahrstuhl* (the lift). Activities include creating word maps and using new terms in descriptive sentences about their daily activities or a trip on the Autobahn.

## Reading Comprehension: Text Analysis and Inference
Students develop skills in analysing and interpreting a variety of texts, including short stories, factual articles, and advertisements. They learn to identify main ideas, supporting details, and the author's purpose. Contextual understanding is emphasised, with students inferring meaning from unfamiliar words and phrases. Examples include reading a fictional story set in Munich during Oktoberfest or an informational text about the history of the Euro. Students answer literal and inferential questions, and summarise key points in their own words to demonstrate comprehension.

## Writing: Structured and Creative Expression
Writing instruction focuses on producing coherent and well-structured texts, such as formal letters, emails, and short narratives. Students learn to organise their ideas with clear introductions, body paragraphs, and conclusions. They practice using appropriate register, punctuation, and paragraphing. Creative writing tasks include drafting an email to a pen pal describing a visit to a Christmas market or writing a descriptive paragraph about their favourite football team in the Bundesliga. Students also learn to edit their work for grammar, spelling, and clarity.

## Listening and Speaking: Interactive Communication
Students engage in listening activities to understand main ideas and specific details from dialogues, announcements, and short presentations. They practice note-taking to identify key information. In speaking exercises, students participate in role-plays, discussions, and short presentations on familiar topics like hobbies, school life, or cultural events. Pronunciation and intonation are refined through guided practice. Examples include listening to a dialogue about ordering food at a market stall or participating in a group discussion about the advantages and disadvantages of public transport in Germany.

## Cultural Studies: German Life and Traditions
This unit explores aspects of German culture, history, and society to provide context for language learning. Students learn about regional traditions, such as the significance of Christmas markets, the Bundesliga football league, and the Autobahn highway system. They compare and contrast cultural practices with their own, fostering intercultural understanding. Topics may include German holidays, school life, and environmental initiatives. Students research and present on a chosen topic, such as the history of the Euro or the importance of recycling in Germany, using English to share their findings.
