# German (Lehrplan) Grade 10 English

## English Literature Analysis
Students analyse complex literary texts such as George Orwell's "1984" or a selection of poems by Rainer Maria Rilke, focusing on theme, character development, and narrative structure. They employ close reading techniques to identify symbolism, irony, and allusion, contextualizing the works within their historical and cultural frameworks. Students learn to structure critical essays, developing coherent arguments supported by textual evidence and incorporating relevant literary theory. Comparative analysis of different texts is a key skill.

## Advanced Grammatical Structures
The curriculum covers complex grammatical concepts including the subjunctive mood for expressing wishes and hypothetical situations, passive voice in various tenses, and complex sentence structures with subordinate clauses. Students master the use of conditionals (Type 1, 2, 3) and reported speech, particularly in formal contexts. Exercises involve rewriting sentences, correcting errors, and applying these structures in written tasks such as formal reports or analytical essays to ensure academic precision and stylistic variation.

## Media and Rhetorical Devices
Students deconstruct media texts, including news articles and televised advertisements, to identify persuasive techniques and rhetorical devices. They analyse the use of ethos, pathos, and logos, as well as figurative language like metaphors and hyperbole, specifically in German media contexts, for example, a report on the Bundesliga or an advert for a German car brand. Students learn to evaluate the credibility of sources and the potential bias of media representations, preparing them for critical consumption in a digital world.

## Argumentative Essay Writing
Students develop their ability to write structured and persuasive argumentative essays on topics relevant to contemporary Germany. They learn to formulate a clear thesis statement, support it with logical arguments and empirical evidence, and address counter-arguments effectively. Emphasis is placed on formal register, sophisticated vocabulary, and correct citation using standard academic referencing styles, such as essays on the environmental impact of the Autobahn or the economic benefits of Christmas markets.

## Intercultural Communication and Presentations
The course focuses on developing effective oral presentation skills for academic and professional settings. Students learn to structure presentations logically, incorporate visual aids like slides or charts, and deliver information with clarity and confidence. Topics for presentations encourage intercultural comparison, for example, "A Comparison of School Systems in Germany and the UK" or "Sustainability in German and British Consumer Culture," fostering an understanding of different cultural perspectives and communication norms.

## Project-Based Research Skills
Students undertake a research project, culminating in a formal written report or a multimedia presentation. They learn to formulate research questions, conduct structured searches using academic databases, and evaluate the reliability of sources. Emphasis is placed on synthesising information from diverse sources to create a coherent argument. The project requires students to manage their time effectively, adhere to academic integrity guidelines, and present their findings professionally, for instance, investigating the history and significance of the Euro in Germany.
