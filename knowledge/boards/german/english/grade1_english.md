# German (Lehrplan) Grade 1 English

## Oral Language Skills
Students learn to listen attentively to stories and simple instructions, raising their hand to speak. They practice clear pronunciation and learn to introduce themselves and their friends, using phrases like "My name is" and "I live in". Children engage in short conversations about their day, favourite foods, or activities like playing football. They learn to ask basic questions using "What is this?" and "Where is the...?", responding with complete sentences. The teacher models standard pronunciation for words such as "Autobahn" and "Bundesliga" to build correct phonetic awareness from the start.

## Phonemic Awareness and Phonics
Pupils develop an awareness of initial sounds in spoken words through games and rhymes (e.g., "The dog is digging"). They learn to associate the most common letter-sound correspondences, particularly for vowels (a, e, i, o, u) and consonants like s, t, p, n. Children practice blending sounds to read simple, regular words such as "cat", "sun", and "mum". They are introduced to initial consonant clusters like "tr" in "train" and "str" in "street". Examples include matching the letter "S" to the /s/ sound in "Straße" and "Bundesliga".

## Reading Comprehension
Students read aloud and understand simple, decodable texts containing high-frequency words. They learn to point to each word as they read to develop one-to-one correspondence. Children demonstrate understanding by answering literal questions about a story, such as "What colour was the ball?" or "Who went to the market?". They retell familiar stories using picture cues and sequence events using words like "first", "then", and "last". An example is reading a short text about a visit to a German Christmas market and identifying the treats mentioned.

## Writing Fundamentals
Children learn to write the 26 letters of the alphabet, both uppercase and lowercase, in a consistent, legible style. They form simple words using learned phonics knowledge, spelling phonetically (e.g., "dog" as "d-o-g"). Students practice writing their own name and simple sentences using a capital letter, finger spaces, and a full stop, for instance, "I see a car.". They are introduced to simple punctuation like a question mark. German examples include writing "I like euros" or "The car is fast."

## Vocabulary and Language Conventions
Pupils expand their vocabulary by learning and using words related to their environment and interests. They are taught common nouns like "house", "tree", "book", and "friend", and action verbs like "run", "jump", and "read". Students learn to use simple descriptive adjectives such as "big", "small", "red", and "happy". They use conjunctions like "and" to join ideas. Examples include identifying colours of German flags or naming objects seen on a walk, like a "Autobahn sign" or a "bread" (Brot).

## Listening and Responding
Students learn to listen for specific information in short stories or instructions. They follow two-step verbal directions, such as "Pick up the pencil and put it in the box". Children respond appropriately by nodding, shaking their head, or giving verbal feedback like "Yes" or "No". They distinguish between statements and questions by intonation. For example, after listening to a description of a Bundesliga match, they can answer who scored a goal or point to the correct picture of a football being kicked.
