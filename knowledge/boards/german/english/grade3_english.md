# German (Lehrplan) Grade 3 English
## Basic Communication and Greetings
Students will master fundamental social greetings and expressions. They will learn to introduce themselves and others, using phrases like "Hello, my name is [Name]" and "This is my friend [Name]." They will practice polite forms such as "please," "thank you," and "you're welcome." Conversational starters like "How are you?" and appropriate responses, including "I am fine, thank you," will be covered. Students will be encouraged to use eye contact and a friendly tone. Role-playing scenarios, such as meeting a new classmate or visiting a Christmas market, will provide context for using these phrases.

## Everyday Objects and Their Uses
Pupils will identify and name common objects found in homes and classrooms. They will learn the specific functions of these items, linking each to its purpose. For example, they will state that a "pen" is used for writing and a "cup" is for drinking. Students will use simple sentences like "This is a [object]" and "A [object] is for [purpose]." They will categorize objects into groups like "things to eat" or "things to play with." Vocabulary will be reinforced with pictures of real-world items, such as a Euro coin, a football from the Bundesliga, or a sign for the Autobahn.

## Action Verbs in Daily Life
This topic focuses on common action verbs used to describe daily activities. Students will learn the base form of verbs like "run," "walk," "read," "write," "eat," "drink," and "play." They will practice forming simple sentences in the present tense, such as "I run in the park" or "She reads a book." They will use adverbs like "quickly" and "slowly" to describe how actions are performed. Instruction will include physical actions where students demonstrate verbs like "jump" or "sing," making learning concrete and kinesthetic.

## Family Members and Personal Information
Children will learn the vocabulary for immediate family members, including "mother," "father," "sister," "brother," and "baby." They will practice asking and answering questions about their families, using "How many people are in your family?" and "I have a [family member]." They will be able to describe their family members using simple adjectives, such as "My father is tall" or "My sister is kind." These concepts will be taught in context, encouraging students to share about their own lives while respecting differences.

## Numbers, Dates, and Time Measurement
Pupils will extend their number knowledge, focusing on numbers up to 100 for counting and simple calculations. They will learn to tell the time to the full hour and half hour, using "It is three o'clock" and "It is half past four." Days of the week, months, and dates will be taught, allowing students to state their birthday ("My birthday is in December") and understand a calendar. They will relate these concepts to their own schedules, such as "School starts at eight o'clock" or "We have a football match on Saturday."

## German Culture and Simple Descriptions
Students will be introduced to iconic elements of German culture to provide a real-world context for their language learning. They will learn to identify and name items like a "pretzel," a "Bundesliga football," and a "Christmas market." They will use simple adjectives to describe these things, for instance, "The Christmas market is beautiful" or "The Autobahn is very long." This section aims to build cultural awareness and pride by connecting vocabulary to familiar, tangible aspects of German life, enhancing both language skills and social understanding.
