# German (Lehrplan) Grade 11 English

## Literary Analysis and Interpretation
Students will deepen their ability to analyse complex literary texts, including novels, plays, and poetry from various English-speaking cultures. They will learn to identify and discuss advanced literary devices such as symbolism, irony, and foreshadowing, and their effects on the reader. Emphasis is placed on constructing evidence-based arguments, using specific textual references to support interpretations. For example, they might analyse the symbolic use of weather in a Shakespearean tragedy or the political satire in a modern dystopian novel. Students will practice writing analytical essays that demonstrate a sophisticated understanding of theme, character development, and narrative structure.

## Academic Writing and Research
This module focuses on developing advanced academic writing skills necessary for university and professional contexts. Students will learn to structure and compose formal essays, research papers, and argumentative texts with a clear thesis, logical organisation, and credible evidence. They will master the correct use of in-text citations and bibliographic formatting according to a specific style guide, such as APA or MLA. Key skills include paraphrasing, summarising, and integrating secondary sources effectively. A practical task involves researching a current social issue, such as environmental policy in the EU, and writing a position paper that incorporates data and expert opinions.

## Advanced Grammar and Language Structure
Students will refine their control of English grammar and syntax to achieve a higher level of precision and fluency. This includes mastery of complex sentence structures (e.g., subordinate clauses, participial phrases), advanced verb tenses (perfect conditional, past perfect), and sophisticated connectors. The curriculum covers nuanced points of usage, such as subjunctive mood in formal requests, inversion for emphasis, and the use of gerunds and infinitives. Students will learn to identify and correct common errors in their own and others' writing, producing polished texts suitable for an academic audience.

## Media Literacy and Critical Discourse
Students will develop critical skills to analyse and deconstruct a wide range of media texts. They will learn to identify the author's purpose, target audience, and underlying bias in news articles, opinion pieces, advertisements, and social media content. The module covers rhetorical strategies, including logical fallacies, emotional appeals (pathos), and credibility appeals (ethos). A key exercise involves comparing the coverage of a single event, such as a Bundesliga match or a major economic announcement, across different German and international news outlets to identify differing perspectives and framing.

## Intercultural Communication and Global Issues
This unit explores the nuances of communication across cultures, focusing on the English-speaking world within a global context. Students will study topics such as cultural dimensions (e.g., individualism vs. collectivism), non-verbal communication, and the role of English as a lingua franca. They will engage with global issues, analysing complex topics like climate change, migration, or global trade through English-language texts from diverse sources. Discussions and presentations require students to articulate different viewpoints respectfully, using specific examples from international relations and cultural phenomena like the EU's internal market or global film industries.

## Oral Presentation and Debating Skills
Students will enhance their ability to deliver structured and persuasive oral presentations and participate in formal debates. They will learn to organise information logically, use visual aids effectively (e.g., PowerPoint presentations), and engage their audience through clear articulation and confident body language. The curriculum covers techniques for constructing and refuting arguments, asking insightful questions, and managing the flow of discussion. A practical assessment involves preparing and delivering a persuasive speech on a topic relevant to Germany, such as the future of the Autobahn system or the cultural significance of Christmas markets, and defending their position in a formal debate.
