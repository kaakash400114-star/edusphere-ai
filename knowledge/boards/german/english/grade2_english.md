# German (Lehrplan) Grade 2 English

## Greetings and Introductions
Children learn to use simple phrases to greet others and introduce themselves. They practice saying 'Hello', 'Goodbye', and 'How are you?'. The responses 'I'm fine, thank you' and 'I'm not very well' are taught. Pupils learn to ask and answer 'What's your name?'. They practice using their own name, for example, 'My name is Anna.' and 'What's your name? My name is Tom.' These interactions are role-played using classroom objects like a pencil case (Federmäppchen) or a book (Buch) to prompt conversation.

## Family and Friends
Pupils learn vocabulary for immediate family members. They use words like 'mother', 'father', 'brother', and 'sister'. The possessive adjective 'my' is introduced, creating phrases such as 'This is my mother.' They learn to describe people using basic adjectives: 'tall', 'short', 'young', 'old'. Example sentences include 'My brother is young.' and 'My friend is tall.' This is reinforced by drawing and labelling a simple family tree (Familienstammbaum).

## My School Day
Children learn vocabulary related to their daily school routine. They use terms like 'school', 'classroom', 'teacher', and 'pupil'. They learn to say 'We read a book.' and 'We write in our notebook.' The phrase 'I like...' is introduced for subjects, for example, 'I like English.' and 'I like maths.' They also learn to say 'I don't like...'. These phrases are practised by talking about their own timetable (Stundenplan).

## My Favourite Things
Pupils expand their vocabulary for personal interests. They learn words for toys, games, and animals. The sentence structure 'I have a...' is used, creating examples like 'I have a teddy bear.' and 'I have a football.' The phrase 'My favourite...' is taught, so children can say 'My favourite animal is the dog.' This is linked to personal experiences, such as 'I go to the Christmas market (Weihnachtsmarkt) with my family.'

## At the Playground
Children learn vocabulary for common playground activities. They use action verbs like 'play', 'run', 'skip', and 'slide'. They learn to ask 'Can you play?' and respond with 'Yes, I can.' or 'No, I can't.' Example dialogues include 'Can you run? Yes, I can run fast.' These phrases are practised through simple action games mimicking activities like kicking a football on the Autobahn Spielplatz.

## Daily Life and Food
Pupils learn common food and drink vocabulary. They use words like 'bread', 'milk', 'apple', and 'water'. The phrase 'I like...' is extended to food, for example, 'I like bread.' They learn to ask 'Do you like apples?' and answer with 'Yes, I do.' or 'No, I don't.' This is connected to everyday life, discussing what they eat for breakfast (Frühstück) or their favourite drink, like Apfelsaft (apple juice).
