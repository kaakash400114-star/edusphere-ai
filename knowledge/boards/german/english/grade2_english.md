# German (Lehrplan) Grade 2 English


## Greetings and Introductions
Children learn to use simple phrases to greet others and introduce themselves. They practice saying 'Hello', 'Goodbye', and 'How are you?'. The responses 'I'm fine, thank you' and 'I'm not very well' are taught. Pupils learn to ask and answer 'What's your name?'. They practice using their own name, for example, 'My name is Anna.' and 'What's your name? My name is Tom.' These interactions are role-played using classroom objects like a pencil case (Federmäppchen) or a book (Buch) to prompt conversation.

## Family and Friends
Pupils master the vocabulary for immediate family members including mother, father, brother, and sister. The correct application of the possessive adjective 'my' is central, enabling students to form descriptive sentences such as 'This is my mother.' They are instructed to identify and use basic adjectives like 'tall', 'short', 'young', and 'old' to describe people, for instance in the sentences 'My brother is young' or 'My friend is tall'. The practical method of drawing and labelling a simple family tree (Familienstammbaum) reinforces this vocabulary and grammatical structure. Through this structured activity, students solidify their ability to identify and describe relations using both nouns and adjectives.

## My School Day
Pupils acquire thematic vocabulary for describing their school environment and activities, including terms such as 'schoolbag', 'pencil case', 'break time', 'lesson', and 'homework'. They learn to formulate complete sentences using the present simple tense to express daily routines, such as "We start our lessons at eight o'clock" and "I put my books in my desk". The core concept of expressing personal preferences is taught through the structures "I like..." and "I don't like..." applied to subjects, for example "I like art" or "I don't like sports". These linguistic structures are practised in communicative activities where pupils describe their own timetables and discuss their favourite school subjects.

## My Favourite Things
Pupils expand their vocabulary for personal interests. They learn words for toys, games, and animals. The sentence structure 'I have a...' is used, creating examples like 'I have a teddy bear.' and 'I have a football.' The phrase 'My favourite...' is taught, so children can say 'My favourite animal is the dog.' This is linked to personal experiences, such as 'I go to the Christmas market (Weihnachtsmarkt) with my family.'

## At the Playground
Students acquire thematic vocabulary for playground equipment and activities, including nouns such as 'swing', 'slide', and 'see-saw', and action verbs like 'climb', 'push', and 'swing'. The core grammatical focus is the modal verb 'can' to inquire about and describe abilities, practising question structures like 'Can you climb the ladder?' and affirmative responses such as 'Yes, I can climb quickly.' A key communicative function involves forming short dialogues to negotiate play, using phrases like 'Can I play on the swing?' and 'Yes, you can.' These linguistic concepts are applied through structured role-play scenarios where students act out playground interactions and use comparative adverbs such as 'high' and 'fast' to describe their actions in context.

## Daily Life and Food
Pupils acquire a targeted vocabulary for food and drink, including core items like 'bread', 'milk', 'fruit', and 'water'. The learning process involves applying the structural formula "I like..." for expressing personal food preferences, for example, "I like cheese." This is extended to the question-and-answer pattern "Do you like...?" with the corresponding formulas for affirmation ("Yes, I do.") and negation ("No, I don't."). Students practice these linguistic structures within the authentic context of daily routines, specifically by discussing meals such as breakfast ('Frühstück') or drinks like Apfelsaft ('apple juice'). This thematic unit ensures the practical application of learned language skills in relatable scenarios.
