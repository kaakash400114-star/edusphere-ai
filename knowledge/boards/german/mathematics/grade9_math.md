# German (Lehrplan) Grade 9 Mathematics  

## Linear Functions and their Applications
Students master the representation of linear functions using the formula y = mx + b, where m is the slope and b is the y-intercept. They learn to calculate the slope from two points (P₁, P₂) using m = (y₂ - y₁)/(x₂ - x₁). Practical applications include determining the cost of Autobahn tolls based on distance, where the fixed fee is the y-intercept and the per-kilometer charge is the slope. Students graph functions and solve real-world problems, such as calculating the total cost of Bundesliga tickets with a base price plus a per-game fee.

## Quadratic Equations and Parabolas
Students solve quadratic equations of the form ax² + bx + c = 0 using factorization, completing the square, and the quadratic formula: x = (-b ± √(b² - 4ac))/(2a). They analyze parabolas by identifying the vertex, axis of symmetry, and roots. Real-world examples include calculating the optimal price for Christmas market stall rentals to maximize profit, modeled by a quadratic function. Students also determine the height of a projectile over time, demonstrating how parabolas describe real-life trajectories.

## Probability and Statistics
Students calculate probabilities of single and compound events, including independent and dependent events. They use tree diagrams and contingency tables to visualize outcomes. Statistical measures involve calculating mean, median, mode, and range for datasets, such as Bundesliga match attendance. Students analyze the probability of drawing a specific Euro coin from a mixed collection and use statistical data to compare the performance of different German states in standardized math tests, fostering data-driven decision-making.

## Trigonometry in Right-Angled Triangles
Students apply trigonometric ratios (sin, cos, tan) to solve right-angled triangles. They use SOHCAHTOA to find unknown side lengths or angles. Practical problems include calculating the height of a Christmas tree using its shadow and the angle of elevation. Students determine the shortest distance across a section of the Rhine River using trigonometry. Exercises involve real-world scenarios like the steepness of Autobahn ramps or the angle of a ladder leaning against a wall.

## Systems of Linear Equations
Students solve systems of two linear equations using substitution, elimination, and graphical methods. They analyze solutions for consistency (one, infinite, or no solutions). Applications include determining the break-even point for a business selling handmade crafts at a Christmas market, where revenue equals costs. Students calculate the number of tickets to sell for a Bundesliga match to cover fixed and variable costs. Mastery ensures proficiency in solving multi-variable problems encountered in economics and engineering.

## Geometric Transformations and Similarity
Students master the core concepts of geometric transformations: translations (shifting figures without rotation or reflection), rotations (turning figures around a fixed point), reflections (flipping figures across a line), and dilations (resizing figures from a fixed point). They understand that translations, rotations, and reflections produce congruent figures, while dilations create similar figures with a constant scale factor relating corresponding dimensions. For example, when scaling a model of a Cologne cathedral, the scale factor determines the relationship between the model's height and the actual structure's height. To apply this, students can calculate the similarity ratio for Bundesliga pitch markings to ensure they are proportional to the regulations, demonstrating how these mathematical principles underpin real-world design and engineering tasks in Germany.
