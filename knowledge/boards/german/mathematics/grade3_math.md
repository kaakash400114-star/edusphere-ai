# German (Lehrplan) Grade 3 Mathematics

## Numbers up to 1000 and Their Representation
Students learn to read, write, and represent numbers up to 1000. They understand the place value of ones, tens, and hundreds (e.g., 375 means 3 hundreds, 7 tens, 5 ones). They practice decomposing numbers into expanded form (e.g., 486 = 400 + 80 + 6). Using German examples, they compare and order numbers like ticket prices at a Christmas market (e.g., €5.50 vs. €8.20). Students also learn to round numbers to the nearest ten or hundred, such as calculating the closest Autobahn exit for a destination.

## Addition and Subtraction of Two-Digit and Three-Digit Numbers
Students master written addition and subtraction methods for numbers up to 1000. They learn to use the column method, ensuring digits are aligned correctly by place value. For regrouping, they understand "carrying over" in addition (e.g., 16 + 8 = 24, write 4, carry over 2) and "borrowing" in subtraction (e.g., 42 - 17 = 25, borrow 1 ten). They solve practical problems, like calculating the total attendance at a Bundesliga match (e.g., 51,234 + 2,456) or finding the difference in points between two teams.

## Multiplication and Division Basics
Students learn multiplication as repeated addition (e.g., 4 × 7 = 7 + 7 + 7 + 7) and division as sharing or grouping. They memorize multiplication tables up to 10 × 10 and use them to solve simple problems. For example, they might calculate how many Bundesliga scarves a shop has if they have 5 boxes of 12 scarves each (multiplication) or how many fans can share 60 tickets equally in 5 groups (division). They also solve word problems involving equal groups or sharing items like chocolates or stickers.

## Time, Money, and Measurement
Students learn to tell time accurately to the minute on both analogue and digital clocks. They calculate durations, such as how long a football match lasts (90 minutes) or the time between two trains. For money, they use euros and cents, calculating amounts and making change (e.g., paying €10 for an item costing €7.50). In measurement, they work with length (meters, centimeters), mass (grams, kilograms), and volume (liters). Examples include measuring the length of a model Autobahn or weighing ingredients for baking.

## Geometry and Spatial Awareness
Students identify and classify 2D and 3D shapes, focusing on properties like sides, vertices, and faces. They learn about triangles, quadrilaterals, circles, cubes, cuboids, and pyramids. They recognize shapes in everyday objects, such as a Christmas market stall (cuboid) or a pyramid-shaped tree decoration. Students also explore symmetry, identifying lines of symmetry in shapes or flags. They practice simple coordinate drawing on a grid, plotting points to create a picture or a map layout.

## Data Handling and Simple Statistics
Students collect, organize, and represent data using tally charts, pictograms, and bar charts. They interpret data by answering questions like "How many more students prefer football than basketball?" (referencing Bundesliga popularity). They learn to create a simple survey, such as asking classmates about their favorite Christmas market treat, and present the results in a bar chart. Students also calculate the mode (most frequent value) and range (difference between highest and lowest values) from small data sets.
