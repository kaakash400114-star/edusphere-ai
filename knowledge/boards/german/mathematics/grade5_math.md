# German (Lehrplan) Grade 5 Mathematics

## Natural Numbers and Operations
Students consolidate their understanding of natural numbers up to one million. They practice written methods for addition and subtraction with large numbers, ensuring correct alignment of place values. For multiplication, they focus on multiplying multi-digit numbers using the standard written algorithm, with examples like calculating the total capacity of a soccer stadium with 50,000 seats. Division extends to long division, dividing numbers with up to three digits by one-digit numbers, using practical examples such as distributing 240 kg of gingerbread from a Christmas market among 8 stalls.

## Fractions
Students deepen their knowledge of fractions, learning to find equivalent fractions by scaling the numerator and denominator. They add and subtract fractions with the same or easily convertible denominators, for example, calculating 1/4 litre of milk plus 1/2 litre for a recipe. They learn to compare fractions and to order them on a number line, relating this to real-world contexts like sharing a whole pizza among friends. The term 'Vereinzelter' is introduced for simplifying fractions to their lowest terms.

## Geometry
In geometry, students explore properties of 2D and 3D shapes. They identify and classify polygons, including regular and irregular shapes. They learn to calculate the perimeter and area of rectangles and squares using formulas (Perimeter = 2 × (length + width), Area = length × width). For example, calculating the area of a football pitch in square meters. The concept of volume is introduced using cubic units, with students building models from cubes to understand how it measures the space a 3D object occupies, like a toy box.

## Measurement
Students work with units of measurement relevant to their daily lives. They learn to convert between different units of length (e.g., km, m, cm), weight (kg, g), and volume (l, ml), applying this in practical scenarios like converting a 4.5 km Autobahn journey into meters. They read and interpret scales on measuring tools. Time-telling skills are extended to calculating durations and converting between hours and minutes, such as calculating the total time for a Bundesliga football match, including halftime.

## Data and Chance
Students learn to collect, organize, and interpret data using bar charts and pictograms. They learn to read and create scaled axes, for example, plotting the number of goals scored by each Bundesliga team over a season. The concept of chance is introduced using simple probability terms like 'certain', 'possible', and 'impossible'. They conduct simple experiments, such as rolling a die, to discuss the likelihood of different outcomes and connect this to everyday decisions like the weather forecast for a Christmas market.

## Problem Solving and Applications
This section focuses on applying mathematical knowledge to solve structured word problems. Students are taught to identify the relevant information, choose the correct operation, and check the reasonableness of their answers. Problems are set in familiar German contexts, such as calculating the total cost of 7 kg of roasted almonds at a Christmas market stall priced at €8.50 per kilogram or determining the average speed of a car travelling 300 km on the Autobahn in 3 hours.
