# German (Lehrplan) Grade 2 Mathematics  
## Number Range to 100  
Students learn to read, write, and understand numbers up to 100. They practice counting forwards and backwards in various steps, including by ones, twos, fives, and tens. Number lines and hundred grids are used to visualize sequences and find missing numbers. Pupils compare and order numbers using terms like 'greater than', 'less than', and 'equal to'. For example, they determine that 65 is less than 78 and arrange numbers from smallest to largest. Real-world connections include counting euros in a piggy bank or counting items at a Christmas market stall.  

## Addition and Subtraction to 100  
Students master addition and subtraction within 100, using methods like jump strategies on a number line and columnar operations for two-digit numbers with and without carrying over/borrowing. They solve simple word problems, such as calculating the total cost of two items in euros or finding the remaining distance to the Autobahn exit. Key vocabulary includes 'sum', 'difference', and 'inverse operation'. For example, they solve 45 + 23 and 78 - 34, verifying answers with estimation or inverse calculations to build accuracy and fluency.  

## Multiplication and Division Introduction  
Students are introduced to multiplication as repeated addition and division as sharing or grouping. They use concrete materials like counters or drawings to model problems. For instance, 3 x 4 is understood as 'three groups of four', while 12 ÷ 3 means 'sharing twelve items among three friends'. Simple multiplication tables (2s, 5s, 10s) are practiced using examples like counting Bundesliga team points or calculating the total number of wheels on five bicycles. Pupils solve basic problems to build a foundational understanding of these operations.  

## Geometry and Spatial Awareness  
Students explore 2D and 3D shapes, identifying and naming them (e.g., square, cube, circle, cylinder). They sort shapes based on properties like the number of sides, vertices, or curved/flat surfaces. Using tangrams or building blocks, they compose and decompose shapes. Real-world examples include recognizing a soccer ball (sphere) or the shape of a Christmas market pyramid (pyramid). Pupils describe positions using terms like 'above', 'below', 'next to', and 'behind', such as locating a toy in a room or describing the route on a Autobahn map.  

## Measurement and Time  
Students learn to measure and compare lengths, masses, and volumes using non-standard and standard units (e.g., centimeters, grams, liters). They use rulers, scales, and measuring cups in practical activities, such as weighing ingredients for a recipe or comparing the heights of classmates. Time is taught to the hour and half-hour using analogue clocks, with real-world examples like the start time of a Bundesliga match or the closing time of a Christmas market. Students also read simple timetables and identify the sequence of daily events.  

## Data Handling and Patterns  
Students collect, organize, and represent data using tally charts, pictograms, and simple bar graphs. For example, they might survey classmates on their favorite Bundesliga team or the most visited Christmas market stall and present the findings. They recognize, describe, and extend patterns in numbers, shapes, or objects, such as alternating colors or number sequences (e.g., 2, 4, 6, 8). Pupils use logical reasoning to predict the next element in a pattern, like the sequence of traffic lights on the Autobahn, connecting math to everyday observations.
