# German (Lehrplan) Grade 1 Mathematics

## Number Sense up to 20
Students learn to count forwards and backwards to 20 from any given number. They identify and write numbers as digits and words. A core skill is recognising number patterns and sequences, such as odd and even numbers. Using the number line, they determine the number before, after, and between two given numbers. For example, they identify the number that comes after 14 and the number that comes before 17. They connect quantities to their numerical representation, understanding that '15' represents fifteen objects like footballs in a Bundesliga goal net.

## Addition and Subtraction within 20
Students are introduced to addition and subtraction using real-world objects and visual aids. They learn to add and subtract small numbers using methods like counting on and counting back. They understand that '+' means combining groups and '-' means taking away. A practical example is calculating the total cost of two items costing 4 euros and 6 euros. Another involves subtraction, like finding how many Christmas cookies are left after eating 5 from an initial 10. They begin to learn number bonds that make ten, a foundational concept for mental arithmetic.

## Spatial Awareness and 2D Shapes
Children develop their understanding of space and shape. They learn to name and describe common 2D shapes such as circle, square, triangle, and rectangle. They identify these shapes in their environment, recognising a square-shaped poster at a Christmas market or a triangular road sign near the Autobahn. Students practise distinguishing between shapes based on properties like the number of sides and corners. They use positional language to describe an object's location, using phrases like 'above', 'below', 'next to', and 'behind' to describe the placement of items on a table.

## Measurement and Comparison
Students learn to compare objects based on different attributes. They compare lengths, masses, and volumes using direct comparison. For example, they use a balance scale to determine whether a football is heavier than a tennis ball. They compare the lengths of two ribbons to see which is longer. They also learn to compare the capacity of two containers by pouring sand or water. They use specific vocabulary like 'heavier', 'lighter', 'longer', 'shorter', 'full', and 'empty' to describe their findings accurately.

## Data Collection and Representation
In this introductory unit, students learn how to gather and display simple data. They conduct class surveys by asking questions like, "What is your favourite Bundesliga team?" They record the results using tally marks. They then represent the collected data in a simple pictograph, where one picture symbol represents one vote. This visual representation helps them to interpret the data, answering questions like, "Which team was the most popular?" This early work in statistics fosters an understanding of information organisation.

## Patterns and Number Sequences
Students identify, describe, and create patterns. They recognise and continue patterns made with colours, shapes, sounds, or numbers. A simple example is completing a pattern like 'red, blue, red, blue'. They also explore number patterns, such as counting in twos (2, 4, 6) or fives (5, 10, 15). They learn to extend these sequences and identify the rule governing them. This skill helps them to predict what comes next and understand the regularity in mathematics, which is fundamental for later algebraic thinking.
