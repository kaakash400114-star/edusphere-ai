# German (Lehrplan) Grade 11 Mathematics

## Differential Calculus and Applications
Students deepen their understanding of differentiation rules, including the product, quotient, and chain rules, to find the derivatives of complex functions. They learn to differentiate polynomial, exponential, logarithmic, and trigonometric functions. The derivative f'(x) is used to analyse the rate of change of a function, with applications to motion problems, such as calculating the speed of a car on the Autobahn given its position function as a function of time. Students also learn to use the derivative to find critical points and determine whether a function is increasing or decreasing.

## Integral Calculus and Its Applications
This section introduces the concept of integration as the reverse process of differentiation. Students learn the fundamental theorem of calculus and how to compute definite and indefinite integrals using basic integration rules. They apply integration techniques, such as substitution, to find the area under a curve. A key example is calculating the total distance traveled by a train from a velocity function. Students also learn to solve simple differential equations and apply integration to compute volumes of revolution, such as finding the volume of a glass vase formed by rotating a curve around an axis.

## Sequences and Series
Students study arithmetic and geometric sequences, learning to find the nth term and the sum of the first n terms. A geometric sequence, for example, could model the depreciation of a car's value over time. They explore convergent and divergent series and learn to determine the limit of a sequence. The concept of an infinite geometric series is applied to financial mathematics, such as calculating the total payout of a lifetime annuity in euros. Students also touch on simple binomial expansions for expressions like (a + b)ⁿ.

## Stochastics: Probability and Combinatorics
This section covers advanced topics in probability, including conditional probability, Bayes' theorem, and the concept of independent events. Students use combinatorial principles to calculate probabilities in complex scenarios, such as determining the likelihood of a particular team winning the Bundesliga based on historical data. They learn to distinguish between permutations and combinations, applying these to problems like calculating the number of possible seating arrangements or lottery outcomes. The emphasis is on systematic problem-solving and modeling real-world probabilistic situations.

## Functions and Their Graphs
Students extend their knowledge of functions by exploring more complex types, including piecewise, inverse, and composite functions. They learn to analyse the properties of functions, such as domain, range, asymptotes, and continuity. Using real-world data, for example, they might model the attendance at a Christmas market over a month with a sinusoidal function. Students also learn to sketch graphs of functions, including transformations like translations and stretches, and to interpret graphs in the context of applied problems, such as profit maximization for a business.

## Financial Mathematics in Context
This practical module focuses on applying mathematical concepts to financial scenarios. Students learn to perform calculations related to simple and compound interest, annuities, and loans. A key example is determining the monthly mortgage payment for a house in Germany using the annuity formula. They also study investment strategies, such as comparing the growth of savings accounts versus stocks over time, and use exponential functions to model long-term financial growth. The goal is to equip students with the quantitative skills needed for personal finance and economic decision-making.
