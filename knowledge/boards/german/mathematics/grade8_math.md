# German (Lehrplan) Grade 8 Mathematics  
## Linear Equations with One Variable  
Students learn to solve linear equations of the form ax + b = c, where a, b, and c are real numbers. The method involves isolating the variable by performing inverse operations. For example, to solve 3x - 7 = 14, add 7 to both sides (3x = 21), then divide by 3 (x = 7). Equations may include fractions or decimals, requiring careful arithmetic. Real-world contexts like calculating the cost of items (e.g., 3 tickets for €15 each plus a €2 fee) reinforce understanding. Students must verify solutions by substitution.  

## Proportional and Inversely Proportional Relationships  
Pupils explore proportional (y = kx) and inversely proportional (y = k/x) relationships. They identify the constant of proportionality, k, from tables, graphs, or word problems. For instance, a car traveling at 80 km/h covers 160 km in 2 hours (k = 80). Inverse proportion is applied to scenarios like speed and travel time. Exercises include calculating fuel consumption rates (liters per 100 km) or population density. Students interpret graphs and distinguish between linear and hyperbolic relationships.  

## Pythagorean Theorem and Right-Angled Triangles  
The Pythagorean Theorem (a² + b² = c²) is used to find unknown sides in right-angled triangles. Students apply it to problems involving distances, such as determining the diagonal of a rectangular field. For example, a ladder leaning against a wall forms a 3-4-5 triangle. The theorem is extended to word problems involving Autobahn ramps or construction. Pupils classify triangles as right-angled, acute, or obtuse based on side lengths. Constructions with compass and protractor reinforce geometric understanding.  

## Statistical Data Analysis  
Students analyze data using mean, median, mode, and range. They interpret frequency tables and bar charts, calculating central tendencies for datasets like Bundesliga matchday attendance. Grouped data is handled with estimated means. Box plots and cumulative frequency graphs illustrate data distribution. Students evaluate sources of bias (e.g., surveying only Christmas market visitors) and distinguish between primary and secondary data. Probability basics include calculating chances for simple events, like rolling a die.  

## Linear Functions and Their Graphs  
Linear functions (f(x) = mx + c) are graphed in the coordinate system. Pupils determine slope (m) and y-intercept (c) from equations or tables. Real-world examples include converting temperatures (°C to °F) or calculating taxi fares (base fee + rate per km). Parallel and perpendicular lines are identified by slopes (e.g., Autobahn lanes with same gradient). Students solve systems of linear equations graphically or algebraically, finding intersection points. Word problems involve budgeting or speed/distance/time relationships.  

## Number Sequences and Patterns  
Arithmetic and geometric sequences are studied, with students finding the nth term formula. For arithmetic sequences (e.g., 2, 5, 8, ...), the formula is aₙ = a₁ + (n-1)d. Geometric sequences (e.g., 3, 6, 12, ...) use aₙ = a₁·r^(n-1). Patterns in nature or architecture (e.g., Fibonacci sequence) are discussed. Students solve problems involving loan repayments (compound interest) or population growth. Recursive formulas are introduced, with exercises predicting future terms or sums of series.
