# German (Lehrplan) Grade 7 Mathematics

## Algebraic Expressions and Equations
Students learn to simplify and manipulate algebraic expressions by combining like terms and applying the distributive property. They solve linear equations with one variable, including those requiring multiple steps and those involving fractions. Equations are solved systematically by isolating the variable using inverse operations. Example: Solve 3x + 5 = 2x - 7. Subtracting 2x from both sides yields x + 5 = -7. Subtracting 5 from both sides results in x = -12. Students also translate word problems into algebraic equations, such as calculating the cost of Bundesliga tickets after a discount.

## Rational Numbers and Percentages
Students extend their understanding to include positive and negative rational numbers, mastering addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. They convert between fractions, decimals, and percentages, applying this to real-world scenarios like calculating VAT on goods or a tip at a Christmas market. Percentage calculations involve finding a percentage of a quantity and determining percentage increase or decrease. For example, a 15% price increase on an item costing €80 results in a new price of €92. Students also solve problems involving interest rates and discounts.

## Geometric Figures and Properties
Students explore the properties of two-dimensional and three-dimensional shapes, including triangles, quadrilaterals, prisms, and pyramids. They calculate the perimeter and area of polygons, such as determining the area of a rectangular plot of land. For three-dimensional figures, they compute surface area and volume, using formulas for prisms and cylinders. The concept of scale is introduced, with students creating scale models of buildings or using maps to calculate actual distances, such as the length of a section of the Autobahn.

## Data Analysis and Probability
Students learn to organize and interpret data using various graphical representations, including bar charts, line graphs, and pie charts. They calculate measures of central tendency, such as the mean, median, and mode, to analyze data sets, like average attendance at a football match. Basic probability concepts are covered, including calculating the probability of simple events. For instance, the probability of drawing a red marble from a bag containing 5 red and 10 blue marbles is 5/15, or 1/3. Students also distinguish between theoretical and experimental probability.

## Functions and Relationships
Students are introduced to the concept of a function as a special relationship between two variables. They represent functions using tables, graphs, and algebraic rules. Linear functions are explored in depth, with students identifying the slope (gradient) and y-intercept from graphs and equations. For example, the function y = 2x + 3 represents a line with a slope of 2 and a y-intercept of 3. Real-world applications include modeling the cost of a taxi ride or the distance traveled over time, using German examples like fuel consumption rates.

## Integers and Coordinate Geometry
Students master operations with positive and negative integers, including addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division, applying rules for signs. They plot points and graph linear equations in the Cartesian coordinate system. Students calculate the distance between two points and the slope of a line connecting them. Practical applications include mapping locations, such as plotting the positions of different Christmas markets on a grid or calculating the slope of a hill using elevation data. The coordinate plane is used to solve geometric problems involving symmetry and transformations.
