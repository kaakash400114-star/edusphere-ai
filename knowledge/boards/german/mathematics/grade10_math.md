# German (Lehrplan) Grade 10 Mathematics  
## Quadratic Functions and Equations  
Students analyse quadratic functions of the form f(x) = ax² + bx + c. They determine the vertex, axis of symmetry, and roots using formulas like x = (-b ± √(b² - 4ac)) / (2a). Examples include calculating the optimal height of a Bundesliga goal kick for maximum distance or determining break-even points for selling Christmas market crafts. Students solve quadratic equations algebraically and graphically, interpreting solutions as x-intercepts. Real-world applications involve parabolic trajectories, such as the path of an Autobahn toll gate sensor's detection beam.  

## Trigonometric Ratios and Right-Angled Triangles  
Students apply sine, cosine, and tangent to solve right-angled triangles. They use SOHCAHTOA and trigonometric formulas, e.g., sin(θ) = opposite/hypotenuse. Problems include calculating the height of a church spire using angle measurements or determining the diagonal distance across a German soccer field. Students convert angles between degrees and radians, solving practical problems like the slope of a ski jump or the angle of elevation for a camera filming a Christmas market parade. Exact values for common angles (30°, 45°, 60°) are emphasized.  

## Linear Equations and Systems of Equations  
Students solve linear equations in one and two variables. Methods include substitution and elimination, applied to real-world scenarios like budgeting for a Euro trip or comparing the fuel efficiency of two cars. Students solve systems of equations with unique, infinite, or no solutions. Example problems involve calculating the break-even point for a small business selling Bundesliga memorabilia or determining the mix of two products to maximize profit from a Christmas market stall. Graphical interpretation of parallel, intersecting, and coinciding lines is reinforced.  

## Statistical Analysis and Probability  
Students analyze data using measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) and dispersion (range, variance, standard deviation). Examples include calculating the average attendance at Bundesliga matches or analyzing the distribution of daily temperatures in Berlin. Students use box plots and histograms to visualize data. Probability topics include calculating simple and compound probabilities, e.g., the likelihood of drawing a Bundesliga team's name from a hat or predicting the chance of rain during a Christmas market event. Conditional probability is introduced with real-world examples.  

## Geometric Transformations and Congruence  
Students study translations, rotations, reflections, and dilations in the coordinate plane. They apply congruence criteria (SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS) to prove triangle congruence. Examples include designing symmetrical patterns for a German flag or scaling a blueprint of a medieval castle. Students use vectors to describe translations and calculate magnitudes. Practical applications involve mapping Autobahn routes or determining if two roof trusses are congruent. The concept of similarity is explored, with examples like resizing a Christmas market poster while maintaining proportions.  

## Financial Mathematics and Exponential Growth  
Students solve problems involving compound interest, loans, and investments using formulas like A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt). Examples include calculating the total cost of a car loan over five years or determining the growth of a savings account for a Euro trip. Students compare simple and compound interest, analyzing scenarios like the appreciation of property values in Munich. Exponential growth and decay are applied to real-world situations, such as the spread of information or depreciation of electronics, using German currency and financial contexts for relatability.
