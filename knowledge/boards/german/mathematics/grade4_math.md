# German (Lehrplan) Grade 4 Mathematics

## Number Range up to 1,000,000
Students master numbers up to one million, understanding place value (Hundred Thousands, Ten Thousands, Thousands, Hundreds, Tens, Ones). They read, write, and compare large numbers, using commas to separate thousands (e.g., 456,789). Students practice rounding to the nearest ten and thousand, such as rounding the Autobahn distance of 487 kilometers to 500 kilometers. They also estimate large quantities, like estimating the number of visitors at a large Christmas market, to build number sense for real-world contexts.

## Multiplication and Division
Students learn the standard algorithm for multiplying multi-digit numbers, like calculating the cost of 23 tickets for a Bundesliga match at €45 each. They also use long division for numbers up to 10,000, dividing a total travel fund to calculate daily spending for a family holiday. Problem-solving involves finding missing factors, such as determining how many boxes of 15 cookies are needed for 120 children, reinforcing practical application of multiplication and division in daily scenarios.

## Fractions
Students explore equivalent fractions using fraction bars and diagrams, understanding that 1/2 is the same as 2/4 or 3/6. They add and subtract fractions with the same denominator, like combining 3/8 of a chocolate bar with 2/8 of another. Comparing fractions with different denominators involves finding a common base, such as determining if 2/5 or 3/8 is a larger piece of a German pretzel. Real-life examples include sharing portions of a meal or dividing a €10 pizza among friends.

## Geometry and Measurement
Students calculate the perimeter and area of rectangles using formulas (Perimeter = 2 × (length + width), Area = length × width), such as finding the area of a garden plot in a German town. They measure and draw angles with a protractor, identifying acute and obtuse angles. Practical tasks include measuring ingredients for a recipe in grams and milliliters or estimating the length of a model section of the Autobahn in centimeters, connecting geometry to everyday life.

## Data Representation
Students collect and organize data using tally charts and frequency tables, such as recording favorite items from a Christmas market survey. They represent this data in bar graphs and line plots, ensuring labeled axes and titles. For example, a bar graph could show the number of goals scored by different Bundesliga teams. Students interpret the data to answer questions, like identifying the most popular market stall, fostering skills in data analysis and visualization.

## Financial Mathematics
Students solve problems involving euros and cents, such as calculating the total cost of items bought at a bakery (e.g., a pretzel for €1.50 and a pastry for €2.30). They practice making change up to €100, like paying with a €50 bill for purchases totaling €32.70. Real-world scenarios include budgeting for a school event or comparing prices at a supermarket, reinforcing the practical use of money and arithmetic skills in everyday German life.
