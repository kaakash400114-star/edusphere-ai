# German (Lehrplan) Grade 2 Science  

## Living Things and Their Needs
Children learn that all living things, like plants, animals, and people, need specific things to survive. Plants need sunlight, water, and soil to grow, as seen in a school garden. Animals need food, water, and shelter. People need food, water, air, and a safe home. For example, a dog needs food and water daily, while a tree needs rain and sun. Children observe how plants grow toward light, showing their need for energy.

## Materials and Their Properties
Children systematically investigate fundamental material properties such as hardness, flexibility, transparency, and conductivity through guided experimentation. They learn to classify objects like wooden blocks, metal spoons, and plastic bottles by applying observable criteria, such as whether a material can be bent, scratched, or allows light to pass through. Practical activities involve testing materials by bending plastic rods to demonstrate flexibility, scratching surfaces with their fingernails to assess relative hardness, and observing how different materials conduct heat using containers filled with warm water. This process establishes a foundational scientific method of observation and comparison, enabling students to categorize everyday objects into groups like 'transparent' (glass), 'opaque' (wood), or 'malleable' (aluminium foil).

## Our Senses and the Environment
Students systematically explore the five human senses—sight, hearing, smell, taste, and touch—as fundamental tools for perceiving and interacting with the environment. Through structured observation, they identify the specific organs involved, such as eyes for sight and ears for hearing, and learn to correlate sensory input with physical phenomena. For instance, they distinguish between loud and quiet sounds in the schoolyard and classify smells as pleasant, like baking bread, or unpleasant, like garbage. They compare human sensory abilities to those of animals, such as the acute olfactory sense of a dog used for tracking. Finally, they apply this knowledge by using precise adjectives to describe environmental attributes, like the crisp, cool texture of autumn leaves or the sharp, sweet aroma of a pine forest.

## Weather and Seasons
Second-grade students in Germany are taught to systematically observe and record local weather phenomena using meteorological symbols for sun, cloud, rain, and wind. They learn to identify and describe the four distinct seasons, focusing on characteristic temperature ranges, appropriate clothing, and seasonal activities. For example, spring is recognised by mild temperatures, rain, and the activity of planting seeds. Summer is defined by high temperatures, abundant sunshine, and activities like swimming, while autumn is characterised by cooling temperatures, strong winds, and falling leaves. Winter is identified by low temperatures, frost, and snowfall, with activities such as building snowmen.

## Forces and Movement
Children explore forces like push and pull. They learn that a force makes things move or change shape. For example, pushing a swing makes it go higher, and pulling a drawer opens it. They experiment with ramps, balls, and toy cars. Gravity pulls things down, like a ball falling. Friction slows things, like a toy car stopping on carpet. Children describe forces in daily life, like kicking a Bundesliga ball.

## Light and Shadows
Children learn about light and how it travels. They discover that light comes from sources like the sun, lamps, and candles. Shadows form when light is blocked by an object. For example, a shadow appears behind a toy when a flashlight shines. Children observe how shadows change size and shape with the light’s angle. They explore natural light from the sun and artificial light from a desk lamp while drawing their shadows.
