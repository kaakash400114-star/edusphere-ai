# German (Lehrplan) Grade 5 Science
## Materials and Their Properties
Students explore how to classify materials based on their observable properties. They learn to differentiate between solids, liquids, and gases by examining their shape and volume. A key concept is density, calculated as mass divided by volume (ρ = m/V), using measurements taken in the lab. For example, a 2 cm³ cube of iron with a mass of 15.6 grams has a density of 7.8 g/cm³, much higher than a 2 cm³ piece of aluminium (mass 5.4 g, density 2.7 g/cm³). This explains why an iron key sinks in water while an aluminium foil boat can float, demonstrating real-world applications of material science.

## Forces and Simple Machines
This unit introduces the concept of force as a push or pull that can change an object's motion or shape. Students learn to identify and measure forces using a spring balance, with the unit of force being the Newton (N). They investigate simple machines like levers and pulleys. For instance, using a lever to lift a 10 N heavy box requires less effort than lifting it directly, demonstrating the principle of mechanical advantage. The Autobahn's steep ramps are a real-world example of how incline planes (a type of simple machine) are used to reduce the force needed for vehicles to climb hills safely.

## Energy and Energy Transfer
Students define energy as the ability to do work or cause change, focusing on kinetic and potential energy. They learn that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred from one form to another, a key principle of conservation of energy. A concrete example is a roller coaster: at its highest point, it has maximum potential energy, which converts to kinetic energy as it speeds down the track. They study energy transfer methods like conduction, where a warm mug of Glühwein from a Christmas market transfers heat to a cold hand, demonstrating heat flow from a hotter to a colder object.

## Our Solar System and the Sun
The solar system is introduced as the Sun and the objects that orbit it, including planets, moons, and asteroids. Students learn about the Sun's role as the central star, providing light and heat through nuclear fusion. They compare the planets, noting that Earth is the only known planet with liquid water. An astronomical fact is the distance from the Earth to the Sun (1 astronomical unit or AU), about 150 million kilometres. This immense distance is why it takes sunlight approximately 8 minutes and 20 seconds to reach us, a fundamental concept in understanding Earth's energy source.

## Weather and Climate
This section distinguishes between weather (short-term atmospheric conditions) and climate (long-term patterns). Students learn to record weather data using instruments like a thermometer (measuring temperature in °C), a rain gauge, and an anemometer (measuring wind speed in km/h). They analyse how weather can impact daily life, such as a sudden downpour cancelling a local football match. They are introduced to climate by comparing the temperate climate of Berlin with the warmer, sunnier climate of Munich, understanding that Germany's position in Europe influences its seasonal variations, including the warm summers and cold winters typical for its latitude.

## Living Things and Their Habitats
Students classify organisms as producers, consumers, or decomposers in a simple food chain. They learn that all living things need specific conditions to survive, such as food, water, and shelter. A producer like a grass uses sunlight to make its own food. A primary consumer, like a rabbit, eats the grass, and a secondary consumer, like a fox, eats the rabbit. The decomposer, like a fungus, breaks down dead matter. This cycle is evident in a German forest, where fallen leaves from oak trees are decomposed by organisms, returning nutrients to the soil to support new plant growth, maintaining the ecosystem's balance.
