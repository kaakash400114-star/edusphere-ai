# German (Lehrplan) Grade 1 Science

## Senses and the Body
Children learn to identify and name their five senses: sight, hearing, smell, taste, and touch. They explore how each sense helps them understand the world. They investigate the functions of body parts like eyes for seeing, ears for hearing, hands for touching, and noses for smelling. Through play-based activities, children identify different tastes (sweet, sour) and smells (flowers, bread). They use their senses to sort objects by their properties, such as sorting soft and hard toys or loud and quiet sounds, developing foundational skills for scientific observation and classification.

## Weather and Seasons
Children learn to describe and identify common types of weather (sunny, cloudy, rainy, windy, snowy) using simple German terms like 'sonnig' or 'regnerisch'. They observe and record daily weather patterns using symbols on a class weather chart. They explore the four seasons (Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter) and their typical characteristics, such as warm sun and blooming flowers in summer or cold air and snow in winter. Children identify seasonal activities like visiting a Weihnachtsmarkt (Christmas market) in winter or playing outside during a warm Sommer (summer).

## Materials and Properties
Children learn to identify and classify common materials such as wood, plastic, metal, glass, and fabric. They explore the properties of these materials by describing them using words like 'hart' (hard), 'weich' (soft), 'glatt' (smooth), and 'rau' (rough). Through hands-on experiments, children test materials to see if they float or sink in water, or if they are transparent or opaque. They discuss everyday objects made from these materials, such as a Holzbrett (wooden board) or a Plastikauto (plastic toy), linking material properties to their function and use.

## Plants and Animals
Children learn to identify and name common local plants, like trees and flowers, and common animals, like pets and garden insects. They observe the basic needs of living things: plants need water, light, and soil to grow, while animals need food, water, and shelter. Children learn about simple life cycles, such as the stages of a plant from seed to sprout, or the stages of a butterfly from egg to adult. They explore their local environment, identifying a local Vogel (bird) or Blume (flower) and discussing its habitat.

## My Local Environment
Children learn to identify key features of their local environment, such as houses, streets, parks, and shops. They explore natural and man-made objects, distinguishing between a Baum (tree) and a building. They begin to understand maps and simple plans of their classroom or school playground, identifying locations like the Spielplatz (playground). They discuss how people use their environment for work and play, connecting places to their daily lives, such as buying Brot (bread) from a local Bäckerei (bakery).

## Daily Life and Objects
Children explore and identify common objects used in daily life and their functions. They sort objects based on their purpose, such as objects used for eating (Messer, Gabel - knife, fork) or for transportation (Auto, Fahrrad - car, bicycle). They learn to describe the shape, size, and colour of familiar objects using precise vocabulary. They are introduced to simple measurement concepts by comparing objects based on length or height, such as determining which is longer, a Bleistift (pencil) or a Lineal (ruler). Children link these objects to cultural contexts, like a Fußball (football) used in a Bundesliga match.
