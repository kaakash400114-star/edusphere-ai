# German (Lehrplan) Grade 10 Science
## Fundamental Chemical Reactions and Stoichiometry
Students learn to define and identify chemical reactions, focusing on synthesis, decomposition, single and double displacement, and combustion. The law of conservation of mass is central. They apply stoichiometry to calculate product quantities using balanced equations and molar masses. For instance, calculating the mass of oxygen needed to combust 250 g of propane (C₃H₈), yielding carbon dioxide and water, demonstrates these principles. Mole conversions and the relationship between mass, moles, and molecules are practiced using the Avogadro constant.

## Energy Transformations and Thermodynamics
This section explores the principles of energy conversion, focusing on mechanical, thermal, and electrical energy. Students learn the first law of thermodynamics, understanding that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed. The concept of efficiency is quantified, comparing useful energy output to total energy input using the formula η = E_useful / E_total * 100%. They analyze the energy flow in a coal-fired power plant, tracking the conversion from chemical energy to thermal, then to kinetic, and finally electrical energy, accounting for significant losses as waste heat.

## Genetics and Molecular Biology
Students study the structure and function of DNA and RNA. They understand the central dogma: DNA is transcribed into mRNA, which is then translated into proteins. The process of protein synthesis is detailed, including the roles of codons and anticodons. Students learn to use a codon chart to determine the amino acid sequence from a given mRNA strand. Real-world examples include analysing the genetic basis for eye colour inheritance or exploring how mutations can lead to conditions like cystic fibrosis.

## Mechanics: Dynamics and Motion
Students apply Newton's laws of motion to analyze forces and resulting movement. They resolve forces acting on objects, like a car accelerating at 3 m/s² on the Autobahn, calculating the net force using F_net = m * a. Concepts of friction, normal force, and weight are differentiated. The equations of motion for constant acceleration (v = u + at, s = ut + ½at²) are used to solve for displacement and final velocity in practical scenarios, such as calculating the stopping distance for a vehicle decelerating at -7 m/s².

## Environmental Science: Ecosystems and Human Impact
This unit explores the structure and function of ecosystems, including energy flow (trophic levels) and nutrient cycles (carbon, nitrogen). Students investigate the significant human impact on these systems. A case study on the Baltic Sea's eutrophication demonstrates how agricultural runoff (nitrates and phosphates) leads to algal blooms and oxygen depletion. They evaluate Germany's environmental policies, such as the Renewable Energy Sources Act (EEG), which aims for a sustainable energy mix and reduce reliance on fossil fuels.

## Electricity and Magnetism
Students analyze electric circuits using Ohm's Law (V = I * R). They calculate current, voltage, and resistance in series and parallel configurations. Magnetic fields around current-carrying wires and the Earth's magnetic field are examined. The principle of electromagnetic induction is introduced, explaining how changing magnetic fields induce an electromotive force (EMF). This is applied to understanding the function of a simple generator in Germany's power grid, converting mechanical energy from wind turbines into electrical energy.
