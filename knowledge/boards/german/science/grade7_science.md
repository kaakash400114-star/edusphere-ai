# German (Lehrplan) Grade 7 Science

## The Structure of Matter and Chemical Reactions
Students learn that all matter is composed of atoms, which consist of a nucleus with protons and neutrons, surrounded by electrons. Atoms of different elements combine to form molecules and compounds. Chemical reactions involve the breaking and forming of chemical bonds. A reaction between iron (Fe) and sulphur (S) creates iron sulphide (FeS), demonstrating a chemical change. The law of conservation of mass states that mass cannot be created or destroyed in a chemical reaction, only rearranged, as shown when 20g of hydrogen reacts with 160g of oxygen to produce 180g of water.

## Forces and Motion in Everyday Life
This unit explores Newton's laws of motion. An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion at a constant velocity unless acted upon by an unbalanced force. Force equals mass times acceleration (F = m x a). When a 75 kg person decelerates from 10 m/s to 0 m/s in 5 seconds, the force exerted is 150 N. Friction and air resistance are forces that oppose motion. A car on the Autobahn requires continuous force to maintain speed, overcoming these resistive forces that align with the principles of classical mechanics.

## Energy Forms and Transformations
Students understand energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted from one form to another. A power plant converts chemical energy from coal into thermal energy, then into kinetic energy to spin a turbine, which finally generates electrical energy. In the human body, chemical energy from food is transformed into kinetic energy for movement and thermal energy to maintain body temperature. The principle of energy conservation dictates that the total energy in a closed system remains constant, whether a roller coaster at the top of a hill or a light bulb glowing in a German Christmas market.

## The Human Body: Nutrition and Respiration
This module focuses on the digestive and respiratory systems. The human digestive system breaks down food into nutrients like glucose, which are absorbed into the bloodstream. Respiration is the process where cells use oxygen to break down glucose, releasing energy in the form of ATP. The equation for cellular respiration is C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + energy. A typical diet for a 12-year-old might require 2000 kcal daily, with the respiratory system processing about 11,000 litres of air to supply the necessary oxygen for this energy production.

## Biodiversity and Ecosystems
Students study biodiversity as the variety of life in an ecosystem. Germany's ecosystems range from the North Sea coast to the Black Forest, each with distinct flora and fauna. An ecosystem consists of biotic (living) and abiotic (non-living) components that interact. A food web in a German forest might show how primary producers like oak trees are consumed by herbivores, such as deer, which are then prey for carnivores like foxes. Human activity, like building a new Autobahn, can disrupt these interactions, reducing local biodiversity through habitat fragmentation.

## The Water Cycle and Resource Management
This section explains the continuous movement of water on, above, and below the Earth's surface, driven by solar energy. The cycle includes evaporation from rivers like the Rhine, condensation into clouds, and precipitation as rain or snow. Germany manages its water resources through a highly regulated system, treating wastewater to a high standard before its release into rivers. The average person in Germany uses about 130 litres of water daily, highlighting the importance of sustainable management in a country where clean water is a critical resource for both industry and domestic use.
