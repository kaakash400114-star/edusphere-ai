# German (Lehrplan) Grade 4 Science

## Plant Life Cycles and Adaptations
Pupils learn the complete life cycle of a flowering plant, from seed germination to pollination and seed dispersal. They identify the essential requirements for plant growth: light, water, and specific nutrients through the roots. Students explore structural adaptations, such as deep roots for desert plants like the cactus for water storage and broad leaves for capturing sunlight. They also learn about how animals, like bees, help with pollination. A key example is observing how a sunflower turns its head to follow the sun, demonstrating phototropism, a response to light stimuli.

## States of Matter and Their Changes
This topic covers the three fundamental states of matter: solid, liquid, and gas. Students learn the defining properties: solids have a fixed shape and volume, liquids have a fixed volume but take the shape of their container, and gases have neither. They explore how adding or removing thermal energy causes state changes, such as ice melting to water or water boiling into steam. A practical example involves heating and cooling chocolate, observing it change from a solid to a liquid and back again. The concept of evaporation, like drying clothes on a line, and condensation, like water droplets on a cold bottle, are central to understanding.

## Forces and Motion in Everyday Life
Pupils investigate basic forces like push, pull, gravity, and friction. They learn that a force is a push or a pull that can change an object's movement or shape. Gravity is the force that pulls objects towards the Earth's center, while friction is a force that opposes motion. Using simple examples, students understand that a heavier object, like a football, requires more force to kick than a lighter one, such as a table tennis ball. They also learn about how smooth surfaces, like ice, have less friction, allowing objects to slide more easily.

## Human Body and Healthy Habits
This unit focuses on the human digestive system. Pupils learn the path food takes: from the mouth, where it is chewed and mixed with saliva, down the esophagus to the stomach for breakdown, and through the intestines for nutrient absorption. They identify major organs and their functions. The lesson connects to healthy living, explaining how a balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, and whole grains, like those found at a German Christmas market, provides the necessary energy and nutrients for the body to function properly.

## Our Solar System and Earth's Place in It
Students learn that the solar system consists of the Sun and objects that orbit it, including the eight planets. They order the planets by their distance from the Sun, starting with the inner, rocky planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) and moving to the outer, gaseous giants. A key concept is that Earth is the only known planet to support life, primarily due to its distance from the Sun, which provides the right amount of heat, and the presence of liquid water. They learn about Earth's rotation causing day and night and its revolution around the Sun causing the year.

## Weather and Climate Patterns in Germany
Pupils observe and describe local weather by measuring temperature, precipitation, and wind using simple instruments. They learn to distinguish between weather (short-term conditions) and climate (long-term patterns) in a specific region. They identify the characteristics of the four seasons in Germany: mild, wet winters and warm summers. Examples include the cold, snowy conditions of a Bavarian winter versus the warm days of a German summer. Students also discuss how weather can impact daily life and activities, like a Bundesliga match being postponed due to heavy rain or ice on the Autobahn in winter.
