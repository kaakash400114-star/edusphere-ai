# German (Lehrplan) Grade 6 Science
## Physics: Forces and Motion
Students learn that force causes a change in an object's state of motion or shape. Forces have magnitude and direction and are measured in Newtons (N), named after Sir Isaac Newton. Examples include the force of gravity pulling a ball towards Earth (approx. 9.8 N/kg) and the tension in a cable supporting a 50 kg bridge segment. An object is in equilibrium when the resultant force is zero. The Autobahn demonstrates high-speed motion, while a car braking experiences a significant deceleration force.

## Chemistry: Materials and Their Properties
Students classify materials as metals, non-metals, or polymers based on their observable properties like lustre, conductivity, and malleability. Iron is a common metal used in car bodies, while plastic (a polymer) is used for protective casings. Materials are separated using techniques such as filtration or magnetic attraction. The Bundesliga stadium seats are made from durable, waterproof polymer materials. Students conduct experiments to test a material's solubility in water, using 10 ml of water to dissolve 5 grams of common salt (sodium chloride).

## Biology: Cells as the Basic Units of Life
Students are introduced to the cell as the fundamental structural and functional unit of all living organisms. They learn to identify the cell membrane, cytoplasm, and nucleus using diagrams of a typical animal cell, such as a human skin cell. Plant cells additionally feature a rigid cell wall and chloroplasts for photosynthesis. Students compare a human cell (approx. 10-30 micrometres in diameter) to a bacterium (approx. 1-2 micrometres). This understanding is foundational for studying the human body and plant structures.

## Earth Science: Rocks, Minerals, and the Rock Cycle
Students learn that rocks are composed of minerals and are classified as igneous, sedimentary, or metamorphic. Granite, an igneous rock, is common in Germany and used for building. The rock cycle describes how rocks are transformed over millions of years through processes like heat, pressure, and weathering. The Black Forest is primarily composed of sandstone, a sedimentary rock formed from compressed sand. Students examine samples of different rocks and identify their primary mineral components.

## Environmental Science: Ecosystems and Interactions
Students study ecosystems as communities of living organisms (biotic factors) and their non-living environment (abiotic factors). An example is a local forest, where pine trees (biotic) interact with soil pH and rainfall (abiotic). Students learn about food chains, showing energy transfer, such as grass → rabbit → fox. A Christmas market ecosystem includes people (biotic) interacting with wooden stalls (abiotic) and generating waste. Human impact, such as littering, can disrupt this delicate balance and harm local wildlife.

## Technology and Engineering: Structures and Stability
Students analyze how forces act on structures like bridges and towers. They learn that a stable structure has a low centre of gravity and a wide, strong base. The design of a modern Autobahn bridge must support the immense weight and dynamic forces of traffic. Students apply this knowledge to design a stable model tower using materials like paper and straws, ensuring it can support a 100-gram weight. This involves understanding concepts like compression and tension to prevent the structure from collapsing.
