# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 5 Mathematics
## Number Sense and Operations
Students deepen their understanding of whole numbers and decimals, mastering operations up to 10,000. They learn to add and subtract five-digit numbers and multiply and divide by a one or two-digit number. Decimal numbers are introduced in the context of money, such as calculating the total cost of sauna essentials. Students practice rounding numbers to the nearest ten, hundred, and thousand, and estimate to check the reasonableness of their answers, like planning a trip to see the Northern Lights.

## Fractions and Percentages
This topic covers comparing and ordering fractions with different denominators and finding equivalent fractions. Students learn to add and subtract fractions with the same denominator. They connect fractions to percentages, understanding that half (½) is 50%. Using real-world examples, they calculate percentages of a quantity, such as figuring out a 10% discount on a Moomins toy in a shop, helping them apply these concepts to everyday financial situations.

## Geometry and Measurement
Students explore two-dimensional and three-dimensional shapes, identifying their properties like faces, edges, and vertices. They measure using metric units, including kilometres, metres, centimetres, grams, and litres. A practical application involves calculating the perimeter of a garden plot. They learn to convert between units, such as converting centimetres to metres, and estimate lengths or weights, like measuring the ingredients for a traditional Finnish rye bread recipe.

## Data Handling and Statistics
In this section, students learn to collect, organize, and interpret data using tables and bar graphs. They practice calculating the mean average of a set of numbers. They engage in projects like tracking daily temperatures for a week to create a graph or surveying their classmates' favourite seasons. This develops their ability to analyze information and draw conclusions, which is a key transversal competence for problem-solving in various contexts.

## Problem Solving and Logical Thinking
Students apply their mathematical skills to solve multi-step problems, requiring logical reasoning and planning. They use strategies like drawing diagrams or making lists. For example, a problem could involve planning a route through Helsinki and calculating distances in euros for public transport. This encourages creative thinking and collaboration, helping students understand how mathematics is used in real-life situations and planning, such as organizing a sauna evening with friends.

## Patterns and Algebraic Thinking
Students identify and create numerical patterns and sequences, learning to express them with simple rules. They solve one-step equations where a variable is an unknown number, such as x + 25 = 75, finding x = 50. This algebraic thinking is introduced using concrete examples, like figuring out how many more euros are needed to buy a specific Moomins book, linking abstract concepts to tangible Finnish experiences and fostering early analytical skills.
