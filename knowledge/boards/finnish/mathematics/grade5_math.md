# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 5 Mathematics

## Number Sense and Operations
Students deepen their understanding of whole numbers and decimals, mastering operations up to 10,000. They learn to add and subtract five-digit numbers and multiply and divide by a one or two-digit number. Decimal numbers are introduced in the context of money, such as calculating the total cost of sauna essentials. Students practice rounding numbers to the nearest ten, hundred, and thousand, and estimate to check the reasonableness of their answers, like planning a trip to see the Northern Lights.

## Fractions and Percentages
Students learn to find equivalent fractions by multiplying or dividing both the numerator and the denominator by the same number, allowing them to compare and order fractions with different denominators. They add and subtract fractions with the same denominator by keeping the denominator and adding or subtracting the numerators, simplifying the result to its lowest terms. Fractions are connected to percentages, where the whole is represented by 100%, so one half (½) is 50% and one quarter (¼) is 25%. Using this connection, they calculate percentages of quantities, for instance, finding the discount on a Moomins toy by calculating 10% of the price. These skills are applied to solve real-world problems involving parts of a whole, proportional reasoning, and everyday financial calculations.

## Geometry and Measurement
Students learn to identify and classify polygons and polyhedra, calculating the perimeter of rectangles and triangles using formulas such as P = 2(l + w). They measure length, mass, and volume using metric units like kilometres, metres, centimetres, grams, and litres, with practical applications including mapping a garden's perimeter. Conversion methods between units, such as changing centimetres to metres or litres to millilitres, are taught using decimal place value. They develop estimation skills, for instance, measuring ingredients for traditional Finnish rye bread, and apply geometric concepts to calculate the area of simple shapes like squares and rectangles.

## Data Handling and Statistics
Students learn to collect and organise data using tally charts and frequency tables, then represent it with bar graphs, ensuring axes are scaled and labelled correctly. They interpret data by identifying the mode, median, and range, with a focus on the mean average, calculated as the sum of values divided by the count of values. For example, they might find the mean height of a group or the average number of books read per student. These skills are applied to real-world scenarios, such as analysing survey results on favourite subjects or tracking weekly weather patterns to compare temperatures. This foundation in data handling and statistics develops their ability to analyse information, draw conclusions, and solve problems systematically.

## Problem Solving and Logical Thinking
Students apply systematic strategies to solve multi-step mathematical problems by planning and employing logical reasoning. Key methods include drawing diagrams, constructing lists, and using tables to organise information effectively. For example, in calculating public transport costs in Helsinki, students must determine individual ticket prices and combine them for total sums, or alternatively, calculate the savings with a travel pass. Another practical task involves using division and multiplication to plan a sauna evening, distributing costs evenly among friends based on shared expenses. This process reinforces the concept of proportional relationships and real-life budgeting, requiring careful calculation and verification of results.

## Patterns and Algebraic Thinking
Students identify and create numerical patterns and sequences, learning to express them with simple rules. They solve one-step equations where a variable is an unknown number, such as x + 25 = 75, finding x = 50. This algebraic thinking is introduced using concrete examples, like figuring out how many more euros are needed to buy a specific Moomins book, linking abstract concepts to tangible Finnish experiences and fostering early analytical skills.
