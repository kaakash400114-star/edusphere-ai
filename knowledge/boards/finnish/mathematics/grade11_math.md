# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 11 Mathematics

## Differential Calculus for Modelling Change
Students explore rates of change using derivatives. They learn to find the derivative of polynomial, exponential, and logarithmic functions using limit definitions and standard rules (power, product, quotient, chain). For example, the derivative of f(x) = 2x³ is f'(x) = 6x². They apply derivatives to analyse instantaneous velocity, like the speed of a sledge during a downhill run, and to determine the maximum efficiency of a sauna's heating element by finding local maxima of a cost function.

## Integral Calculus for Determining Accumulated Quantities
Students master the techniques of integration to calculate areas, volumes, and accumulated change. They learn the antiderivative and the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus. For instance, integrating the velocity function v(t) = 10 - 9.8t determines the distance a Northern Lights observation drone has travelled from its launch point. They use definite integrals to calculate the area under a curve, such as the total energy consumption of a household over a month, by integrating a power function.

## Vectors and Matrices in Geometric and Physical Applications
Students perform operations with vectors and matrices to solve geometric and physics problems. They calculate dot and cross products, and understand their geometric interpretations. For example, the dot product helps determine the angle between two paths hikers take towards a viewing point for the Northern Lights. Students use matrices to represent and solve systems of linear equations, such as optimising the production of Moomin-themed merchandise to meet resource constraints in a factory.

## Statistical Analysis and Probability Distributions
Students deepen their understanding of statistical inference. They analyse data using normal distributions, confidence intervals, and hypothesis testing. For example, a study might be conducted to determine the average cost of a sauna visit in Finland, with a 95% confidence interval. Students work with real-world data, such as tracking the frequency of solar flares that cause the Northern Lights, applying binomial and Poisson distributions to model rare but significant events.

## Trigonometry and Periodic Modelling
Students apply trigonometric functions to model periodic phenomena. They use sine and cosine functions to describe oscillatory motion, such as the fluctuating price of heating oil in euros over winter. Students analyse the function h(t) = A·sin(B(t - C)) + D to model the height of the sun above the horizon at different times of the year in Finland, or the periodic intensity of the aurora borealis based on an 11-year solar cycle.

## Discrete Mathematics and Network Models
Students explore graph theory and combinatorial principles used in computer science and logistics. They learn to find Eulerian and Hamiltonian paths, such as planning the most efficient route for a postal delivery service in a city laid out on a grid. Students apply permutations and combinations to determine the number of possible Moomin character arrangements in a display window. They also use simple Markov chains to model the transition of market shares between competing technology companies.
