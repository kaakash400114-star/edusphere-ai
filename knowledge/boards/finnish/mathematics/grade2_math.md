# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 2 Mathematics
## Numbers up to 100
Children learn to read, write, and compare numbers up to 100, understanding their place value (tens and units). They practice counting forward and backward in steps of one, two, and five. For example, they can count the number of seats in a small sauna, which might be 16, or group 20 euros into two sets of ten. A practical task is sorting a collection of 50 Moomin character cards into piles of five, reinforcing number sequences and grouping skills in a tangible, play-based context.

## Addition and Subtraction within 100
Pupils develop strategies for adding and subtracting two-digit numbers, using concrete materials like sticks or blocks. They learn to break down numbers, for example, adding 25 euros and 30 euros by combining the tens (50) and then the units (5). Subtraction is approached by taking away, such as figuring out how many lingonberries are left after eating 15 from a basket of 47. These methods are rooted in real-life scenarios, like calculating the total cost of two items at the market.

## Measurement and Time
Children explore length, mass, and volume using informal units. They might compare the weight of a kilogram of potatoes to a kilogram of apples using a balance scale. Time is learned by reading the clock to the full hour and half-hour, connecting it to daily routines. For example, they can say the Northern Lights usually appear at 9 PM or that a sauna session lasts for one hour. Measuring ingredients for a simple recipe with decilitres and grams makes these concepts concrete and relevant.

## Geometry and Shapes
Pupils identify and name basic two- and three-dimensional shapes, such as circles, squares, cubes, and cylinders. They observe shapes in their environment, like the round shape of a sauna stove or the cubic shape of a Moomin's blue house. They learn about concepts like edges and corners by building models with wooden blocks. Activities involve creating patterns with different shapes and finding examples of symmetry in natural objects, like a snowflake or a ladybug.

## Data Handling and Simple Graphs
Children learn to collect and organize simple data, such as their classmates' favourite Moomin characters or the number of different-coloured autumn leaves they find. They represent this information using real-object graphs and pictographs. For example, they might use drawings of euros to show the amount of pocket money each child has saved. This introduces the idea of interpreting and visualizing data in a straightforward, hands-on way that mirrors everyday problem-solving.

## Problem Solving and Patterns
Pupils apply their mathematical skills to solve simple, open-ended problems based on Finnish life. For example: "If a sauna costs 10 euros per hour and you use it for two hours, how much does it cost?" They also recognize and create patterns, like alternating colours of beads (red-white-blue for the Finnish flag) or continuing number sequences. This develops logical thinking and the ability to make predictions using known information.
