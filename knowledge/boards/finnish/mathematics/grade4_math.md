# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 4 Mathematics  
## Number Sense and Calculation  
Students consolidate their understanding of numbers up to 10,000, practicing reading, writing, and comparing them. They master addition and subtraction with numbers up to 10,000 using written methods, including carrying over and borrowing. Multiplication tables up to 10 are reinforced, and students learn to multiply a two-digit number by a single-digit number. For example, they calculate 35 × 4 using partitioning (30 × 4 + 5 × 4) to find the answer 140. Real-life contexts, like calculating the total cost of Moomin books at €8 each, are used to apply these skills.  

## Geometry and Measurement  
Students explore two-dimensional shapes, identifying properties of triangles, quadrilaterals, and regular polygons. They learn to measure and draw lines using a ruler to the nearest millimeter. In measurement, they practice converting between euros and cents (e.g., 4.50 € = 450 cents) and calculate perimeters of simple shapes. Time is covered by reading analog and digital clocks and solving problems involving durations. A practical task involves planning a sauna evening, calculating the time needed to heat the sauna and how long they can relax before dinner.  

## Fractions  
Students are introduced to fractions, understanding them as parts of a whole. They learn to name and write fractions with denominators up to 10, using visual aids like divided circles or rectangles. Comparing fractions with the same denominator is practiced, as well as finding equivalent fractions using concrete materials. For example, they show that 3/5 is the same as 6/10 using fraction strips. They solve simple problems, like sharing a 5-euro candy bar equally among 4 friends, determining each person gets 1.25 € or 5/4 of a euro.  

## Data Handling and Statistics  
Students learn to collect, organize, and interpret data using tally charts and bar graphs. They conduct simple surveys, such as asking classmates about their favorite Northern Lights colors, and present the results. Pictographs are used with a key, where one symbol represents multiple units. Students answer questions based on the data, like "Which color was chosen most often?" This develops skills in reading and creating simple charts to represent real-world information.  

## Problem Solving with Money  
Students solve practical problems involving money, including calculating total costs, finding change, and budgeting. They practice adding and subtracting sums of euros and cents, such as buying souvenirs for €12.30 and €7.50 and paying with a 20-euro note to get €0.20 change. Word problems involve real-life scenarios like planning a family outing, estimating total expenses for tickets and snacks. This reinforces arithmetic skills while understanding the value and use of money in everyday Finnish contexts.  

## Everyday Mathematics  
Students apply mathematical skills to everyday situations, such as measuring ingredients for baking using grams and liters. They solve problems related to time, like calculating how long a trip to a relative's house takes at a given speed. Estimation is practiced when guessing the number of Moomin characters in a picture. These activities help students see the relevance of math in their daily lives, fostering confidence and competence in using numbers to make decisions and understand the world around them.
