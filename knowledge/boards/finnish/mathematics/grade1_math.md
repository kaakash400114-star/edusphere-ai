# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 1 Mathematics

## Numbers and Counting
Children learn to recognise, name, and sequence numbers from 1 to 20. They practise counting forward and backward, and understand the concept of one more or one less. Using concrete objects like pebbis or Moomin characters, they connect quantities to numerals. For example, they might count 10 euro coins or 13 lingonberries. They also learn to identify the cardinality of small groups of items, ensuring that the last number spoken represents the total quantity in the set. This skill is fundamental for all later arithmetic.

## Basic Addition and Subtraction
Students explore addition and subtraction as combining and separating sets. They use physical items like tokens or blocks to model simple equations, such as 2 + 1 = 3 or 5 - 2 = 3. The focus is on understanding the operations, not just memorisation. Stories involving Moomin characters sharing berries or gaining new friends make these concepts relatable. They learn to write simple number sentences using '+' and '-' symbols to represent their actions with the materials.

## Measurement and Size
Children compare and describe objects using everyday measurement language. They learn terms like 'shorter', 'longer', 'heavier', and 'lighter'. Activities might involve ordering sticks by length or comparing the weight of sauna stones and apples. They begin to understand that measurement requires a consistent unit, like using footsteps to measure the distance to a swing. The goal is to develop a practical, qualitative understanding of size and weight before moving to formal units.

## Shapes and Space
Pupils identify and name common two-dimensional shapes such as circle, square, and triangle, and three-dimensional forms like sphere and cube. They hunt for these shapes in their environment, finding circles in a pannukakku (pancake) or rectangular doors in a Moominhouse. They learn to describe the position of objects using words like 'on', 'under', 'next to', and 'inside', often using the Northern Lights as a reference for 'above' the house.

## Data Handling and Patterns
Students collect, sort, and organise data based on simple attributes, such as grouping toy animals into sets of those with and without spots. They create real graphs using physical objects, like arranging coloured stones to show the favourite sauna scents of their friends. Recognising and continuing simple patterns, like red-blue-red-blue with berries, is another key skill, helping them to identify regularity and order.

## Time and Daily Routines
Children learn to sequence events in their daily lives, such as the morning routine: waking up, having breakfast, and going to school. They use analogue clocks to tell the hour, connecting the position of the big hand to the hour of the day. Understanding the days of the week and their sequence is practiced, often with a calendar that marks special days or Moomin birthdays. This builds a sense of time and personal experience.
