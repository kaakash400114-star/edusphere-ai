# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 6 Mathematics

## Arithmetical Skills and Proportional Reasoning
Students consolidate skills in calculation with whole numbers and decimals. They use the order of operations and the rules for fractions, including finding common denominators for addition and subtraction, and multiplying by cross-products. They learn to solve simple percentage problems, such as calculating discounts on items priced in euros, and recognise the connection between fractions, decimals, and percentages. For example, finding 20% of a 50-euro sauna ticket involves calculating one-fifth of the price.

## Geometry and Measurement
Students study the properties of 2D and 3D shapes, focusing on calculating the area of triangles, parallelograms, and circles using the formulas A = (base * height)/2, A = base * height, and A = πr², respectively. They calculate the volume of prisms and cylinders using V = base area * height. Practical applications include calculating the area of a rectangular Moominhouse plot or the volume of a cylindrical sauna stove, using units like square metres and cubic metres.

## Representing and Interpreting Data
Students learn to create and interpret statistical charts, including bar charts, pie charts, and histograms, using discrete and continuous data. They calculate basic statistical measures such as mean, median, and mode. A key skill is distinguishing between different types of data, for example, recording the number of Northern Lights sightings over a month (discrete) or the temperature on each day (continuous), and choosing an appropriate chart to represent the findings.

## Algebraic Thinking
Students begin formal algebra by understanding expressions with variables, such as 3x + 5. They learn to solve simple linear equations of the form ax + b = c using inverse operations. They use letters to represent unknown quantities in real-world situations, for example, finding the value of 'x' in an equation like x + 15 = 50 to determine how many euros are left after spending 15 from 50.

## Problem Solving and Mathematical Modelling
Students apply their mathematical knowledge to solve open-ended and real-world problems. They learn to choose suitable operations and strategies, such as working systematically or creating a simpler version of the problem. A task might be to plan a school event with a 500-euro budget, calculating costs for materials and refreshments. The focus is on explaining the reasoning and justifying the solution using clear mathematical language.

## Location and Movement
Students work with the Cartesian coordinate system to plot and read coordinates in all four quadrants. They use coordinates to describe the position of points and to draw simple shapes. They also understand concepts like translation and reflection on a coordinate grid. An activity involves mapping a route for a Moomin character on a grid, translating its location by a given number of steps to the right and up, and noting its new position.
