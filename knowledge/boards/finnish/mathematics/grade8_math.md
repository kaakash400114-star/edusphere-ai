# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 8 Mathematics

## Functions and their applications
A function is a rule that assigns exactly one output value to each input value from a set. This relationship can be described using an equation, a table, or a graph. Students learn to interpret the notation f(x) for the value of the function at x. Linear functions are expressed as f(x) = kx + m, where k is the slope and m is the y-intercept. The slope indicates the rate of change; for example, a function f(t) = 5t + 50 could calculate the total cost of a sauna visit in euros, where t is the time spent and there's a fixed 50-euro base fee.

## Rational numbers and their operations
Rational numbers are numbers that can be expressed as a fraction p/q, where p and q are integers and q is not zero. Students extend their understanding of arithmetic operations to include negative fractions and decimals. Key methods include finding a common denominator for addition and subtraction and using the rule that multiplying or dividing by the same non-zero number preserves the value of the fraction. For instance, to calculate profit, one might find the difference between revenue of 375.50 euros and expenses of 275.25 euros, resulting in a profit of 100.25 euros.

## Geometric figures and their measurement
Students explore the properties of two- and three-dimensional geometric shapes, focusing on polygons and their transformations. The core tasks involve calculating perimeters, areas, and surface areas. The formula for the area of a circle is A = πr², where r is the radius. To connect to local phenomena, a student might calculate the viewing area of the Northern Lights, approximating it as a segment of a circle with a known radius to understand its scale. The volume of prisms and cylinders is calculated with the formula V = Abh, where Ab is the area of the base and h is the height.

## Algebraic expressions and equations
Algebraic expressions consist of numbers, variables, and operations, while equations are mathematical statements asserting the equality of two expressions. Students learn to expand expressions using the distributive property (a(b+c) = ab + ac) and to simplify them by combining like terms. Solving linear equations involves isolating the variable through inverse operations, such as adding or subtracting the same term from both sides. For example, to solve 2x + 10 = 30, one would first subtract 10 from both sides, then divide by 2 to find x = 5.

## Statistics and probability
Statistics involves collecting, organizing, and interpreting data. Students learn to calculate and interpret measures of central tendency: the mean, median, and mode. The mean is the sum of all values divided by the count of values. Probability is the measure of the likelihood of an event, expressed as a value from 0 to 1. Students learn to calculate theoretical probability by dividing the number of favorable outcomes by the total number of possible outcomes. For instance, the probability of drawing a Moomin character from a set of five distinct characters is 1/5, or 0.2.

## Problem solving and logical reasoning
This section emphasizes applying mathematical knowledge to solve complex, multi-step problems that may require several different concepts and methods. Key skills include creating mathematical models from real-world situations, choosing an appropriate strategy, and justifying the solution logically. Students learn to identify relevant information and to present their reasoning clearly. A problem might involve planning a trip to a summer cottage, calculating distances, fuel costs, and time, requiring the integrated use of functions, rates, and arithmetic operations to find the most efficient route.
