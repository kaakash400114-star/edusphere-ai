# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 10 Mathematics
## Algebraic Expressions and Equations
Students master the manipulation of polynomial expressions, including expanding, factoring, and simplifying. They solve quadratic equations using factorisation, the quadratic formula, and completing the square. For example, solving x² - 5x + 6 = 0 involves finding the roots x = 2 and x = 3. Students also practice solving systems of linear equations with two variables using the substitution and elimination methods, applying these to practical problems like calculating costs for sauna materials.

## Functions and Their Graphs
This unit focuses on defining functions, identifying domains and ranges, and interpreting graphs. Students explore linear, quadratic, and polynomial functions. They learn to sketch graphs and analyse their key features, such as intercepts, turning points, and end behaviour. A practical application involves calculating the area of a Northern Lights observation deck as a function of its width, represented by a quadratic equation. Students also use functions to model real-world phenomena like temperature variations.

## Geometry and Trigonometry
Students study the properties of geometric shapes, focusing on circles, triangles, and their applications. They solve problems involving angles, arcs, and chords. In trigonometry, students use sine, cosine, and tangent ratios to solve right-angled triangles and apply the sine and cosine rules to non-right-angled triangles. For example, calculating the height of a Moomin house using the angle of elevation and a known distance. They also explore the unit circle and trigonometric identities.

## Probability and Statistics
This unit covers basic probability concepts, including calculating probabilities of events, conditional probability, and independent events. Students analyse data using statistical measures such as mean, median, mode, and standard deviation. They represent data with histograms, box plots, and scatter graphs. A practical example involves analysing the distribution of Finnish euros in a sample to understand currency circulation. Students also learn to interpret statistical reports and make informed conclusions.

## Financial Mathematics
Students learn to apply mathematical concepts to financial contexts, including simple and compound interest. They calculate interest earned or paid on savings loans, using formulas like A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt). Budgeting problems involve calculating costs for events, such as a sauna party, by considering expenses for wood, towels, and snacks. Students also explore currency exchange rates, for example, converting Euros to Japanese Yen for travel, understanding the impact of exchange rate fluctuations.

## Problem-Solving and Modelling
This unit emphasises applying mathematical reasoning to solve complex, real-world problems. Students learn to break down problems, formulate hypotheses, and test solutions. For instance, modelling the best time to view the Northern Lights based on latitude and cloud cover data. They use logical deduction and critical thinking to justify their methods and solutions, often working on collaborative projects that integrate multiple mathematical strands, such as designing a sustainable community garden.
