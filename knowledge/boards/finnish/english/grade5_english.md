# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 5 English

## Understanding Stories and Characters
In Year 5, students learn to identify the main characters, setting, and plot in English stories. They practise using descriptive words to talk about characters' feelings and actions, for example, describing the kind Moomin family enjoying a peaceful summer day. Students compare characters from different stories, like a brave knight from an English folktale and the adventurous Moomintroll. They discuss how a character's choices affect the story's events and use a story map to visually sequence the main plot points.

## Writing Simple Instructions
Students learn to write clear, step-by-step instructions using simple imperative verbs. They focus on using chronological order words like 'first', 'then', and 'finally'. For example, they write 'First, heat the sauna to 80°C. Then, enter the sauna with a towel.' They include a list of materials needed, like 'You will need: water, birch branches, and a bucket'. The instructions are easy for a friend to follow without any confusion or extra questions.

## Describing Places Using Senses
Students learn to describe places using all five senses in English sentences. They use specific vocabulary for sight, sound, smell, touch, and taste. For example, 'The Northern Lights are beautiful to see (sight). The crackling sound of the campfire is loud (sound). The smell of smoke fills the air (smell). The air feels cold and crisp (touch). The hot chocolate is sweet and warm (taste).' They practice describing locations like a snowy forest or a lakeside cottage.

## Using Euros for Simple Maths Problems
Students apply their English vocabulary for numbers and money to solve simple maths problems. They practise reading word problems involving euros and cents, for example, 'If a book costs 15 euros and a pen costs 3 euros, what is the total cost?' They use addition, subtraction, and multiplication with real-world prices. They also write their own simple word problems, such as 'How much are two tickets to the museum if one ticket costs 8 euros?'

## Talking About Daily and Seasonal Activities
Students learn to talk about daily routines and seasonal activities using the present simple tense. They use time phrases like 'in the morning' and 'in winter'. For example, 'In the morning, I eat porridge for breakfast. In winter, I go skiing and build snowmen.' They discuss Finnish seasonal events, such as watching the Northern Lights in autumn or swimming in a lake during summer. They compare their own routines with routines in other English-speaking countries.

## Understanding Different Cultures Through Stories
Students read simple stories from other cultures to understand different perspectives. They compare traditions, like a midsummer celebration in Finland with a Christmas celebration in Australia. They use a Venn diagram to find similarities and differences. For example, both cultures might have special foods. They learn new vocabulary related to these traditions, such as 'sauna' or 'barbecue', and discuss how culture influences the characters' actions and feelings in a story.
