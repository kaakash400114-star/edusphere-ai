# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 7 English

## Descriptive Writing Techniques
Students learn to craft vivid sensory descriptions using precise adjectives and figurative language. They practice organizing details spatially (e.g., from near to far) and chronologically. Key techniques include using similes for comparisons (e.g., "The winter air was as sharp as a sauna's heated stones") and personification (e.g., "The Northern Lights danced across the dark sky"). Students describe familiar settings like a snowy forest or a bustling market in Helsinki, incorporating sensory details related to sight, sound, smell, and touch to create immersive scenes for their audience.

## Narrative Structure and Plot Development
This unit explores the fundamental elements of narrative storytelling. Students analyze the classic three-act structure: introduction, rising action, climax, falling action, and resolution. They learn to develop complex characters using dialogue and internal monologue. For practice, students craft short stories, often using Finnish settings or themes, such as finding a lost object during a summer festival or navigating a challenge with friends. Emphasis is placed on creating a clear plotline with a central conflict that builds suspense to a satisfying conclusion.

## Research Skills and Information Synthesis
Students acquire essential skills for gathering and evaluating information from diverse sources, including online encyclopedias, books, and reputable websites. They practice identifying credible authors and checking for potential bias. The culminating project involves writing an informative report on a Finnish cultural topic, like the history of the sauna, the significance of the euro in Finland, or the science behind the Northern Lights, requiring them to synthesize information from at least three different sources into a coherent, well-structured text.

## Grammatical Accuracy and Complex Sentences
Building on prior knowledge, students master advanced sentence structures to improve clarity and sophistication in their writing. This includes correctly using complex sentences with subordinate clauses (e.g., "While many tourists visit Helsinki for its design, Finns cherish the Moomin's philosophical messages about friendship") and compound sentences. They practice identifying and correcting common errors, such as subject-verb agreement with compound subjects and the proper use of relative pronouns like 'that' and 'which' to provide precise detail.

## Persuasive Writing and Critical Thinking
This module teaches students how to formulate a clear argument with supporting evidence. They learn to structure a persuasive text with an introduction stating a claim, body paragraphs with evidence (e.g., facts, statistics, expert opinions), and a conclusion that reinforces the position. Students practice debating topics relevant to their lives, such as whether the school year should be extended or the value of learning multiple languages, using rhetorical devices like rhetorical questions and appealing to logic and emotion to persuade their audience.

## Media Literacy and Text Analysis
Students develop the ability to critically analyze various media forms, distinguishing between fact and opinion in news articles and advertisements. They learn to identify persuasive techniques and potential hidden messages. By analyzing texts about Finnish phenomena—such as articles promoting tourism or advertisements for local products—students evaluate how language and imagery are used to influence an audience. This unit emphasizes becoming a discerning consumer of information in a digital world.
