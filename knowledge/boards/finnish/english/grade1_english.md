# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 1 English
## Everyday Greetings and Politeness
Children learn to use basic English greetings and phrases politely. They practice saying "Hello" and "Goodbye" in different situations. They learn to say "Please" and "Thank you" when receiving something, like a Moomin sticker or a piece of bread. Students practice responding to "How are you?" with "I'm fine, thank you" or "I'm good". These phrases are modelled and practiced through role-play with puppets and classmates, encouraging positive social interaction and respect, core transversal competencies.
## Basic Personal Information
This topic covers introducing oneself and others. Children learn to say "My name is [name]" and ask "What is your name?". They practice answering "My name is..." in response. They also learn to count from one to five to state their age, "I am [number] years old". These skills are practiced in circle time games where children greet each other and share their name and age, using real-world examples like introducing a new friend in the classroom.
## Classroom Objects and Actions
Children learn common English names for classroom items and actions. They identify words like "pencil", "book", "chair", and "table". They also learn action verbs used in the classroom, such as "sit", "stand", "listen", and "open". Students practice by playing a game where the teacher says "Show me the book" and they point to the correct object, connecting concrete items to their English labels through a hands-on, play-based approach.
## Colours and the World Around Us
Students learn to identify and name basic colours: red, blue, yellow, green. They connect these colour names to objects in their environment, both indoors and outdoors. For example, they learn to say "The sky is blue" or "A strawberry is red". A popular activity is to go outside and find something "red" or "green" during a nature walk, reinforcing vocabulary by using concrete, familiar Finnish examples like red berries or green pine needles.
## Counting and Simple Numbers
Children practice counting from one to ten in English. They connect the number names to quantities of objects. They learn to say "one apple", "two pencils", and "three friends". This is practiced by counting the number of children present, fingers on a hand, or items in a small collection. Using physical objects, like euros or blocks, helps them understand the concrete meaning of each number in a play-based learning context.
## Listening to and Learning Stories
Students develop their listening and comprehension skills by listening to short, simple stories in English. The stories often feature well-known characters like the Moomins or animals. Children practice identifying the main character and a simple event from the story. Through these stories, they also learn about Finnish culture, such as seeing the Northern Lights in a picture book, expanding their vocabulary and understanding of the world in an engaging way.
