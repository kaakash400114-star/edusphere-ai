# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 9 English

## Advanced Literary Analysis: Character and Theme
Students analyse complex character development and thematic depth in prose and drama. They identify explicit and implicit traits using textual evidence and understand how authors use dialogue and internal monologue. Students explore universal and culturally specific themes such as identity, conflict, and social change. A key task involves comparing a Finnish novel like *The Unknown Soldier* with an English classic, examining how different cultures portray heroism and sacrifice during war. This requires close reading skills and supporting interpretations with specific quotations.

## Developing Persuasive Writing and Rhetoric
Pupils craft persuasive texts using sophisticated rhetorical devices. They learn to formulate clear, debatable theses and construct logical, evidence-based arguments. This includes understanding ethos, pathos, and logos, and employing figures of speech like metaphor and hyperbole for effect. Students practice writing formal letters and opinion pieces for a newspaper, for instance, arguing for the preservation of a local natural site like the Northern Lights viewing area, using factual data and emotional appeal to strengthen their position.

## The Mechanics of Advanced Syntax and Style
This module focuses on mastering grammatical structures to enhance clarity and style. Students learn to use subordinate clauses, participial phrases, and appositives to create more complex and varied sentences. They examine sentence rhythm and structure and practice combining short, choppy sentences into fluid, elegant prose. An exercise involves transforming a simple factual report about a sauna's cultural significance into a stylistically rich descriptive paragraph, applying learned grammatical rules to elevate the language.

## Media Literacy and Critical Viewing
Students critically deconstruct media messages and understand the power of visual and audio-textual elements. They analyse advertising, news broadcasts, and films by identifying the target audience, persuasive techniques, and potential biases. A practical application involves creating a storyboard for a short advertisement promoting a Finnish product, like a local brand of rye bread. Students must justify their choices of imagery, music, and scripting based on the principles of persuasion and audience appeal.

## Interpreting Non-Fiction Texts in Context
Students learn to identify the authorial purpose and contextual background of non-fiction texts, such as feature articles and research reports. They apply specific methods to distinguish factual statements from subjective opinions and logical inferences. A key concept is evaluating source reliability by analysing author credentials, publication date, and potential bias, as seen when contrasting reporting on economic policy. Learners engage in comparative analysis of texts on the same event, such as different news outlets' coverage of a eurozone decision. This requires cross-referencing factual claims and identifying rhetorical strategies to understand varying perspectives and construct a comprehensive view of the issue.

## Project-Based Learning: Research and Presentation
Students apply an inquiry-based methodology to formulate specific research questions and establish evaluation criteria for source credibility, such as academic articles versus websites. They employ digital tools for collaborative information synthesis and structured report writing using standard formatting conventions. Oral presentations are developed to include rhetorical strategies and multimodal elements for audience engagement, following a clear organizational framework. For instance, a project could investigate local sustainability practices by gathering data from municipal reports and expert interviews. The process culminates in a defendable argument or narrative and a reflective analysis of the research and collaboration processes.
