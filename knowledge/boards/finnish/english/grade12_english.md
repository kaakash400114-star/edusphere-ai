# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 12 English
## Critical Media Analysis
Students develop competence in analysing diverse media texts by applying critical frameworks. They learn to deconstruct multimodal sources such as news articles and social media campaigns, identifying persuasive techniques, rhetorical devices, and potential biases. A key method involves examining the socio-cultural context and target audience, using examples of Finnish political discourse or a major public health campaign. Students evaluate the author's intent, the reliability of evidence, and the impact of framing on public perception, particularly concerning issues of national interest like EU policy or Arctic environmental concerns.

## Societal and Cultural Perspectives
This section explores the interplay between language and societal structures. Students examine how language reinforces or challenges social norms and power dynamics, focusing on concepts like linguistic relativity and sociolinguistic variation. Through case studies of Finnish culture, such as the linguistic evolution of "sisu" or communication patterns in a sauna context, they analyse how language reflects national identity. Students discuss the impact of globalisation on local languages and cultures, using specific examples of international business negotiations within Finland or the global reach of brands like Marimekko.

## Academic and Professional Communication
Students refine their ability to produce formal, context-specific texts essential for higher education and professional life. They master the conventions of academic writing, including structuring arguments, integrating evidence, and using citation styles like APA. For professional contexts, they learn to craft effective business correspondence, reports, and proposals. A key component is analysing the pragmatic conventions of workplace communication in Finland, such as the nuanced use of the formal "Sinä" or the collaborative tone of emails in a tech startup like Supercell, preparing them for authentic international interactions.

## Creative and Expressive Texts
This module focuses on analysing and producing literary and creative works to deepen understanding of human experience. Students engage with complex forms such as the modernist novel or post-dramatic theatre. They apply theoretical lenses like psychoanalysis or postcolonial theory to texts, for instance, by exploring themes of isolation and nature in the work of Väinö Linna or the symbolic world of the Moomins. Students also practice their own creative writing, producing pieces like a short story or a dramatic monologue that reflect a distinct cultural perspective or a personal response to a global phenomenon.

## Argumentation and Rhetoric
Students master the principles of logical argumentation and rhetorical persuasion. They learn to construct well-reasoned claims supported by evidence, anticipate counter-arguments, and identify logical fallacies. Through a structured process, they develop skills in debate and formal persuasive speaking, utilising classical rhetorical canons like ethos, pathos, and logos. Practical application involves preparing and delivering arguments on contemporary Finnish or international issues, such as the economic implications of adopting the Euro or ethical debates surrounding the Northern Lights tourism industry, honing their ability to communicate with clarity and conviction.

## Global English and Linguistic Variation
Students investigate the role of English as a global lingua franca and its linguistic diversity. They study major varieties of English, including British, American, and emerging World Englishes, analysing phonological, lexical, and grammatical differences. A key focus is on pragmatic competence, understanding how communication styles and cultural contexts affect meaning. They examine the function of English in international contexts like the EU, where Finnish professionals must navigate multilingual environments, and analyse the use of English in global media, exploring its influence on local cultures and identities.
