# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 4 Science

## Northern Lights and the Sky
Students learn about the Northern Lights, or Revontulet, as a natural phenomenon. They understand they are caused by charged particles from the sun colliding with gases in Earth's atmosphere. The science behind this involves the magnetic field of the Earth protecting us. Students can identify the typical colours, green being the most common from oxygen, and sometimes red from higher altitudes. They learn that this phenomenon is best seen in Finland during the dark winter months and is a key part of Finnish natural heritage.

## Properties of Everyday Materials
This topic focuses on understanding material properties like conductivity, strength, and transparency. Students perform experiments to test which materials are good conductors of heat, such as metal versus wood. They classify materials based on whether they are transparent, like a clear window, or opaque, like a wall. Through practical tasks, students learn how these properties are important in designing objects, for example, why a sauna's interior is made of wood to insulate heat and why a cooking pot uses metal for efficient heat transfer.

## Life Cycle of a Forest
Students explore the life cycle of a forest, focusing on the growth of pine and birch trees common in Finland. They learn that trees start from seeds, grow into saplings, and then mature over many years. The science includes understanding how trees make their own food through photosynthesis using sunlight. They also learn about the role of fungi and insects in the forest ecosystem, breaking down dead matter. An example is learning how a single pine cone can release dozens of seeds, and how some trees can live for hundreds of years.

## Basic Concepts of Weather
Students learn to observe and describe basic weather conditions. They understand the water cycle, explaining how water evaporates from lakes and the sea, forms clouds, and falls as rain or snow. The science includes reading a thermometer to measure temperature in Celsius, which is essential for understanding Finnish weather. Students learn to identify different cloud types and make simple predictions, for example, that cumulonimbus clouds often bring the heavy rain or thunderstorms common in summer.

## The Science of a Sauna
The sauna is used as a practical example of heat transfer. Students learn about conduction, as the heat from the stove warms the wooden walls and benches. They understand that the steam, or löyly, is created by pouring water on the hot stones. The science also involves evaporation and perspiration, explaining why sweating helps cool the body. Students learn about safety, such as the recommended time of about 15 minutes inside and the importance of cooling down with a cold shower or dip in a lake afterward.

## Money and Saving in a Community
This topic connects science with everyday life through the concept of euros. Students learn that euros are a form of currency used for exchanging goods and services. They understand that money is created and managed by the central bank of a country. A practical example is learning that 100 cents make one euro, and that prices for goods like a Moomin mug or a bus ticket are listed in euros. Students learn the basic idea of saving money to buy something more expensive in the future.
