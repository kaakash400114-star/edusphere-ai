# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 3 Science

## Everyday Materials and Their Properties
Grade 3 students learn to identify and categorize common materials like wood, plastic, metal, and fabric based on observable properties such as hardness, flexibility, and transparency. They discover how these properties determine a material’s suitability for a specific purpose, for instance, why glass is used for windows or why rubber is ideal for靴子 (boots). Children conduct simple experiments to observe how materials change shape, like twisting metal wire or bending plastic spoons, understanding form and function in everyday objects like a Moomin mug made from sturdy ceramic.

## Light and Shadow
In this unit, pupils explore how light travels in straight lines and can be blocked to create shadows. They learn that the size and shape of a shadow change depending on the object's position relative to the light source. Using a flashlight and their hands, they create shadow puppets, observing how moving the light makes the shadow grow larger or smaller. Students identify materials that let light pass through (transparent, like a window), block it (opaque, like a wooden book), or let some light through (translucent, like a frosted bathroom window, linking to the privacy of a sauna).

## Weather and the Seasons
This module focuses on the patterns of weather and the four distinct seasons in Finland. Students learn to measure and describe basic weather conditions, including air temperature (using a thermometer in Celsius), wind speed, and whether it is raining or snowing. They identify seasonal characteristics, such as the long, dark winter with its snow and the possibility of seeing the Northern Lights, and the bright, warm summer. They also learn to associate seasonal changes with activities, like using a sauna in winter to warm up or enjoying a picnic in summer.

## Simple Machines in Our Lives
Grade 3 students identify the use of simple machines like levers, wheels and axles, and inclined planes in their daily environment. They discover that a seesaw is a lever, a door handle uses a wheel and axle, and a ramp is an inclined plane. Children explore how these machines make work easier by giving us a mechanical advantage. For example, they might see how a ramp allows a wheelchair to move up with less force than climbing stairs, connecting the concept to everyday aids for people.

## Growth and Needs of Living Things
This unit covers the basic needs of plants and animals: water, light, air, and nutrients. Students observe how a plant, such as a bean in a clear cup, needs these things to grow. They learn about different life cycles, like the metamorphosis of a frog from a tadpole, which can be observed in Finland’s lakes. Pupils also consider how different animals adapt to their environment, like how a reindeer has special fur for the cold Finnish winters, and what humans must do to help plants and animals survive.

## The Value and Use of Money
This module introduces the concept of money as a tool for exchange. Students identify and become familiar with Finnish euros (€), both coins and banknotes, and their different values. They learn that money is used to buy goods and services, such as paying for a bus ticket, a book, or entry to a museum. Through role-playing, children practice simple transactions, understanding that earning money requires work and that one must make choices about how to spend it, linking it to saving for a specific goal like a new toy.
