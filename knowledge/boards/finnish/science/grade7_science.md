# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 7 Science
## Biological Diversity and Evolution
Students learn that species are defined by their ability to reproduce and produce fertile offspring. They study Charles Darwin's theory of evolution by natural selection, where organisms with advantageous traits are more likely to survive and pass those genes to the next generation. The genetic basis for variation is explained, alongside evidence from the fossil record. An example used is the divergence of Darwin's finches in the Galápagos, where different beak shapes evolved to exploit various food sources on the islands.
## Forces and Motion
Students explore Isaac Newton's three laws of motion. The first law, inertia, states an object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion unless acted upon by an unbalanced force. The second law states force equals mass times acceleration (F=ma), and the third law states for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. Students apply this by calculating the force required to accelerate a 50-kilogram sled to 2 m/s².
## Energy Forms and Transfers
Students learn that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred or transformed, known as the law of conservation of energy. They identify different forms, including kinetic, potential, thermal, and chemical energy. An example is a rollercoaster: at the highest point, it has maximum gravitational potential energy, which converts to kinetic energy as it descends. They also study energy efficiency, calculating what percentage of input energy becomes useful output in devices like a sauna heater.
## Structure and Properties of Matter
Students understand that matter is composed of atoms, which consist of protons, neutrons, and electrons. They learn that the periodic table organizes elements by their atomic number and properties. The states of matter (solid, liquid, gas) are explained by the arrangement and energy of particles. A practical example is examining the phase change of water (H₂O) in a sauna, where it transitions from liquid to gas (steam) due to increased thermal energy from the kiuas.
## The Interconnectedness of Ecosystems
Students analyze how organisms interact within an ecosystem through food webs and energy pyramids. They learn about producers, consumers, and decomposers. Key cycles are studied, including the carbon cycle, where plants absorb carbon dioxide during photosynthesis and release it through respiration. An example is a forest ecosystem in Finland, where pine trees act as producers, herbivores like reindeer are primary consumers, and fungi decompose fallen logs, returning nutrients to the soil.
## Technology and Sustainable Development
Students evaluate the relationship between technology, its impact on the environment, and sustainable development goals. They assess life cycle analyses of products, from raw material extraction to disposal. A Finnish example is the lifecycle of a single euro coin; its production uses energy and metals, but its long lifespan and recyclability reduce its environmental footprint compared to single-use items. Students also explore renewable energy solutions like wind power to meet future needs.
