# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 9 Science
## Atomic Structure and Chemical Bonding
Students learn that atoms consist of a nucleus with protons and neutrons, surrounded by electrons in energy shells. The atomic number defines the element, while the mass number is the sum of protons and neutrons. Chemical bonding occurs when atoms interact to achieve stable electron configurations. Ionic bonding involves electron transfer, forming ions like Na+ and Cl- to create table salt (NaCl). Covalent bonding is the sharing of electrons, as in a water molecule (H₂O), where two hydrogen atoms share electrons with one oxygen atom. Finnish research at VTT uses this knowledge for developing new materials.
## The Moomin universe: An analogue for an ecosystem
An ecosystem, like the Moominvalley, is a community of living organisms interacting with their non-living environment. It is defined by energy flow and nutrient cycling. Primary producers, such as the Moominhouse garden plants, convert solar energy into chemical energy via photosynthesis. Consumers, like Moomintroll and his friends, depend on these producers. Decomposers, breaking down waste, return nutrients to the soil. The balance of these interactions in the Moominvalley illustrates how a stable ecosystem functions, similar to the archipelago ecosystems found along Finland's coast.
## Forces and Motion in Everyday Life
Newton's laws of motion govern the movement of all objects. The first law states an object remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by a net force, demonstrated by a still sauna stone until someone lifts it. The second law, F = ma, explains that a force (F) causes acceleration (a) proportional to an object's mass (m). For example, pushing a grocery cart with a 10 kg mass of rye bread (F=50N) results in an acceleration of 5 m/s². The third law states that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction, like a swimmer pushing against water to move forward.
## The Aurora Borealis: A Case Study in Magnetism
The Aurora Borealis, or Northern Lights, is a natural phenomenon resulting from solar wind interactions with Earth's magnetic field. Charged particles from the sun are guided by the magnetic field lines towards the poles. Upon entering the atmosphere, these particles collide with oxygen and nitrogen atoms, exciting their electrons. When the electrons return to their ground state, they release energy as light, creating the characteristic green and red colours. Finnish scientific stations in Lappland, like those operated by the Finnish Meteorological Institute, actively study this space weather phenomenon to understand its impacts.
## Energy Efficiency in Finnish Society
Energy efficiency is a core principle for sustainable development in Finland. It involves reducing energy consumption while maintaining service levels. This is achieved through technological innovation, such as highly efficient district heating systems used in cities like Helsinki, and behavioural changes. The formula for calculating energy efficiency is useful output energy divided by total input energy. For example, an energy-efficient LED bulb uses 10 watts of electrical power to produce 800 lumens of light, whereas an old incandescent bulb might use 60 watts for the same output, representing a significant saving of 50 watts.
## Genetics and Heredity in Populations
Genetics explains how traits are passed from parents to offspring. Inheritance is governed by alleles, variants of a gene located on chromosomes at specific positions called loci. Gregor Mendel's laws describe this: the law of segregation states that alleles for a trait separate during gamete formation, and the law of independent assortment states that genes for different traits are inherited independently. Finnish examples include the study of hereditary diseases and the conservation of native livestock breeds, like the Eastern Finncattle, which are monitored for genetic diversity to ensure the health of the population.
