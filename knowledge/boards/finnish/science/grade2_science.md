# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 2 Science
## Everyday Materials and Their Properties
Children learn to identify and describe common materials like wood, metal, plastic, and fabric. They explore properties such as hardness, flexibility, transparency, and conductivity. For example, they discover that a metal spoon is hard and conducts heat well, while a plastic spoon is flexible. In Finland, they might compare the smoothness of a birch bark craft to the roughness of a granite stone found near a sauna. They sort objects based on their material and describe how a material's properties make it suitable for a specific use, like a wool Moomin hat keeping someone warm.
## Weather and Seasons in Finland
Students observe and describe seasonal weather changes in Finland. They learn to identify key features of spring (rain, budding trees), summer (warmth, midnight sun), autumn (falling leaves, frost), and winter (snow, ice, -10°C temperatures). They use simple thermometers to measure the air temperature and record their observations. A core activity involves tracking the length of daylight through the seasons and connecting it to the phenomena of the Northern Lights in winter, which are caused by particles from the sun interacting with the Earth's atmosphere.
## Living Things and Their Needs
This topic introduces children to the basic needs of living things: water, food, air, and a suitable environment. They observe plants like spruce trees and flowers in the school garden, noting they need soil, water, and sunlight. For animals, they learn about common Finnish creatures like squirrels and hedgehogs and their habitats. They understand that all living things grow and change. A key concept is that a tree, like the famous birch, needs its roots for support and water, just like a child needs clean drinking water and a safe home to grow.
## Forces and Simple Movements
Children explore the concept of force as a push or a pull. They experiment with moving objects, observing that a gentle push makes a toy car move slowly, while a strong push makes it go faster. They investigate friction by trying to slide a block of wood on different surfaces like smooth linoleum and rough carpet. A Finnish example involves pulling a sleigh in the snow and feeling the resistance. They learn that changing the strength or direction of the force changes the movement of the object.
## Light and Shadow
Students learn that light travels in straight lines and can be blocked to create shadows. They conduct simple experiments by shining a flashlight on their own hands or objects to observe the size and shape of the shadows. They discover that bringing an object closer to the light source makes its shadow bigger. They explore sources of light, including the sun, a lamp, and even the glow of the Northern Lights. They discuss how shadows change throughout the day, linking it to the sun's position in the Finnish sky.
## Using Money in Everyday Life
Children are introduced to the concept of currency and its use in exchanging goods and services. They learn to identify the common Euro coins (1c, 5c, 10c, 20c, 50c, €1, €2) and their values. Through play, they practice buying and selling items like craft supplies or snacks using toy Euros. A practical Finnish example involves a pretend visit to a Kiosk, where they count out the correct amount of money to buy a juice or a piece of candy, understanding that money is needed to obtain things they want or need.
