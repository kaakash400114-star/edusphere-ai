# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 5 Science
## Matter and Its States
Pupils learn that matter exists in solid, liquid, and gas states. They identify that changing temperature alters these states, such as ice (solid) melting into water (liquid) at 0°C. They observe water evaporating from a puddle and condensing on a cold window. The three forms of water—vapour, liquid, and ice—are used as a concrete example. They understand that matter retains its mass during state changes but changes volume and shape. The Northern Lights (revontulet) are mentioned as a natural phenomenon involving gas in Earth's atmosphere interacting with solar energy.

## Forces and Motion
Pupils explore how forces cause movement and change shape. They identify gravity as a force pulling objects downward and friction as a force slowing motion. Using toy cars, they observe how a harder push creates faster movement. The concept of balanced forces (e.g., a book resting on a table) is introduced. They learn about simple machines like levers, seen in a seesaw. An example of force in daily life is pushing a sled on snow, where friction is low. The speed of a sledge pulled by huskies can be discussed as an application of force and motion.

## The Structure of the Earth
Pupils learn about the Earth's layered structure: the crust, mantle, and core. They understand that the crust is the outermost, solid layer forming continents and ocean floors. They learn about plate tectonics and how movement of these plates causes earthquakes and forms mountains. Volcanoes are presented as openings where molten rock from the mantle reaches the surface. The presence of granite bedrock in Finland's landscape is a concrete example of the Earth's crust. They also study soil as a mixture of weathered rock, organic matter, and minerals.

## The Diversity of Living Things
Pupils classify animals into groups based on shared characteristics, such as mammals, birds, fish, and insects. They understand that mammals have hair or fur and produce milk for their young. Examples include reindeer, moose, and brown bears, common in Finland. Pupils learn about animal adaptations, such as the thick fur of a polar bear for cold climates. They explore habitats, including the Finnish boreal forest (taiga), and how organisms depend on each other. The life cycle of a butterfly is studied as an example of a complete metamorphosis.

## Weather and Climate
Pupils distinguish between weather (short-term atmospheric conditions) and climate (long-term patterns). They learn to measure weather using instruments like a thermometer for temperature and a barometer for air pressure. They observe cloud types and relate them to weather changes. The climate of Finland is described as having cold, snowy winters and mild summers. They learn about seasonal changes and their effects on nature, such as trees losing leaves in autumn. The sauna is presented as a cultural activity deeply tied to Finnish weather and seasonal adaptation.

## Light and Sound
Pupils learn that light travels in straight lines and is necessary for seeing objects. They explore how light reflects off surfaces like a mirror and refracts through water, causing objects to appear bent. They learn about shadows being formed when an object blocks light. For sound, they understand it is produced by vibrations and travels through air, water, or solids. They identify that pitch depends on how fast an object vibrates. A traditional Finnish wooden kantele is an example of an instrument that produces sound through vibrations, its tones clear and resonant in a quiet lakeside setting.
