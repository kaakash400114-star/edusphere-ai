# Finnish (FNBE) Grade 1 Science
## The World Around Us
Children learn to observe their immediate environment using all five senses. They identify basic materials like wood, metal, and plastic and describe their properties such as hardness, colour, and texture. For example, they compare a smooth rock to a rough pinecone. They explore natural phenomena, noting the sun's warmth during the day and the coolness of the evening air. Simple observations are recorded through drawings or verbal descriptions, fostering an initial understanding of the physical world.

## Changes in Nature
This topic focuses on observing seasonal changes, particularly in Finnish nature. Children learn to notice the colours of autumn leaves turning yellow and red before falling. They observe the ground freezing in winter and the melting of snow in spring. They connect weather patterns to their own experiences, like the feeling of warm summer sun versus cold winter winds. These observations help them understand the cyclical nature of the environment and how living things adapt to these changes.

## Light and Shadow
Children explore how light works and creates shadows. They conduct simple experiments, such as shining a flashlight on a toy to see how its shadow changes shape based on its distance from the wall. They learn that light travels in straight lines and that opaque objects cast shadows. The children enjoy making shadow puppets, observing how the puppet's shape is projected. This provides a concrete basis for understanding the relationship between light sources, objects, and the darkness we call shadows.

## Everyday Materials
This section teaches children to identify and classify common materials found in their homes and school. They distinguish between natural materials like wood and cotton, and human-made materials like plastic and glass. They learn about the purpose of different materials; a wooden chair is strong, and a glass window is transparent. A practical example is identifying the materials of a €1 coin (a steel-nickel alloy) and comparing them to a wooden spoon, understanding why each material is chosen for its specific function.

## Living Things and Their Needs
Children identify basic living things like plants, animals, and themselves. They learn that all living things need a few simple things to survive: water, food, and air. They observe how a houseplant needs watering to stay alive, just like they need to drink water. They also learn about basic life cycles, such as a seed growing into a plant. This foundational knowledge helps them understand the connection between living things and the resources they need from the environment.

## Senses and Body
Children explore how their five senses help them learn about the world. They learn to name the senses: sight (seeing), hearing (listening), smell (smelling), taste (tasting), and touch (feeling). They use these senses during play, like smelling fresh baked goods or feeling the warmth of a sauna. They understand that their body is a tool for gathering information, and they start to connect sensory input to their feelings and understanding, such as bright lights hurting their eyes or loud noises being uncomfortable.
