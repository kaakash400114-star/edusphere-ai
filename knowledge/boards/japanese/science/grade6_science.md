# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 6 Science
## The Structure and Function of Living Things
Students learn that living things are composed of cells. They observe plant and animal cells using a microscope, identifying structures like the cell wall, nucleus, and cytoplasm. They understand that cells form tissues, tissues form organs, and organs form systems. For example, they examine a leaf's cells to see the cell wall and chloroplasts, which are essential for photosynthesis. This hierarchical organization is fundamental to understanding the complexity of life, from a single cell to a bento box filled with rice, vegetables, and fish, each a "system" for a balanced meal.

## Chemical Changes and Energy
Students investigate chemical changes, where new substances with different properties are formed. They learn to distinguish these from physical changes, such as melting ice. Through experiments like the rusting of an iron nail (生じる錆) or the burning of a candle, they identify signs of chemical change, such as color change, gas production, or energy release in the form of heat and light. These reactions follow the law of conservation of mass; the total mass of the reactants equals the total mass of the products, a principle as certain as the punctuality of a Shinkansen train.

## Forces and Motion
Students explore the relationship between forces and motion, particularly through the concept of friction. They learn that friction is a force that opposes motion and is essential for everyday activities. By sliding a book across a table with different surfaces (e.g., smooth wood versus coarse sandpaper), they can feel and measure the difference in friction. This force is what allows us to walk without slipping or to stop a moving bicycle. An example is the friction between tires and the road, which is critical for the safety of the Shinkansen, Japan's high-speed train.

## Electricity and Magnetism
Students understand that electricity is the flow of electrons and can be generated by chemical reactions in batteries. They construct simple electric circuits using a battery (電池), wires, and a small light bulb (電球), learning the terms: power source, conductor, load, and switch. They observe that an electric current creates a magnetic field. This principle is used in electromagnets. A practical application is the electromagnetic braking system used in some Shinkansen models, which provides a smooth and powerful stop using magnetism instead of traditional friction.

## The Solar System and Earth's Environment
Students learn that the solar system consists of the Sun and celestial bodies orbiting it, including eight planets. They understand the Earth's rotation on its axis causes day and night, and its revolution around the Sun causes the seasons. They also study the Earth's atmosphere, which protects us from harmful solar radiation. This seasonal change is deeply felt in Japan during Hanami (cherry blossom viewing), as the timing of the blossoms is a direct result of the seasonal tilt of the Earth and the warming temperatures of spring.

## Measurement and Data Representation in Science
Students learn the importance of accurate measurement and scientific data recording. They practice using appropriate measuring instruments for length (ruler), mass (balance scale), and volume (graduated cylinder). They record their measurements with correct units, such as meters (m), grams (g), and milliliters (mL). They then organize their data into tables and create simple graphs, such as bar graphs or line graphs, to visually represent their findings and identify trends, much like a company might track its profit in yen over a fiscal year to show growth.
