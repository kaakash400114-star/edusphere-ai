# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 1 Science

## Observing Living Things
Students learn that living things grow and change. They observe plants like cherry trees and radish sprouts, noting how they develop from seeds. They also observe small animals such as goldfish and mealworms, describing their movements and features. By caring for these living things, students understand that they need food, water, and air to live and grow. This hands-on experience develops an appreciation for nature and the basic requirements for life.

## Observing Non-Living Things
Students explore the properties of non-living things around them. They observe objects based on their material, such as wood, plastic, metal, and glass. For example, they compare a wooden bento box with a plastic one, noting differences in texture and weight. They also group objects by function, like things used for eating or things used for playing. This helps them classify the world and understand that objects have different characteristics based on what they are made of.

## Observing the Sky and Sun
Students observe the sun and changes in the sky. They learn that the sun provides light and warmth and that its position changes throughout the day. They note the sun's path across the sky from morning to afternoon, using simple words like 'high' and 'low'. They also observe different types of weather, such as sunny, cloudy, and rainy days. This connects them to natural cycles and the importance of the sun for daily life on Earth.

## Observing Magnets
Students learn that some materials, like magnets, can attract or repel certain objects. They explore different types of magnets, such as bar and ring magnets, and test various materials to see if they are magnetic. For instance, they might test a coin, a nail, and a plastic eraser. They discover that magnets can push away from each other (repel) or pull together (attract), and this force can work through materials like paper.

## Observing Water
Students investigate the properties of water in its different states. They observe liquid water, see how ice is a solid form of water, and note that water turns into a gas (steam) when heated. They conduct simple experiments, like seeing if objects float or sink in a bowl of water. They also learn how water is used in daily life, such as for drinking, cooking, and cleaning, reinforcing its essential role for all living things.

## Observing Light and Sound
Students explore how light travels and how sound is made. They observe that light allows us to see objects and that shadows are formed when an object blocks a light source. For sound, they experiment with different materials like drums, bells, and shinkansen models to see how making an object vibrate creates sound. They learn to distinguish between loud and quiet sounds, connecting these observations to their everyday environment.
