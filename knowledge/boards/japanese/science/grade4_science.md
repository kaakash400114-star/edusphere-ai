# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 4 Science
## The Living World and Environment
Plants produce their own food through photosynthesis. This process uses sunlight, water absorbed by roots, and carbon dioxide from the air to create glucose (sugar) and release oxygen. Students learn that plants have essential parts: roots for anchoring and absorbing water, stems for support and transport, and leaves for photosynthesis. For example, a large tree in a Japanese park provides oxygen, shade, and a habitat for birds, demonstrating how plants are vital for the environment and human life, much like a forest in Japan.

## Properties of Matter and Change
Matter has properties such as state (solid, liquid, gas), colour, and texture. A physical change alters the form of a substance, like crushing ice or tearing paper. A chemical change creates a new substance, like burning wood or cooking an egg. Students explore examples from daily life. Mixing flour and water makes a dough, a physical change. However, cooking the bento (boxed lunch) rice is a chemical change, as the heat transforms the raw rice into edible rice with different properties and structure.

## Forces and Motion
Forces, like push and pull, cause objects to move or change shape. Gravity is a force that pulls objects towards the Earth, causing dropped items to fall. Friction is a force that resists motion between surfaces. When a student pushes a toy shinkansen model on a table, a push force moves it forward. The sliding contact with the table creates friction, which eventually stops the model. Understanding these forces explains why the real shinkansen train has a streamlined shape to reduce air friction and move very fast.

## Electricity and Magnetism
Electricity is the flow of electrical charge through a conductor like a wire. A complete path, called a circuit, is needed for electricity to power a device. A battery is a common source of electrical energy. Students learn about simple circuits. For instance, when they connect a small battery to a bulb using wires, a complete circuit is formed, allowing the bulb to light up. This is the same principle used to power the lights in a classroom or the display on a digital watch.

## The Earth and Sky
The Earth rotates on its axis every 24 hours, causing day and night. The Sun appears to rise in the east and set in the west due to this rotation. Students observe the Sun's path across the sky. For example, during hanami, the flower-viewing festival, people enjoy the daytime view of cherry blossoms. As the Sun moves across the sky from east to west, the shadows of the trees change length and position, providing a clear, observable sign of the Earth's daily rotation.

## Measurement and Data
Accurate measurement is essential for science. Length is measured in millimeters (mm), centimeters (cm), and meters (m). Mass is measured in grams (g) and kilograms (kg). Volume is measured in milliliters (ml) and liters (l). Students practice measuring objects. For example, a student might measure a pencil to be 15 cm long, a textbook to have a mass of 300 g, and a juice carton to hold 500 ml of liquid. Recording these measurements accurately is the first step in analyzing data, such as finding the average mass of items.
