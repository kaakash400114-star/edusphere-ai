# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 7 Science
## The Structure and Properties of Matter
Matter is composed of small particles called atoms and molecules. Elements are made of one type of atom, and compounds are formed from two or more different types of atoms bonded together. The three common states of matter are solid, liquid, and gas, distinguished by how their particles are arranged and how they move. For example, the iron in a Shinkansen train is a solid, while the steam from a hot bento is a gas. This structure determines properties like density, which is calculated by dividing mass by volume, such as the density of water being 1 g/cm³.

## Chemical Changes and Reactions
A chemical change occurs when substances react to form new materials with different properties. These changes are often accompanied by observable signs, such as color change, formation of a gas, production of light, or change in temperature. In Japan, combining vinegar (an acid) and sodium bicarbonate to create sodium acetate and carbon dioxide gas is a common example. The Law of Conservation of Mass states that the total mass of the reactants equals the total mass of the products, meaning matter is not created or destroyed in a chemical reaction.

## The Diversity and Classification of Living Things
All living things are classified into a hierarchy: domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, and species. This system groups organisms based on shared characteristics and evolutionary relationships. For instance, the Japanese macaque (*Macaca fuscata*) belongs to the Animalia, Chordata, Mammalia, Primates, Cercopithecidae, and *Macaca* genera. Classification helps scientists understand the vast diversity of life on Earth, including endemic species like the Japanese giant salamander found in clear, fast-flowing rivers.

## Energy and Its Transformations
Energy is the capacity to do work and cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another. The Law of Conservation of Energy states that the total amount of energy in a closed system remains constant. A common transformation is in an electric train, like a Shinkansen, where electrical energy is converted into kinetic energy (motion) with some energy lost as heat and sound. Another example is photosynthesis, where light energy is converted into chemical energy stored in plants, such as the rice in a traditional bento box.

## The Human Body and Health
The human body is organized into systems of organs that work together for specific functions. The digestive system, for example, breaks down food to provide nutrients for energy and growth. This system includes organs like the stomach and intestines. Maintaining good health requires a balanced diet, regular exercise, and adequate hygiene. In Japan, the traditional bento box often provides a balanced meal of rice, fish, and vegetables, reflecting the importance of nutrition in culture and physical well-being.

## The Earth and the Universe
The Earth is part of a solar system within a galaxy called the Milky Way. The Earth is divided into several layers: the solid inner core, the liquid outer core, the mantle, and the crust, upon which we live. The Japanese archipelago is located in a region with high seismic and volcanic activity, a result of its position on the Pacific Ring of Fire. The country invests heavily in technology to monitor these natural phenomena, exemplified by the advanced earthquake early warning system that helps protect citizens.
