# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 12 Science
## Quantum Mechanics and Atomic Structure
Students learn the probabilistic nature of the electron, described by the wave function Ψ and the concept of atomic orbitals. They apply the Schrödinger equation to model the hydrogen atom, deriving quantized energy levels (En = -13.6 eV / n²). The Heisenberg uncertainty principle (ΔxΔp ≥ ħ/2) is studied, emphasizing that precise position and momentum cannot be simultaneously known. These principles underpin the design of advanced semiconductors in Japanese electronics, such as those in the Shinkansen's control systems.

## Thermodynamics and Energy Conversion
This topic covers the laws of thermodynamics with a focus on energy efficiency. Students analyze the Carnot cycle and calculate the maximum theoretical efficiency (η = 1 - Tc/Th) for heat engines. Entropy (ΔS) is explained as a measure of disorder, with the second law stating its universal increase. These concepts are applied to the design of high-efficiency power plants, like those supplying energy for Tokyo's dense urban infrastructure, and to the thermal management of the Shinkansen's propulsion motors.

## Organic Chemistry: Biomolecules and Synthesis
Students explore the structure and function of biomolecules, focusing on proteins, carbohydrates, lipids, and nucleic acids. They study peptide bond formation and the four levels of protein structure, using the example of hemoglobin. The synthesis of organic compounds, including esters and polyamides, is covered, with students applying reaction mechanisms and stoichiometric calculations. This knowledge is vital in Japan's pharmaceutical industry, developing new medicines, and in food science for improving traditional products like miso and bento ingredients.

## Electromagnetic Induction and Modern Applications
The topic details Faraday's law of induction (ε = -dΦB/dt) and Lenz's law. Students analyze the operation of generators and transformers, using Faraday's law to calculate induced electromotive force in systems like the Shinkansen's overhead power lines. The principles of electromagnetic induction are extended to eddy current braking systems, used in many Japanese bullet trains for safe, efficient deceleration without physical contact, exemplifying precision engineering.

## Nuclear Physics and Radiation
Students study the properties of atomic nuclei, including mass defect and binding energy. They learn nuclear decay processes (alpha, beta, gamma) and apply Einstein's mass-energy equivalence (E=mc²) to calculate energy released in reactions. The distinction between nuclear fission and fusion is made, with applications in nuclear power generation, which supplies a significant portion of Japan's electricity. The topic also covers radiation safety protocols and measurement techniques, crucial in a country with advanced nuclear technology and a high population density.

## Evolutionary Biology and Genetics
This section explores the mechanisms of evolution, including natural selection, genetic drift, and gene flow. Students analyze the Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (p² + 2pq + q² = 1) as a model for studying allele frequencies in populations. Modern genetics topics, such as DNA sequencing and polymerase chain reaction (PCR), are covered, with applications in fields like forensic science and medicine. This knowledge supports biodiversity conservation efforts in Japan, such as protecting unique species found in regions like Yakushima, and improving agricultural yields for the nation's food security.
