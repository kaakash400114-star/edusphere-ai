# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 8 Science

## Properties of Matter and Changes
Students learn that matter consists of particles in constant motion. They distinguish between elements, compounds, and mixtures through their structures. A chemical change forms new substances, such as iron rusting, while a physical change alters form, like freezing water. They classify changes as exothermic (releasing heat, e.g., a hand warmer) or endothermic (absorbing heat, e.g., a cold pack). The Law of Conservation of Mass states that mass is neither created nor destroyed during any change. For example, 50 grams of magnesium reacts with 32 grams of oxygen to form exactly 82 grams of magnesium oxide.

## Electricity and Circuits
Students study electric current, voltage, and resistance, understanding Ohm's Law (V = I × R). They construct series and parallel circuits to observe differences. In a series circuit, current is the same at all points; if one lightbulb fails, the entire circuit stops. A parallel circuit provides multiple paths, allowing other bulbs to stay lit. For example, a Shinkansen train uses high-voltage electricity in a complex parallel system to power multiple cabin lights and air conditioning units independently, ensuring reliable operation over long distances.

## Forces and Motion
Students explore Newton's three laws of motion. An object at rest stays at rest unless acted upon by a net force, demonstrating inertia. Force equals mass times acceleration (F = ma). For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. A practical example is a rocket launch; the engine exerts a downward force on exhaust gases, and the gases exert an upward thrust on the rocket. This principle is also applied in braking systems, where the brake pads exert a force on the wheels to slow the vehicle.

## Energy Transformations
Students analyze how energy is converted from one form to another. In a bento box, thermal energy from a hot drink transfers to the surrounding food, warming it. Electrical energy powers a smartphone, converting it into light, sound, and heat. The Law of Conservation of Energy states that energy cannot be created or destroyed. For instance, in a traditional onsen, geothermal energy heats the water, which then transfers its thermal energy to a person's body when they bathe, illustrating a clear energy transfer process.

## Structure and Function of Living Things
Students explore the relationship between an organism's structure and its function. The root system of a plant anchors it and absorbs water and minerals from the soil. Leaves have a large surface area to capture sunlight for photosynthesis. A neuron has a long axon to transmit electrical signals efficiently. For example, the cherry blossom (sakura) has delicate petals adapted for wind pollination. This structural feature maximizes the chance of successful reproduction during hanami season.

## Interaction of Living Things and the Environment
Students learn how organisms interact within ecosystems. A food chain shows the transfer of energy, like grass being eaten by a rabbit, which is then eaten by a fox. Decomposers, such as fungi, break down dead matter and return nutrients to the soil. This process is vital for maintaining the ecosystem. An example of this interaction is found in a forest ecosystem, where fallen leaves decompose to enrich the soil, allowing new plants to grow and support the entire food web, from insects to large animals.
