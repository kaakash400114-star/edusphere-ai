# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 3 Science

## The Living World and Environments
Students learn that living things, such as cherry trees and goldfish, have specific needs like sunlight, water, and food. They observe how trees grow taller in spring and shed leaves in autumn, a cycle called seasons. Animals adapt; for example, sparrows build nests in spring to raise chicks. By comparing a forest and a schoolyard garden, they identify non-living things like stones and desks. This understanding helps them appreciate how environments, such as a park during hanami, provide the necessary conditions for diverse life to thrive.

## Matter and Its Properties
Students explore the states of matter: solid, liquid, and gas. They observe that ice (solid) melts into water (liquid) when heated, and water evaporates into steam (gas). They learn that matter can change state without changing its identity, like water becoming ice. Using familiar examples, they classify objects: a solid desk, a liquid drink like tea, and the invisible gas in a balloon. This foundational knowledge builds their understanding of the physical world around them.

## Forces and Motion at Work
Students investigate push and pull forces. They learn that a push makes a ball roll forward, while a pull brings it back. They observe gravity by dropping an eraser; it always falls down. Using a toy shinkansen model, they discover that a stronger push makes it go faster and farther. By experimenting with magnets, they feel the invisible force of attraction and repulsion. This hands-on approach helps them understand how forces are essential for all movement in daily life.

## Light and Shadow Formation
Students learn that light travels in straight lines and is necessary for seeing. They observe how a lamp creates a shadow of an object, like a bento box, on a wall. Changing the object's distance from the light source alters the shadow's size. They learn that opaque objects block light, creating sharp shadows, while transparent objects, like a clear glass, allow light to pass through. This knowledge connects to activities like watching a shadow puppet show, demonstrating the principles of light in a fun way.

## Earth and Sky Changes
Students study the sun, moon, and stars. They learn that the sun rises in the east and sets in the west daily, causing day and night. The moon's shape appears to change over weeks, showing phases like a crescent or full moon. They observe weather changes, such as sunny days for hanami and rainy days requiring umbrellas. By recording daily temperatures, they understand that weather can change from day to day, influencing their activities and clothing choices.

## Everyday Life and Technology
Students recognize that technology solves problems in daily life. They learn that a train, like the shinkansen, uses powerful engines to transport people quickly. A refrigerator uses electricity to keep food fresh, like milk and vegetables. They observe how simple tools, such as scissors, make work easier. By comparing an old-style kerosene lamp with a modern electric light, they appreciate how technology improves safety and convenience, making life more comfortable for everyone.
