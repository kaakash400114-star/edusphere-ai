# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 9 Science

## Newton's Laws of Motion and Gravitational Force
Students master Newton's three laws of motion. An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion at constant velocity unless acted upon by a net external force. The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass (F = ma). For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. Gravitational force between two masses is calculated using F = G(m₁m₂)/r². A 70 kg person exerts a gravitational force of approximately 686 N on the Earth.

## Chemical Reactions and Energy Changes
Students analyze chemical reactions, distinguishing between exothermic and endothermic processes. Exothermic reactions release heat, like combustion in a butane lighter used for a school bento's flame, while endothermic reactions absorb heat, such as the reaction in a cold pack. The principle of conservation of mass-energy is applied, with bond-breaking requiring energy and bond-forming releasing it. A common reaction is the neutralization of hydrochloric acid with sodium hydroxide, producing salt water and releasing measurable thermal energy.

## Wave Motion and Sound
Students explore the nature of mechanical waves, including transverse and longitudinal types. The wave equation, v = fλ, relates wave speed, frequency, and wavelength. Sound waves are longitudinal pressure waves that require a medium. The speed of sound in air at 20°C is approximately 340 m/s. Intensity, measured in decibels (dB), describes loudness; a normal conversation is about 60 dB. Resonance occurs when an object's natural frequency matches an external frequency, like the specific note that makes a wine glass vibrate destructively.

## Structure and Function of Living Things
Students study the hierarchical organization of life, focusing on cells, tissues, and organ systems. The cell theory states all living things are composed of cells. Key organelles include the nucleus for genetic control and mitochondria for energy production through cellular respiration. In plants, the xylem transports water and minerals upward, while phloem distributes sugars. The human circulatory system functions as a transport network, analogous to the Shinkansen network, efficiently delivering oxygen and nutrients throughout the body.

## Electricity and Magnetism
Students apply Ohm's Law (V = IR) to analyze simple electric circuits, measuring voltage, current, and resistance in units of volts (V), amperes (A), and ohms (Ω). The relationship between electrical energy (E) and power (P) is E = Pt, with power measured in watts (W). A common problem involves calculating the cost of using a 1,500 W heater for 5 hours at a rate of ¥30 per kilowatt-hour, totaling ¥225. The right-hand rule is used to determine the direction of the magnetic field around a current-carrying wire.

## Environmental Science and Resource Management
Students examine the interdependence of organisms within ecosystems and the impact of human activity. The carbon cycle involves the exchange of carbon between the atmosphere, biosphere, and geosphere through processes like photosynthesis and combustion. Resource management emphasizes sustainability; for example, Japan's high recycling rate for PET bottles is a key practice. Students analyze energy consumption, comparing the efficiency of various power sources. Conservation efforts, like protecting habitats for hanami cherry blossoms, highlight the cultural and ecological importance of preserving natural environments.
