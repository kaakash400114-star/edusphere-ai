# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 5 Science
## The Properties of Air
Air has mass and exerts pressure, which we can demonstrate with a simple experiment. Using two balloons of equal size, inflate one and leave the other deflated. Place them on a balance scale to observe that the inflated balloon is heavier. Air pressure is measured in units like pascals (Pa). In Japan, weather reports often mention atmospheric pressure in hectopascals (hPa). A standard bicycle pump works by compressing air, increasing its pressure inside the tire, demonstrating how we can use the force of air.

## How Sound Travels
Sound is a form of energy that travels in waves, requiring a medium like air, water, or a solid. It cannot travel through a vacuum. Sound waves are created when an object vibrates, causing the particles around it to vibrate as well. The pitch of a sound depends on the frequency of these vibrations; high-pitched sounds like a ringing bell have high frequency, while low-pitched sounds like a large drum have low frequency. The loudness of a sound, measured in decibels (dB), is determined by the amplitude of the wave. A bento box dropping on the floor creates a loud sound due to a large amplitude.

## Magnets and Their Forces
Magnets produce a magnetic field, an invisible area of force that can attract or repel other magnetic materials like iron or nickel. Every magnet has two poles: a north pole and a south pole. Like poles (north-north or south-south) repel each other, while opposite poles (north-south) attract. The strength of a magnet is greatest at its poles. This principle is used in many everyday objects. For example, the Shinkansen bullet trains use magnetic levitation (maglev) technology, where powerful magnetic forces lift the train above the tracks, allowing it to move at very high speeds with minimal friction.

## Circuits and Electric Current
An electric current is the flow of electric charge, usually through a wire made of a conductor like copper. For a current to flow continuously, a complete circuit is required, which includes a power source like a battery, connecting wires, and an electrical component such as a light bulb. The brightness of a light bulb depends on the amount of current flowing through it. If the circuit is broken, the current stops and the light turns off. The yen coin contains metallic elements that are conductors, allowing electricity to flow if used in a simple circuit, demonstrating how different materials interact with electric current.

## Changes in Matter
Matter can change its state through processes like melting, freezing, boiling, and condensation. When a solid like ice is heated, it absorbs energy and melts into a liquid, water. If this water is heated further to 100°C at standard atmospheric pressure, it boils and turns into a gas, steam. These are physical changes because the substance itself does not change into a new material. The process of making miso soup involves physical changes; when heated, the water changes state, and solid ingredients like tofu absorb the liquid, changing their form without their chemical makeup being altered.

## The Solar System and Gravity
Gravity is a force that pulls objects toward each other. The Earth's gravity keeps us on the ground and the Moon in orbit around it. The Sun's immense gravity holds the entire solar system together, keeping the planets, including Earth, in their orbits. The Moon orbits Earth because Earth's gravity is stronger than the Moon's, but the Moon's gravity affects Earth, causing the ocean tides. The seemingly slow but continuous process of growth in a plant, like a cherry tree, is influenced by gravity pulling water and nutrients from the roots up the stem. This fundamental force is what allows for hanami, where the fallen petals of the sakura gracefully drift down to the ground.
