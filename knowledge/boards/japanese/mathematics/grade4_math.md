# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 4 Mathematics
## Four Operations with Numbers up to 10,000
Students master the vertical calculation methods for addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division using numbers up to 10,000. For addition and subtraction, they learn to regroup (carry over and borrow) correctly. In multiplication, they calculate numbers with up to three digits multiplied by a one-digit number. For division, they solve problems where a four-digit number is divided by a one-digit number, finding the quotient and remainder. For example, a bento box with 3,580 yen in receipts is divided by 4 people, resulting in 895 yen per person with a remainder of 0 yen.

## Fractions
Students learn the concept of fractions as parts of a whole. They compare unit fractions like 1/2 and 1/4 and find the size of fractions with the same denominator, such as 3/8 versus 5/8. They practice writing fractions for diagrams and solving word problems. For instance, if a bento is divided into 8 equal parts and one part is eaten, the remaining portion is 7/8. They also learn to express fractions in simplest form, like reducing 2/4 to its equivalent, 1/2.

## Solid Figures
Students study the properties of three-dimensional shapes like cubes, rectangular prisms, pyramids, cylinders, and spheres. They identify and describe the number of faces, edges, and vertices. They learn to visualize and draw the shapes from different perspectives. An example is observing a cube-shaped onigiri (rice ball) and identifying its 6 square faces and 12 edges. Students also classify these figures into categories based on their geometric properties and can recognize them in everyday objects like a cylinder-shaped water bottle.

## Measurements of Length and Area
Students learn standard units of length: millimetre (mm), centimetre (cm), metre (m), and kilometre (km). They convert between these units, knowing that 1 kilometre equals 1,000 metres. For area, they use square centimetres (cm²) and square metres (m²). They calculate the area of rectangles and squares by multiplying their length by width. For example, a rectangular bento measuring 15 cm by 10 cm has an area of 150 cm². Students apply this to find the area of simple floor plans in a traditional house.

## Simple Data Representation
Students learn to create and interpret bar graphs and pictographs using scaled data. They understand that graphs represent information visually and can answer questions about the data, such as finding the largest value or the difference between two values. They practice drawing graphs with titles, labels, and a key. For instance, a graph could show the number of cherry blossom (sakura) trees in four parks: Park A (25 trees), Park B (40 trees), Park C (30 trees), and Park D (15 trees). Students then interpret this data to compare park sizes for hanami (flower viewing).

## Quantity and Expression
Students learn to express quantities using numbers and letters. They write simple expressions like 3 × □ to represent an unknown number and solve for the unknown. They also learn to interpret and create simple formulas based on real-life situations. For example, the total cost of Shinkansen tickets can be expressed as '1,000 yen × number of passengers'. If 7 people are traveling, the expression is 1,000 × 7, which equals 7,000 yen. They understand how variables represent changing quantities in a problem.
