# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 6 Mathematics
## Understanding the Number System
Grade 6 students solidify their understanding of place value for whole numbers and decimals, extending to the trillions. They master reading, writing, and comparing numbers, including those with up to six decimal places. A key focus is the concept of significant figures, where students learn to round numbers to a specified digit. For example, they would understand that the price of a bento box, ¥1,234.56, rounded to the nearest yen is ¥1,235. They apply this to approximate the cost of multiple items.

## Fractions and Decimal Operations
Students deepen their knowledge of fractions by finding the least common multiple and greatest common divisor for simplification and finding common denominators. They proficiently add, subtract, multiply, and divide fractions, including mixed numbers. Decimal operations extend to division by a whole number and by a decimal number, using concepts like multiplying both numbers by a power of ten to create an integer divisor. This is used to calculate the average cost of several Shinkansen tickets when the total price is given.

## Ratio and Proportion
This section introduces the formal concept of ratio as a way to compare quantities. Students learn to express ratios in colon, fraction, and word forms (e.g., 3:2, 3/2, "three to two"). They explore the property of ratios and solve simple word problems involving proportional relationships, such as determining the correct ratio of soy sauce to mirin for teriyaki sauce or scaling a recipe for a larger group of hanami picnic attendees.

## Basic Expressions and Equations
Students begin using letters to represent unknown numbers (variables) in simple algebraic expressions. They learn to write one-step equations from word problems involving operations like addition, subtraction, and simple multiplication. The primary method for solving these equations is isolating the variable through inverse operations. For example, to solve for 'x' in the equation x + 450 = 1,000, students would subtract 450 from both sides.

## Area and Volume of Solids
Building on prior knowledge, students calculate the area of trapezoids and prisms using the formula: (Sum of parallel sides × height) / 2. They are formally introduced to the concepts of volume and capacity. Students learn the formulas for the volume of a rectangular prism (length × width × height) and a cube (edge³). They apply this to solve practical problems, such as finding the volume of a water tank or the number of small boxes that fit inside a larger shipping container.

## Data Representation and Analysis
Students learn to create and interpret more complex data visualizations. They construct double bar graphs and pie charts (circle graphs) to represent and compare two sets of categorical data. A key focus is calculating the mode, median, and mean of a data set. They apply these measures of central tendency to analyse information, for instance, finding the average number of visitors to a local park during a specific week or identifying the most popular food item at a school festival.
