# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 10 Mathematics

## Equations and Inequalities
Students learn to solve quadratic equations through factorisation, completing the square, and using the quadratic formula. They apply these methods to find roots and analyse the nature of solutions based on the discriminant. Students also solve linear inequalities with two variables and represent their solution sets on a coordinate plane. For example, they might calculate the time a Shinkansen train takes to overtake another, formulating the situation as a quadratic equation to solve for the precise time in minutes.

## Functions and Graphs
Students study the properties of quadratic functions, expressed in the form $y = ax^2 + bx + c$. They learn to determine the vertex, axis of symmetry, and direction of the parabola's opening. The concept of the composite function $f(g(x))$ is introduced, along with its application to real-world problems. For instance, they might model the area of a bento box's contents based on its price in yen, using a quadratic function to predict the optimal size for maximum volume within a budget.

## Geometric Figures and Measurements
Students explore the properties of circles, including theorems related to chords, tangents, and angles. They calculate the lengths of tangents from an external point to a circle and the measures of angles formed by chords and secants. The area and volume of spheres and cylinders are also covered. A practical example involves determining the amount of soil needed for a circular Zen garden, requiring calculations based on the garden's area and depth, measured in metres.

## Trigonometric Ratios and their Applications
Students define sine, cosine, and tangent as ratios of sides in a right-angled triangle, such as the sine of an angle being the length of the opposite side divided by the hypotenuse. These ratios are applied to solve problems in surveying, for example, using an angle of elevation and a measured distance to find the height of a building. The unit circle extends these definitions to all angles, defining sine and cosine as the y and x-coordinates of a point on the circle, respectively, enabling the analysis of non-right-angled triangles. Trigonometric functions and their reciprocal relationships, such as secant, cosecant, and cotangent, are formally introduced. Students learn to use calculators to find values and angles, essential for solving complex real-world problems in navigation and civil engineering.

## Probability
Students calculate probabilities of single and compound events. They learn to distinguish between independent and dependent events, using the formula $P(A \cap B) = P(A) \times P(B)$ for independent events. The concept of conditional probability is introduced, alongside the use of tree diagrams and tables to organise information. For example, they might calculate the probability of selecting a specific type of sushi roll from a platter, considering different fillings and their likelihoods.

## Statistical Data Analysis
Students learn to represent and interpret bivariate data using scatter diagrams and the concept of correlation. They calculate the line of best fit by the method of least squares to make predictions. Measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) and dispersion (variance, standard deviation) are used to analyse data. A common application involves analysing the number of visitors to a hanami festival over several years to predict future attendance, using linear regression on the collected data points.
