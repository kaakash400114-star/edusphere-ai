# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 3 Mathematics

## Numbers and Calculations
Students extend their understanding of numbers up to 9,999. They learn to read and write numerals in kanji and Arabic form, such as 三千 (3000) and 4521. They master vertical addition and subtraction with four-digit numbers, ensuring precise alignment of place values. Key skills include regrouping (carrying and borrowing) to solve problems like 5,872 - 3,459 = 2,413. Students apply these skills to solve real-world problems involving quantities, such as calculating the total cost of multiple bento boxes, each costing 650 yen.

## Multiplication and Division
Students begin learning vertical multiplication with one-digit multipliers and two- or three-digit dividends. For example, they calculate 23 x 4 = 92 using the standard algorithm. In division, they focus on exact division of two-digit numbers by one-digit numbers, such as 84 ÷ 7 = 12. They use objects and diagrams to understand these operations. A common problem asks how many packets of 5 strawberries each can be made from 30 strawberries, reinforcing the relationship between multiplication and division through practical examples.

## Quantities and Measurements
Students learn about standard units of length (meter, centimeter), mass (gram, kilogram), and time (hour, minute). They measure objects like a pencil in centimeters and a book in grams. A key concept is converting between smaller and larger units, such as 100 centimeters = 1 meter and 1,000 grams = 1 kilogram. They solve problems involving time duration, for instance, calculating that a Shinkansen trip taking 2 hours and 30 minutes ends at 12:20 if it started at 9:50.

## Geometry and Shapes
Students study properties of 2D and 3D shapes. They identify and name plane figures (triangles, quadrilaterals, circles) and solid figures (prisms, pyramids, spheres). They learn about vertices, edges, and faces, identifying a cube has 8 vertices, 12 edges, and 6 faces. They explore symmetry by finding lines of symmetry in shapes like a square or a leaf. A practical activity involves arranging different 3D blocks to understand how shapes combine, like building a small structure from various prisms.

## Word Problems and Applications
Students solve multi-step word problems that require applying multiple arithmetic operations. A typical problem states: "A family bought 3 bento boxes for 800 yen each and 2 bottles of juice for 200 yen each. How much did they spend in total?" Students must calculate 3 x 800 + 2 x 200 = 2,800 yen. They learn to identify the necessary information and choose the correct operations to find the solution, developing logical thinking and precision in their mathematical reasoning.

## Data Handling
Students acquire the ability to gather and compile data through methods such as simple classroom surveys and record the results using tally marks. They then organize this data into two distinct types of graphs: pictographs, where each icon represents a single unit, and bar graphs, where the height of a bar corresponds to the quantity of items surveyed. For instance, after surveying classmates on their preferred hanami flowers, they represent the findings. The key skill of interpretation is then developed, enabling students to extract factual information such as identifying the most popular item or calculating the numerical difference between two data sets.
