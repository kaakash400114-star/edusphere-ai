# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 12 Mathematics
## Complex Numbers and Plane Geometry
Students master the properties of complex numbers, expressed in the form z = a + bi, where i is the imaginary unit. They learn to perform arithmetic operations, including addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division, on complex numbers. The modulus and argument of a complex number are studied, leading to the polar form, r(cos θ + i sin θ). Geometrically, a complex number corresponds to a point on the complex plane. Students solve polynomial equations, including the quadratic formula yielding complex roots, and connect the Argand diagram to vector addition, visualizing the displacement of a Shinkansen train on a track as a sum of its velocity components.

## Sequences and Series
This unit focuses on understanding and manipulating arithmetic and geometric sequences and series. Students derive and apply formulas for the nth term (an) and the sum of the first n terms (Sn) for an arithmetic sequence: an = a1 + (n-1)d and Sn = n/2(a1 + an). For geometric sequences, they use an = a1r^(n-1) and Sn = a1(1 - r^n)/(1 - r). Infinite geometric series are explored with the condition |r| < 1 for convergence. Problems involve calculating the total cost of ingredients for a weekly bento box using arithmetic progression or the total distance traveled by a bullet train that slows down geometrically over successive time intervals.

## Functions and Limits
Students deepen their understanding of functions, focusing on continuity and differentiability. They apply the chain rule and product rule for differentiation, analyzing the behavior of functions such as polynomials, rational functions, and trigonometric functions. The concept of a limit is formally introduced as a foundational principle for calculus. Students evaluate limits at a point and infinity, understanding their connection to horizontal and vertical asymptotes. They learn to determine where a function, like the price of a seasonal hanami picnic, is increasing or decreasing on a closed interval by examining its first derivative.

## Differential Calculus and Applications
In this advanced unit, students use calculus for precise analysis of functions. They learn to find the equations of tangent and normal lines to curves at a given point. The second derivative is used to determine concavity and to identify points of inflection. Optimization problems are solved by finding the maximum or minimum value of a function, such as calculating the dimensions of a cylindrical bento container to maximize its volume for a given surface area. Related rates problems, like determining how fast the radius of a spreading ink stain changes, are solved using implicit differentiation with respect to time.

## Integral Calculus and Applications
Students master the techniques of definite and indefinite integration. They learn the fundamental theorem of calculus, linking differentiation and integration. Rules for integrating common functions, such as polynomials and trigonometric expressions, are applied. The area between two curves is calculated using integration, which can model the difference in land area covered during two different hanami festivals. The volume of a solid of revolution is found by rotating a function's graph around an axis, representing a practical method for designing Japanese lanterns of specific volumes.

## Probability and Statistics
This unit extends probability concepts to continuous distributions. Students study the normal distribution, calculating probabilities and percentiles using z-scores. They learn to distinguish between discrete and continuous random variables. The central limit theorem is introduced, explaining the distribution of sample means. Correlation and regression analysis are used to model the relationship between two variables, such as analyzing the correlation between the number of cherry blossoms (sakura) in bloom and the number of visitors to a park during hanami season. Students interpret the coefficient of determination to assess the model's predictive power.
