# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 9 Mathematics
## 1. Numbers and Expressions
Students explore real numbers, focusing on square roots and irrational numbers. They learn to approximate irrational numbers like √2 or π and distinguish between rational and irrational numbers. Expressions involving square roots are simplified using rules like √a × √b = √(ab). Students also solve quadratic equations by factoring, completing the square, and using the quadratic formula, x = [-b ± √(b²-4ac)] / 2a. Practical examples include calculating the side length of a square garden with an area of 50 square metres, which is an irrational number.

## 2. Functions and Graphs
Students study functions, particularly linear, quadratic, and proportional relationships. They learn to represent functions as equations, tables, and graphs. For quadratic functions, y = ax² + bx + c, students identify the parabola's vertex, axis of symmetry, and intercepts. They analyse real-world relationships, such as the height of a rocket over time or the profit from selling bento boxes, creating and interpreting graphs to solve problems and make predictions.

## 3. Geometric Figures and Proofs
This section covers congruence and similarity of triangles. Students prove triangles are congruent using criteria like SSS, SAS, ASA, and RHS. For similar triangles, they use the property that corresponding sides are proportional and corresponding angles are equal. Examples include proving that two triangles formed by the shadow of a Shinkansen pillar are similar, allowing the calculation of the pillar's height from its shadow's length using proportional reasoning.

## 4. Measurement and Mensuration
Students calculate the area and volume of more complex shapes, including prisms, pyramids, cylinders, and spheres. Formulas for the volume of a sphere (V = 4/3πr³) and the area of a sector (A = (θ/360) × πr²) are applied. They solve problems involving real-world objects, such as finding the volume of a cylindrical water tank to determine the amount of water needed for a hanami festival or calculating the surface area of a bento box to design an appropriate lid.

## 5. Probability and Statistics
Students investigate probability through experimental and theoretical approaches. They calculate probabilities of single and compound events, including independent and dependent events. In statistics, they represent and interpret data using frequency tables, histograms, and box-and-whisker plots. A practical example is analysing the probability of different train delays on the Shinkansen line or surveying classmates' favourite bento ingredients to calculate the likelihood of choosing a specific combination.

## 6. Problem Solving with Mathematical Processes
Students develop advanced problem-solving skills using logical reasoning, algebraic manipulation, and geometric properties. They solve multi-step problems, such as optimising the cost of materials for a product or determining the most efficient route. These problems often integrate multiple topics, requiring students to translate real-world scenarios, like planning a trip within a budget using yen, into mathematical models to find precise solutions and justify their reasoning.
