# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 8 Mathematics

## Numerical Expressions and Equations
Students learn to simplify and solve linear equations with one variable and simultaneous linear equations with two variables. The method of elimination is taught to find integer solutions, such as calculating the cost of items in a bento box where an onigiri costs ¥150 and a tamagoyaki costs ¥200, forming equations like `x + y = 5` and `150x + 200y = 900`. Students practice rearranging equations to isolate variables, applying these skills to solve real-world problems involving proportional relationships and unknown quantities.

## Functions
Students are introduced to the concept of functions as relationships between two quantities, represented by letters like `x` and `y`. They learn to express functions using formulas, tables, and graphs. For example, a function representing the time (`y`) in hours for a Shinkansen traveling at 300 km/h over a distance (`x`) in kilometers is `y = x/300`. Students explore linear functions, identifying the slope and y-intercept, and apply these to model simple scenarios, such as converting temperatures or calculating total cost based on quantity.

## Figures and Measurements
Students study the properties of plane and solid figures, focusing on congruence and similarity. They learn to apply the conditions for triangle congruence (SSS, SAS, ASA) and similarity (AA). For example, determining if two triangular park sections are similar by comparing their corresponding angles. The Pythagorean theorem is used to solve for unknown side lengths in right-angled structures, like calculating the diagonal of a rectangular field. Students also learn to calculate the volume and surface area of prisms, cylinders, and cones using precise formulas.

## Probability
Students explore probability through theoretical calculations and experiments. They learn to calculate the probability of simple and compound events, such as drawing a specific type of sushi from a set or determining the chance of rain during hanami. The formula `P(event) = (number of favorable outcomes) / (total number of possible outcomes)` is applied. Students also study independent events, using tree diagrams to represent scenarios like flipping a coin and rolling a die, ensuring they understand the fundamental principles of chance and data prediction.

## Quantitative Relationships
Students analyze proportional and inverse relationships in various contexts. They learn to express these relationships using equations, such as `y = kx` for direct proportion (e.g., converting yen to dollars at a fixed rate) and `y = k/x` for inverse proportion (e.g., the number of workers and time to complete a task). Students solve word problems involving rates and ratios, like comparing the speeds of two trains or calculating the cost per person for a group activity, emphasizing precise calculations and logical reasoning.

## Mathematical Communication
Students practice clearly and logically communicating mathematical ideas using accurate terminology and symbols. They are taught to write step-by-step solutions, explaining their reasoning when solving problems like finding the optimal number of items to purchase within a budget. Through group activities, students present their findings on topics such as planning a school event, ensuring their arguments are mathematically sound. This skill is essential for demonstrating understanding and collaborating on complex problems in higher education and future careers.
