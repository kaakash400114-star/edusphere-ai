# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 7 Mathematics

## Integers and Rational Numbers
Students master the four operations with positive and negative integers, emphasizing procedural fluency. They apply rules such as "a positive number multiplied by a negative number results in a negative product." The curriculum extends to operations with rational numbers, including fractions and decimals, with a focus on precision in converting between forms. For example, students calculate the total cost of a ¥1,200 bento box and a ¥500 juice, then find the change received after paying with a ¥5,000 bill, demonstrating practical application of integer addition and subtraction.

## Algebraic Expressions and Equations
Students learn to formulate and solve simple linear equations in one variable. The process involves isolating the unknown using inverse operations, such as adding or subtracting the same value from both sides. They translate word problems into algebraic expressions, like representing the cost of 'x' number of shinkansen tickets at ¥14,000 each. The emphasis is on logical, step-by-step manipulation to find the value of 'x' that satisfies the equation, ensuring accuracy and understanding of the equality principle.

## Geometric Figures
The study of geometric figures focuses on the properties of plane figures, particularly triangles and quadrilaterals. Students learn to classify triangles based on angles and side lengths and apply the angle sum property (180 degrees). They explore the characteristics of parallelograms, rectangles, and squares, calculating their perimeter and area. A practical task might involve calculating the area of a rectangular park during hanami, where students use the formula length × width to determine the space available for picnics.

## Ratio and Proportion
Students understand the concept of ratio as a comparison of two quantities, often simplified to their lowest terms. They solve problems involving direct proportion, such as determining the cost of 5 kg of apples when 2 kg cost ¥800. The unitary method is a key technique, where the value of one unit is found first. Real-world examples include recipe scaling, such as calculating the correct amount of rice for a larger group of people for a school event, reinforcing the practical use of proportional reasoning.

## Functions
Students are introduced to the concept of functions as a relationship where one value (the dependent variable) is determined by another (the independent variable). They learn to represent functions using simple formulas, tables, and graphs. For instance, a function can represent the total fare on a train journey based on the distance traveled. The curriculum emphasizes identifying the rule that connects the input to the output and understanding that one input corresponds to exactly one output, laying the foundation for more advanced study.

## Basic Statistics
Students learn to organize and interpret data using bar graphs and pie charts. They calculate the three averages of central tendency: the mean, median, and mode. A practical example involves analyzing the scores of students in a class competition to determine the average performance. The focus is on correctly identifying the median from an ordered list and understanding that the mean is the sum of all values divided by the number of values, providing a foundation for data-based decision-making.
