# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 5 Mathematics
## Numbers and Operations
Grade 5 students deepen their understanding of whole numbers and decimals. They master reading and writing numbers up to the hundred billions place. Students learn to perform the four operations with multi-digit whole numbers using the column method, developing proficiency in calculations such as 45,678 × 9. They extend this to decimal numbers, focusing on addition and subtraction with up to three decimal places using the same principles. This builds the foundation for more complex calculations and ensures a high level of computational accuracy and speed required for problem-solving.

## Fractions
In this unit, students learn to compare and order fractions with different denominators. They master the process of finding a common denominator to compare fractions like 2/3 and 3/4. Students practice converting between improper fractions and mixed numbers, for example, changing 7/2 into 3 ½. They also learn to add and subtract fractions with different denominators, using the bento box as a visual aid to understand equal parts. This develops their ability to handle fractional quantities, a crucial skill for real-world applications like dividing food or materials.

## Basic Geometry
Students explore properties of plane shapes, focusing on quadrilaterals. They classify them as squares, rectangles, parallelograms, trapezoids, and rhombuses based on their properties. They learn to draw these shapes accurately and to calculate their perimeters. For example, they calculate the perimeter of a square bento box if one side is 12 cm. The concept of parallel and perpendicular lines is reinforced through the study of these shapes, providing a visual and concrete foundation for understanding more advanced geometric concepts in later grades.

## Area and Volume
This unit focuses on calculating the area of rectangles and squares, and the volume of rectangular prisms. Students learn the formula for area (Area = length × width) and apply it to calculate the area of a Japanese garden plot. They then progress to volume, learning the formula for the volume of a rectangular prism (Volume = length × width × height). They practice calculating the volume of a shoebox, connecting the concept to real-world objects. This hands-on approach ensures they can distinguish between area (two-dimensional) and volume (three-dimensional) measurements.

## Measurement
Students build on their measurement skills by learning the Japanese units. They practice converting between smaller and larger units of length (e.g., converting 1,500 mm to 1 m 500 cm) and capacity (e.g., converting 2 L 500 mL to 2,500 mL). They use these skills to solve practical problems, such as calculating the length of a shinkansen train or the amount of water needed for a school event. This reinforces the practical application of measurement in daily life and develops their estimation and conversion skills.

## Problem Solving
Students learn to apply their mathematical knowledge to solve multi-step word problems. They are taught to identify the key information, choose the correct operation, and formulate a plan to find the solution. For instance, a problem might involve calculating the total cost of several items in a store, adding consumption tax. Problems can also be related to cultural events, such as figuring out how many yen is needed for sakura-themed snacks for hanami. This develops their logical thinking and application skills, preparing them for complex real-world challenges.
