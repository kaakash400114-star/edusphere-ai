# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 5 English
## Greetings and Introductions
Students will master the standard formal greetings used in Japan. They will learn to say "How do you do?" and "I am pleased to meet you." They will practice responding with "It is a pleasure to meet you, too." Students will also learn to give their full name and introduce their school, for example, "My name is Tanaka. I am from Sakura Elementary School." This practice ensures polite and culturally appropriate social interactions for new situations, such as meeting exchange students or visitors.

## Daily Routines
Learners will describe their daily activities using the simple present tense. They will focus on a structured schedule, for example: "I wake up at 6:30. I eat breakfast at 7:00. I go to school by train." Sentences will use time markers like "in the morning," "at noon," and "in the evening." Students can include personal details like packing their bento box. This helps students clearly explain their typical day to others using precise time-based language.

## Classroom Objects and Subjects
Students will identify common classroom objects and subjects of study. They will learn vocabulary such as "pencil," "notebook," "textbook," "eraser," and "ruler." They will associate these with subjects, for example: "This is my math textbook" or "That is our science notebook." They will practice asking "What is this?" and responding with "It is a map of Japan." This builds the ability to name and inquire about items used for learning, enhancing classroom communication.

## Describing Places and People
Learners will use adjectives to describe locations and individuals. They will learn to describe size, shape, and colour, for example: "The Tokyo Tower is very tall and red." When describing people, they will use traits like "kind," "hardworking," and "friendly." For instance, "My teacher is very patient." They will practice asking "What is your school like?" and answering with sentences containing two descriptive adjectives to provide clear and detailed information.

## Shopping and Money
Students will learn vocabulary related to shopping using Japanese contexts. They will identify items like "bento," "umbrella," and "stamp." They will practice asking for prices: "How much is this bento?" and responding with the yen symbol, "It is 800 yen." They will use simple addition to calculate totals, for example: "One apple is 100 yen. Two apples are 200 yen." This teaches practical numerical and transactional language for everyday shopping situations.

## Hobbies and Free Time Activities
Learners will discuss their interests and free time pursuits. They will use verbs like "read," "draw," "play," and "watch" to form sentences, for example: "I read manga" or "I play kendama." They will also learn to ask "What do you do on the weekend?" and respond with "I enjoy hanami with my family." This vocabulary allows students to share personal information and ask about the hobbies of others, promoting social conversation.
