# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 12 English

## Advanced Rhetorical Analysis and Argumentation
Students learn to deconstruct complex arguments in English-language texts, identifying logical fallacies, rhetorical appeals (logos, pathos, ethos), and sophisticated persuasive devices. They analyze how authors use tone, diction, and structure to build compelling cases, using examples from academic journals and respected media. This skill is applied to evaluate the efficacy of arguments and to construct their own logically sound and evidence-based responses on global topics like economic policy or technological ethics.

## Academic English for STEM and Social Sciences
This module focuses on mastering the specialized vocabulary and grammatical structures of academic and scientific writing. Students learn to write precise lab reports, abstracts, and research papers using formal language. They practice integrating quantitative data, such as economic figures in yen or demographic statistics, into their analyses. Emphasis is placed on clarity, objectivity, and the conventions of citation, preparing them for university-level discourse in their chosen fields.

## Sociocultural Context and Literary Analysis
Students analyze major works of English-language literature through the lens of their historical and sociocultural contexts. They explore themes of globalization, cultural identity, and technological change, drawing parallels to modern Japanese society. For instance, a novel’s depiction of fast-paced life might be compared to the efficiency of the Shinkansen. This develops critical thinking about how cultural values shape narratives and how universal themes resonate across different societies like Japan and the UK.

## Presentation and Debate for Global Citizenship
Students learn to structure and deliver sophisticated persuasive presentations, incorporating visual aids and engaging rhetorical techniques. They participate in formal debates on complex international issues, honing skills in argumentation, rebuttal, and cross-cultural communication. Topics could include the global impact of Japanese pop culture or the ethics of AI development. The goal is to articulate nuanced viewpoints clearly, respectfully challenge opposing perspectives, and represent ideas with the precision expected in global forums.

## Critical Media Literacy
This section teaches students to critically analyze and evaluate information from diverse English-language media sources. They learn to identify bias, propaganda, and misinformation by examining sources, authors, and underlying agendas. Using examples from international news coverage of events in Japan, students assess how language and framing shape public perception. This skill is vital for discerning credible information in an era of digital overload and forming well-informed, independent judgments.

## Project-Based Learning and Independent Research
Students undertake a culminating research project on a topic of their choice, requiring them to formulate a thesis, conduct independent research in English, and synthesize findings from multiple sources. They must present their work with a high degree of linguistic and structural accuracy, possibly analyzing the influence of English on modern Japanese business or the global perception of traditional practices like Hanami. This project demonstrates mastery of all prior skills and prepares them for self-directed academic study.
