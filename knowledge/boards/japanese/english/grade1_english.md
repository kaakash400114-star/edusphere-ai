# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 1 English
## Self-Introduction
Students learn to state their name and simple personal information. They memorize the sentence pattern "My name is [name]." They learn to ask "What's your name?" and respond to it. Students practice basic classroom greetings like "Hello," "Good morning," and "Goodbye." They use these phrases in role-play with classmates and the teacher, focusing on clear pronunciation and polite intonation. For example, a student will say "My name is Yuki" when asked their name.

## Classroom Commands
Students memorize and respond to essential English commands used in the classroom. They learn actions for "Stand up," "Sit down," "Open your book," and "Close your book." These commands are practiced through "Simon Says" to build listening skills. Students perform the actions quickly and correctly when given the instruction. The goal is to develop fluency in following multi-step directions in English. For example, they will stand up when they hear the teacher say "Stand up."

## Basic Nouns: Objects
Students learn the English names for common objects in their classroom and daily life. The vocabulary list includes "pencil," "book," "desk," "chair," and "door." They practice saying these words while pointing to the actual objects. They are also introduced to the question "What's this?" and the answer "It's a [noun]." They build a foundation for identifying and naming items correctly. For example, they will point to a pencil and say "It's a pencil."

## Numbers 1-20
Students master the correct pronunciation and order of English numerals from one to twenty. They practice counting objects, such as pencils or erasers, up to twenty. Students learn to recognize the written form of each number and connect it to the spoken word. They also practice simple counting songs and chants to reinforce memorization. This includes using numbers for classroom activities, like counting how many students are present. For example, they will count ten erasers aloud.

## Colours and Shapes
Students learn the English names for primary and common colours: red, blue, yellow, green, and black. They also learn basic shapes: circle, square, and triangle. Students practice identifying colours and shapes by playing games like "I Spy," saying "I spy something red." They describe objects using both colour and shape, such as "a red square." This builds descriptive vocabulary and observational skills. For example, they will point to a blue circle.

## Japanese Cultural Words
Students are introduced to specific Japanese cultural words in English to build vocabulary and cultural identity. They learn "yen" for the currency, "bento" for a lunch box, "shinkansen" for the bullet train, and "hanami" for cherry blossom viewing. They practice saying these words while looking at pictures or real objects. This connects their English studies directly to their familiar environment, making the language relevant and meaningful. For example, they will say "shinkansen" when shown a picture of the train.
