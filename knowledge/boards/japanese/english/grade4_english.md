# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 4 English
## Daily Activities and Routines
Students learn to describe their daily schedules using the simple present and present continuous tenses. They master telling time by the hour and half-hour using both digital and analogue clock formats. Key phrases include "I get up at 7 o'clock" and "I am studying English now". They practice structuring sentences to explain their sequence of morning, school, and evening activities. Vocabulary includes specific school subjects, such as "kokugo" (Japanese language), "sansu" (mathematics), and "rika" (science), reflecting a typical primary school day in Japan.
## Food and Meals
This unit focuses on food vocabulary and polite requests. Students learn to name common Japanese foods such as "onigiri" (rice balls), "bento" (boxed lunch), and "sushi". They practice using polite forms like "May I have some...?" and "Can I get...?". They also learn to express likes and dislikes with "I like/dislike...". Counting items is reinforced with counters like "hon" for long objects. The lesson emphasizes polite dining etiquette, a key aspect of Japanese culture, when ordering or requesting food at a restaurant or from a home meal.
## Transportation and Travel
Students learn vocabulary for different modes of transport. They focus on high-speed trains ("shinkansen"), subways ("chikatetsu"), and buses. Key phrases include "I go to school by bus" and "The shinkansen is fast". They learn to ask for and give simple directions using prepositions like "next to" and "across from". The unit uses practical examples like navigating from Tokyo Station to Asakusa, using landmark vocabulary. They practice converting between kilometers and meters, connecting language with practical, real-world measurements used in Japan.
## Weather and Seasons
Students learn to describe the weather using adjectives like "sunny", "rainy", and "cloudy". They expand this to discuss the four distinct seasons of Japan: spring ("haru"), summer ("natsu"), autumn ("aki"), and winter ("fuyu"). They learn seasonal vocabulary such as "sakura" for spring and "yuki" for snow. Key sentence patterns are "It is [adjective]" and "I like [season] because...". This connects language to cultural events like "hanami" (cherry blossom viewing) and winter sports, fostering an appreciation of Japan's seasonal cycles.
## Hobbies and Free Time
This unit introduces vocabulary for hobbies and free-time activities. Students learn to use phrases like "I play [sport]" and "I enjoy [activity]". Specific Japanese examples include "kendō" (kendo) and "shōgi" (chess). They learn to ask about others' interests using "What do you do in your free time?". The unit also covers frequency adverbs like "always", "sometimes", and "never". Students practice comparing their activities with others, using comparative structures like "I play soccer more often than tennis".
## Shopping and Money
Students learn vocabulary for shopping in a Japanese "depāto" (department store) or "konbini" (convenience store). They practice asking for prices using "How much is this?". The lesson focuses on Japanese currency, the "yen" (¥), and amounts like "100 yen" and "500 yen". Students learn simple calculations, totaling costs of multiple items (e.g., "An apple is 100 yen and a banana is 150 yen. The total is 250 yen."). The unit reinforces polite phrases for buying items, such as "Sumimasen" (excuse me) and "Arigatō gozaimasu" (thank you).
