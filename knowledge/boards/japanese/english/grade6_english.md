# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 6 English

## Basic Interpersonal Communication Skills (BICS)
Students develop fluency in everyday conversations through structured dialogues and role-play. They practice initiating, responding to, and closing simple exchanges using polite forms. Lessons focus on basic question-and-answer routines, such as inquiring about the weather, hobbies, or weekend plans using target grammar patterns. For example, students might create dialogues asking, "Did you eat a bento for lunch?" or "I visited my grandparents over the weekend."

## Listening Comprehension: Authentic Short Talks
Students listen to short, clear audio or video clips, such as a teacher’s explanation of school rules or a friend’s description of their weekend. They identify key details, sequence of events, and main ideas. Listening tasks involve answering "who, what, where, and when" questions about familiar topics using familiar vocabulary. A sample task could be listening to a description of a hanami picnic and answering specific questions about the location and food eaten.

## Reading Comprehension: Narrative and Informational Texts
Students read short stories, folktales, or simple informational passages with familiar vocabulary. They identify characters, settings, main events, and simple cause-and-effect relationships. Students are introduced to strategies like skimming for the main idea and scanning for specific information, such as prices in yen or the operating hours of a shinkansen station. A reading sample might be a short passage about a traditional Japanese festival.

## Writing: Descriptive Paragraphs
Students learn to write well-structured descriptive paragraphs with a clear topic sentence, supporting details, and a concluding sentence. They use specific and sensory vocabulary to create images, describing places, objects, or events. For example, a student might write a paragraph describing the view from a train window, focusing on the landscape, or a paragraph about the taste and ingredients of their favorite bento box.

## Grammar: Adverb Clauses of Time and Reason
Students study the structure and use of adverb clauses to show time and reason. They learn to combine simple sentences using conjunctions like "when," "because," and "so." For example, they practice transforming "The train was late. I missed my meeting" into "I missed my meeting because the train was late." Students analyze correct sentence structures and apply them in their own writing about personal experiences.

## Cultural Awareness: Social Customs in Japan
Students explore and discuss common social customs in Japan. They learn appropriate language and behavior in specific contexts, such as making a polite request or offering a small gift. Lessons involve understanding concepts like "omotenashi" (hospitality) through scenarios such as being a guest at a home or ordering food at a restaurant, using polite set phrases to interact respectfully with others.
