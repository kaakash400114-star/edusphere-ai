# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 6 English


## Basic Interpersonal Communication Skills (BICS)
Students in Grade 6 acquire foundational interpersonal communication skills through structured dialogues and role-play, focusing on initiating, responding to, and closing exchanges with appropriate politeness. They learn key formulas for conversational management, including methods for asking direct and indirect questions using target grammatical structures. For example, students practice using polite forms to inquire about activities, such as by asking, 「週末に何をしましたか？」 (Shuumatsu ni nani o shimashita ka?), to initiate dialogue. They also respond appropriately using sentence patterns like 「はい、公園で遊びました」 (Hai, kouen de asobimashita). These activities build fluency in essential interpersonal interactions necessary for academic and social settings.

## Listening Comprehension: Authentic Short Talks
Students listen to short, clear audio or video clips, such as a teacher’s explanation of school rules or a friend’s description of their weekend. They identify key details, sequence of events, and main ideas. Listening tasks involve answering "who, what, where, and when" questions about familiar topics using familiar vocabulary. A sample task could be listening to a description of a hanami picnic and answering specific questions about the location and food eaten.

## Reading Comprehension: Narrative and Informational Texts
Students analyze narrative texts, including modern short stories and folktales, to identify main characters, settings, and the sequence of events, while also interpreting simple cause-and-effect relationships. For informational texts, such as guides to local museums or pamphlets explaining historical sites, students learn to distinguish fact from opinion and summarize key details. They are explicitly taught comprehension strategies, including skimming to determine the main idea of a passage and scanning to locate specific information, such as a train fare or the opening hours for a city library. A sample task involves reading a fictional diary entry about a school trip to Nara and identifying the protagonist's feelings towards the Todai-ji Temple.

## Writing: Descriptive Paragraphs
Students learn to write a cohesive descriptive paragraph using a clear topic sentence, followed by 4-6 supporting sentences that use specific details, sensory language, and vivid vocabulary to create a mental image. A concluding sentence should summarize the key impression without introducing new information. For instance, a student could describe a crowded festival by detailing specific sounds (clapping, laughter), sights (colorful yukata, lanterns), and smells (grilled corn, sweet pancakes), all supporting the topic sentence "The summer festival is a celebration of the senses." They are also taught to apply a "show, don't tell" approach, transforming a simple sentence like "The room was messy" into a descriptive one that reveals the mess through specific objects and details.

## Grammar: Adverb Clauses of Time and Reason
Students analyze adverb clauses of time and reason to understand how they express relationships between ideas and subordinate one clause to another. They learn to identify subordinating conjunctions such as "when," "while," "after," and "because," which connect these dependent clauses to main clauses. They study the structure that a dependent clause must begin with a subordinating conjunction and cannot stand alone as a complete sentence, unlike the main clause it modifies. Students practice correctly combining simple sentences to show cause-and-effect, such as changing "It started to rain. The game was canceled" into "The game was canceled because it started to rain." They also learn to express temporal sequence with sentences like "I will do my homework after I finish dinner.

## Cultural Awareness: Social Customs in Japan
Students will analyze Japanese social customs and the specific linguistic structures required for appropriate interaction. They will study the formula for polite requests using the honorific form *kudasai* and the necessity of offering gifts (*omiyage*) when visiting someone's home, demonstrating an understanding of group reciprocity. Lessons will cover the concept of *keigo* (honorific language), focusing on the distinction between respectful (*sonkeigo*) and humble (*kenjougo*) speech through scenario-based practice. Additionally, students will explore the practical applications of *teineigo* (polite language) in contexts such as ordering food at a restaurant and using set phrases like sumimasen to get attention politely. Finally, the cultural concept of *omotenashi*, or anticipating a guest's needs, will be examined as a core element of Japanese hospitality.
