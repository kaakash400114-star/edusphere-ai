# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 3 English

## Classroom and Greetings
Grade 3 students master essential classroom directives and polite greetings through structured practice. They learn to follow commands such as "Stand up," "Sit down," and "Please open your textbook on page 15." Greetings focus on appropriate time-based responses, like "Good morning" before noon and "Good afternoon" after noon. Students are taught to formally address teachers by adding "-sensei" to their name. To demonstrate attendance, they must respond clearly to their name being called with "Hai!" (Yes!). This systematic instruction establishes a respectful and orderly classroom environment, ensuring students understand the rules for interaction and daily classroom routines.

## Self-Introduction and Friends
Students master two core self-introduction formulas: "I am [name]" and "This is [name]" when introducing others. They learn to pose the direct question, "What's your name?" and formulate the response, "My name is [name]." To build conversational fluency, these structures are practiced in dialogues, such as greeting a peer with "Hello! What's your name?" and responding, "My name is Yamada. This is my friend, Watanabe." This method enables students to identify classmates and actively participate in classroom social interactions using standard polite phrases.

## Numbers, Time, and Money
Students practice numbers up to 100 and tell simple times like "seven o'clock." They learn to count yen using the Japanese currency system. For example, "This is 100 yen" or "I have 500 yen." They can identify coins and bills and make simple amounts. This practical skill connects math to real-life situations in Japan, such as buying things at the school store or understanding prices for items like a bento lunch.

## Daily Routines and the Calendar
Students learn to talk about their daily schedules using time phrases. They use verbs like "get up," "eat breakfast," and "go to school" with times, e.g., "I eat breakfast at seven o'clock." They also learn days of the week and months, linking them to school events. For instance, "We have a sports day in October" or "I see cherry blossoms in April." This structures their understanding of time and recurring events.

## Food and Bento Lunches
Students learn names of common foods and the phrase "I like / I don't like [food]." They describe a bento lunch, a common Japanese meal. For example, "I have rice, fish, and apples in my bento." They can ask, "Do you like sushi?" and answer "Yes, I do" or "No, I don't." This vocabulary helps students discuss their meals and food preferences, reflecting on a key part of daily life.

## Transportation and Scenery
Students expand their vocabulary to include transportation modes such as "taxi," "ship," and "bicycle," and natural scenery words like "forest," "beach," and "cityscape." They learn to use the preposition "by" to indicate method of travel, for example, "I go to the park by bicycle." The curriculum introduces the comparative structures "~er than" and "more ~ than," enabling sentences like "A bus is slower than a shinkansen." Furthermore, students practice forming compound sentences using "and" to connect a mode of transport and a destination, such as "The train goes through the mountain and to the sea." Through structured sentence patterns, they describe movement between various locations and the surrounding environment.
