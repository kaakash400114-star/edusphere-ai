# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 2 English


## Basic Greetings and Introductions
Students learn standard polite greetings for different times of day. They master the formula: "Good morning/afternoon. My name is [Name]. Nice to meet you." They practice responding with "Nice to meet you, too." Students use this formula with peers. For example, a student says, "Good morning. My name is Sora. Nice to meet you." The correct response is, "Nice to meet you, too, Sora." This structure is used for first-time meetings and classroom roll call.

## Classroom Instructions and Objects
Students master imperative verbs for core classroom routines through drill and response practice, focusing on commands like "open," "close," "stand up," and "sit down." Concurrently, they acquire the vocabulary to identify common objects such as "pen," "pencil," "eraser," and "book." The pedagogy involves clear, verbal instructions where the teacher models an action and students replicate it, for instance, "Stand up, please," with students performing the corresponding physical movement. Instructional sequencing pairs commands with objects, demonstrated in activities like "Show me your pencil," requiring immediate physical identification. This integrated methodology fosters precise comprehension and application of essential language for daily classroom interaction.

## Counting and Numbers to 100
Students systematically develop proficiency with numbers and operations from 1 to 100, focusing on composing and decomposing two-digit numbers into tens and ones. They learn to count forward and backward from any given number and to skip-count by twos, fives, and tens, establishing number patterns and relationships. Concrete manipulatives, such as base-ten blocks or yen coin groupings, are used to visually represent the place value system, for instance, showing 43 as 4 tens and 3 ones. Students master reading and writing numerals and their corresponding number words, ensuring accurate representation of quantities. The curriculum emphasizes applying these skills to practical word problems involving addition and subtraction within 100, using diagrams and equations to model their thinking.

## Daily Routines and Time
Students master vocabulary for daily activities such as wake up, eat breakfast, and go to school, connecting them to specific clock times expressed in hours. They learn to formulate simple sentences using the structure "I [verb] at [time]" to describe their personal schedules, for example, "I eat lunch at 12:00." Using a 12-hour clock format, students practice distinguishing between morning, afternoon, and evening times by incorporating time-of-day adverbs like a.m. and p.m. The curriculum emphasizes using this vocabulary in pair work to ask and answer questions about daily routines, thereby reinforcing the practical application of time concepts in real-world contexts.

## Simple Sentences: "This is..." and "I Like..."
Students learn to construct declarative sentences using the pattern "This is a/an [Noun]" to identify and describe objects, such as "This is a bento box" or "This is an eraser." They are introduced to the indefinite article "a" before consonant sounds and "an" before vowel sounds. For expressing personal preferences, students master the simple structure "I like [Noun]" to state their favourites, exemplified by phrases like "I like apples" or "I like the shinkansen." This pattern allows them to communicate positive feelings towards specific items or concepts. Instruction focuses on mastering the correct article usage and the accurate pronunciation of target nouns through structured practice.

## Traditional and Cultural Vocabulary
Students expand their vocabulary by learning English terms for specific Japanese cultural items and concepts. The instruction method involves presenting words such as "yen" (currency), "bento" (lunchbox), "shinkansen" (bullet train), and "hanami" (flower viewing), followed by structured practice. Students are required to construct simple, grammatically correct sentences using these terms, like "I save my yen" or "We eat bento for lunch." They then apply their knowledge by identifying these words from visual aids and reading short, contextualized passages. This pedagogical approach directly reinforces vocabulary acquisition and fosters a connection between language learning and the students' daily cultural environment.
