# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 2 English

## Basic Greetings and Introductions
Students learn standard polite greetings for different times of day. They master the formula: "Good morning/afternoon. My name is [Name]. Nice to meet you." They practice responding with "Nice to meet you, too." Students use this formula with peers. For example, a student says, "Good morning. My name is Sora. Nice to meet you." The correct response is, "Nice to meet you, too, Sora." This structure is used for first-time meetings and classroom roll call.

## Classroom Instructions and Objects
Students learn imperative verbs for classroom activities, such as "open," "close," "listen," and "stand up." They identify common objects like "pen," "pencil," "eraser," and "book." For example, a teacher says, "Please open your book." Students must locate their textbook and perform the action. Students practice with real objects: "Show me your eraser." This develops immediate comprehension of essential classroom commands and vocabulary.

## Counting and Numbers to 100
Students master counting from 1 to 100. They learn the pattern for numbers 11-19 and the tens (twenty, thirty). They practice counting objects, such as pencils or yen coins. For example, a student counts 45 yen coins correctly: 40, 41, 42, 43, 44, 45. They also identify numbers in written form. Daily drills focus on rote counting to build speed and accuracy, which is a foundation for later mathematics.

## Daily Routines and Time
Students learn vocabulary for daily activities like "wake up," "eat breakfast," "go to school," and "sleep." They link these to simple clock times. For example, "I eat breakfast at 7:00" or "I go to school at 8:00." They use these sentences to describe their own schedule. A student might say, "I wake up at 6:30." This structure connects daily life with the concept of time in hours.

## Simple Sentences: "This is..." and "I Like..."
Students master the simple sentence structure "This is a/an [Noun]." They use it to identify objects, such as "This is a bento box." They also learn "I like [Noun]." They express preferences with this structure. For example, "I like apples" or "I like the shinkansen." Students practice by describing items in pictures and their own favourite things, focusing on correct article usage and noun pronunciation.

## Traditional and Cultural Vocabulary
Students learn specific Japanese cultural terms in English. These include "yen," "bento," "shinkansen," and "hanami." They use these words in simple sentences. For example, "My mother makes a bento for me" or "The shinkansen is fast." They practice identifying these words in pictures and short descriptions. This builds vocabulary that connects their English studies directly to their cultural identity and environment.
