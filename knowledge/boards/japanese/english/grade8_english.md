# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 8 English
## Present Perfect Tense
Students master the structure for past actions with present results: have/has + past participle. This tense connects past events to the current moment, as in "I have visited Kyoto" (indicating the experience is relevant now). We use it with 'ever' and 'never' for lifetime experiences: "Have you ever seen the shinkansen?" or "She has never eaten a bento box." It is also used for actions continuing to the present: "He has lived in Tokyo for five years." The distinction between the simple past and present perfect is a key focus for precise expression.
## Comparative and Superlative Adjectives
Students learn to compare qualities using three adjective forms. For short adjectives, add -er for comparative (taller) and -est for superlative (tallest). For long adjectives, use 'more' and 'most' (more beautiful, most beautiful). A key rule is spelling changes, like dropping the 'e' in nicer (not niceer). A typical Japanese example is comparing prices: "A bento from the station is more expensive than one from the convenience store, making it the most expensive option."
## Describing Places and Directions
This unit focuses on precise vocabulary for giving and understanding directions. Students learn prepositions of place: 'next to', 'between', and 'across from'. We practice constructing sentences using cardinal directions: "The library is north of the city hall." An activity involves describing locations using familiar landmarks, such as, "The post office is between the park and the market." Mastery includes following and writing multi-step directions, like navigating from the school to a local shrine.
## Daily Routines and Time Expressions
Students learn to express habitual actions and sequences using the simple present and time expressions. Vocabulary includes verbs related to daily life (wake up, eat breakfast, commute) and time phrases ('in the morning', 'every day'). A key example is describing a school day: "I usually leave home at 7:00 AM and arrive at school by 8:00 AM." Students practice comparing routines, such as "My friend goes to bed earlier than I do," using adverbs of frequency ('always', 'usually', 'sometimes').
## Reading for Information and Main Idea
This unit develops the skill of extracting key information from a text. Students learn to identify the topic sentence, which is often the first sentence of a paragraph. They practice skimming for main ideas and scanning for specific details, like names, dates, and numbers. A text might describe the shinkansen, and students must identify its top speed and main routes. The goal is to move beyond literal comprehension to understanding the author's primary purpose in a text about a familiar cultural event like hanami.
## Basic Paragraph Structure with Evidence
Students learn to construct a formal paragraph with a clear topic sentence, supporting sentences, and a concluding sentence. A critical skill is providing specific evidence to support a main idea. For example, to argue that Tokyo is an exciting city, a student might write: "Tokyo is an exciting city because it has many unique events. For instance, the annual hanami festival draws millions of people to see the cherry blossoms. This shows the city's vibrant culture." Emphasis is placed on logical order and using cohesive devices ('for example', 'in conclusion').
