# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 9 English

## Information Structure and Organization
Students learn to logically structure information using clear topic sentences, supporting details, and concluding statements. They master the organization of paragraphs using cohesive devices such as 'furthermore,' 'in contrast,' and 'consequently.' For example, a paragraph explaining the efficiency of the Shinkansen would begin with a topic sentence, provide details about punctuality and safety records as supporting evidence, and conclude with its impact on Japan's economy. This skill is applied to writing expository texts and preparing outlines for speeches.

## Grammatical Accuracy and Complex Sentence Construction
Students focus on mastering complex sentence structures for precise expression. They learn to form conditional sentences (e.g., 'If you visit Japan during hanami, you will experience beautiful cherry blossoms'), relative clauses (e.g., 'The bento, which is a carefully prepared lunchbox, is a popular meal'), and passive voice constructions (e.g., 'The new high-speed line will be opened next year'). Exercises involve transforming simple sentences into complex ones to convey nuanced relationships between ideas, such as cause and effect or hypothesis.

## Critical Reading and Text Analysis
Students develop skills to analyze texts in depth, identifying the author's purpose, main argument, and supporting evidence. They learn to differentiate between facts, opinions, and persuasive language. A key exercise involves reading an article about Japan's economic policies, identifying specific data points like the GDP growth rate, and evaluating the author's conclusion. Students are also taught to recognize bias and compare different viewpoints on a single topic, such as the debate over traditional versus modern education in Japan.

## Presentation and Public Speaking Skills
Students learn to deliver clear and engaging presentations using effective vocal projection, pace, and body language. They practice structuring a speech with a strong introduction, well-organized body, and memorable conclusion using rhetorical devices. For instance, a persuasive speech on the importance of environmental protection might include personal anecdotes and statistics on Japan's recycling rates. Peer evaluation is used to provide feedback on clarity and persuasiveness, with an emphasis on formal presentation etiquette suitable for an academic setting.

## Sociocultural Context and Communication
Students explore how language reflects culture by studying Japanese concepts such as *omotenashi* (hospitality) and their English equivalents. They analyze how communication styles differ, such as the indirectness often used in Japanese business English versus the directness common in Western contexts. Through role-plays, students practice navigating scenarios like negotiating a price in yen or giving directions to a tourist near the Senso-ji temple, building cross-cultural awareness and pragmatic competence essential for global interactions.

## Research and Documentation Skills
Students are taught to conduct research using reliable sources and to document their work correctly. They learn to formulate research questions, gather information from academic journals, websites, and books, and synthesize it into a coherent report. A key task is writing a research paper on a topic like the history of the yen, requiring proper in-text citations and a bibliography in a standardized format. This project emphasizes academic integrity and the ability to present factual information with precision and objectivity.
