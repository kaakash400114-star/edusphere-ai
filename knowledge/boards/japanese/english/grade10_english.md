# Japanese (MEXT) Grade 10 English

## Understanding Complex Reading Texts
Students will analyse sophisticated English texts, including essays and news reports, to identify main arguments and supporting details. They will learn to interpret authorial intent, tone, and perspective. For example, students can read an article about the efficiency of the Shinkansen and deconstruct how statistics and positive language are used to present a favourable view. They will practice extracting key information and understanding implied meanings through context, moving beyond literal comprehension to inferential analysis.

## Structured Essay Writing for Academic Purposes
Students will master the five-paragraph essay structure, focusing on clear thesis statements, logical paragraphing with topic sentences, and supporting evidence. They will learn to write introductions that present a clear argument and conclusions that synthesise main points. For instance, a student might write an essay arguing the cultural significance of hanami, using specific examples of cherry blossom festivals in locations like Ueno Park as supporting evidence for each paragraph's central idea.

## Advanced Vocabulary for Precision
This unit focuses on expanding academic and nuanced vocabulary to achieve precision in expression. Students will learn synonyms, antonyms, and word families to avoid repetition and convey subtle differences in meaning. For example, they will distinguish between ‘enormous’, ‘substantial’, and ‘considerable’ when describing economic figures in yen, or use ‘meticulous’ instead of ‘careful’ to describe the preparation of a traditional bento box. The goal is to elevate language from simple description to sophisticated analysis.

## Formal Grammar and Syntax
Students will refine their control of complex English grammatical structures. This includes mastering advanced conditional sentences (e.g., mixed conditionals), passive voice for formal contexts, and the use of relative clauses to create detailed descriptions. For example, a student might write a sentence like, "The new policy, which was implemented to boost tourism, has significantly increased the number of foreign visitors using the shinkansen," demonstrating their ability to integrate clauses for more formal and informative writing.

## Critical Listening and Note-Taking
Students will develop skills to understand extended spoken English, such as lectures and presentations, on academic topics. They will practice active listening to identify key points, arguments, and examples. Concurrently, they will learn efficient note-taking systems, such as Cornell or mind-mapping, to record information. For instance, while listening to a talk about the Japanese economy, they must distinguish between main ideas like GDP growth and specific data points, such as a 2.3% increase in the value of the yen against the dollar.

## Oral Presentation and Discussion Skills
Students will learn to deliver formal, structured oral presentations on complex topics, using clear organisation, visual aids, and formal language. They will practice speaking with appropriate pronunciation, intonation, and pacing. Furthermore, they will engage in structured debates and discussions, learning to present arguments clearly, rebut opposing viewpoints with evidence, and ask pertinent questions. For example, a student might present the case for traditional versus modern bento designs, followed by a class discussion on the merits of each.
