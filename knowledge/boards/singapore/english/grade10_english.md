# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 10 English
## Synthesis and Evaluation of Information
Students learn to evaluate and synthesise information from multiple texts, distinguishing fact from opinion. They analyse authors' perspectives, biases, and intentions, particularly in Singaporean media reports on topics like urban planning or national policies. This involves cross-referencing data from at least two sources to form a coherent, evidence-based conclusion. For example, students compare articles on the Cross Island Line MRT project, assessing economic and environmental viewpoints to formulate a balanced response, honing critical thinking essential for informed citizenship and future academic pursuits.

## Critical and Inferential Reading
Students develop critical reading skills to analyse how writers use stylistic devices and rhetorical techniques to shape meaning and evoke emotion. They examine narrative texts like short stories set in Singapore, identifying the use of setting, characterisation, and dialogue to explore themes such as identity or societal change. A key focus is making inferences about characters' motivations and the underlying social commentary, allowing them to engage deeply with texts and appreciate the nuances of language in diverse cultural contexts.

## Argumentative and Persuasive Writing
Students master writing structured, persuasive arguments on contemporary issues relevant to Singapore. They formulate clear, defensible theses using logical reasoning, textual evidence, and persuasive devices like rhetorical questions or appeals to shared values. For instance, an essay on the coexistence of heritage shophouses with modern HDB estates must present a cogent argument, anticipate counter-arguments, and use formal, academic language to convince the reader, preparing them for argumentative components in national examinations like the O levels.

## Nuances in Language and Style
Students explore how to adapt language and style for different purposes, audiences, and contexts. This includes analysing and writing various genres, such as formal proposals for a community project in an HDB estate or engaging travel blogs about hawker centres like Lau Pa Sat. They learn to shift between registers, employing precise vocabulary and varied sentence structures to achieve specific communicative goals, demonstrating sophisticated control of language for effective and appropriate expression.

## Visual Text Analysis
Students learn to deconstruct and analyse multimodal texts, such as infographics on Singapore’s water sustainability or public service announcements on racial harmony. They identify key visual elements like images, layout, and colour, and explain how these interact with written language to convey a specific message and influence the viewer. This skill is crucial for understanding how information is presented in a media-rich environment and for evaluating the persuasive power of visual communication in daily life.

## Research and Information Literacy
Students acquire advanced research skills to locate, select, and evaluate relevant information from credible sources, including academic journals and reputable online databases. They learn to attribute sources correctly using a standard citation format and synthesise findings into a coherent research report on a Singapore-centric topic, such as the history of the Singapore River or the economic impact of tourism. This process develops digital literacy and academic integrity, preparing them for independent research at higher levels of education.
