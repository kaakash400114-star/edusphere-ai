# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 7 English

## Narrative Writing
Students learn to craft engaging narratives with a well-structured plot, effective character development, and vivid sensory details. They focus on techniques such as foreshadowing and building suspense to create a compelling story arc. A key element is the use of appropriate and varied vocabulary to enhance description. For example, a student might describe the bustling atmosphere of a hawker centre using onomatopoeia like "sizzle" and "clatter" to immerse the reader. Writing conventions include correct paragraphing and dialogue formatting to maintain clarity and flow.

## Comprehension and Visual Text Analysis
Students develop skills to interpret and evaluate both written and visual texts, including advertisements, infographics, and posters. They learn to identify the creator's purpose, target audience, and key persuasive devices such as imagery, slogans, and colours. For instance, an MRT poster promoting a new line would be analysed for its use of national symbols and call-to-action language. The Concrete-Pictorial-Abstract approach is applied, where students first describe what they literally see (concrete), then interpret its meaning (pictorial), and finally evaluate its overall effectiveness (abstract).

## Grammar and Vocabulary in Context
The curriculum expands students' grammatical knowledge, focusing on complex sentence structures including conditional clauses, passive voice, and reported speech. Emphasis is placed on accurate subject-verb agreement and the correct use of punctuation, particularly with clauses. Vocabulary acquisition is contextual, learning words through themes like Singaporean culture (e.g., 'merdeka', 'kampong'). Students are taught to use a thesaurus to find more sophisticated synonyms, moving from 'happy' to 'elated' or 'contented' to add precision and flair to their writing.

## Summary Writing
Students are taught to distil information from a given text into a concise summary, capturing only the main points and supporting arguments while omitting minor details and examples. They must adhere to a specified word limit, typically one-third of the original text's length. The process involves identifying the central theme, topic sentences, and logical connectors. A summary of a passage about HDB estate planning must retain the core objectives of the policy, such as providing affordable housing for Singapore citizens, without extraneous details about architectural styles.

## Situational Writing
Students practice composing formal and semi-formal texts for specific real-world contexts, such as writing an email to request feedback on a school project or composing a letter to the town council about a community issue. Each piece has a clear purpose and target audience. Key components include a proper salutation and closing, a well-structured body using paragraphs, and a polite and appropriate tone. For example, a complaint about a broken lift in an HDB block must be factual, respectful, and clearly state the desired action.

## Oral Communication
Students learn to deliver structured and engaging oral presentations, such as a class debate or a report on a social issue. They focus on using clear articulation, appropriate volume, and varying vocal pace for emphasis. The use of non-verbal cues, like eye contact and gestures, is also taught. A topic like the benefits of Singapore's food culture could be presented, requiring the student to organise key points logically, use persuasive language, and field questions from the audience confidently and thoughtfully.
