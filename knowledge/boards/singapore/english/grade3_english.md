# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 3 English


## Grammar: Sentence Structures and Tenses
Students learn to construct grammatically correct sentences with simple and compound subjects. They are introduced to the present continuous tense (e.g., "The boy is playing at the void deck.") to describe ongoing actions. Regular past tense verbs are also taught, using contexts like "My family ate chicken rice yesterday." The curriculum reinforces the correct use of subject-verb agreement, ensuring sentences like "She runs in the park" are accurately formed. Exercises often use local scenarios such as children flying kites at East Coast Park.

## Vocabulary Development: Thematic Word Lists
Pupils expand their vocabulary through themed units, including words related to local transport like "MRT," "bus interchange," and "ERP." Hawker centre food terms such as "chicken rice," "roti prata," and "kaya toast" are introduced to build contextual understanding. Synonyms and antonyms are explored using Singaporean examples, like comparing "HDB block" and "condominium." Weekly spelling tests focus on these thematic words, helping students accurately describe their daily experiences in a multilingual society.

## Reading Comprehension: Local Texts
Students read short stories and non-fiction texts set in Singapore, such as accounts of visiting the Singapore Zoo or celebrating Hari Raya. They learn to identify the main idea and supporting details in passages. Using the "before, during, after" reading strategy, pupils predict content from titles and illustrations. Comprehension questions often require students to infer character feelings from phrases like "Ah Lok felt excited when he saw the fireworks." Texts incorporate Singlish terms like "shio" (delicious) to reflect local language use.

## Writing: Descriptive and Narrative Paragraphs
Grade 3 students practice writing descriptive paragraphs about local landmarks, using sensory details like "The Merlion statue spouts water into the river." For narratives, they craft short stories with a clear beginning, middle, and end, often drawing from personal experiences like a trip to Sentosa. The "show, don't tell" technique is emphasized, encouraging phrases like "My palms were sweaty as I climbed the stairs" instead of "I was scared." Graphic organisers help structure ideas before drafting.

## Listening and Speaking: Oral Presentations
Pupils develop oral skills by presenting short talks on familiar topics like their favourite hawker stall or a family trip to Gardens by the Bay. They practice using appropriate volume, pace, and eye contact. Role-play activities simulate real-life situations, such as ordering food in a food court or explaining directions to the nearest MRT station. The "traffic light" system (green, amber, red cards) is used to provide peer feedback on clarity and expression, fostering confident communication.

## Media Literacy: Interpreting Visual Texts
Students learn to systematically decode visual texts by applying a framework to analyse advertisements for local products or events like the National Day Parade. This includes identifying explicit persuasive techniques, such as the use of bold national symbols, patriotic colour schemes, and memorable slogans designed to evoke specific emotions and appeal to a target audience. For picture-based comprehension, students describe scenes from local storybooks and comics by extracting factual details to articulate the characters' emotions, the setting, and the sequence of events. They develop foundational digital literacy skills by comparing and contrasting information from online sources and print materials about Singaporean culture, learning to distinguish between factual statements and opinions to evaluate source reliability.
