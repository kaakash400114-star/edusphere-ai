# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 11 English

## Rhetorical Analysis of Non-fiction Texts
Students learn to identify and analyse rhetorical strategies used in Singaporean non-fiction texts. They examine how writers use ethos, pathos, and logos to construct persuasive arguments. The syllabus requires analysing opinion pieces from The Straits Times, National Day Rally speeches, and ministerial statements. Students learn to evaluate the effectiveness of these strategies in achieving the writer's purpose and influencing the target audience. They are expected to use specific textual evidence to support their analysis and discuss the impact of Singapore's multicultural and historical context on rhetorical choices.

## Critical Reading and Appreciation of Poetry
Students engage with complex poems that explore Singaporean identity, culture, and social issues. They learn to analyse poetic devices such as imagery, metaphor, and symbolism in the context of local experiences. Key poets include Alfian Sa'at and Charmaine Lye, whose works often reflect on life in HDB estates or the cultural tapestry of the nation. The curriculum requires students to explore how poets use language to evoke emotional responses and comment on societal values. They must evaluate the poet's craft and connect themes to the Singaporean narrative, demonstrating a deep understanding of poetic form and content.

## Academic Writing and Argumentation
Students are taught to construct sophisticated academic arguments relevant to Singaporean contexts. They learn to formulate clear thesis statements and develop them with logical reasoning and relevant evidence. This includes structuring essays for examinations, using the PEEL (Point, Evidence, Explanation, Link) method. Students analyse model answers discussing topics like the role of Singapore's education system or challenges facing a multilingual society. They learn to use appropriate academic language and transition words, ensuring their writing is cohesive, persuasive, and meets the rigorous standards of the GCE 'A' Level examinations.

## Listening and Viewing Comprehension
Students develop skills in analysing spoken and visual media from Singapore. They listen to extracts from Parliament debates, forums on social issues like the MRT system, and panel discussions on Singapore's future. The curriculum requires identifying speakers' main arguments, distinguishing fact from opinion, and evaluating the effectiveness of persuasive language. For viewing tasks, students analyse local advertisements, documentaries on Singaporean life, or news reports. They must infer speakers' intentions, recognise tone and bias, and articulate how multimedia elements enhance the message, demonstrating critical and analytical skills.

## Research and Writing for Purpose
Students undertake independent research projects on Singaporean topics, such as sustainable living in HDB estates or the evolution of hawker culture. They learn to formulate research questions, gather credible sources, and synthesise information to write reports or proposals. The syllabus emphasises proper citation and avoiding plagiarism. Students learn to structure their work clearly, present data effectively, and write in a formal, objective tone suitable for academic or professional contexts, preparing them for university-level research and written communication requirements.

## Understanding and Using Singaporean English
Students explore the features of Singapore Colloquial English (Singlish) and its relationship with Standard English. The curriculum teaches them to code-switch appropriately for different contexts, recognising the grammatical and lexical features of Singlish, such as particles like 'lah' and sentence-final particles. Students analyse how Singlish functions as a marker of local identity and analyse its portrayal in local media. The goal is for students to appreciate the linguistic diversity of Singapore while mastering Standard English for formal academic and professional communication, ensuring they meet the syllabus's bilingual proficiency requirements.
