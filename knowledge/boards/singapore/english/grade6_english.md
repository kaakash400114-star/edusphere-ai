# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 6 English
## Reading Comprehension and Vocabulary
Students develop the ability to infer an author's unstated assumptions and evaluate the effectiveness of an argument's evidence. They learn to identify a writer’s tone and purpose, and to distinguish between fact and opinion. Vocabulary instruction focuses on understanding and using multi-word units, such as phrasal verbs (e.g., 'look up to') and subject-specific terminology. Students also expand their repertoire of adjectives to create vivid imagery. They are expected to read and comprehend longer, more complex texts with increasing independence.

## Grammar and Language Conventions
In this grade, students consolidate their knowledge of grammatical rules for formal writing. They learn to correctly use complex sentence structures, including conditional clauses (e.g., 'If it rains, the event will be postponed'). The curriculum places strong emphasis on mastering subject-verb agreement with collective nouns and compound subjects. Students also learn to identify and correct common errors in punctuation, such as the appropriate use of commas in complex sentences and the correct placement of apostrophes in possessive and plural nouns.

## Writing for Purpose and Audience
Students are taught to plan, draft, and edit a variety of functional and continuous writing tasks. They learn to write formal emails with a clear purpose and appropriate tone, such as applying for a library card. For continuous writing, they are guided to create structured narratives or expository texts with well-developed plotlines and descriptive details. A key focus is on using the model method to plan compositions by breaking down a story or idea into sequential steps or cause-and-effect relationships before writing.

## Listening and Speaking Skills
The curriculum develops students' ability to listen for key information and specific details in various contexts, such as a public announcement on the MRT. Students are taught to differentiate between facts and opinions in spoken texts. They learn to present oral reports clearly and coherently, using appropriate verbal and non-verbal communication to engage the audience. Skills include structuring a talk with an introduction, main points, and conclusion, and using visual aids like bar graphs to support their presentation on topics such as favourite hawker centre dishes.

## Literature Appreciation
Students analyse how authors use language, structure, and characterisation to achieve their intended effects. They learn to describe a character's traits using evidence from the text and to explore the motivations behind a character's actions. The curriculum introduces students to literary devices such as foreshadowing and symbolism. Students are guided to make connections between themes in literary texts and their own experiences, relating stories to everyday Singaporean life, such as the sense of community in an HDB estate.

## Media and Text Analysis
Students learn to critically evaluate different forms of media, including print and digital advertisements. They are taught to identify the target audience, purpose, and persuasive techniques used, such as bandwagon or emotional appeals. The curriculum also involves comparing and contrasting different perspectives on a single issue presented in various texts. Students develop the skill to synthesise information from multiple sources, such as a news report on a new policy and a letter to the editor on the same topic, to form a balanced viewpoint.
