# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 5 English

## Grammar: Parts of Speech and Sentence Structure
Students consolidate their knowledge of the eight parts of speech, with a focus on correctly using modal verbs (e.g., can, should, must) and conjunctions to show cause and effect (e.g., because, so, therefore). They learn to identify and use different types of clauses—main clauses and subordinate clauses introduced by subordinating conjunctions like 'although', 'because', and 'if'. This skill helps them construct more complex and varied sentences, moving beyond simple sentences to compound and complex ones.

## Vocabulary: Word Roots, Prefixes, and Suffixes
Pupils explore how word roots, prefixes, and suffixes change a word’s meaning. For example, they learn that the prefix 'un-' changes 'happy' to 'unhappy' and the suffix '-ful' changes 'care' to 'careful'. They analyse word families and expand their vocabulary by learning new words related to familiar roots, such as 'transport', 'export', and 'portable'. This strategy is crucial for understanding and unlocking the meaning of new, challenging words in their reading.

## Reading Comprehension: Identifying Main Ideas and Supporting Details
Students develop the ability to identify the main idea, or gist, of a text. They learn to distinguish between the central message and the supporting details that explain or prove it. Using a text about the Singapore MRT, for instance, they would identify 'efficient public transport' as the main idea and details like 'trains run every five minutes' and 'the network connects most of the island' as supporting evidence. This skill is fundamental for summarising and understanding non-fiction texts.

## Writing: Organising Information in Expository Texts
Pupils learn to structure expository texts, such as reports or explanations, using a clear introduction, body paragraphs, and a conclusion. Each paragraph in the body should focus on one main idea supported by relevant facts and examples. For a report on HDB living, a student might write one paragraph on community facilities, another on the design of the flats, and a third on the history of public housing, ensuring a logical flow of information throughout the piece.

## Listening and Speaking: Presenting and Responding to Questions
Students are trained to speak confidently and present their ideas to an audience, often using visual aids like posters or slides. They learn to organise their thoughts, speak at a suitable volume and pace, and make eye contact. During question-and-answer sessions, they practice responding thoughtfully and respectfully. For example, after presenting their favourite hawker centre dish, they must be prepared to answer questions about its ingredients or its cultural significance.

## Oral, Visual, and Audio Texts: Interpreting Visual Information
Learners practise interpreting information from a variety of visual and audio sources, such as advertisements, infographics, and public service announcements. They learn to analyse the message, target audience, and persuasive techniques used. For instance, when viewing an advertisement for the National Day Parade, they would identify the symbols (like the red and white) and the language used to evoke a sense of national pride and unity, and explain how these elements work together.
