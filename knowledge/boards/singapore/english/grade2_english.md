# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 2 English

## Phonics and Word Recognition
Students learn advanced phonics patterns, including vowel digraphs (ee, oa, ie) and trigraphs (igh, ear, ure). They are taught to identify and read these sounds within words like 'feet', 'boat', and 'night', improving decoding skills. They learn to recognise high-frequency sight words instantly. A key focus is distinguishing between long and short vowel sounds, using word families such as 'make' and 'back'. This builds fluency for reading aloud passages set in familiar Singapore contexts like describing food at a hawker centre.

## Grammar and Language Structure
Students are introduced to basic sentence structures, including the subject and predicate. They learn to use simple past tense verbs ('walked', 'ate') to narrate events. Punctuation rules are reinforced, specifically the use of full stops, question marks, and exclamation points. They learn to form plurals, adding 's' or 'es' as in 'cats' and 'buses'. Correct use of common conjunctions like 'and' and 'but' to join simple clauses is also a key component.

## Vocabulary Development
Students expand their vocabulary by learning thematic word sets. This includes modes of transport such as 'MRT', 'bus', and 'taxi', and places like 'HDB block', 'park', and 'school'. They learn synonyms and antonyms to understand word relationships, for example, 'big' and 'small'. New words are introduced through stories and real-life experiences. A focus is placed on understanding descriptive words (adjectives) to make their speech and writing more vivid, such as describing the taste of 'chilli crab'.

## Comprehension Skills
Students learn to answer "Wh-" questions (Who, What, When, Where, Why) about a given text. They identify the main idea and simple supporting details in a story. The skill of sequencing events in the correct order is developed, for example, ordering steps to take on the MRT. Students also begin to make simple inferences about a character's feelings based on the text. All comprehension passages are based on Singaporean contexts, such as a story about visiting the Singapore Zoo.

## Writing and Spelling
Students learn to form upper and lower case letters correctly and legibly. They are guided to write simple, complete sentences on familiar topics. Spelling rules are practised, such as the 'magic e' rule where the final 'e' makes the vowel say its name (e.g., 'cake'). Students learn to write a short descriptive paragraph with a clear topic sentence. For instance, they might write about their favourite dish from a hawker centre, using descriptive words and correct punctuation.

## Listening and Speaking
Students practise active listening to understand and follow simple, two-step instructions. They are encouraged to speak clearly and audibly in full sentences. A key activity is participating in show-and-tell sessions, describing an object or experience using details. Students learn to express opinions in a structured way, using phrases like 'I like...' or 'I think...'. Classroom discussions often involve recounting personal experiences, like a weekend trip to Sentosa, to develop oral fluency and confidence.
