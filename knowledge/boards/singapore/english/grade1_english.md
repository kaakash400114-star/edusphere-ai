# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 1 English
## Phonemic Awareness and Sounds
Students learn to identify and produce the 42 main phonemes of English. They practise blending phonemes to read simple CVC (consonant-vowel-consonant) words like 'cat', 'pin', and 'mop'. Segmenting skills are developed by breaking down spoken words into individual sounds, for example, hearing /c/ /a/ /t/ in 'cat'. These foundational skills are reinforced through playful activities using picture cards and sound lotteries, focusing on clarity in articulation to support accurate reading and spelling.

## High-Frequency Words Recognition
Students memorise and recognise high-frequency sight words that do not follow regular phonetic patterns. These words, such as 'the', 'said', 'was', and 'have', are practised through daily flashcard drills and are a key focus in guided reading sessions. Children learn to recognise these words instantly to build reading fluency and confidence, enabling them to tackle simple storybooks independently. A weekly word wall in the classroom displays these key words for constant visual reinforcement and reference.

## Guided Reading and Comprehension
Students engage in guided reading sessions in small groups using decodable readers. They learn basic comprehension strategies like identifying the 'who' and 'what' in a simple story. Teachers ask literal questions about the text, for example, 'Who is the main character?' or 'Where did the story happen?'. Children are introduced to retelling a story in sequence using picture cues, developing their ability to understand and recall information from texts, which is essential for their language development journey.

## Writing and Handwriting
Students are taught to form lower-case and capital letters correctly, following a standard sequence that aids muscle memory. They practise letter formation using sand trays, playdough, and writing lines, ensuring correct starting points and stroke directions. Simple sentence construction is introduced, starting with a capital letter, ending with a full stop, and leaving a finger space between words. They may write a simple sentence like, 'I see a cat.', connecting spoken words to written text.

## Oral Communication and Listening Skills
Students develop oral language through structured Show-and-Tell sessions where they describe an object using simple sentences, for instance, 'This is my toy car. It is red.'. They learn to listen actively to peers and teachers, following simple two-step instructions, such as 'Please pick up the book and put it on the table.'. Role-play scenarios, like ordering at a hawker centre, are used to encourage speaking in full sentences and building confidence in verbal expression.

## Vocabulary and Language in Context
Students build their vocabulary by learning theme-based words, such as 'HDB', 'MRT', and 'market', connected to their daily life in Singapore. They are taught to use these words in simple sentences. The teacher introduces comparative language like 'bigger' and 'smaller' using concrete objects. Word families, for example, the '-at' family (cat, hat, mat), are explored to help children understand patterns in words and expand their language skills through context-rich activities.
