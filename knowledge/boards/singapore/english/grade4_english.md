# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 4 English

## Grammar Fundamentals
Pupils consolidate knowledge of parts of speech, focusing on pronouns, adverbs, and prepositions. They learn to use different pronoun types (personal, possessive, reflexive) correctly within sentences, such as "The children rode their bicycles to the park." Adverbs are taught to describe verbs, like "He quickly finished his kway teow." For prepositions, students identify words showing location or time (e.g., "under the HDB block," "during the school holiday") to add detail and clarify relationships between ideas in their writing.

## Vocabulary Building
This section focuses on expanding vocabulary through thematic units and contextual learning. Pupils learn words related to Singaporean life, such as 'hawker centre', 'MRT station', 'chilly crab', and 'national day parade'. They also study antonyms and synonyms to enhance word choice, for example, 'bright' (synonym: 'luminous') and 'noisy' (antonym: 'quiet'). Students practice these new words in sentences to describe local experiences and use a dictionary to check word meanings and discover related terms.

## Comprehension Skills
Pupils develop the ability to understand and interpret written texts. They learn to identify the main idea and supporting details in a passage, for instance, finding that the central theme of a story about visiting the Singapore Zoo is 'a fun family outing'. Students practise answering literal and inferential questions, such as "What time did the family leave home?" and "Why was the child excited?" using information directly stated or implied by the text to demonstrate a deeper understanding of the content.

## Composition Writing
Students are taught to structure coherent and creative compositions. They learn to write a well-organised paragraph with a clear topic sentence, supporting details, and a concluding sentence. For example, a paragraph describing a favourite food at a hawker centre would start with "My favourite food is chicken rice," followed by details about its taste and smell, and end with a summary statement. Pupils also focus on using descriptive language to make their writing more vivid and engaging for the reader.

## Oral Communication
This section equips pupils with the skills for effective speaking and listening. Students learn to express their thoughts clearly and confidently during class discussions and presentations, such as sharing their weekend experience at the Gardens by the Bay. They are taught to use appropriate volume, eye contact, and body language. Pupils also practise listening actively to understand others' points of view and respond appropriately, demonstrating respect and effective communication in group settings.

## Word Problems in Context
While primarily a maths skill, this English module integrates language comprehension for real-world problem-solving. Pupils read simple word problems involving familiar Singaporean scenarios. For example: "Ah Beng buys 3 packets of 'kaya toast' from a hawker stall. Each packet costs $2. How much does he spend in total?" Students identify key numbers (3, $2), the operation (multiplication), and write a complete sentence answer to solve the problem, connecting numerical operations with everyday language.
