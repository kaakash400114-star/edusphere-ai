# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 10 Mathematics
## Indices and Standard Form
Students extend their understanding of indices, learning to apply the laws of indices for integral and fractional exponents. They master the manipulation of numbers in index form, such as expressing roots as fractional powers and simplifying complex expressions. The standard form (scientific notation) is used to represent very large or small numbers, crucial for scientific and engineering contexts. For example, Singapore's land area of 728 km² is expressed as 7.28 × 10² km². Proficiency in this area is essential for handling data from sources like Singapore's population censuses.

## Algebraic Manipulation and Formulae
This topic deepens algebraic skills, focusing on factorising quadratic expressions and solving quadratic equations using various methods like factorisation, completing the square, and the quadratic formula. Students learn to manipulate algebraic fractions and derive formulae from given expressions. The application of algebra is demonstrated through solving real-world problems, such as calculating the time taken for an MRT train to travel between stations using the formula `time = distance/speed`. These skills are foundational for advanced mathematics and physics.

## Graphs of Functions and Graphs in Practical Situations
Students explore the graphical representation of linear, quadratic, exponential, and logarithmic functions. They learn to interpret key features like gradients, intercepts, and turning points. The application of graphs is extended to practical situations, such as analysing the depreciation value of a new HDB flat over time or modelling the number of visitors to a hawker centre. Using a graphing tool or graph paper, students draw and interpret graphs to solve problems involving rates of change and optimisation.

## Trigonometry
This module introduces the three primary trigonometric ratios—sine, cosine, and tangent—applied to right-angled triangles. Students extend their knowledge to non-right-angled triangles using the sine rule and cosine rule for finding unknown sides and angles. The topic also covers the area of a triangle formula. Practical applications include calculating the height of buildings in Singapore's skyline or determining the angle of elevation for the Singapore Flyer, demonstrating the relevance of trigonometry in surveying and architecture.

## Mensuration
Students delve into the calculation of perimeter, area, surface area, and volume for various 2D and 3D shapes, including circles, cones, and spheres. They learn to derive and apply formulae for complex composite solids. This knowledge is applied to solve real-world problems, such as calculating the volume of concrete required for a new HDB block or determining the surface area of a water tank in a reservoir. Mastery of mensuration is vital for fields like civil engineering and construction.

## Probability and Statistics
This section covers the principles of probability, including mutually exclusive and independent events, and the use of tree diagrams and tables. In statistics, students learn to represent and interpret data using various charts, including box-and-whisker plots. They calculate and interpret measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) and spread (range, interquartile range). For example, students might analyse the probability of choosing a specific dish from a hawker centre stall or interpret survey data on favourite local foods.
