# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 9 Mathematics

## Algebraic Manipulation and Formulae
Students learn to expand and factorise algebraic expressions, including perfect squares and differences of squares. They handle quadratic expressions using identities like (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b². Students solve quadratic equations by factorisation and the quadratic formula, x = [-b ± √(b² - 4ac)] / 2a. They manipulate formulae to find a specified letter, such as changing v = u + at to a = (v - u) / t. These skills are applied to real-world contexts like calculating stopping distances for trains along the MRT line.

## Coordinate Geometry and Graphs
Students explore the Cartesian plane, finding gradients and y-intercepts from linear equations in the form y = mx + c. They learn to find the equation of a straight line given two points or a point and the gradient. The concept of parallel and perpendicular lines is covered, with their respective gradient relationships. For example, if one MRT line has a gradient of 2, a perpendicular bus route would have a gradient of -1/2. Students also sketch the graphs of quadratic functions, identifying turning points and y-intercepts.

## Trigonometry and Its Applications
Students extend their trigonometric knowledge to angles of any size, learning about the three primary ratios (sine, cosine, tangent) in the four quadrants. They apply the Sine Rule (a / sin A = b / sin B) and Cosine Rule (c² = a² + b² - 2ab cos C) to solve non-right-angled triangles. These methods are used in practical problems like determining the height of a HDB block from a given angle and distance. Students also use the area formula (1/2 ab sin C) to find the area of triangular land plots.

## Mensuration and Properties of Circles
This module covers the area and circumference of circles, sectors, and arcs, using π (pi). Students calculate the surface area and volume of three-dimensional solids, including pyramids, cones, and spheres. For instance, a student might calculate the volume of a round fishball soup bowl to ensure it fits a certain capacity. The properties of circles, such as tangents and the relationship between angles at the centre and the circumference, are used to solve complex geometric problems involving hawker centre stall layouts.

## Data Analysis and Probability
Students learn to present and interpret data using cumulative frequency curves and histograms. They calculate mean, median, mode, and standard deviation to analyse datasets, such as the prices of different dishes at a food court. The concepts of probability are extended, including understanding mutually exclusive and independent events. Students use tree diagrams to solve multi-stage probability problems, such as calculating the chance of a specific MRT train being delayed during peak hours. Basic set notation is used to describe sample spaces.

## Vectors in Two Dimensions
Students are introduced to column vectors and scalar multiplication, understanding vector notation such as (a / b). They learn to add and subtract vectors, including the geometric representation of resultant vectors. The concept of a position vector is covered, and students use the formula for the magnitude of a vector: √(a² + b²). These skills are applied to solve problems involving direction and distance, like plotting a delivery route for a catering service between multiple HDB blocks and food centres.
