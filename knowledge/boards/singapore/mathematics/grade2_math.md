# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 2 Mathematics
## Numbers to 1000
Pupils read, write, and compare numbers up to 1000. They identify place values (hundreds, tens, ones) and complete number patterns. For example, 725 has 7 hundreds, 2 tens, and 5 ones. Pupils use terms like 'greater than' (>), 'less than' (<), and 'equal to' (=). They compare numbers like 458 and 482, understanding that 458 is smaller. Real-world examples include counting MRT station passengers or HDB unit numbers, building a strong foundation for later arithmetic problem-solving.
## Addition and Subtraction
Pupils add and subtract numbers within 1000, focusing on mental and written methods. They learn to regroup (carry over) for addition and decompose (borrow) for subtraction. For instance, 345 + 278 involves adding ones, then tens, then hundreds. A practical example is calculating the total cost of hawker food items, such as $5 for chicken rice and $3 for Teh C Peng, to find a total of $8. These skills are vital for managing daily finances and solving word problems.
## Multiplication and Division
Pupils learn multiplication as repeated addition and division as sharing or grouping. They recall multiplication tables of 2, 3, 4, and 5. For example, 4 x 5 means adding 4 five times (4+4+4+4+4) to get 20. Division is shown as sharing 20 sweets among 5 friends, with each friend getting 4. Using Singaporean examples, pupils might solve how many plates of Satay they can share equally among 4 people, reinforcing the link to real-life situations.
## Length, Mass, and Volume
Pupils measure and compare length, mass, and volume using standard units like metres, centimetres, kilograms, and litres. They use rulers and balances for accurate measurement. For instance, a pencil might be 15 cm long, and a bag of rice might be 2 kg heavy. Real-world contexts include estimating the length of a school bus or the amount of water needed for a pot of Laksa, developing estimation skills and practical measurement understanding for everyday tasks.
## 2D and 3D Shapes
Pupils identify and name 2D shapes (circle, square, rectangle, triangle) and 3D shapes (cube, cuboid, sphere, cone). They explore properties like the number of sides and corners. For example, a rectangle has 4 sides and 4 right angles. Using Singaporean examples, pupils might recognise the shape of a Kaya toast square or the round shape of a MRT tunnel entrance, connecting geometry to familiar local objects and environments.
## Data Representation
Pupils collect, organise, and interpret data using pictographs and bar graphs. They represent information using symbols or pictures where one picture represents a specific number of items. For example, a pictograph showing favourite hawker centres might use one picture of a Laksa bowl to represent 3 pupils. Pupils analyse the data to answer questions like, "Which food is the most popular?" This develops analytical skills for understanding and presenting information from their surroundings.
