# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 7 Mathematics
## Algebraic Expressions and Formulae
Students learn to use letters to represent numbers and form algebraic expressions. They simplify expressions by expanding using the distributive law (e.g., 3(2x + 5) = 6x + 15) and factorising by identifying common factors (e.g., 4x - 8 = 4(x - 2)). Substitution is key: for a formula like h = 5t + 2, finding h when t = 3 involves calculating 5(3) + 2. This foundational skill is used in calculating costs or distances, such as finding the total fare on an MRT journey where the fare is an algebraic expression based on distance travelled.
## Ratio and Proportion
Students deepen their understanding of ratios by writing them in simplest form (e.g., 15:20 becomes 3:4) and finding the missing term in a proportion. They solve problems involving ratios of three quantities, such as splitting the cost of a meal at a hawker centre among friends in a 2:3:5 ratio. The unitary method is used to find the value of one part before determining the total. This is also applied to map scales, where a 1:5000 scale map is used to find the actual distance between an HDB block and a nearby MRT station.
## Percentage
Students handle percentages as fractions and decimals, converting them (e.g., 65% = 65/100 = 0.65). They find percentages of quantities to solve problems like calculating the discount on a new phone at an electronics store. A key focus is percentage increase and decrease, using the formula new value = original value × (1 ± p/100). This is crucial for understanding price changes, such as the 7% GST applied to goods and services in Singapore, allowing students to compute the final price of a purchase.
## Rate and Speed
The concept of rate is formalised as a quantity with a unit per another unit (e.g., km/h or $/kg). Students solve problems involving speed, distance, and time, using the formula distance = speed × time. A common example is calculating the duration of a train trip from Orchard to Jurong East on the MRT line given the average speed and distance. They also explore average speed for journeys with different speeds, applying this to planning travel times across Singapore or understanding fuel consumption rates for vehicles.
## Geometry
Students formally encounter geometrical properties and deductive reasoning. They learn the sum of interior angles of a polygon formula, S = 180(n - 2), to calculate unknown angles. The properties of parallel lines and transversals are used, such as finding corresponding angles. They also study the properties of triangles, including the Pythagorean theorem for right-angled triangles, which can be used to find the shortest walking path between two diagonal points in an HDB flat's grid layout.
## Statistics and Probability
Data is presented and interpreted using dot diagrams, stem-and-leaf diagrams, and mean, mode, median. The mean is calculated from an ungrouped data set (e.g., finding the average number of MRT trips made by a class in a week). Probability is introduced as a measure of chance from 0 to 1. Students find the probability of simple events, like the likelihood of choosing a favourite type of local hawker food from a list, where each outcome is equally likely. This develops skills for data-driven decision-making.
