# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 5 Mathematics

## Numbers and Operations
Grade 5 pupils work with numbers up to 10 million, learning to read, write, and order them. They master the four operations: addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. For multiplication, they use the standard algorithm for whole numbers and decimals, ensuring correct placement of the decimal point. Division extends to dividing by a 2-digit number. They solve problems involving combined operations using the correct order of operations (BODMAS), such as calculating the total cost of multiple plates of chicken rice from a hawker centre at varying prices.

## Fractions and Decimals
Students deepen their understanding of fractions, focusing on addition and subtraction of mixed numbers with different denominators. They convert between mixed numbers and improper fractions and find the fraction of a quantity, like calculating half the number of seats in an MRT carriage. Decimal concepts include multiplication and division by 10, 100, and 1000, and expressing measurements like the height of an HDB block in decimal form. They also learn to round decimals to a specified place value for estimation.

## Geometry and Measurement
Pupils identify properties of triangles and quadrilaterals, classifying them based on their sides and angles. They calculate the area and perimeter of squares, rectangles, and triangles, applying these to find the area of a rectangular HDB flat block. They learn to find the volume of cubes and rectangular prisms using the formula length × breadth × height. Measurement conversions between metric units for length, mass, and capacity are practised, such as converting the weight of a durian from kilograms to grams.

## Ratio and Percentage
The concept of ratio is introduced, with students expressing relationships between quantities using the colon symbol or in its simplest form. They solve problems involving ratios, like dividing the number of students in a class into equal groups. Percentage is taught as a fraction of 100, where pupils convert fractions and decimals to percentages and vice versa. They apply percentages to solve real-world problems, such as calculating a 20% discount on a new school uniform.

## Data Analysis and Probability
Students read and interpret data from tables, bar graphs, and line graphs. They learn to construct bar graphs and line graphs to represent information, such as the number of visitors to a Singapore park over a week. The concept of average (mean) is introduced, where they calculate the average of a set of numbers, like finding the average daily rainfall. Simple probability activities involve using fractions to describe the likelihood of events, such as the chance of picking a specific coloured kueh from a selection.

## Word Problems and Model Method
Pupils apply the model method (bar model) to solve a variety of multi-step word problems. This involves drawing bars to represent unknown and known quantities in problems related to speed, distance, and time. They also solve problems involving fractions, ratios, and percentages using this visual strategy. For instance, a student might draw bars to find the original price of a Playstation game after a discount. The focus is on a structured, logical approach to break down complex problems into manageable parts.
