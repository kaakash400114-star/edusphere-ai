# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 12 Mathematics


## Applications of Integration
Students learn to apply integration techniques to solve problems involving areas and volumes. They calculate the area under a curve and between two curves, and determine the volume of revolution about the x- and y-axes. The fundamental theorem of calculus is used to evaluate definite integrals. For example, they might find the volume of a roti prata when a curve y = x² between x=0 and x=2 is rotated 360 degrees around the x-axis, calculating the exact volume in cubic centimetres.

## Differential Equations
Students study first-order differential equations, focusing on solving separable equations. They learn to formulate and solve differential equations to model real-world scenarios, such as population growth or cooling of teh tarik. The general and particular solutions are found using integration, with initial conditions used to determine constants. For instance, a problem might involve finding the temperature of a cooling kaya toast at time t, given an initial temperature and the ambient temperature of an air-conditioned HDB flat.

## Binomial Theorem
Students expand expressions of the form (a + b)ⁿ using the binomial theorem and Pascal's Triangle. They identify the coefficients using combinations, C(n, r), and write the general term of the expansion. The theorem is applied to simplify complex algebraic expressions and approximate values. For example, they expand (1 + 0.02)¹² to estimate the total amount in a savings account after one year with 2% monthly compound interest, a relevant calculation for financial planning in Singapore.

## Probability and Distributions
Students explore discrete and continuous probability distributions, specifically the Binomial and Normal distributions. They apply the formula for binomial probability, P(X = r) = C(n, r)pʳ(1-p)ⁿ⁻ʳ, to model scenarios like the number of MRT train breakdowns in a week. For normal distributions, they calculate probabilities using z-scores and the standard normal table, such as finding the probability of a student's PSLE T-score falling within a certain range for school streaming.

## Parametric Equations
Students master parametric equations by defining curves using two equations, x = f(t) and y = g(t), with the parameter t. To eliminate the parameter and find the Cartesian equation, they employ algebraic methods, such as substitution or using trigonometric identities, as in converting x = cos(t) and y = sin(t) into x² + y² = 1. They sketch curves by plotting points for various t values, recognising that t may represent time, as in the projectile motion of a ball modelled by x = 20t and y = 40t - 5t². For applications, they calculate the gradient using dy/dx = (dy/dt)/(dx/dt) and find the area under the curve using the integral of y(t)x'(t) dt.

## Complex Numbers
Students master operations on complex numbers in rectangular and polar forms, applying the modulus-argument formula to convert between them. They execute addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division, with division using the complex conjugate in rectangular form. De Moivre’s Theorem, (r cis θ)ⁿ = rⁿ cis nθ, is used to find powers and roots of complex numbers. For example, they can find the fourth roots of a complex number or calculate the impedance in AC circuits by representing resistance and reactance as real and imaginary parts. This also enables solving geometric problems, such as finding the new coordinates after rotating a point z = x + yi in the complex plane by an angle θ.
