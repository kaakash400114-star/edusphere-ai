# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 4 Mathematics

## Numbers to 10,000
Pupils read, write and order numbers up to 10,000. They understand the concept of place value: thousands, hundreds, tens, and ones. For example, 4,578 is 4 thousands, 5 hundreds, 7 tens, and 8 ones. Pupils use number discs and place value charts to represent numbers. They compare and arrange numbers from smallest to largest and vice versa. They round numbers to the nearest ten or hundred. They also identify the value of each digit in a 4-digit number, such as the digit '9' in 3,925 being 90.

## Addition and Subtraction of Whole Numbers
Pupils learn to add and subtract numbers up to 10,000 using the standard vertical algorithm. For example, to calculate 3,456 + 2,178, they align the numbers by place value and add from right to left, regrouping when necessary. For subtraction, such as 5,831 - 2,469, they regroup from the tens or hundreds column if the top digit is smaller. They use number bonds and the part-whole concept to check their answers. Word problems involving HDB flat renovations, like calculating the total cost of materials, are used for practice.

## Multiplication and Division of Whole Numbers
Pupils multiply a 4-digit number by a 1-digit number (e.g., 2,135 x 3) and divide a 4-digit number by a 1-digit number (e.g., 4,864 ÷ 4) using the standard algorithm. They use the multiplication table up to 10 as a foundation. They interpret 'remainder' in division problems, such as sharing 50 packets of chicken rice among 4 families. Pupils use the model method to visualise multiplication and division, drawing bars to represent quantities like the number of MRT carriages in a train.

## Fractions
Pupils compare and order fractions with related denominators. They find equivalent fractions by multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator by the same number. For example, 2/5 is equivalent to 4/10 and 6/15. They add and subtract fractions with the same denominator (e.g., 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/8). They learn to express a fraction in its simplest form, reducing 8/12 to 2/3. Real-world examples include dividing a plate of Satay for friends and finding fractions of a group of items at a hawker centre.

## Area and Perimeter
Pupils calculate the perimeter of rectilinear figures by adding the lengths of all sides. For a rectangle, they use the formula P = 2 x (length + width). They understand that the area is the space covered within the boundary. For squares and rectangles, they use the formula A = length x width. They measure area in square units (cm², m²). They solve problems involving a garden around an HDB block, calculating the perimeter of the fence and the area of the grass to be planted.

## Data Analysis
Pupils read and interpret data from tables, bar graphs, and line graphs. They learn to identify the main features: title, axes, and key. They collect data, such as the favourite types of local food from their class, and present it using a bar graph where one unit on the axis can represent more than one item, like 2 students. They solve problems by finding the total, difference, and most popular/least popular category from the data, for instance, comparing the number of people taking the North-South MRT line to the East-West line.
