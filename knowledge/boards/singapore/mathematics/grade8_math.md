# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 8 Mathematics  

## Algebraic Expressions and Formulae
Students will learn to simplify algebraic fractions involving linear and quadratic expressions. They will expand and factorise quadratic expressions, including the difference of two squares. Students will solve quadratic equations by factorisation, completing the square, and using the quadratic formula. They will manipulate algebraic formulae, including finding the subject of a formula, to solve for unknowns. Real-life contexts, such as calculating the area of a plot of HDB land, are used to demonstrate application.

## Linear Equations and Inequalities
Students will solve linear equations, including those with fractional coefficients and fractional answers, by applying the balance method to isolate the unknown variable. They will formulate and solve equations from word problems, using the bar model to visualise and represent relationships between quantities. For linear inequalities in one variable, students will apply rules for manipulating inequalities to find the solution set and represent this set graphically on a number line. Additionally, they will solve systems of simultaneous linear equations using the algebraic methods of substitution and elimination, applying these techniques to solve contextual problems such as calculating costs at a hawker centre.

## Angles, Triangles, and Polygons
Students will apply the properties of angles associated with parallel lines and transversals. They will understand and use the angle sum properties of triangles and quadrilaterals. Students will solve problems involving congruent and similar triangles using their properties and the concept of scale factors. The properties of special quadrilaterals are explored, and problems involving bearings and angles of elevation and depression are solved, often related to navigation within the MRT network.

## Mensuration
Students will apply the formulas for the area of plane figures, including circles and sectors, to solve problems involving composite shapes like finding the shaded area on an architectural blueprint. They will calculate the volume and surface area of prisms, pyramids, cones, and spheres to determine quantities such as the amount of concrete required for a pillar or the material needed to construct a national monument. The concepts include the application of π for circular measurements and the relationship between an object's volume and its capacity when filled with water. For example, they would find the volume of water in a cylindrical tank by using the formula V = πr²h.

## Data Analysis and Probability
Students will calculate the mean, median, and mode for ungrouped data sets, such as test scores or the number of library books borrowed, to determine central tendency and draw conclusions about the data. They will interpret and construct box-and-whisker plots to visualise and analyse data distribution, identifying outliers and comparing spread across different sets. For probability, students will apply the basic principles to determine the theoretical probability of a single event, such as selecting a specific type of fruit from a basket. Furthermore, they will understand the concept of relative frequency to find the experimental probability of an event, like the likelihood of a bus arriving on time based on collected arrival data.

## Coordinate Geometry and Graphs
Students will plot and interpret linear and quadratic graphs on the coordinate plane. They will find the gradient of a line and the equation of a straight line given two points. Students will use linear graphs to solve simultaneous equations graphically. Quadratic graphs are used to model real-life situations, such as the trajectory of a lift in a HDB building or the profit curve of a business at a local market.
