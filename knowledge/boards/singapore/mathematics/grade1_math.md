# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 1 Mathematics  

## Numbers to 10
Pupils learn to count, read, and write numbers from 1 to 10. They match numerals to number words and use concrete materials like counting blocks to represent quantities. They compare numbers using terms 'more than', 'less than', and 'as many as', ordering numbers from smallest to largest. For example, they might count 5 durians or 7 packets of kaya toast, linking numbers to everyday objects found in a local kopitiam.

## Number Bonds
Pupils master number bonds, focusing on pairs that make numbers up to 10. Using physical objects like linking cubes or coloured counters, they discover combinations such as 3 and 4 making 7. This skill forms the foundation for addition and subtraction. For instance, a bond of 2 and 3 to make 5 can be visualised with 2 packets of Teh C and 3 packets of Milo, making 5 drinks in total.

## Addition and Subtraction Within 10
Grade 1 pupils master addition and subtraction within 10, understanding numbers as composite wholes. Addition is introduced through the "count all" and "count on" strategies, for example, solving "3 apples and 2 apples" by counting from 1 or counting on from the larger number. Subtraction is taught using the "take away" concept, as in "8 cookies minus 2 cookies," and the complementary "part-whole" relationship of number bonds, where knowing 3 and 2 make 5 helps solve 5 minus 2. Concrete aids such as ten frames and physical objects are essential for visualising these operations. Pupils develop foundational fluency by relating these concepts to real-world problems, such as finding the total of two groups or the remaining quantity after some are removed.

## Length
Pupils learn to compare and measure the length of objects using consistent non-standard units such as paper clips or straws. The core method is direct comparison, where objects are placed side-by-side to determine which is longer or shorter, or indirect comparison by lining them up end-to-end. Length is calculated by counting the number of uniform units needed to cover an object completely, for instance, measuring a schoolbag’s length using 15 paper clips. Key vocabulary includes terms like "longer than," "shorter than," and "the same length as," applied to real-world comparisons like an HDB block being taller than a playground slide. Emphasis is placed on using uniform units to ensure accurate and reliable measurements.

## Picture Graphs
Pupils learn to interpret and construct simple picture graphs using one-to-one correspondence, where each picture or symbol represents a single data item, such as a favourite hawker food dish. They collect data by tallying responses from classmates, recording counts for items like roti prata or chicken rice, and arranging this information systematically into rows and columns. When creating the graph, pupils ensure each category is clearly labelled and the pictograms are evenly spaced for accurate comparison. They use comparative language like "most," "fewest," and "same number of" to describe the data's findings, for example, identifying that more students prefer laksa than mee siam. This process develops foundational skills in data representation and analysis.

## Shapes and Patterns
Pupils identify and name 2D shapes, including circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles, and 3D shapes like cubes, spheres, and cylinders. They learn to differentiate between 2D and 3D objects, for example, distinguishing a flat square on a chessboard from a solid cube building block. Pupils extend their knowledge by creating and extending patterns based on attributes such as shape, colour, or size, like arranging a sequence of circle, square, circle, square. They also explore patterns in orientation, such as turning a shape to follow a rotation pattern. These skills are applied by recognising the rectangular shape of a Singapore MRT ticket or the circular top of a teh tarik cup, developing spatial awareness through familiar local contexts.
