# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 1 Mathematics  
## Numbers to 10  
Pupils learn to count, read, and write numbers from 1 to 10. They match numerals to number words and use concrete materials like counting blocks to represent quantities. They compare numbers using terms 'more than', 'less than', and 'as many as', ordering numbers from smallest to largest. For example, they might count 5 durians or 7 packets of kaya toast, linking numbers to everyday objects found in a local kopitiam.  

## Number Bonds  
Pupils master number bonds, focusing on pairs that make numbers up to 10. Using physical objects like linking cubes or coloured counters, they discover combinations such as 3 and 4 making 7. This skill forms the foundation for addition and subtraction. For instance, a bond of 2 and 3 to make 5 can be visualised with 2 packets of Teh C and 3 packets of Milo, making 5 drinks in total.  

## Addition and Subtraction Within 10  
Pupils learn addition and subtraction using numbers within 10. They solve problems with concrete aids like number bonds or ten frames. For example, "I have 4 satay sticks. My friend gives me 3 more. How many do I have?" They use "count all" or "count on" strategies. Subtraction involves "take away" (e.g., 7 HDB flats minus 2 flats leaves 5) or "part-whole" concepts.  

## Length  
Pupils compare and measure length using non-standard units (e.g., paper clips, straws). They compare objects directly ("The MRT train is longer than the bus") or indirectly by lining them up. Activities include measuring the length of a schoolbag or a table using uniform units. Vocabulary includes "longer," "shorter," and "same as," with real-world examples like comparing the height of an HDB block to a playground slide.  

## Picture Graphs  
Pupils interpret and create simple picture graphs using data about their class or school. They count and tally items (e.g., favourite hawker food dishes like laksa or chicken rice) and represent them using one-to-one correspondence. For example, a graph might show 6 students choosing roti prata and 4 choosing char kway teow. They use terms like "most" and "fewest" to describe the data.  

## Shapes and Patterns  
Pupils identify and name common 2D shapes (circle, square, triangle, rectangle) and 3D shapes (cube, sphere, cylinder) in their environment. They create patterns using shapes, colours, or sizes (e.g., a red block, blue block, red block). Examples include recognising the circular shape of a roti prata or the rectangular shape of an MRT ticket, reinforcing spatial awareness through local contexts.
