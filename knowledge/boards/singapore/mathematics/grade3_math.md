# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 3 Mathematics

## Numbers to 10,000
Students read, write, and compare numbers up to 10,000. They learn to identify the place values (Thousands, Hundreds, Tens, Ones) in 4-digit numbers. For example, in the number 4,562, the digit '4' is in the thousands place. Students use number lines and number bonds to compare numbers, determining if one number is greater than, less than, or equal to another. They also learn to complete number patterns by adding or subtracting a constant value, such as 1,200, 1,300, 1,400, 1,500.

## Addition and Subtraction within 10,000
Students master standard written methods for addition and subtraction of numbers up to 10,000. They learn to regroup (rename) values in both addition and subtraction. For example, in 3,456 - 1,789, one regroups a thousand into 10 hundreds before subtracting. Students solve real-world problems like calculating the change after buying kway teow for $8.50 and a drink for $2.30, starting with a $10 note. They use the bar model to represent the numbers and the difference in a visual way.

## Multiplication and Division
Students understand multiplication as repeated addition and division as sharing or grouping. They learn the multiplication tables up to 10 and use them to solve related division facts. For example, if 7 x 8 = 56, then 56 ÷ 7 = 8. Students solve problems involving equal groups, such as calculating the total number of chicken wings in 6 plates of satay, with each plate containing 5 wings. They also learn to solve division word problems like sharing 32 packets of Horlicks equally among 4 friends.

## Money
Students count, add, and subtract money in dollars and cents, converting between dollars and cents (e.g., $5.60 is 560 cents). They solve problems involving real-life purchases at a hawker centre. For example, finding the total cost of a bowl of laksa for $4.50 and a kaya toast for $1.20. Students also calculate change, such as the amount received from a $50 note after buying items costing $32.40. They use bar models to represent the total cost and the change to be found.

## Length, Mass, and Volume
Students measure length in metres (m) and centimetres (cm), mass in kilograms (kg) and grams (g), and volume in litres (l) and millilitres (ml). They learn to choose the appropriate unit for measurement, such as measuring the length of an MRT carriage in metres or the mass of a durian in kilograms. Students solve problems comparing and combining measurements, like finding the total length of three 120cm-long tables or calculating the total mass of a 2kg watermelon and four 500g bags of rambutans.

## Fractions
Students understand fractions as parts of a whole and equivalent fractions. They learn to express a fraction in its simplest form, for example, simplifying 6/12 to 1/2. Students compare fractions with the same denominator, such as 3/5 and 4/5. They solve word problems involving real-world contexts like sharing a bowl of rojak, finding what fraction of the 12 pieces of fruit are mangoes if there are 3 mangoes. Students also learn to add and subtract fractions with the same denominator.
