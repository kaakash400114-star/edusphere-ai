# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 3 Science

## Diversity of Living Things
Living things are classified into groups based on their shared characteristics. They can be sorted into plants and animals. Plants can be further divided into flowering and non-flowering plants, like the bougainvillea. Animals can be grouped by how they move or what they eat, for instance, herbivores like the Samba deer. All living things need air, water, and food to survive. Some, like the guppy fish in our reservoirs, live in water, while others, like the garden lizard, live on land.

## Cycles in Nature
Living things go through a life cycle. A butterfly starts as a caterpillar, then forms a chrysalis before emerging as an adult. Plants grow from seeds into seedlings and then mature plants. They produce new seeds, starting the cycle again. Many of our local plants, like the banana tree, show this growth pattern. Water also moves in a cycle. The sun heats water in our reservoirs, causing it to evaporate and form clouds. The clouds then bring rain, which falls back into the reservoirs.

## Materials and Their Uses
Materials have properties that make them useful. Wood is hard and strong, used for making HDB block frames. Metals, like those in the MRT train, are strong and conduct heat. Plastic is light and waterproof, used for food containers in hawker centres. We choose materials based on these properties. For example, we use a metal fork to eat because it is strong and does not break easily, unlike a plastic one.

## Forces and Their Effects
Forces are pushes or pulls. A force can make a stationary object move, like pushing a durian on the ground. It can also make a moving object stop, like pressing a brake on a bicycle. Gravity is a force that pulls everything down towards the Earth. When we drop a pen, gravity pulls it to the floor. Magnets are materials that can push or pull other objects without touching them, like a magnet picking up a paperclip.

## Light and Shadow
Light travels in straight lines. It allows us to see objects. When light hits an object, it can pass through (transparent), like a glass window, or be blocked (opaque), like a wooden door. An opaque object casts a shadow. The position of the light source changes the shape and size of the shadow. At night, the streetlights on a housing estate cast long shadows, while during the day, the sun creates shorter ones.

## Heat and Temperature
Heat is a form of energy that flows from a hotter object to a colder one. We measure temperature using a thermometer. In Singapore, the hot weather means food left outside, like a can of soft drink, will become warm quickly. A conductor of heat, like a metal spoon, allows heat to pass through it easily. An insulator, like a wooden spatula, does not let heat pass through easily. We use insulating materials in our flats to keep the rooms cooler.
