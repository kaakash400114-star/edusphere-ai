# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 4 Science

## Diversity of Living Things
Students classify organisms based on observable characteristics like shape, size, and habitat. They sort plants into flowering and non-flowering types, and animals into vertebrates and invertebrates. Vertebrates are further grouped into mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, and fish. In Singapore, examples include the long-tailed macaque (mammal) in the Bukit Timah Nature Reserve and the changeable lizard (reptile) in parks. Students use Venn diagrams to compare features, such as how the Changeable Lizard and House Crow both lay eggs but differ in skin type and body covering.

## Cycles in Nature
Students explore the life cycles of common plants and animals, focusing on complete and incomplete metamorphosis. The butterfly’s life cycle (egg, larva, pupa, adult) is a key example of complete metamorphosis, while the grasshopper's (egg, nymph, adult) is incomplete. In Singapore, students study the life cycle of the durian tree, from a seedling to a fruit-bearing plant. They also learn about the water cycle (evaporation, condensation, precipitation), using examples like rain in a HDB estate or puddles drying up on a sunny day after a storm.

## Forces and Energy
Students investigate different types of forces, such as push, pull, and friction. They learn about gravity, which pulls objects downwards towards the Earth’s surface, and magnetism, which attracts or repels magnetic materials. Using the model method, students can calculate the net force when multiple forces act on an object. For example, a 5N push and a 3N pull in the same direction result in a net force of 8N. Real-world Singaporean examples include pushing a tray at a hawker centre and feeling the friction when walking on a wet MRT platform.

## Heat and Temperature
Students understand that heat is a form of energy that flows from a hotter object to a colder one. They use thermometers to measure temperature in degrees Celsius (°C), learning that ice melts at 0°C and water boils at 100°C at sea level. They explore heat conductors, like metal, which transfer heat quickly, and insulators, like plastic, which slow down heat transfer. An example is why a stainless steel Milo spoon gets hot in a warm cup, while a plastic spoon from a kopi shop does not.

## Materials and Their Uses
Students classify materials into solids, liquids, and gases based on their properties. They learn about the states of matter and how materials can change state, like water freezing into ice or boiling into steam. In Singapore, examples include using solid concrete for HDB buildings, liquid soy sauce in our food, and the gaseous state of steam from a kettle. They also explore material properties, like why a claypot from a zi char restaurant retains heat well for cooking.

## Magnets and Their Effects
Students explore the properties of magnets, including attraction and repulsion. They learn that like poles (North-North or South-South) repel each other, while opposite poles (North-South) attract. They identify materials that are magnetic (e.g., iron, steel) and non-magnetic (e.g., wood, plastic). Using the bar method for word problems, students can calculate the number of magnetic and non-magnetic objects in a set. An example is sorting items at a hawker stall, identifying which metal utensils are magnetic and which are not.
