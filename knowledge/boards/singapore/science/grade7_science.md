# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 7 Science  
## Diversity of Living Things  
Students classify organisms using a hierarchical system: Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species. They explore biodiversity in Singapore's ecosystems, from the mangroves of Sungei Buloh to the corals of Pulau Semakau. Key examples include the Rafflesia, the world's largest flower, and the long-tailed macaque, a common primate. Adaptations, like the resilience of the goby fish in brackish waters, illustrate survival traits.  

## Cells as the Basic Unit of Life  
Students learn cells are the fundamental units of all living organisms. They identify plant and animal cell structures and their functions, such as the nucleus controlling activities and mitochondria producing energy. In Singapore, students observe cells in local flora like the orchid or common food items like the banana. They use microscopes to investigate cheek cells and onion epidermis, reinforcing the abstract concept of cellular organisation through concrete observation.  

## Forces and their Effects  
Forces, measured in Newtons (N), cause changes in an object's motion. Students apply Newton's Laws of Motion to everyday situations in Singapore, such as the deceleration of an MRT train or the force a hawker stall exerts to push a heavy cart. Friction and gravity are explored through practical activities like testing different soles for grip on a wet floor, linking abstract physics to tangible local experiences.  

## Energy Forms and Conversion  
Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted from one form to another. Students trace energy transformations in common Singaporean contexts: the chemical energy in char kway teow is converted to thermal energy during cooking, while gravitational potential energy in the Singapore Flyer changes to kinetic energy as it moves. The model method is used to calculate energy efficiency in household appliances.  

## The Air Around Us  
Air is a mixture of gases, primarily nitrogen and oxygen. Students investigate air quality in Singapore using the Pollutant Standards Index (PSI), relating it to health. Examples include the haze from regional burning and its impact on daily life. They conduct experiments like the candle in a jar to demonstrate oxygen consumption, using pictorial models to represent the invisible process of combustion.  

## Heat and Temperature  
Temperature is a measure of the degree of hotness of an object, while heat is thermal energy transferred between objects. Students learn about conductors and insulators using local examples, such as the metal of a satay stick versus the wooden handle. They explore heat transfer in HDB flats, like how convection currents cool apartments, and use the bar method to solve word problems involving specific heat capacity of water.
