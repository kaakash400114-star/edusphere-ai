# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 6 Science

## Diversity of Living Things
In this topic, students learn to classify organisms based on common characteristics using a hierarchical system. They explore the five kingdoms: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalae. For example, a durian tree is classified under Plantae, while a housefly is under Animalae. Students also understand the concept of biodiversity in Singapore, such as the wide variety of hawker centre dishes, each using unique ingredients from different plant and animal sources. This diversity is essential for ecosystem stability and resilience.

## Cycles in Matter
Students investigate the continuous movement of matter, focusing on the water cycle, nitrogen cycle, and carbon cycle. They learn how water evaporates from the Singapore Reservoir, condenses to form clouds, and precipitates as rain, which then flows back. The carbon cycle is illustrated by plants in HDB estates absorbing carbon dioxide during photosynthesis and releasing oxygen. These cycles are vital for sustaining life on Earth and demonstrate how matter is conserved and reused in natural systems.

## Forces and Energy
This topic explores the different types of forces, such as gravitational, frictional, and magnetic forces. Students understand that gravity pulls objects towards the Earth's centre, causing a dropped packet of mee goreng to fall. They learn about energy forms, including kinetic and potential energy, using examples like an MRT train moving (kinetic) and a roller coaster at its peak (potential). The principle of conservation of energy states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another.

## Electricity and Magnetism
Students learn about electric circuits, identifying components like batteries, wires, bulbs, and switches. They construct simple circuits to understand that a complete, closed path is needed for electricity to flow and light a bulb. For example, they might use a battery to power a small model of a Singaporean landmark. They also explore the relationship between electricity and magnetism, learning that an electric current can create a magnetic field, which is the principle behind electric motors in household appliances.

## Heat
This topic examines heat as a form of energy transfer. Students learn that heat flows from hotter objects to colder ones through conduction, convection, and radiation. A practical example is feeling the warmth of a hot bowl of laksa through the ceramic bowl (conduction). They understand that materials like wood are good insulators, while metals are good conductors. This knowledge is applied in designing energy-efficient HDB flats to minimise heat transfer and maintain comfortable indoor temperatures.

## Science and Technology in the Service of Man
Students explore how scientific knowledge and technology are applied to solve real-world problems. They study water purification processes in Singapore, such as NEWater, which uses advanced technology to convert wastewater into a clean resource. They also examine the role of technology in transportation, like the efficient MRT system that helps reduce traffic congestion. These applications demonstrate how science and technology improve the quality of life and support sustainable development in Singapore.
