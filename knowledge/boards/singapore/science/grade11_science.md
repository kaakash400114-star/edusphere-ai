# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 11 Science
## Cell Biology and Molecular Genetics
Students investigate the structure and function of eukaryotic cells, focusing on organelles like the nucleus, mitochondria, and endoplasmic reticulum. They master the central dogma, understanding DNA replication, transcription, and translation. The lac operon model is used to explain gene regulation. Experimental work includes extracting DNA from strawberries using lysis buffer and observing chromosome behaviour during mitosis and meiosis under a microscope, linking genetic concepts to hereditary patterns in organisms.

## Chemical Bonding and Energetics
Learners analyse different types of chemical bonds, ionic and covalent, through electron dot and Lewis structures. They apply the VSEPR theory to predict molecular geometry, such as the linear shape of carbon dioxide. In energetics, students use Hess's Law to calculate reaction enthalpies and understand the spontaneity of reactions through Gibbs free energy. Practical experiments include measuring the enthalpy change of neutralisation using a polystyrene cup calorimeter, a common method in Singaporean laboratories.

## Dynamics and Thermodynamics
This module covers Newton's laws of motion and their application to solve complex problems, including those involving connected bodies on an inclined plane, similar to calculating forces in an MRT train's acceleration. Students extend their knowledge to rotational dynamics and torque. In thermodynamics, they learn the first law, apply the ideal gas law (PV=nRT), and calculate work done in isothermal processes. Bar models are used to represent energy flow systems, such as a refrigerator's cooling cycle, to visualise energy transformations.

## Electromagnetism
Students apply Fleming's left-hand rule to determine the force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field. They use the right-hand grip rule to find magnetic field patterns around solenoids, a principle in MRT electromagnetic brakes. Calculations involve finding magnetic flux, induced electromotive force (EMF) using Faraday's Law, and applying Lenz's Law. Experiments include verifying the laws of electromagnetic induction by moving a magnet through a coil connected to a galvanometer, demonstrating principles used in power generation for Singapore's grid.

## Waves and Optics
Learners study the wave nature of light, explaining phenomena like interference and diffraction using path difference calculations for Young's double-slit experiment. They apply the lens formula (1/f = 1/u + 1/v) and magnification formula to solve problems involving converging and diverging lenses. Real-world applications include analysing the optical principles behind HDB window glare and designing corrective lenses. Polarisation is also explored, with examples related to LCD screens in common electronic devices.

## Energy and Climate Change Science
This topic explores various energy sources, including solar and nuclear power, analysing their energy density and environmental impact. Students study the greenhouse effect and climate change, using data on Singapore's carbon footprint and national strategies like the Singapore Green Plan 2030. They calculate the efficiency of energy conversion systems and discuss sustainable solutions, such as retrofitting older HDB blocks for better energy conservation, reflecting Singapore's commitment to environmental sustainability.
