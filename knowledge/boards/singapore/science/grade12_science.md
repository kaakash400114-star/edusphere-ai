# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 12 Science

## Kinematics and Dynamics
Students apply Newton's laws to analyse motion in the context of a train braking at an MRT station. They calculate the deceleration required for a train moving at 25 m/s to stop within 100 metres. The equations of motion, v = u + at and s = (u+v)t/2, are used to determine the force exerted by the brakes, assuming a train mass of 200,000 kg. Free-body diagrams are essential, showing the net force F_net = ma, where F_net is the sum of all forces acting on the train.

## Work, Energy, and Power
This topic explores energy conversion and efficiency using Singapore's iconic hawker centres. Students calculate the useful work done by an electric fan in a food court, converting 1,500 J of electrical energy. The efficiency is found using η = (Useful Energy Output / Total Energy Input) × 100%, determining it to be 80%. The power rating of the fan is derived from P = E/t, showing a 1,500 W appliance operating over 10 seconds, directly linking to real-world energy consumption and cost-saving practices.

## Thermal Physics
Students analyse the thermal properties of materials in HDB flat construction. They apply the principle of thermal expansion, calculating the change in length of a 50-metre steel reinforcement bar when its temperature increases from 30°C to 40°C, given a linear expansion coefficient of 12 × 10⁻⁶ °C⁻¹. The formula ΔL = αL₀ΔT is used. Conduction and convection are examined in the context of air-conditioning systems, which maintain the optimal 24°C for thermal comfort in high-density living environments.

## Waves
The study of waves involves analysing sound propagation from a neighbourhood kiosk in a HDB estate. Students calculate the wavelength of a 440 Hz sound wave travelling at 340 m/s using λ = v/f. The Doppler effect is applied to an ambulance siren, calculating the observed frequency change as it approaches and recedes from a stationary observer at 30 m/s. Resonance is explored in the context of designing buildings to avoid structural vibration from nearby MRT trains.

## Electricity and Magnetism
Students analyse the electrical wiring in a Singaporean HDB flat. Using Ohm's Law, V = IR, they calculate the current drawn by a 2.2 kW water heater operating at 230 V. The concept of series and parallel circuits is applied to determine the equivalent resistance of a lighting circuit. Electromagnetic induction is studied in the context of the Mass Rapid Transit (MRT) system, where the relative motion between train magnets and tracks induces currents for braking and propulsion, demonstrating Faraday's law.

## Modern Physics
This topic introduces the photoelectric effect, explaining why touchscreens in HDB void decks function. Students calculate the threshold frequency of a metal surface using the equation hf = Φ, where h is Planck's constant and Φ is the work function. Nuclear physics covers the mass defect and binding energy in nuclear reactors, relating E = mc² to energy production. The stability of isotopes is discussed in the context of radiation safety in Singapore's healthcare and industrial sectors.
