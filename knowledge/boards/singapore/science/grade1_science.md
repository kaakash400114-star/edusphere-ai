# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 1 Science
## Everyday Materials and Their Uses
Students identify and name common materials like wood, plastic, metal, and fabric. They learn to describe material properties using simple terms: hard, soft, shiny, dull. In Singapore, we use materials from our HDB homes, like metal gates and wooden furniture. Children discover why certain materials are chosen for objects: plastic for lunch boxes because it is light and waterproof. They sort objects based on their material and explain their choices, connecting learning to everyday items like their school bag or water bottle.
## Senses and Our Environment
Students explore the five senses: sight, hearing, smell, taste, and touch. They use their senses to gather information about their surroundings. For example, at a hawker centre, they see bright food, hear sizzling pans, smell delicious dishes, taste different flavours, and feel the warmth of a drink. Children learn to use descriptive words like sweet, sour, loud, quiet, and rough. This helps them understand how we experience the world and how to stay safe, like feeling a hot cup.
## Living and Non-Living Things
Students learn to sort objects into living things and non-living things. Living things grow, eat, move, and reproduce. Non-living things do not. Students observe common animals in Singapore, such as cats, birds, and fish, and plants like the bougainvillea. They learn about their basic needs, like food, water, and air. Children can visit the Singapore Zoo or a park to see living things. They understand that a toy car is non-living while the cat sleeping on the HDB block's corridor is living.
## Our Body and Health
Students learn the basic external parts of the human body: head, neck, arms, hands, legs, and feet. They identify and name the five senses and their corresponding body parts: eyes for sight, ears for hearing, nose for smell, tongue for taste, and skin for touch. Children learn about simple daily habits to stay healthy, like washing hands before eating and after playing. Using examples like drinking water to stay cool on a hot day or eating fruits for energy, they connect habits to personal well-being.
## Observing Weather and Seasons
Students observe daily weather conditions in Singapore: sunny, cloudy, rainy, or windy. They learn to describe the weather using simple words and associate it with how they feel, like feeling cool on a windy day. They learn about the four seasons experienced in Singapore, which are based on rainfall: Northeast Monsoon, Southwest Monsoon, and two shorter inter-monsoon periods. Children understand how weather affects their daily activities, such as bringing an umbrella on a rainy day to the MRT station.
## Pushes and Pulls
Students explore forces through simple actions of pushing and pulling. They identify pushes and pulls in everyday situations, like pushing a swing or pulling a drawer. Children learn that pushes and pulls can make objects move, stop, or change shape. Using a simple toy car, they can investigate how a stronger push makes it go faster. They connect this to Singaporean life, such as pulling a trolley in a supermarket or pushing a button for the MRT door.
