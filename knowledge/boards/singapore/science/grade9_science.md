# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 9 Science
## Topic: Diversity of Living Things
Students learn to classify organisms using a hierarchical system of Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, and Species. They examine the five kingdoms: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalae. They use dichotomous keys to identify local species, such as the Sunda Pangolin or the Raffles's Flower. The classification is based on shared characteristics like cell structure and mode of nutrition. Students apply this knowledge to understand biodiversity in Singapore's nature reserves, like the one in MacRitchie Reservoir Park.

## Topic: Cell Biology and Organisation
Students explore the structure and functions of plant and animal cells, identifying organelles such as the nucleus, mitochondria, and chloroplasts. They understand the cell as the basic unit of life. Students study the levels of organisation, from cells to tissues, organs, and organ systems. They relate this to the human digestive system, breaking down hawker food like chicken rice into simpler molecules for absorption into the bloodstream for energy.

## Topic: The Transport System in Humans
Students learn about the structure of the human heart, including the four chambers and associated valves. They trace the path of blood through the pulmonary and systemic circuits. Students study the composition and functions of blood, including red blood cells, white blood cells, platelets, and plasma. They connect the circulatory system to overall health, using the analogy of the MRT system delivering essential 'passengers' (oxygen and nutrients) to all 'stations' (body cells) efficiently.

## Topic: Energy and Chemical Processes in Living Things
Students understand the processes of photosynthesis and respiration. They formulate the chemical equation for photosynthesis: carbon dioxide + water -> glucose + oxygen. They learn that plants store glucose as starch. For respiration, they use the equation: glucose + oxygen -> carbon dioxide + water + energy. Students apply this knowledge to explain why our bodies need energy from food to walk to the HDB market and why plants are vital for producing the oxygen we breathe in our densely populated city.

## Topic: Thermal Physics
Students learn about the three states of matter and their molecular arrangements. They understand the concept of thermal energy as the kinetic energy of molecules. Students apply the principles of conduction, convection, and radiation to explain everyday phenomena, such as why a metal spoon in a bowl of hot Katong Laksa gets hot quickly (conduction) and why sea breezes occur near the coast in Singapore (convection), cooling the land after a hot day.

## Topic: Transfer of Thermal Energy
Students study the three modes of heat transfer: conduction, convection, and radiation. They apply this knowledge to understand the design of HDB flats for energy efficiency. For example, double-glazed windows reduce heat transfer by conduction and radiation. Students learn about heat insulators and conductors, explaining why a Thermos flask can keep soup hot for a long time, preventing heat loss through these three modes to maintain the desired temperature.
