# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 2 Science  

## Diversity of Materials
Students learn that materials can be grouped based on properties like hardness, transparency, and texture. They conduct simple tests to observe how materials respond to forces (e.g., bending, stretching) or changes (e.g., dissolving in water). For instance, they classify objects as opaque or transparent, like an HDB window (transparent) and a wooden door (opaque). They explore how different materials, such as metal utensils and plastic plates, are suited for specific uses based on their characteristics.

## Cycles in Nature
Pupils observe and describe natural cycles, focusing on the life cycle of common plants like the chilli plant, from seed to fruit. They learn about the water cycle, using pictorial models to show evaporation, condensation, and precipitation. Singapore’s weather, with daily rain showers, serves as a relatable example. They also note seasonal changes, such as heavier monsoon rains affecting plants in their school garden or neighbourhood park, connecting abstract concepts to local observations.

## Living and Non-Living Things
Students learn to distinguish living and non-living things by identifying four key characteristics: movement, growth, reproduction, and response to stimuli. Living things, such as a cat in a park or a potted plant on a balcony, demonstrate all these traits, while non-living things, like a wooden bench or a toy car, do not. They apply this framework to classify examples, understanding that while a robot can move, it is non-living because it cannot grow or reproduce. Through the Concrete-Pictorial-Abstract approach, students sort objects, like a seed (living) and a pebble (non-living), and images of a dog (living) and a bicycle (non-living). This reinforces the concept that living things have specific life processes essential for their survival.

## Pushes and Pulls
Learners explore forces by identifying pushes and pulls in daily life. They use toy cars to observe how a push makes it move and a pull stops it. Real-world examples include pushing a stroller in a hawker centre or pulling a drawer to get utensils. They draw bar models to represent the strength of forces, like comparing a gentle push (short bar) to a strong push (long bar) on a box, linking concrete actions to abstract representations.

## Our Five Senses
Pupils investigate how the five senses help us interact with the environment. They conduct experiments like identifying mystery food items by smell (e.g., durian or satay) or distinguishing sounds from different locations (e.g., an MRT train versus a school bell). They use pictorial charts to match senses to activities, like tasting food at a food court (sense of taste) or feeling the texture of a playground surface (sense of touch), grounding learning in sensory experiences.

## Care for Earth
Students learn the importance of keeping the environment clean, focusing on reducing, reusing, and recycling. They classify waste into paper, plastic, and food scraps, using examples from school lunches or household items. They discuss Singapore’s efforts, like recycling bins in HDB estates and the NEA’s anti-littering campaigns. Through simple projects like making a compost bin for plant waste, they understand how small actions help care for their community and country.
