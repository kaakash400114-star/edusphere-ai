# Singapore Curriculum (MOE) Grade 8 Science
## Cell Structure and Organisation
Students learn that all living organisms are made of cells, the basic units of life. They identify plant and animal cells and their structures, such as the cell membrane, nucleus, cytoplasm, and chloroplasts in plants. The Cell Theory is studied, stating all living things are composed of cells, cells arise from pre-existing cells, and cells are the fundamental units of structure and function. For example, a typical plant cell in a HDB potted plant has a rigid cell wall for support, unlike the flexible cell of a human cheek cell observed in lab.
## Classification of Living Things
Students explore the system of classifying all life forms into five main kingdoms: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia. They learn the hierarchical system of Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, and Species. For instance, the durian fruit is classified under Kingdom Plantae, while the mosquito that bothers you at a hawker centre is under Kingdom Animalia. Students also learn about binomial nomenclature, where organisms have a two-part scientific name, such as *Homo sapiens* for humans.
## Forces and Moments
Students investigate the effects of forces and understand that a force is a push or a pull. They learn about balanced and unbalanced forces, which affect an object's motion. The principle of moments (torque) is introduced, explaining how a force causes an object to turn about a pivot. The formula for a moment is Moment (Nm) = Force (N) × Perpendicular Distance (m). For example, the seesaw at a neighbourhood playground balances when the clockwise moment equals the anticlockwise moment.
## Heat Transfer
Students study the three methods of heat transfer: conduction, convection, and radiation. Conduction occurs in solids, like a metal spoon in a bowl of hot Katong laksa getting hot. Convection happens in liquids and gases, such as boiling water in an electric kettle. Radiation is the transfer of heat without a medium, like the warmth from the sun. They also learn about conductors and insulators; metals are good conductors, while materials like wood are good insulators.
## The Water Cycle and Weather
Students learn about the continuous movement of water on, above, and below the surface of the Earth, known as the water cycle. The key processes are evaporation (water turning into vapour from the sea), condensation (vapour forming clouds), and precipitation (rain falling). This cycle influences Singapore's weather, which is hot and humid year-round. They understand that the MRT system can be affected by heavy precipitation, leading to service disruptions due to flash floods on certain routes.
## Energy Forms and Conservation
Students identify different forms of energy, including kinetic, potential (gravitational and elastic), light, sound, heat, and chemical energy. They understand the Law of Conservation of Energy, which states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another. For example, when a roller coaster at an amusement park moves down a hill, its gravitational potential energy is converted into kinetic energy. They also learn about energy efficiency in everyday appliances, which is important in Singapore to reduce overall energy consumption.
