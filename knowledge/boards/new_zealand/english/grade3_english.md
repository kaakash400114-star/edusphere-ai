# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 3 English

## Phonics and Spelling
Students learn to decode words by understanding letter-sound relationships, including consonant blends like 'scr' and 'wh', and vowel digraphs such as 'ai' and 'oa'. They are taught to recognise common spelling patterns, like the 'ck' sound at the end of words (e.g., 'duck', 'sock'). Students also explore homophones, distinguishing between 'their', 'there', and 'they're'. They use simple strategies to check and correct their spelling, such as sounding out words and using wall charts with familiar Māori and English words, including 'kākā' and 'kite'.

## Reading Comprehension
Students develop the ability to understand and make meaning from texts. They learn to identify the main idea and supporting details in a story, such as understanding that the main idea of a text about the 'All Blacks' is their success in rugby. Students use comprehension strategies like predicting what happens next and asking questions about the story. They connect ideas within and between texts, for example, linking a character's feelings to their own experiences. Visual aids, like pictures of a 'haka', help them infer information not explicitly stated.

## Writing: Structure and Purpose
Students learn to write simple, structured texts for different purposes. They are taught to write recounts of personal events, like a school trip to see 'kiwi', using a clear beginning, middle, and end. For instructions, they use sequential language (e.g., 'First, add flour'). Students learn to write simple descriptions, using adjectives to make their writing interesting (e.g., 'The shiny piupiu swayed in the breeze'). They understand that the purpose of their writing changes depending on the audience and topic.

## Grammar and Punctuation
Students study basic grammar rules to improve their writing. They learn to identify and use nouns (people, places, things) and verbs (action words) in sentences, for example, 'The 'haka' is a powerful performance'. They are introduced to simple punctuation, including full stops, question marks, and capital letters for proper nouns like 'Aotearoa'. Students learn to use conjunctions like 'and' and 'but' to join short sentences, creating more complex sentences. They also explore how to use adjectives to describe nouns, making their writing more vivid.

## Speaking and Listening
Students develop skills for clear and confident communication. They learn to listen actively to others, maintaining eye contact and showing they are engaged. Students practise speaking in front of a small group, using a clear voice and appropriate volume. They are taught to share ideas on topics, such as their favourite part of a 'pōwhiri', with relevant details. Students learn to ask clarifying questions to better understand others. They also participate in simple role-plays and group discussions, building their ability to express opinions and listen to different viewpoints.

## Vocabulary Development
Students expand their vocabulary to improve reading, writing, and speaking. They learn and use new words from a range of contexts, including subject-specific terms like 'volcano' and 'legend'. They explore word meanings through shared stories, identifying words that describe feelings or settings. Students are introduced to common Māori words used in everyday New Zealand English, such as 'kai' for food and 'whānau' for family, deepening their understanding of New Zealand's culture. They practise using new words in their own sentences to build confidence and comprehension.
