# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 6 English  

## Understanding Text Structures  
Students learn to identify and analyse how texts are structured for different purposes. They examine narrative arcs, report formats, and persuasive structures, explaining how organisation influences meaning. For example, they compare a news report on the All Blacks' performance with a myth about the creation of Aotearoa. Students use graphic organisers to map the sequence of events in a story or the main arguments in a persuasive essay about protecting kiwi habitats, demonstrating how structural choices impact audience engagement.  

## Developing Critical Reading Skills  
Students apply critical thinking to infer authorial intent and evaluate bias in texts. They analyse language choices, such as emotive vocabulary in an advertisement for a local iwi event or statistical evidence in an article about climate change. Students compare multiple perspectives on an issue, like the use of piupiu in haka, and identify how viewpoints are shaped by cultural context. They learn to question reliability by checking sources and cross-referencing information, distinguishing between fact and opinion in complex texts.  

## Writing for Different Audiences  
Students practise tailoring writing for specific purposes and audiences. They craft formal emails to inquire about a museum exhibit on Māori carving and engaging social media posts promoting a school fundraiser. They learn to adjust tone, vocabulary, and formality, for instance, writing a detailed procedure for making a hangi versus a playful story for junior students. Students experiment with persuasive techniques, like rhetorical questions or appeals to community pride, to advocate for a cause such as preserving a native forest.  

## Exploring Language Features and Conventions  
Students deepen their understanding of grammar, punctuation, and figurative language. They study clauses, complex sentences, and subject-verb agreement, applying these rules to improve clarity in their writing. They analyse metaphor and simile in descriptions of New Zealand landscapes, e.g., "the mountains stood like weary giants." Students also expand their vocabulary with te reo Māori terms, such as *waiata* (song) or *whakapapa* (genealogy), using them accurately in context to enrich their expression.  

## Engaging with Multimodal Texts  
Students interpret and create texts that combine written, visual, and oral elements. They analyse how images, videos, and text interact in documentaries about New Zealand’s biodiversity or advertisements for the Rugby World Cup. Students design their own multimodal presentations, like a digital storyboard about the history of haka, integrating captions, sound, and layout to convey meaning. They evaluate how different modes enhance or contradict information, developing media literacy skills.  

## Using Research and Inquiry Skills  
Students conduct short research projects to gather and synthesise information. They use digital tools and primary sources, like interviews with kaumātua about traditional Māori navigation, to explore topics such as New Zealand’s unique ecosystems. Students organise findings using note-taking strategies and cite sources correctly. They present their research through reports, speeches, or visual displays, demonstrating how to structure information logically and attribute ideas, fostering academic integrity and curiosity.
