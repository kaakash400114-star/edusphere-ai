# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 5 English


## Understanding Visual Language
Students learn to analyse how images and text work together in advertisements, posters, and picture books to convey meaning. They identify techniques such as colour, composition, and symbols, and discuss how these choices influence the audience. For example, they might deconstruct a tourism poster featuring a kiwi in a lush native forest, explaining how the image of a rare bird and vibrant greenery promotes a sense of natural beauty and the need for conservation in Aotearoa.

## Structuring Written Narratives
Students will learn to structure narratives by applying the orientation, problem, and resolution framework. They will plan stories using graphic organisers to map key events and character development. Descriptive language techniques, including similes and sensory details, will be used to build vivid settings and characters. A key learning focus is writing a rising action that logically builds to a climax. For example, students will write a story about a child preparing for a school haka performance, building tension from nervous excitement to the final moment of cultural pride and success.

## Exploring Māori Myths and Legends
Students read and respond to well-known pūrākau (Māori legends) such as Māui and the Sun or Rona and the Moon. They analyse the characters, settings, and lessons within these stories, understanding their significance in Māori culture. Students learn to make connections between traditional narratives and modern life. For instance, they might discuss the themes of cleverness and perseverance embodied by Māui and relate them to personal experiences of overcoming challenges.

## Writing Information Reports
Students learn to research and write factual information reports on a chosen topic. They identify key information from a range of sources and structure their writing with a general statement and specific details, often using headings and subheadings. A common task is to create a report on a native New Zealand animal, such as the tuatara, detailing its habitat, diet, and unique features like the "third eye" on its head.

## Understanding Figurative Language
Students in Year 5 develop a systematic understanding of figurative language by analysing the construction and function of key devices. They learn to identify and differentiate between similes, which use 'like' or 'as' for direct comparison, and metaphors, which imply a comparison by stating one thing is another, such as comparing a piupiu's sound to 'the whisper of a hundred voices'. A core concept is personification, where human attributes are given to inanimate objects or ideas, like saying the wind 'danced' around the marae. Students apply these concepts by deconstructing examples in texts and using them purposefully in their own writing to enhance description, understanding that figurative language requires specific structural cues to create vivid imagery.

## Participating in Discussions
Students develop the skills to actively listen to others and share their own ideas in small and large group discussions. They learn to build on the ideas of others, ask clarifying questions, and justify their opinions with evidence. This might involve a discussion about what it means to be a New Zealander, where students share their personal experiences and connect them to broader cultural concepts like kaitiakitanga (guardianship) and manaakitanga (hospitality).
