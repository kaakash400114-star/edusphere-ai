# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 9 English

## Understanding and Using Written Texts
Students will learn to analyse and evaluate a range of written texts, including novels, short stories, and non-fiction articles. They will identify and explain the writer’s purpose, audience, and use of language and structural features. For example, they might analyse how a journalist uses emotive language to persuade readers in a newspaper article about a local environmental issue. Students will also learn to make connections between the text and their own lives and the wider world, drawing on Kiwi examples like the themes of identity in a story set in a small New Zealand town.

## Understanding and Using Visual and Oral Texts
Students will explore and interpret visual and oral texts, such as advertisements, documentaries, and speeches. They will learn to identify and explain the techniques used to convey meaning, including camera angles in a documentary about the All Blacks or rhetorical devices in a speech about Māori culture. Students will also consider how texts reflect and shape New Zealand society, analysing how a short film might represent biculturalism or how a social media campaign uses imagery to promote a local cause like protecting a native forest (ngahere).

## Creating Written Texts
Students will develop skills in creating a range of written texts for different purposes and audiences. This includes writing persuasive essays arguing a position on a local issue, such as the development of a new marae, and crafting imaginative narratives with a New Zealand setting, perhaps involving a haka performance or a journey through the bush. Students will learn to structure their writing logically, use appropriate language and features for the task, and edit their work for clarity, accuracy, and impact.

## Creating Visual and Oral Texts
Students will create their own visual and oral texts, developing their ability to communicate ideas effectively. This could involve creating a short film or podcast about a local event or designing a multimedia presentation on a topic related to New Zealand identity, such as the significance of Waitangi Day. Students will learn to use techniques appropriate to the format, such as sound in a podcast or layout in a brochure, and consider how to engage their audience, perhaps by incorporating te reo Māori or local examples.

## Grammar and Language Conventions
Students will develop a deeper understanding of grammar and language conventions to improve their accuracy and control when speaking and writing. This includes learning about complex sentence structures, punctuation (such as semicolons and colons), and the correct use of te reo Māori terms like *whānau* or *kia ora* in context. Students will study how language choices affect meaning and tone, and apply this knowledge to edit their own and others' work, ensuring their writing meets the standards for a NZC Level 5 learner.

## Research and Inquiry Skills
Students will learn to conduct independent research to find and select relevant information from a variety of sources. They will develop skills in note-taking, paraphrasing, and summarising, using New Zealand examples like finding information about a local iwi or historical event. Students will learn to evaluate the credibility and reliability of sources and to acknowledge their sources correctly using a consistent referencing style. They will also present their findings in a structured way, such as in a report or an oral presentation, demonstrating their ability to synthesise information.
