# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 2 English

## Oracy: Speaking and Listening
Students learn to speak clearly and listen actively in group discussions, sharing ideas about familiar topics like their favourite kai or weekend activities. They learn to take turns speaking, look at the person they are talking to, and respond to others' ideas. Students practice asking and answering questions, using simple sentences. They are introduced to the concept of whanaungatanga (building relationships) through respectful conversation. For example, a student might share their experience at a marae and classmates ask questions using "He aha te mea nui?" (What is the important thing?).

## Written Language: Writing
Students develop their ability to write simple, grammatically correct sentences. They learn to start sentences with a capital letter and end them with a full stop. Students use their knowledge of letter-sound relationships to spell common words and attempt to write unknown words phonetically. They are encouraged to write about their own experiences, such as describing a piupiu or a trip to the beach. Handwriting focuses on forming letters correctly and consistently. Students also learn to write simple instructions, like how to make a hangi.

## Reading Comprehension
Students read a variety of texts, including picture books, simple stories, and poems, with increasing independence. They learn to understand the main idea and identify key details, such as the main character in a story. Students are introduced to basic comprehension skills, like predicting what might happen next and making connections to their own lives. They practice reading aloud with fluency and expression. Texts often feature New Zealand contexts, like stories about kiwi or the All Blacks. Students begin to use visual cues like illustrations to help them understand the meaning.

## Visual Language: Viewing and Presenting
Students learn to interpret visual information found in pictures, diagrams, and simple charts. They discuss the purpose of different images, such as those used to advertise a local event or a haka performance. Students start to create their own visual presentations, such as drawing a picture to accompany a story they have written. They are introduced to the idea of an audience and how presentations need to be clear and interesting. This includes learning about the significance of symbols, like the koru in Māori art, and how they are used to tell a story.

## Punctuation and Grammar
Students build on their understanding of basic punctuation, including using capital letters for names, places, and the start of sentences, and full stops to end them. They learn about question marks (?) and exclamation marks (!) and when to use them in simple sentences. In grammar, they focus on identifying and using nouns (e.g., 'whare', 'moa') and verbs (e.g., 'run', 'play') in their writing. Students are introduced to pronouns like 'I', 'you', and 'he' to make their sentences less repetitive. They practice constructing simple compound sentences using 'and'.

## Vocabulary Development
Students expand their vocabulary by learning new words related to their learning and personal experiences. They are encouraged to use more descriptive language, such as words for colour, size, and feeling (e.g., 'kōwhai', 'roimata'). Students are introduced to simple te reo Māori words and phrases, like 'kia ora' and 'whakamihi', and learn to use them appropriately in context. They are taught strategies for learning new words, such as looking at pictures or asking for the meaning. This helps them build a richer vocabulary to use in their speaking and writing about Aotearoa.
