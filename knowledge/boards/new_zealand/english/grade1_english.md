# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 1 English

## Reading and Comprehension
Students learn to read high-frequency words and simple decodable texts. They identify letters, their sounds, and basic letter clusters like 'sh' and 'ch'. Students retell a familiar story using the pictures and key events, showing understanding of the sequence. They make connections between their own experiences and the stories they read, such as linking a story about sharing kai to their own lunchtime at school. The use of big books and group guided reading is common, supporting learners to build confidence with print.

## Writing and Spelling
Students write simple, coherent sentences about their personal experiences and interests, like "I saw a kiwi in the bush." They learn to form lower-case and capital letters correctly, using a pencil with good control. Simple spelling is developed through learning letter-sound relationships (phonics) and high-frequency words. A student might write about a favourite haka, trying to spell words like "ha!" and "ka!" as they hear them. Their writing is often supported by drawings and is shared with peers and kaiako (teachers).

## Oral Language and Listening
Students listen and respond to instructions, stories, and poems, showing they understand by asking questions or acting out what they heard. They share ideas and information in group discussions, using 'I think' and 'I wonder' to begin sentences. Students learn to take turns when speaking and listen to others without interrupting. They use increasingly descriptive language to talk about their experiences, such as describing the feel of a piupiu skirt or the sound of an All Blacks haka before a game.

## Punctuation and Grammar
Students learn the basic purpose of a full stop at the end of a sentence. They begin to use capital letters for the names of people (like their own) and places (like New Zealand or Aotearoa). In grammar, they identify naming parts (who or what) and telling parts (what they do or feel) in simple sentences, for example, "The taniwha (naming part) swims in the river (telling part)." This builds a foundation for constructing accurate sentences.

## Vocabulary Development
Students expand their vocabulary by learning new words from stories, poems, and topic studies. They are introduced to te reo Māori words that are relevant to their learning and daily life, such as 'kia ora' (hello) and 'whānau' (family). Students learn to describe objects, events, and feelings using more interesting words, for instance, using 'enormous' instead of 'big' when talking about a pumpkin. They are encouraged to use new words in their own speaking and writing.

## Visual Language
Students learn to identify and describe the main subject in an image, such as a picture of a silver fern. They understand that images tell a story and convey feelings, much like written words. Students explore simple picture books and posters, discussing the colours, characters, and events shown. They can make connections between the images and the text they are reading, for example, matching the picture of a haka to the words describing the powerful movement and sound of the performance.
