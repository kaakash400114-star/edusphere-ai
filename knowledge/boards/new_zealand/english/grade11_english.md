# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 11 English

## Critical Analysis of Literary and Visual Texts
Students develop analytical skills by studying a range of written and visual texts, including novels, short stories, poetry, and films. They learn to identify and analyse key elements such as characterisation, plot structure, setting, and thematic concerns. Students must explore texts within their social and historical contexts. They are taught to use specific textual evidence to support their interpretations. For example, a student might analyse the use of conflict in a novel like *The Whale Rider* or the cinematography in a film like *Once Were Warriors* to understand how meaning is constructed.

## Research Skills and Academic Writing
Students learn to conduct independent research to form and support a well-structured argument. They are taught to locate credible sources using libraries and digital databases. Key skills include synthesising information, paraphrasing effectively to avoid plagiarism, and using appropriate citation methods. They learn to construct a formal academic essay, including an introduction with a clear thesis statement, body paragraphs with topic sentences, and a conclusion that synthesises findings. Students must adhere to a specific referencing style, such as APA, to ensure academic integrity.

## Close Reading and Unseen Texts
This module focuses on the skill of close reading, where students learn to extract deep meaning from texts they have not seen before. Students are taught to analyse language, structure, and style. They learn to identify literary devices such as metaphor, simile, alliteration, and symbolism and explain their effect. The ability to infer meaning from contextual clues is essential. Students practice responding to unseen passages under timed conditions, honing their ability to craft a coherent analysis based on a first impression of the text.

## Creative Writing and Genre
Students explore and experiment with various creative writing genres, such as short fiction, drama, or poetry. They learn fundamental storytelling techniques, including narrative structure, character development, and dialogue. Specific NZC Level 6 skills involve understanding how to create a believable world and voice for the audience. Students might write a short story inspired by a local legend or a piece of poetry that reflects a uniquely Kiwi perspective. The focus is on originality and effective use of language to engage the reader.

## Oral Communication and Presentations
Students refine their oral communication skills through structured presentations and discussions. They learn to structure a speech with a clear introduction, body, and conclusion, using rhetorical devices for impact. Students are assessed on their ability to speak confidently, use appropriate body language, and manage their time effectively. They also learn to listen actively and engage in respectful debate. A key task might be presenting a researched topic on a New Zealand cultural issue, such as the significance of the haka or the role of the All Blacks in national identity.

## Understanding New Zealand Literature in Context
Students delve into literature that reflects and shapes New Zealand identity. They explore how texts engage with key themes such as biculturalism, colonial history, and social issues. Students analyse how authors use local settings, imagery, and language to create a sense of place. For example, they might examine the works of Patricia Grace or Witi Ihimaera, considering how Māori and Pākehā perspectives are represented. Students also connect historical events, like the signing of the Treaty of Waitangi, to the themes explored in these literary works.
