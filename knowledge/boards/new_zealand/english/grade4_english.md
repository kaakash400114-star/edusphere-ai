# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 4 English

## Oral Language: Whakarongo pāngarau
Students at NZC Level 3 learn to build on ideas in discussions, using relevant te reo Māori and English terms like kōrero (discussion) and whakawhiti kōrero (sharing ideas). They practice active listening by asking questions such as "He aha te take?" (What is the reason?) to clarify information. Students participate in structured group talks about topics like the All Blacks’ haka or a local kiwi’s habitat, sharing personal viewpoints and building on others’ ideas. They use visual prompts like pictures of a piupiu to describe events and experiences.

## Written Language: Pānui
Students at NZC Level 3 read a variety of texts, including stories about New Zealand culture, such as the legend of Māui, and informational texts on native birds like the kiwi. They learn to infer characters’ feelings and motivations from illustrations and text clues, using context to understand unfamiliar words. Students identify the main idea and supporting details in texts, for example, explaining how the haka is used to show strength and unity. They use strategies like skimming and scanning to find specific information in a text about the All Blacks.

## Written Language: Tuhia
Students at NZC Level 3 learn to write structured, factual texts, such as reports on a native animal like the kiwi. They use simple paragraphs to organize information with a topic sentence and supporting details. Students apply punctuation rules, including commas in lists and apostrophes for possession (e.g., "the All Blacks’ jersey"). They incorporate te reo Māori vocabulary naturally, like using "piupiu" when describing traditional clothing. Students draft, revise, and edit their work, checking for clarity and grammatical correctness.

## Visual Language: Whakaahua
Students at NZC Level 3 analyze how images and text work together to convey meaning in advertisements, storybooks, and posters. They identify techniques like perspective, colour, and composition in pictures of a haka or a New Zealand landscape. Students describe how visual elements enhance the message, for example, how the power in an All Blacks’ photo reinforces teamwork. They create their own simple multimodal texts, combining words and images to explain a topic, like the life cycle of a kiwi, using layout and design effectively.

## Grammar: Te Reo Tātai
Students at NZC Level 3 expand their knowledge of sentence structures, including compound sentences using conjunctions like "and," "but," or "because." They learn to use te reo Māori sentence starters such as "He whakaaro ēnei" (These are ideas) in their writing. Students identify and use parts of speech, including nouns (e.g., "haka," "kiwi") and verbs (e.g., "kōrero," "huri"). They practice subject-verb agreement, ensuring sentences like "The piupiu sways in the wind" are grammatically correct.

## Vocabulary: Kupu Hou
Students at NZC Level 3 develop their vocabulary by learning new words related to New Zealand contexts, such as "kaitiakitanga" (guardianship) when discussing environmental protection. They use dictionaries and online tools to find meanings and check pronunciation of te reo Māori terms. Students expand their word knowledge through reading, identifying synonyms and antonyms for words like "strength" and "weakness" in texts about the All Blacks. They practice using new words in context, for example, describing the texture of a piupiu as "rough and woven."
