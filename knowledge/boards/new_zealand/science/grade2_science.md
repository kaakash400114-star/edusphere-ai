# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 2 Science  
## Features of Living Things  
Living things have special features. Kiwi birds have soft feathers, strong legs for running, and a long beak to find insects in the soil. Like all living things, they need food, water, and air to live and grow. Children observe how a plant’s roots hold it in the soil and how its leaves reach for the sun. Through play, they learn that living things move, grow, and change over time, just like a seed growing into a rākau (tree).  

## Material Properties  
Materials have different properties. A pōhaku (stone) is hard and feels rough. A piu piu (traditional flax skirt) is flexible and dries quickly. Children explore and sort objects into groups based on what they are made from, like wood, metal, or plastic. They notice that some materials, like a kete (flax basket), are strong for carrying things, while others, like paper, are easy to tear. This helps them understand why we choose certain materials for different tasks in everyday life in Aotearoa.  

## Forces and Motion  
Pushes and pulls are forces that make things move. When a child pushes a trolley in the playground, it moves. When they pull a rope, it stretches. The All Blacks use a big push to pass the rugby ball. Children experiment with rolling a ping pong ball and a heavy ball, noticing that heavier things need a bigger force to make them move. They also learn about gravity, which makes things fall down, like when a ripe apple falls from a tree.  

## Weather Patterns  
The weather changes often in Aotearoa. Some days it’s sunny, and other days it’s rainy. Children learn to observe and describe the weather, like "Today is wera (hot)," or "There is a lot of ua (rain)". They can see rain clouds gathering and feel the wind on their faces. By keeping a simple weather chart over a week, they notice patterns, like it often being windy at the beach, which helps them understand how the weather affects what we do, like playing outside or wearing a raincoat.  

## Light and Sound  
Light helps us see, and sound helps us hear. When we stand in the sun, we make a shadow. Children can make their shadows dance and notice how they get longer in the afternoon. Sound is made when things vibrate, like when they hit a drum. The haka is a loud sound that comes from strong voices and stomping feet. They learn that quiet sounds, like a whisper, need our ears close to hear, while loud sounds, like a bell, can be heard from far away.  

## Care for Our Environment  
We need to care for Papatūānuku (Mother Earth). We can put rubbish in the bin to keep the environment clean, like after a picnic at the park. Children learn that plants, animals, and people all share the same whenua (land). They can help by recycling paper and plastic, and by not picking the flowers from a rākau (tree). This teaches them to look after their local places, such as a stream or a reserve, so that future generations can enjoy them too.
