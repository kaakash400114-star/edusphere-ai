# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 10 Science

## Electricity and Magnetism
Students apply concepts of electric circuits and electromagnetic effects. They analyse series and parallel circuits using Ohm’s Law (V = IR) to calculate voltage, current, and resistance. They investigate factors affecting resistance and power dissipation (P = IV). Students relate concepts to New Zealand’s infrastructure, such as the national grid and the Wairakei Geothermal Power Station. They build and test circuits, using ammeters and voltmeters to measure variables and validate their calculations against real-world data.

## Waves and Acoustics
This topic covers the properties of mechanical and electromagnetic waves, focusing on transverse and longitudinal waves. Students explore wave behaviour, including reflection, refraction, diffraction, and the Doppler effect. They investigate the relationship between wave speed, frequency, and wavelength (v = fλ). Using examples from Aotearoa, such as the acoustics of the Tūrangawaewae marae or the resonant frequency of a pūtorino (flute), students analyse how sound waves propagate and interact with different environments.

## Chemical Reactions
Students classify reactions as synthesis, decomposition, single replacement, or double replacement. They write and balance chemical equations, using state symbols (s, l, g, aq). They explore factors affecting reaction rates, such as concentration, temperature, and catalysts. Students use New Zealand contexts, such as the thermite reaction in metal extraction or the role of catalysts in the Haber process for producing fertiliser, to apply their understanding of energy changes and collision theory.

## Genetic Variation and Evolution
Students study genetic variation, including mutation, meiosis, and sexual reproduction. They use Punnett squares to predict inheritance patterns for traits like eye colour in kiwi or the fleece type of Romney sheep. They examine the mechanisms of evolution, focusing on natural selection and adaptation. Students analyse how New Zealand’s unique geology, like the formation of the Southern Alps, has driven speciation in species like the tuatara and various alpine plants over millions of years.

## Ecosystem Dynamics
Students explore interdependence within ecosystems, focusing on food webs and energy flow. They calculate energy transfer efficiencies between trophic levels, typically around 10%. They investigate biotic and abiotic factors, using New Zealand’s native bush as a case study. They analyse human impacts, such as introduced predators like stoats affecting the tīeke (saddleback) population, and discuss conservation strategies, including predator-free 2050, to maintain biodiversity.

## Earth's Processes and Resources
Students examine geological processes, including plate tectonics and their influence on New Zealand’s landscape. They classify rocks as igneous, sedimentary, or metamorphic, linking types to local formations like the Moeraki Boulders. They investigate renewable and non-renewable resources, such as the geothermal energy in the Taupō Volcanic Zone and the mining of ironsands on the West Coast. They analyse how these resources shape the economy and environment of Aotearoa.
