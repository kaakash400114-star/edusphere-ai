# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 11 Science  
## Wave Properties and Electromagnetic Spectrum  
Students explore the nature of waves, distinguishing between transverse and longitudinal types. Key principles include the wave equation: v = fλ, where velocity is 3.0 x 10⁸ m/s for light. New Zealand examples include analysing radio waves from Auckland’s Sky Tower and visible light used in traditional Māori star navigation (Mārama). Safety protocols for ultraviolet radiation (from the sun) are studied, linking to the NZC Level 6 focus on physical world contexts. Experiments measure wave properties using ripple tanks or laser pens, reinforcing empirical investigation.  

## Energy Transfers in Ecosystems  
This topic covers energy flow through food webs and trophic levels, emphasising the 10% energy transfer rule. New Zealand case studies include the kākāpō’s diet of rimu berries and its position as a primary consumer. Students calculate energy budgets, such as comparing photosynthesis rates in pohutukawa forests versus exotic pine plantations. The role of decomposers, like fungi breaking down fallen rātā wood, is highlighted. Concepts align with NZC Level 6, integrating biotic and abiotic factors in local ecosystems, with te reo terms like *kaitiakitanga* (guardianship) applied to conservation efforts.  

## Chemical Reactions and Equilibrium  
Students balance chemical equations and explore Le Chatelier’s principle, using industrial examples like the Haber process for ammonia. New Zealand contexts include the thermal decomposition of calcium carbonate (lime) in Oamaru’s quarries and the redox reactions in geothermal pools like those in Rotorua. Dynamic equilibrium is modelled with the formation of *kōwhai* flower pigments. Quantitative analysis involves calculating concentrations and yields, adhering to NZC Level 6 requirements for quantitative problem-solving in chemistry.  

## Evolution and Genetic Diversity  
This unit examines natural selection and genetic variation, using kiwi flightlessness as a classic example. Students apply Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium to model allele frequencies in small populations, such as the critically endangered takahē. The impact of genetic drift in island biogeography is studied, with reference to the Chatham Islands. Concepts of adaptation and speciation are linked to Māori perspectives on whakapapa (genealogy), enriching the NZC Level 6 focus on life sciences and cultural integration.  

## Mechanics and Motion  
Students analyse Newton’s laws, using the All Blacks’ scrum as a real-world example of force and equilibrium. Calculations include projectile motion of a rugby ball kicked with an initial velocity of 15 m/s at a 30° angle. Kinematic equations (e.g., v = u + at) solve problems involving acceleration, such as the stopping distance of a waka ama. Friction and air resistance are modelled using data from bungee jumps in Queenstown, aligning with NZC Level 6’s emphasis on motion in physical contexts.  

## Earth’s Resources and Sustainability  
This topic covers rock cycles and mineral extraction, focusing on New Zealand’s geology like the Taupō Volcanic Zone. Students evaluate sustainability, contrasting coal mining in Huntly with renewable geothermal energy in Wairakei. Environmental impacts are assessed through *kaitiakitanga*, using the restoration of native forests (e.g., Whanganui) as a case study. Quantitative skills include calculating carbon footprints from dairy farming, integrating NZC Level 6’s focus on Earth systems and human interaction.
