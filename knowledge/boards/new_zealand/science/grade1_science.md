# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 1 Science

## Living Things
Living things are plants and animals that grow, eat, and reproduce. In Aotearoa, we observe kōwhai trees in our school gardens. We learn about the life cycle of the pukeko, seeing them forage for food near our streams. Children care for classroom plants like the native rātā, ensuring they have sun and water. We explore how kiwi chicks hatch from eggs and need protection. This helps us understand that all living things need a suitable place to live, food, water, and air to stay alive and healthy, like our class goldfish in its tank.

## Materials and Their Properties
We explore different materials such as wood, metal, fabric, and plastic. We test their properties by feeling their textures: rough bark from a pōhutukawa tree or smooth stones from the beach. We observe if materials float or sink in water, like a waka (canoe) versus a heavy rock. In Aotearoa, we use harakeke (flax) to weave small baskets, feeling its strong, fibrous nature. We learn that materials have different uses: piupiu skirts are made from woven flax because it is strong and flexible.

## Forces and Movement
Forces are pushes and pulls that make things move. We observe forces in play by pushing toy cars and pulling toy trains. We feel the wind push us during a game of Kī-o-rahi. We learn about gravity by dropping leaves and watching them fall to the ground. When we play rugby, we see how we need to use force to pass the ball. We explore magnetism by testing which classroom items are attracted to magnets, like a steel teaspoon but not a wooden koru.

## Our Senses
Our five senses help us understand the world around us. We use sight to see the bright colours of a rainbow after rain. We use hearing to listen to the sound of a pūtātara (trumpet) or the bell before the All Blacks haka. Our sense of smell helps us detect the sweet scent of a native mānuka flower. We use taste during shared fruit time, describing the sweet taste of kūmara (sweet potato). We use touch to describe objects: a smooth pounamu (greenstone) or a spiky pinecone.

## Weather and Seasons
We observe daily weather and how it affects us. We learn to identify if the sky is sunny, cloudy, or rainy. We discuss how Matariki, the Māori New Year, marks the start of the new season when the star cluster is visible. We notice the difference in temperature between summer (hot) and winter (cold). We dress appropriately for the weather, like wearing a raincoat during a sudden downpour. This helps us understand the pattern of seasons and how weather changes.

## Light and Sound
We explore how light travels and how sounds are made. We make shadows by blocking light with our hands and see how they change shape. We use torches to make light patterns on the wall. We create sound by vibrating objects: plucking a harp string, shaking a rattle, or clapping our hands. We listen to the different sounds of nature like the tūī singing in the trees. We learn that sound can be quiet, like a whisper, or loud, like a haka performance.
