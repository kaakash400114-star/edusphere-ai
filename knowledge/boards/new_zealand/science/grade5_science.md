# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 5 Science

## Living World: Tuna and Ecosystems (NZC Level 3)
Students explore the life cycle of tuna (eels) in Aotearoa’s rivers. They learn that tuna lay eggs in the ocean, and the young swim upstream to grow in freshwater. They investigate how a river ecosystem supports tuna with food like insects and clean water. Students identify parts of a simple food chain, such as grass → caterpillar → tuna, and understand that losing one animal, like the native shag, can affect the whole system. They create diagrams to show how all parts of the river are connected, much like a haka brings people together.

## Planet Earth and Beyond: Sun, Earth, and Seasons (NZC Level 3)
Students learn that Earth orbits the Sun in a year and spins on its axis in a day. They observe that this causes day and night, just as the All Blacks’ training changes with the time of day. They investigate how Earth’s tilt creates seasons, explaining why summer has long, warm days for playing rugby and winter has short, cool days. Using models or diagrams, students show how the Sun’s angle changes the amount of light and heat reaching Aotearoa at different times of the year.

## Physical World: Light and Sight (NZC Level 3)
Students explore how light travels in straight lines to let us see. They conduct experiments with torches and objects to show how shadows form, much like a piupiu’s pattern blocks light. They learn that our eyes have different parts, like the lens, that focus light to create images. Students investigate reflective surfaces, such as water or a shiny haka cloak, and understand why we can see ourselves in a mirror but not in a wall, as light bounces off differently.

## Nature of Science: Investigating Like a Scientist (NZC Level 3)
Students practice the steps of a scientific investigation: asking a question, making a prediction, carrying out a test, and recording results. For example, they might ask, “Which material insulates a hot drink best?” They test materials like wool (from a sheep) or flax (harakeke) and measure temperature changes. They share their findings by creating a simple report, just as a navigator would share directions to a new spot, ensuring others can understand and repeat their work.

## Material World: Properties of Materials (NZC Level 3)
Students examine the properties of common materials used in Aotearoa, such as wood, stone, and Pāua shell. They test materials for strength by seeing how many books a cardboard box can hold or for flexibility by bending a flax leaf. They learn that materials are chosen for their uses—like using strong steel for a bridge or soft wool for a jersey. Students classify materials into groups based on their observable features, such as shiny, rough, or transparent.

## Processing and Using Information: Weather Patterns (NZC Level 3)
Students collect and record local weather data, like temperature, rainfall, and wind speed, over a week. They learn to use simple instruments, such as a rain gauge or a wind sock, similar to how Māori navigators read the sky and waves. They analyze the data to identify patterns, such as more rain on Tuesday and Wednesday, and discuss how weather affects daily life, like deciding to wear a raincoat or cancel a rugby game.
