# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 9 Mathematics

## Number and Algebra
Students consolidate skills with integers and fractions, applying them to solve problems involving percentages, such as calculating discount prices on rugby jerseys. They extend their understanding of exponents, including working with powers of whole numbers and simple negative exponents. Students explore the properties of real numbers and apply the order of operations (BEDMAS) to simplify and evaluate expressions. They learn to convert between fractions, decimals, and percentages, using these skills to analyse statistics from the All Blacks' win rates.

## Measurement and Geometry
Students calculate the perimeter and area of rectangles, triangles, and circles, using pi (π) in their formulas. They apply these concepts to find the area of a circular *pā harakeke* (flax garden). Students explore the volume of prisms, including cuboids and cylinders, using real-world examples like calculating the volume of a water tank. They also study properties of 2D and 3D shapes, such as symmetry and nets, and learn to apply the Pythagorean theorem to solve problems involving right-angled triangles, such as the height of a *piupiu*-decorated pou (pole).

## Algebraic Expressions and Equations
Students simplify and expand algebraic expressions, including those with brackets and multiple terms. They learn to solve linear equations and inequalities, using methods like balancing and backtracking. For example, they might solve an equation to find the cost of a haka performance costume. Students also write and solve simple problems using formulas, such as calculating the distance travelled using *d = s × t*. They explore the relationship between linear equations and graphs, identifying features like slope and intercept.

## Patterns and Relationships
Students identify, describe, and extend linear and quadratic patterns, such as the sequence of numbers in a traditional Māori chant. They represent these patterns using tables, graphs, and rules (formulae). Students learn to find the rule for a linear pattern by analysing the relationship between consecutive terms. They also explore non-linear patterns, such as the growth of a kiwi population over time. This includes calculating the rate of change and making predictions based on the identified pattern.

## Statistics and Probability
Students collect, display, and interpret data using graphs like bar graphs, histograms, and pie charts. They might analyse the results of a survey on favourite rugby teams. Students calculate and interpret measures of central tendency, including mean, median, and mode, and measures of spread, such as range. They explore probability by calculating simple probabilities of events, like the chance of drawing a specific card from a deck, and distinguishing between experimental and theoretical probability.

## Transformation and Trigonometry
Students explore geometric transformations, including translation, reflection, rotation, and enlargement, applying these to create tukutuku (weaving) patterns. They learn to describe transformations using mathematical language. In trigonometry, students apply the sine, cosine, and tangent ratios to solve right-angled triangle problems, such as calculating the height of a cliff. They use SOH CAH TOA to find unknown side lengths and angles, linking these skills to real-world contexts like surveying land for a new marae.
