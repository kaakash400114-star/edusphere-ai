# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 1 Mathematics

## Number and Algebra: Counting and Ordering
Students count forwards to at least 100 and backwards from 20. They read, write, and order numbers to 50. Students group objects in twos, fives, and tens to support counting. They find the number that is one before or after a given number. Students solve simple addition and subtraction problems using materials like pebbles or kūmara (sweet potatoes). For example, finding how many piupiu (traditional flax skirts) are left after two are given away from a group of five.

## Number and Algebra: Patterns and Relationships
Students create and continue repeating patterns with objects, shapes, and sounds. They identify the next element in a pattern like 'red, yellow, red, yellow'. Students sort objects based on one or two attributes, such as colour or size. They describe patterns using words like 'next' and 'after'. For example, continuing a clapping pattern from a heka (playful action) or arranging kōura (freshwater crayfish) by size.

## Geometry and Measurement: Length and Volume
Students compare the length, volume, and capacity of objects using direct comparison. They use words like 'longer', 'shorter', 'taller', 'more', and 'less'. Students fill containers with water or sand to compare capacity. They order objects by length or volume. For example, comparing the length of different raukura (feathered ornaments) or filling pōhā (traditional bags) with kūmara to see which holds more.

## Geometry and Measurement: Time and Shapes
Students name and describe common 2D shapes like circles, triangles, and squares. They identify 3D objects like cubes and cylinders. Students understand the concept of time, using language like 'morning', 'afternoon', and 'yesterday'. They sequence events in their day, such as eating breakfast before going to kura (school). For example, naming shapes they see on a tukutuku (wall panel) or talking about the All Blacks game being in the evening.

## Statistics: Sorting and Graphing
Students collect data about their interests, such as favourite fruits or sports. They sort objects into categories and display the results using pictographs or tally marks. Students interpret simple graphs by answering questions like 'Which has the most?'. For example, sorting pictures of native birds like kiwi or tūī into categories and making a graph showing which one children like best.

## Problem Solving: Using Mathematics
Students use counting and basic operations to solve problems in real-life contexts. They act out problems with materials or drawings. Students share their thinking and solutions with others. They explore different ways to solve a problem, like sharing kai (food) evenly among friends. For example, figuring out how many children can share a packet of pāua (abalone) shells if each child gets two.
