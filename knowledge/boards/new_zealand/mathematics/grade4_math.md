# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 4 Mathematics
## Number Knowledge and Place Value
Students develop their understanding of place value by reading, writing, and ordering numbers up to 100,000. They recognise the value of each digit in a five-digit number, such as 28,764. Students round whole numbers to the nearest ten, hundred, or thousand for easier calculation. They use materials like place value blocks to show numbers. Students also explore simple fractions, recognising that one-quarter (hautoru) of a pizza is one of four equal parts. They connect fractions to everyday situations, such as dividing a hangi meal fairly among a group.

## Addition and Subtraction Strategies
Students use efficient mental and written strategies to solve addition and subtraction problems with numbers up to 10,000. They apply the standard vertical algorithm, ensuring digits are correctly aligned by place value. For example, to solve 3,465 + 1,783, they add ones, then tens, hundreds, and finally thousands. Students use rounding to estimate answers, like rounding 2,874 to 3,000 to check their result. They solve problems involving money, such as calculating the total cost of several harakeke flax kits, ensuring correct decimal point placement for dollars and cents.

## Multiplication and Division
Students learn multiplication facts (times tables) to 10 x 10 and use them to solve related problems. They solve multiplication problems using strategies like partitioning, for example, calculating 6 x 47 as (6 x 40) + (6 x 7). For division, they share items into equal groups or use repeated subtraction. Students connect multiplication to real-world contexts, such as finding out how many rugby balls are needed if each team of 15 needs 3 balls. They solve simple division problems with remainders, like sharing 17 pieces of kūmara among 4 friends.

## Measurement: Length and Perimeter
Students measure length using metric units (millimetres, centimetres, metres, and kilometres) with rulers and tape measures. They choose appropriate units for a task, such as measuring the length of a waka in metres or a piupiu in centimetres. Students calculate the perimeter of simple shapes by measuring all sides and adding them together. For a rectangular marae ātea, they might measure the two lengths and two widths to find the total distance around. They solve practical problems, like calculating how much edging is needed for a garden bed.

## Geometry: Shapes and Symmetry
Students identify and describe the features of 2D and 3D shapes. They name common shapes like triangles, hexagons, cubes, and prisms, noting their sides, vertices, and faces. Students explore symmetry, identifying line symmetry in shapes like the koru and in objects from nature, such as a fern frond. They create symmetrical patterns using paper folding or digital tools. Students learn about tessellation, observing how shapes like hexagons fit together, much like the panels on a traditional wharenui wall.

## Statistics: Interpreting Data
Students collect, display, and interpret data using tally charts, pictographs, and bar graphs. They pose questions for investigation, such as "What is the most popular haka performed at our school?" and gather data from their peers. They analyse the graphs to answer questions, like "How many more students prefer kapa haka than rugby?" Students learn that data represents information and that graphs help them see patterns. They connect their learning to real-world events, like graphing the scores of the All Blacks over a few games to see their performance.
