# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 8 Mathematics

## Number and Algebra: Rational Numbers and Linear Equations
Students at NZC Level 4 extend their understanding of number by working with positive and negative integers, fractions, and decimals. They learn to perform the four operations with these rational numbers, applying the correct order of operations. Students solve linear equations with one variable, such as 3x + 7 = 22, and use inverse operations to find the value of the unknown. This skill is applied to problems, like calculating the cost of a kapa haka performance where the cost per performer and total cost are known.

## Measurement: Geometric Properties and Volume
In this topic, students calculate perimeters and areas of rectangles, triangles, and parallelograms using the correct formulas. They also find the volume of rectangular prisms (cuboids) using V = l × w × h. A practical application involves designing a wharenui (meeting house) model, where students calculate the volume to understand how much space it occupies, connecting mathematical concepts to Māori architecture and spatial design.

## Geometry: Angles, Symmetry, and Transformation
Students apply their knowledge of angle properties to find unknown angles in diagrams involving intersecting lines, parallel lines, and triangles. They identify and describe the line and rotational symmetry of shapes, including the koru, which is a spiral motif commonly found in Māori carvings (whakairo). Furthermore, students perform and describe reflections, translations, and rotations, applying these transformations to create tukutuku patterns, reflecting the symmetry in traditional weaving.

## Statistics: Statistical Investigations and Probability
Students conduct statistical investigations by collecting, organising, and displaying multivariate data using appropriate graphs like stem-and-leaf plots or bar graphs. They analyse data by finding the measures of central tendency: mean, median, and mode. For probability, students calculate probabilities of simple events, such as the likelihood of a team like the All Blacks winning a game, and express them as fractions, decimals, or percentages to make informed predictions.

## Patterns and Relationships: Coordinate Geometry and Graphs
This topic introduces students to the Cartesian plane, where they plot points and interpret relationships. They learn to create graphs from linear equations, such as y = 2x + 1, and use them to solve problems. Students explore real-world examples, like tracking the distance travelled during a haka practice, using a graph to represent the relationship between time and distance, thus connecting abstract concepts to tangible cultural activities.

## Problem Solving: Financial Literacy and Ratio
Students develop skills in financial literacy, calculating percentages for discounts, GST, and simple interest. For example, they might calculate the sale price of a piupiu (traditional garment) or the total cost including GST. They also work with ratios to solve problems, such as scaling down a recipe for a haka feast or comparing the ratio of boys to girls in a kapa haka group, ensuring they can apply these skills in everyday and culturally relevant contexts.
