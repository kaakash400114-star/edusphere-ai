# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 8 Mathematics


## Number and Algebra: Rational Numbers and Linear Equations
Students at NZC Level 4 extend their understanding of number by working with positive and negative integers, fractions, and decimals. They learn to perform the four operations with these rational numbers, applying the correct order of operations. Students solve linear equations with one variable, such as 3x + 7 = 22, and use inverse operations to find the value of the unknown. This skill is applied to problems, like calculating the cost of a kapa haka performance where the cost per performer and total cost are known.

## Measurement: Geometric Properties and Volume
Students develop skills to calculate the perimeter of rectangles by summing all side lengths and determine area using specific formulas for rectangles (A = l × w), triangles (A = ½ × b × h), and parallelograms (A = b × h). They distinguish between perimeter (distance around a shape) and area (space enclosed within its boundaries. For three-dimensional space, students calculate the volume of rectangular prisms using V = l × w × h, understanding it represents the space occupied. An example involves designing a marae garden, where they calculate the area of rectangular vegetable plots and the volume of a soil-filled planter box to determine its capacity for growing kumara.

## Geometry: Angles, Symmetry, and Transformation
Students apply angle properties to calculate unknown angles, using rules such as vertically opposite angles being equal and angles in a triangle summing to 180 degrees. For example, they find a missing angle in a triangle by subtracting the known angles from 180 degrees. They identify line and rotational symmetry in shapes, like the koru in Māori carving, which possesses both a line of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order 1. Students perform transformations like reflection over a mirror line, translation by moving a shape in a straight line, and rotation around a point. They apply these methods to create geometric patterns for tukutuku panels, ensuring designs reflect symmetry through precise translation of a single reflective motif.

## Statistics: Statistical Investigations and Probability
Students conduct multivariate statistical investigations by collecting and organising data using appropriate displays, such as comparative bar graphs for analysing different groups. They analyse data by calculating measures of central tendency: the mean (average), median (middle value), and mode (most frequent value), with the mean being the sum of all values divided by the number of values. For probability, students determine the likelihood of simple events, such as the probability of drawing a red marble from a bag, by calculating the ratio of favourable outcomes to the total number of possible outcomes. They then express this probability as a fraction, decimal, or percentage to make informed predictions about real-world events, like sports outcomes or weather forecasts.

## Patterns and Relationships: Coordinate Geometry and Graphs
Students will learn to plot and identify ordered pairs on the Cartesian plane, understanding the convention of the x-axis (horizontal) and y-axis (vertical). They will explore linear patterns using equations in the form y = mx + c, where 'm' represents the gradient (rate of change) and 'c' is the y-intercept. Students will create graphs from these equations and, conversely, derive equations from graphs. For example, they might graph a line with a gradient of 3 and y-intercept of 2 (y = 3x + 2) or determine the equation of a line passing through specific points like (1, 5) and (2, 8). Problem-solving will involve using these graphs to find unknown values and analyse relationships between two variables.

## Problem Solving: Financial Literacy and Ratio
Students develop skills in financial literacy, calculating percentages for discounts, GST, and simple interest. For example, they might calculate the sale price of a piupiu (traditional garment) or the total cost including GST. They also work with ratios to solve problems, such as scaling down a recipe for a haka feast or comparing the ratio of boys to girls in a kapa haka group, ensuring they can apply these skills in everyday and culturally relevant contexts.
