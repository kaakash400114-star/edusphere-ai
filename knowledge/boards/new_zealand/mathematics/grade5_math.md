# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 5 Mathematics  
## Number Knowledge and Strategies  
Students develop advanced strategies for addition and subtraction, including using place value with numbers to 1,000,000. They learn to solve problems by rounding and compensating (e.g., 78 + 29 = 78 + 30 – 1). For multiplication, they recall multiplication facts to 10 × 10 and use them for larger numbers (e.g., 3 × 60 = 180). Division strategies include sharing and grouping, like dividing 200 marbles equally among 5 friends. They explore decimals to three places, linking them to money (e.g., $5.75 is five dollars and 75 cents).  

## Fractions, Decimals, and Percentages  
Students compare and order fractions with denominators like 2, 3, 4, 5, 10, and 100. They learn that ½ is the same as 50% or 0.5. Using real-life examples, they find fractions of sets, such as ⅓ of 30 rugby balls is 10 balls. They connect decimals to measurements (e.g., 1.5 metres = 150 centimetres). Percentages are introduced through everyday contexts, like calculating 20% off a $10 piupiu (a traditional Māori garment), which is a $2 discount.  

## Measurement  
Students measure length, mass, and volume using metric units. They measure classroom objects in metres and centimetres, like a 3.5-metre-long tīeke (saddleback) poster. For mass, they compare items such as a 1-kilogram bag of kūmara (sweet potato) to a 500-gram bag of apples. Volume is explored using containers, like filling a 2-litre bottle with water. They also calculate perimeter and area of rectangles, e.g., a 4m × 3m All Blacks haka practice space has a perimeter of 14m and an area of 12m².  

## Geometry  
Students name, describe, and classify 2D and 3D shapes. They identify angles in everyday objects, like the 90° corners of a wharenui (meeting house). For 3D shapes, they explore symmetry in a koru (fern frond) or a rugby ball. They learn about transformations, such as flipping a kākahi (mussel) shell over a line of symmetry. Using maps, they describe positions using grid references, like locating the Auckland Sky Tower at (G, 7).  

## Statistics  
Students collect and display data using tally charts, bar graphs, and pictographs. They might survey their class on favourite fruits, showing results with a graph where each kiwi picture represents 2 votes. They interpret graphs by finding the most popular or least popular choice. Probability is introduced using simple events, like determining the chance of drawing a yellow marble from a bag of 3 yellow and 2 red marbles (3 out of 5).  

## Algebra  
Students recognise and continue patterns in number and shape. For example, the pattern 2, 4, 6, 8 increases by 2 each time. They use symbols to represent unknown numbers in equations, like □ + 15 = 30 (□ = 15). Real-world problems include working out how many haka steps are needed if each team of 5 does 3 steps (5 × 3 = 15 steps). They also explore inequalities, such as 20 > 10, using All Blacks scores as examples.
