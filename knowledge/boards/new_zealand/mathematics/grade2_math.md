# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 2 Mathematics

## Number Knowledge
Students learn to read, write, and order whole numbers to at least 100, grouping them in tens and ones. They understand simple fractions like one-half and one-quarter by dividing objects like piupiu or kākano (seeds). Students can count forwards and backwards in twos, fives, and tens. For example, they might count pairs of pōhā (traditional kelp bags) or sets of five kete (baskets), connecting number patterns to everyday activities in te ao Māori (the Māori world).

## Addition and Subtraction
Students use materials like counters or pebbles to solve addition and subtraction problems within 20. They learn to solve problems by counting on or back, and to find unknown numbers in equations like 7 + ☐ = 12. Word problems could involve sharing hangi (food) or counting players in a haka group, ensuring the maths is meaningful and relatable to their cultural experiences. Students are encouraged to use mental strategies before recording their thinking.

## Multiplication and Division
Students explore multiplication as repeated addition, for example, 3 groups of 4 kete makes 12 kete. They explore division by sharing equally, such as dividing 12 raukura (feathers) between 3 children. While they don't learn formal times tables, they work with simple problems using groups of two, like pairing up rugby balls for the All Blacks, or sharing food at a noho marae (marae gathering), building a foundation for future multiplicative thinking.

## Measurement
Students measure objects using non-standard units like hand spans or pencils, then compare lengths using words such as longer, taller, and shorter. They learn to tell time to the hour and half-hour on an analogue clock, connecting it to daily routines at kura (school) or home. They can also compare the weight of objects by hefting, using words like heavier and lighter, perhaps weighing a kūmara against a potato in a practical lesson.

## Geometry
Students identify and name 2D shapes such as triangles, circles, squares, and rectangles in their environment, like the shape of a tukutuku panel or a paua shell. They describe features, like how many sides or corners a shape has. For 3D shapes, they recognise spheres, cubes, and cylinders, perhaps using natural objects like rīnga (circles of wood) or stones. They learn to create and continue simple patterns with shapes and colours.

## Statistics
Students gather data by sorting or counting objects, such as collecting leaves or shells. they display the data in simple pictographs, where one picture represents one object (e.g., one picture of a kiwi for each one seen). They can answer questions about the data, like "How many more kiwi than tūī?" This provides a playful introduction to organising information and making sense of data in their world.
