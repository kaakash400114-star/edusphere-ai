# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 6 Mathematics

## Number Knowledge and Operations
Students at NZC Level 3 build on number sense by understanding place value to millions and decimals to three places. They learn to solve problems involving addition, subtraction, and multiplication of whole numbers. Students use the standard written algorithm for multi-digit multiplication, connecting this to real-world contexts like calculating All Blacks scores. They also explore divisibility rules, finding factors and primes, for example, seeing that 7 is a prime number, like the unique kiwi.

## Fractions, Decimals, and Percentages
Students explore equivalent fractions and simplifying them, e.g., 16/40 is 2/5. They add and subtract fractions with related denominators, such as 3/4 + 1/2. They order decimals and find percentages of whole numbers, like calculating 25% of the price of a piupiu. Students also understand that percentages, decimals, and fractions are different ways to show the same value, much like a haka has one essence but can be performed in different ways.

## Measurement
Students use metric units for length, mass, and volume, converting between them (e.g., 1.5 km = 1500 m). They calculate perimeters of rectangles and areas of rectangles and triangles using the formula A = ½bh. For example, they might find the area of a rectangular marae ātea. They also read time to the minute and solve problems involving duration, such as planning the timeline for a school pōwhiri.

## Geometry and Spatial Reasoning
Students name and draw 2D shapes, including quadrilaterals and triangles. They identify nets for simple 3D shapes like cubes. They explore transformation, using reflection and rotation to create tukutuku patterns. Students use simple coordinates to plot points on a grid, like mapping out the positions in a haka formation, building their understanding of spatial relationships in a culturally rich context.

## Statistics
Students collect and display data using tally charts and bar graphs. They pose and answer statistical questions, like finding the most popular rugby team in the class. They calculate the mean (average) of a simple data set, for instance, finding the average score of the All Blacks over five games. They also learn to analyse the data to make simple conclusions, connecting their findings to their experiences of NZ culture.

## Algebraic Thinking
Students continue patterns with a single rule, e.g., 2, 6, 18, 54. They write simple equations with a missing number (e.g., 4 × _ = 24) and solve them. They explore the concept of a variable using letters, like finding the value of 'c' if c + 7 = 13. This logical thinking prepares them for more complex problems, much like the strategy needed to win a game of ki-o-rahi.
