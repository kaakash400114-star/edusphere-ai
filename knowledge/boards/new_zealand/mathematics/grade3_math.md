# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 3 Mathematics

## Number Knowledge and Strategies
In Grade 3, students learn to recall and use basic facts for addition and subtraction within 20. They use place value to understand three-digit numbers, recognising that 376 is 3 hundreds, 7 tens, and 6 ones. Students solve problems involving money, such as calculating the cost of two piupiu priced at $45 each. They also learn to count forwards and backwards in tens and hundreds, using examples like counting the number of seats in a marae that holds 200 people.

## Patterns and Algebra
Students identify, describe, and create repeating and growing patterns using shapes, numbers, or objects. For example, they might create a pattern with the colours of a haka performance: red, black, red, black. They continue patterns and find missing elements. Students also explore equal groupings, linking this to multiplication concepts. They can model simple equations like 3 + 5 = 8 using counters or drawings, connecting maths to everyday activities like sharing kai with friends.

## Geometry and Measurement
Students explore the features of 2D shapes, such as triangles and hexagons, and find examples in nature, like the shape of a pūweto (fern frond). They measure lengths using centimetres and metres, comparing the height of a student to a tall tree. Capacity is introduced by measuring liquids in litres and millilitres, perhaps filling a container for a haka celebration. Students also learn to tell time to the nearest quarter-hour, linking it to daily routines like school start times or All Blacks training schedules.

## Statistics and Probability
In Grade 3, students collect and sort data about familiar topics, such as favourite rugby teams or types of native birds like kiwi. They display this information in simple graphs, including tally charts and bar graphs. Students answer questions from the graphs, like "How many more students prefer the All Blacks than the Silver Ferns?" They also explore chance, using words like "likely," "unlikely," and "impossible" to describe events, such as it being unlikely to snow in summer.

## Fractions
Students learn to recognise and name unit fractions, such as 1/2, 1/3, and 1/4. They find fractions of sets and shapes, for example, sharing a basket of pāua (abalone) so each person gets 1/4 of the total. They can show that 1/2 is the same as 2/4 by folding paper. Fractions are connected to real-life contexts, like dividing a hangi meal into equal portions or sharing a chocolate bar with friends, making maths relevant and practical.

## Problem Solving
Students use a range of strategies to solve maths problems, such as drawing a picture, using materials, or acting it out. For example, they might solve "There are 18 children doing the haka. If they stand in 6 rows, how many in each row?" by using counters to model the situation. They explain their thinking and check if their answer makes sense. This encourages logical reasoning and creative problem-solving, connecting maths to cultural activities like preparing for a festival.
