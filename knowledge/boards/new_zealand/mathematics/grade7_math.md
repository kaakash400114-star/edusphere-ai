# New Zealand Curriculum (NZC) Grade 7 Mathematics

## Number Operations
Students solve problems with all four operations using integers and decimals, moving beyond whole numbers. They use the order of operations (BEDMAS/PEMDAS) to correctly evaluate multi-step expressions. For example, calculating the average points scored by the All Blacks over five games involves dividing the total by 5. Students also explore percentages in real-life contexts like calculating GST on a new piupiu or discounts on a kapa haka performance ticket. They consolidate their understanding by checking reasonableness, like confirming a sale price makes sense.

## Algebraic Thinking
Students continue to develop their understanding of algebraic expressions, now using variables more formally. They can substitute values into formulas to solve for an unknown, such as finding the area of a local marae using `A = l × w`. They also explore simple linear relationships by creating tables of values and graphing them on a coordinate plane, connecting patterns to real-world scenarios like tracking the growth of a native plant. The concept of a constant rate of change is introduced using familiar examples like the speed of a waka ama.

## Ge Reasoning
Students classify 2D and 3D shapes based on their properties, such as the number of faces, edges, and vertices on a polyhedron. They calculate the perimeter and area of rectangles, triangles, and composite shapes, perhaps finding the area of a tukutuku panel design. A key concept is pi (π), used to calculate the circumference and area of a circle, like the size of a ceremonial drum. Students also explore symmetry, including reflection and rotational symmetry, in Māori art patterns (kowhaiwhai) and nature.

## Measurement Skills
Students become more precise with their measurement. They convert between different units of length (mm, cm, m, km), volume (ml, L), and mass (g, kg), such as converting a recipe from grams to kilograms for a large hāngi. They calculate volumes of rectangular prisms and simple cylinders. A practical application involves calculating the amount of material needed for a project, like the fabric for a costume or the volume of a container for preserving harvested kūmara. Accuracy is emphasised in all calculations.

## Statistical Investigation
Students plan and conduct statistical investigations, identifying a clear question. They choose an appropriate survey method for their data, such as asking peers about their favourite native bird. They collect data, then sort, display, and interpret it using a range of graphs, including stem-and-leaf graphs and bar graphs. They analyse their findings to answer the original question, for example, determining whether more students prefer the kiwi or the tūī. They also evaluate their process, identifying any potential bias in their sample.

## Spatial Reasoning
Students interpret and create scale maps and plans, understanding the concept of scale. They can use a simple scale like 1:100 to find the actual dimensions of a room on a building plan. They also explore transformations, specifically reflections (whakaata), rotations (hurihanga), and translations (whakatakotoranga), often using Māori-inspired designs. Activities might involve reflecting a koru pattern across a line or rotating a shape to create a new design for a tāniko (traditional weaving) pattern.
