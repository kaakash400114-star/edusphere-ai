# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 11 Mathematics

## Algebraic and Quadratic Functions
Students explore quadratic functions in the form f(x) = ax² + bx + c. They learn to find the vertex, axis of symmetry, and roots through completing the square and using the quadratic formula, (-b ± √(b² - 4ac)) / 2a. They solve quadratic inequalities and graph parabolas, understanding the effect of the coefficient 'a'. For example, analysing the profit function of a small souk business operating at different capacities involves determining the maximum profit point by finding the vertex of its quadratic revenue curve.

## Trigonometric Functions and Identities
Students study the unit circle and trigonometric ratios for angles in radians and degrees. They learn the fundamental identities, such as sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 and compound angle formulas for sin(A±B) and cos(A±B). They prove identities and solve trigonometric equations within a specified domain. For instance, calculating the height of the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque's minaret using trigonometry involves applying tangent ratios to the known angle of elevation and base distance.

## Coordinate Geometry and Vectors
Students master the derivation of linear equations using the two-point formula and point-gradient form, extending to parallel and perpendicular lines where gradients must satisfy specific multiplicative relationships. They apply vector operations like addition, subtraction, and scalar multiplication to model real-world quantities. Key concepts include calculating a vector's magnitude using the root-sum-square of its components and applying the dot product formula, **a · b = |a||b|cosθ**, to determine the angle between two vectors, such as decomposing a vehicle's velocity into directional components for a desert rally navigation.

## Calculus: Differentiation
Students understand the concept of the derivative as the gradient of a tangent line. They learn the power rule, product rule, quotient rule, and chain rule for differentiating functions. They apply differentiation to find stationary points, determine whether they are maxima or minima, and solve optimisation problems. A practical example is finding the minimum surface area of a cylindrical water tank for a desert camp to conserve material while holding a specific volume.

## Calculus: Integration
Students learn integration as the inverse operation of differentiation, mastering the power rule for finding indefinite integrals, such as ∫xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1) + C. They calculate definite integrals using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus to evaluate expressions like ∫[a,b] f(x)dx, which also determines the area under a curve bounded by the x-axis. The disk method is used to find the volume of revolution by integrating the cross-sectional area, for instance, calculating the volume of a symmetrical dune formed by revolving the curve y = f(x) around the x-axis. Applying integration, students can solve for volumes using the formula V = π ∫[a,b] [f(x)]² dx.

## Probability and Statistics
Students study discrete random variables, their probability distributions, and calculate expected values. They learn about the binomial distribution and its application to problems with two outcomes. In statistics, they calculate and interpret variance and standard deviation. An example is analysing the probability of a specific number of power outages in a UAE city during summer, where the probability of an outage on any given day is known, using the binomial model.
