# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 1 Mathematics
## Number and Number Sense
Students learn to read, write, and order numbers from 0 to 50. They understand the cardinal value, meaning the number 5 represents five items. They use concrete objects like blocks and dirham coins to count forwards and backwards. Students compare numbers using greater than, less than, and equal to signs. They partition numbers into tens and ones, for example, 24 is two tens and four ones. This is practiced using the UAE's currency, counting dirhams to understand place value through hands-on activities with real-world materials.

## Addition and Subtraction
Students learn to add and subtract within 20. They use concrete resources like counters, beads, or toys to understand combining and taking away. The addition symbol '+' means to put groups together, for example, 5 + 3 = 8. The subtraction symbol '−' means to take a group away, for example, 10 − 4 = 6. They solve simple word problems based on UAE contexts, such as finding the total number of ghaf tree seedlings after planting more, or calculating remaining dates in a box after some are eaten.

## Measurement
Students compare and measure length, mass, and capacity using direct comparison. They use non-standard units like paper clips, cubes, and hands. For example, they compare the length of two camels to see which is longer. They learn vocabulary like longer, shorter, heavier, lighter, and holds more. They explore mass by comparing a small falcon to a heavy bag of sand, and capacity by seeing which water bottle holds more liquid, linking these concepts to their local environment.

## Time and Money
Students tell the time to the hour and half-hour on an analogue clock. They understand the position of the hour and minute hands, for example, when the big hand is on the 12, it is o'clock. They identify the times they have breakfast or go to school. They identify and know the value of the 1 and 5 dirham coins. They practice counting coins and buying simple items at a mock souk, paying for a juice with 5 dirhams or exchanging two 1 dirham coins for one 2 dirham coin.

## Geometry and Shape
Students identify, name, and describe common 2D and 3D shapes. They recognize and name circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles. In 3D, they identify cubes, spheres, and cylinders. They find these shapes in their surroundings, like the circular shape of a pomegranate, the rectangular shape of a door in a Dubai villa, or the cylindrical shape of a water tank. They create patterns and pictures using these shapes, understanding that the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque is built using geometric principles.

## Data Handling
Students sort and classify objects into groups based on one criterion, such as colour, shape, or size. They can sort desert animals like camels and oryx, or types of local fruits like dates and mangoes. They record their data using tally marks and simple pictograms. For example, they can count and draw a picture of each type of car seen in the school car park, arranging their data in order to see which is the most common, making their first steps into the world of statistics.
