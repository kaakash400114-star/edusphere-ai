# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 10 Mathematics

## Number and Algebra
Students will extend their understanding of rational and irrational numbers, performing operations with both. They learn to solve complex linear equations and inequalities in one and two variables, including those with fractional coefficients. A key focus is on simplifying and manipulating algebraic expressions, applying the laws of indices to include negative and fractional exponents. Students will also learn to factorize quadratic expressions of the form ax² + bx + c and use this skill to solve quadratic equations, applying these concepts to real-world problems such as calculating profits in a Dubai souk or determining travel times across the Emirates.

## Functions and Graphs
This module covers the concept of a function, using function notation f(x). Students learn to identify and sketch the graphs of linear, quadratic, and cubic functions, understanding key features such as intercepts, turning points, and lines of symmetry. They will also explore the properties of direct and inverse proportionality, using them to model real-life situations, such as the relationship between dirhams exchanged for a foreign currency or the intensity of sunlight during a desert day. Transformations of functions, including translations and reflections, are studied to understand how graphs can be manipulated.

## Geometry and Measurement
Students delve into the properties of two- and three-dimensional shapes, calculating perimeter, area, and surface area for more complex figures like sectors, prisms, and pyramids. The core of the topic is trigonometry, where students learn to use the sine, cosine, and tangent ratios to find unknown sides and angles in right-angled triangles. These skills are then extended to non-right-angled triangles using the sine and cosine rules. Applications include calculating the height of a minaret or the length of a shadow cast by the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque at a specific time of day, linking geometry to practical engineering and architectural problems.

## Statistics and Probability
Students learn to collect, organise, and represent univariate and bivariate data using histograms, frequency polygons, and scatter graphs. They will calculate and interpret measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) and dispersion (range, interquartile range). For probability, students extend their understanding to include the calculation of theoretical probability for single and combined events. They learn to use tree diagrams and Venn diagrams to solve complex probability problems. An example includes analysing the number of visitors to a UAE heritage site or the probability of a rainy day in Al Ain during a specific month.

## Equations and Inequalities
This section focuses on solving systems of simultaneous linear equations in two variables using graphical and algebraic methods (substitution and elimination). Students are introduced to algebraic fractions, learning to simplify, add, subtract, multiply, and divide them. Furthermore, they learn to solve linear inequalities and represent the solution sets on number lines. A key application involves solving word problems that require setting up and solving systems of equations, such as determining the cost of different items in a local market or analysing the break-even point for a small business in a UAE souk.

## Vectors and Transformations
Students explore the concept of vectors, learning to describe them using magnitude and direction or column notation. They perform vector addition, subtraction, and scalar multiplication. The topic of transformations is deepened to include combinations of transformations, such as a reflection followed by a rotation or translation. Students learn to describe transformations using mathematical language and identify them from given diagrams. These concepts are applied to problems in navigation, such as plotting a course for a dhow between different ports in the Gulf, or in computer graphics, linking abstract mathematics to real-world technological and geographical applications.
