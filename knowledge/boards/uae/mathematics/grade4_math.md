# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 4 Mathematics
## Number and Number Sense
Students deepen their understanding of numbers up to one million. They learn to read, write, and order these numbers, using place value to millions. They compare numbers using greater than (>), less than (<), and equal to (=) symbols. Students can round numbers to the nearest ten, hundred, and thousand. For example, they will know that the population of a city like Dubai, which is around 3,300,000, can be rounded to 3,300,000 or to 3,000,000. They also practise expanding numbers, such as 456,789 as 400,000 + 50,000 + 6,000 + 700 + 80 + 9.

## Four Operations
Students master the four operations with whole numbers. They add and subtract numbers up to one million using the column method. They understand and use multiplication, including multiplying a three-digit number by a one-digit number (e.g., 345 x 5) and a two-digit number by a two-digit number (e.g., 24 x 15). They divide up to four-digit numbers by one-digit divisors using long division. Real-life problems involve money, like calculating the total cost of 8 gold bars costing 2,450 dirhams each or sharing a camel ration of 840 kg among 7 camels.

## Fractions and Decimals
Students compare and order fractions with the same and different denominators. They learn to add and subtract fractions with the same denominator, simplifying the answer to its lowest terms. They identify equivalent fractions and use them to solve problems. Students are introduced to decimals up to thousandths, relating them to fractions (e.g., 0.75 is 75/100). They compare and order decimals and begin adding and subtracting them. An example involves adding 1.5 litres of water to a falaj system and then using another 0.75 litres, for a total of 2.25 litres.

## Measurement
Students measure length, mass, and capacity in metric units. They convert between different units, such as kilometres to metres, grams to kilograms, and litres to millilitres. They calculate the perimeter of rectangles and squares and the area of squares and rectangles using the formula A = l x w. For example, they can find the area of a date farm that is 50 metres long and 30 metres wide (50 x 30 = 1,500 square metres). They also learn to read the time on both analogue and digital clocks, solving problems involving duration.

## Geometry and Spatial Sense
Students identify and classify 2D and 3D shapes. They learn properties like the number of sides, vertices, and faces. They identify lines of symmetry in shapes like the star on the UAE flag. Students learn about angles, classifying them as acute, obtuse, or right angles, often using architectural examples like the arches of a souq. They understand the difference between 2D and 3D shapes and can draw 2D nets for 3D shapes like cubes and pyramids, visualising how structures are built.

## Data Handling and Probability
Students collect, organise, and display data using pictographs, bar graphs, and tables. They interpret the data to answer questions and find the mode (most frequent item) of a set of data. For example, they could create a bar graph showing the number of dates sold at a souk each day and identify the busiest day. Students begin to understand basic probability by using words like 'certain', 'likely', 'unlikely', and 'impossible' to describe the chance of events, such as it being 'certain' that the sun will rise in the desert.
