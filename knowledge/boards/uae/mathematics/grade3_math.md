# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 3 Mathematics

## Numbers to 9999
Students read, write, and order numbers up to 9,999. They use place value to understand thousands, hundreds, tens, and ones. For example, the number 5,678 has 5 thousands, 6 hundreds, 7 tens, and 8 ones. Students can compare numbers using greater than (>) or less than (<) symbols. They practice rounding numbers to the nearest ten and hundred. In class, they might count the number of dirhams in a collection of 100-dirham, 10-dirham, and 1-dirham notes to understand the value of each place.

## Addition and Subtraction
Students use the column method to add and subtract numbers with up to four digits. They learn to regroup, or exchange, ten ones for one ten and ten tens for one hundred. For instance, to solve 4,250 - 1,878, one must exchange a ten for ten ones in the ones column. They solve word problems involving real-life situations, such as calculating the total cost of items from a souk or finding the change from a 500-dirham note after buying gifts.

## Multiplication and Division
Students recall and use multiplication facts for the 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, and 10 times tables. They multiply a two-digit number by a one-digit number using the grid or expanded methods. For example, to calculate 24 x 6, they might solve (20 x 6) + (4 x 6) = 120 + 24 = 144. They learn simple division by sharing or grouping, dividing numbers like 60 into 5 groups to find the quotient. They solve problems such as sharing 40 dates equally among 8 friends.

## Fractions
Students understand, name, and write fractions. They recognize unit fractions (like 1/2, 1/3, 1/4) and non-unit fractions (like 3/4). They find fractions of a set or a shape, for example, shading 3/4 of a rectangle or finding 1/3 of 24 camel toys. They learn that two fractions are equivalent if they represent the same value, understanding that 1/2 is the same as 2/4. They compare fractions with the same denominator, knowing that 5/8 is larger than 3/8.

## Measurement and Length
Students measure and compare lengths using metric units (centimetres and metres). They use rulers and measuring tapes to find the length of objects, like the height of a door or the length of a classroom. They solve problems involving addition and subtraction of lengths. For example, they might find the total length of two fabrics sewn together or the difference between the height of Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque and a local building. They also convert between centimetres and metres, knowing 100 cm = 1 m.

## Data Handling
Students collect, organize, and represent data in pictograms and bar charts. They can tally the number of students who prefer different fruits from a souk or the favourite animals in their class. They create a pictogram where one picture represents a certain number of items. They also interpret the information in charts to answer questions, such as "Which animal is the most popular?" or "How many more students like apples than oranges?" They use the data to make simple comparisons and conclusions.
