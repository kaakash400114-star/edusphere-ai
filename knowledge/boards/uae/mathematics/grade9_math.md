# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 9 Mathematics  

## Number and Algebra
Students will solve complex algebraic equations, including quadratic equations of the form ax² + bx + c = 0, using the quadratic formula and factorisation methods. They will manipulate algebraic fractions, simplify expressions, and solve linear and simultaneous equations with two variables. Real-world applications include calculating profit in a souk business or determining the cost of materials for a project. Students will also use index laws to simplify expressions involving positive and negative fractional indices.

## Geometry and Measures
This unit focuses on properties of 2D and 3D shapes, including surface area and volume calculations for prisms, cylinders, and spheres. Students apply trigonometric ratios (sine, cosine, tangent) to solve problems involving right-angled triangles, such as determining the height of a minaret or the length of a shadow. They also learn about circle theorems, including the angle subtended by an arc at the centre and circumference. Constructions using compasses and rulers are practised, with examples drawn from Islamic geometric patterns.

## Statistics and Probability
Students will learn to collect and classify data into grouped and ungrouped frequency tables, then represent it using histograms and cumulative frequency curves. They will calculate measures of central tendency, including the mean, median, and mode, along with dispersion metrics such as the range and interquartile range. In probability, students determine theoretical and experimental probabilities and use tree diagrams to solve problems involving independent and dependent events. A key concept is distinguishing between these events, for instance, when calculating the likelihood of two consecutive tourists choosing a specific hotel versus one visitor’s choice influencing another. Students will apply these methods to analyse UAE-specific data, such as interpreting monthly rainfall statistics in Abu Dhabi or forecasting camel race outcomes based on historical results.

## Equations and Inequalities
Students master solving linear and quadratic inequalities by applying rules of reversal when multiplying or dividing by negative numbers, with solutions demonstrated on number lines and graphed regions. They learn to find intersection points of simultaneous equations by substitution or graphical methods, such as solving y = 2x + 1 and x² + y² = 9 to find their exact points of contact. Algebraic techniques include factorizing quadratic expressions to solve equations like x² - 5x + 6 = 0. Formulas for calculating the discriminant (b² - 4ac) determine the nature of roots for quadratic equations, distinguishing real or imaginary solutions. Practical applications involve creating and solving inequalities to model constraints, for instance, determining maximum ticket prices for a school event while staying within a AED 5000 budget.

## Functions and Graphs
Students master identifying and sketching linear functions (y = mx + c), quadratic functions (y = ax² + bx + c), cubic functions (y = ax³ + bx² + cx + d), and reciprocal functions (y = k/x), recognising their characteristic shapes. They determine key features such as gradients and y-intercepts for linear functions, and turning points and roots for quadratic functions using methods like completing the square or applying the formula x = [-b ± √(b² - 4ac)] / 2a. Graphical transformations are applied, including vertical and horizontal translations (e.g., y = f(x) + k and y = f(x ± h)) and reflections (e.g., y = -f(x)). Students solve equations and inequalities by identifying x-values where graphs intersect the x-axis or lie above/below other lines, applying this to model UAE contexts like the area of an Al Ain plantation versus water cost.

## Problem-Solving and Applications
Students apply mathematical reasoning to solve multi-step problems involving number, algebra, geometry, and statistics. They use real-life scenarios, such as calculating the area of land for a new villa or the time required for a camel race. Estimation techniques and unit conversions (e.g., dirhams to euros, metres to kilometres) are emphasised. Students present solutions logically, using clear notation and precise terminology, reflecting the UAE's focus on analytical thinking and innovation.
