# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 2 Mathematics
## Number and Place Value
Students read and write numbers from 1 to 100 in numerals and words. They understand that two-digit numbers are made of tens and ones, using place value apparatus like Base-10 blocks. They compare and order numbers, using the greater than (>), less than (<) and equal to (=) symbols. For example, they recognise 57 is composed of 5 tens and 7 ones, and that 65 is greater than 48. They count in steps of 2, 3, and 5, beginning at 0, and can count backwards from 100. This skill is applied when counting items in a souq, such as baskets of dates.

## Addition and Subtraction
Students develop mental and written methods for addition and subtraction within 100. They learn to add two two-digit numbers, including those that require a ten to be exchanged, such as 47 + 25. They use number lines and counting on to find the difference between two numbers, for example, finding how many more dirhams are needed to buy a toy costing 75 dirhams when only 30 dirhams are saved. They solve simple one-step word problems involving both operations, encouraging them to think about real-life situations, like sharing camel treats equally among friends.

## Multiplication and Division
Students are introduced to multiplication as repeated addition and division as sharing or grouping. They learn to count in multiples of 2, 5, and 10 and connect this to multiplication facts, understanding that 5 x 3 means '5 groups of 3'. They explore division by sharing a set of items into equal groups, such as dividing 24 sea shells equally among 4 children. They use concrete objects and drawings to represent these problems. An example is calculating the number of camel rides available if there are 10 camels and each ride takes one person at a time.

## Measurement
Students measure and compare lengths, masses, and volumes using standard units. They use rulers to measure lengths in centimetres, weighing scales to measure mass in kilograms, and measuring jugs for volume in litres. They compare objects using terms like longer, heavier, and more full. For instance, they might find that a bottle of water holds more than a can of juice. They begin to tell the time to the nearest 15 minutes using an analogue clock, connecting this to daily routines and timings at school or visiting the mosque.

## Geometry: Properties of Shapes
Students identify and describe the properties of common 2D and 3D shapes. They name and describe circles, triangles, rectangles, squares, and pentagons, focusing on the number of sides and corners. They name and describe cubes, cuboids, spheres, and cylinders. They can find these shapes in their environment, such as the circular dome of a building or the rectangular shape of a school bus. They use language like 'faces', 'edges', and 'corners' when describing 3D shapes, and make models of simple shapes using clay or construction materials.

## Handling Data and Problem Solving
Students construct and interpret simple pictograms, tally charts, and block graphs to represent data. They can create a pictogram to show the favourite colours of children in their class, using a symbol to represent each vote. They ask and answer simple questions about the data they have created, such as 'Which was the most popular choice?'. They apply their mathematical skills to solve problems in real-life contexts related to the UAE, like finding the total number of people visiting a local park in a day or counting the number of cars in a desert convoy.
