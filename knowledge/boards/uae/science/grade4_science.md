# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 4 Science

## Living Things and Their Habitats
Students learn to classify living things into groups like plants and animals, and vertebrates and invertebrates. They explore how animals are adapted to their desert environment, such as the camel's hump for storing fat and its thick eyelashes for sand protection. They examine local examples like the Arabian oryx and Ghaf tree, understanding how living things depend on non-living things like sunlight and water for survival.

## Materials and Their Properties
This topic covers grouping materials based on their observable properties, such as transparency, hardness, and magnetism. Students test everyday materials from the UAE, like gold for conductivity and aluminium for being lightweight, using magnets to sort metallic from non-metallic items. They learn about reversible changes, like melting ice in the heat, and irreversible changes, like burning wood for a campfire.

## Sound
Students discover that sound is made by vibrations and needs a medium like air to travel. They explore how changing the length and tightness of a string on an oud changes the pitch, making a higher or lower sound. They learn about volume, understanding why a car horn is louder than a whisper. They identify sounds from their local environment, such as the call to prayer from a mosque or the bustle of a souk.

## Electricity
Students learn about simple, complete circuits, using components like batteries, wires, bulbs, and switches. They construct circuits to light up a model of the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque, understanding that electricity flows from the battery through the wire to the bulb. They differentiate between conductors, like copper wires, and insulators, like plastic casings, using this knowledge to design safe circuits.

## The Earth and Beyond
Students identify the Sun, Earth, and Moon as major celestial bodies, learning that Earth's rotation causes day and night. They explore the UAE's location in a hot desert climate, understanding how the Sun's energy heats the land. They investigate the phases of the Moon, from a full moon like a bright pearl in the night sky to a crescent moon, observing these changes over a lunar month.

## States of Matter
Students compare solids, liquids, and gases, using water as a key example. They observe ice (solid) melting into water (liquid) in the desert heat and then evaporating into water vapour (gas). They learn about the water cycle, condensation forming dew on plants in the cool desert morning and precipitation as rare, heavy rainfall. They explore how gases fill a container, like the air filling a room.
