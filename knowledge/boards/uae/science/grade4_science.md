# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 4 Science


## Living Things and Their Habitats
Students classify organisms hierarchically, beginning with the broad categories of plants and animals before distinguishing vertebrates by their backbone from invertebrates without one. They analyse structural and behavioural adaptations of desert animals, such as the camel's hump for fat storage and the Arabian oryx's ability to conserve water. Plant adaptations are also studied, using the Ghaf tree as a key example of how its deep roots find water and its small leaves reduce moisture loss. The core concept that all living things depend on non-living components like sunlight, air, water, and soil for survival is a fundamental principle. These dependencies form an intricate ecosystem where producers, consumers, and decomposers interact within a balanced habitat.

## Materials and Their Properties
Students classify materials according to specific physical properties such as transparency, hardness, magnetism, and electrical conductivity, using these attributes to sort and group items. They distinguish between states of matter and observe how heat causes reversible changes, like melting ice into water, or freezing water into ice. Irreversible changes, such as burning wood or cooking food, are identified as permanent alterations that cannot be easily reversed. Practical investigations involve testing the conductivity of metals like gold and aluminium, and using magnets to separate metallic items, such as coins, from non-metallic ones. This builds a foundational understanding of how material properties dictate their function and behaviour in everyday applications within the UAE environment.

## Sound
Students discover that sound is made by vibrations and needs a medium like air to travel. They explore how changing the length and tightness of a string on an oud changes the pitch, making a higher or lower sound. They learn about volume, understanding why a car horn is louder than a whisper. They identify sounds from their local environment, such as the call to prayer from a mosque or the bustle of a souk.

## Electricity
Grade 4 students learn that a complete circuit requires an uninterrupted path for electricity to flow from the battery, through wires and components, and back to the battery. They construct simple circuits using batteries, wires, bulbs, and switches to light up a model of the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque. Students identify conductors, such as copper wires, which allow electricity to pass through, and insulators, like plastic casings, which prevent electricity from flowing and are used for safety. They learn that breaking the circuit with a switch stops the flow of current, causing the bulb to turn off. This knowledge is applied to design safe and functional circuits for various applications.

## The Earth and Beyond
The Earth rotates on its axis every 24 hours, creating the cycle of day and night. This rotation causes sunlight to hit the Earth at different angles, making one side bright and warm while the other side is dark and cool. The Moon orbits the Earth, and the changing angle of sunlight reflecting off its surface creates its phases: new moon, waxing crescent, first quarter, waxing gibbous, full moon, waning gibbous, last quarter, and waning crescent. This complete lunar cycle, which determines the Islamic calendar, takes approximately 29.5 days. Located near the Tropic of Cancer, the UAE experiences a hot desert climate where the Sun's intense energy dominates, directly influencing the local environment.

## States of Matter
Students classify matter into solids, liquids, and gases based on properties like shape and volume. Solids, such as a desert rock, have a fixed shape and volume, while liquids like water in a pan have a fixed volume but take the shape of their container. Gases, such as the air in a balloon, have neither a fixed shape nor volume and expand to fill any space. The water cycle is a key process: heat from the sun causes liquid water to evaporate into a gas, which then cools and condenses into liquid again, forming dew on plants before precipitating as rain. This demonstrates the continuous transformation of matter between these three states.
