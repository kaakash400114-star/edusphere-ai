# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 5 Science

## Properties of Materials
Students learn to classify materials as solids, liquids, or gases based on their observable properties. They understand that solids have a fixed shape and volume, while liquids take the shape of their container but have a fixed volume. Gases have neither a fixed shape nor volume. For example, a camel’s saddle is a solid, while water in a desert oasis is a liquid. A hot air balloon filled with gas demonstrates how gases expand to fill all available space. They also learn about reversible and irreversible changes, such as freezing water into ice (reversible) or burning wood (irreversible).

## Forces and Motion
Students investigate the effects of forces on objects. They learn that forces can push or pull and can change an object’s speed or direction. Gravity is a force that pulls objects towards the Earth. For example, a date fruit falls from a tree due to gravity. Friction is a force that slows down moving objects, like a sandal rubbing against the desert floor. They explore how simple machines, such as levers and pulleys, make work easier. Students conduct experiments to see how the force of wind can propel a small sailboat across a water tank.

## Living Things and Their Habitats
Students explore the diversity of life in different habitats within the UAE, including the desert and coastal mangroves. They classify plants and animals based on their observable features, such as cacti with spines to store water and camels with humps for fat storage. They learn about the interdependence of living things; for instance, desert foxes eat rodents, and date palms provide food and shelter. Food chains are used to show how energy is passed from one organism to another, from a desert shrub to an insect to a lizard.

## Earth and Space
Students learn about the Earth, Sun, and Moon as spheres. They understand that the Earth rotates on its axis once every 24 hours, causing day and night, and revolves around the Sun once a year, creating the seasons. They study the phases of the Moon, from new moon to full moon, which are visible over the Arabian Gulf. Locally, they learn about the formation of sand dunes by wind erosion in the desert. They also understand that fossils are the remains of ancient organisms found in rocks, providing evidence of past life on Earth.

## Energy and Electricity
Students investigate different forms of energy, including light, heat, sound, and electrical energy. They learn that energy can be converted from one form to another, such as chemical energy in a battery being converted to electrical energy in a toy car. They construct simple electrical circuits using batteries, wires, bulbs, and switches to light up a model of the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque. They learn about conductors (like copper wires) that allow electricity to flow and insulators (like plastic) that prevent it, ensuring safety.

## Caring for the Environment
Students understand the importance of protecting the UAE's unique environment. They learn about pollution, such as plastic waste in souks or oil spills in the sea, and its harmful effects on wildlife. They study the concept of sustainability by learning about how solar panels are used in some UAE buildings to create clean energy. They identify ways to conserve resources, like turning off lights in empty rooms to save electricity and reusing water in school gardens. They learn that caring for the desert ecosystem helps protect animals like the Arabian oryx.
