# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 10 Science

## Chemical Bonding and Structure
Students learn how atoms achieve stable electron configurations through ionic and covalent bonding. Ionic bonding involves the transfer of electrons from metals to non-metals, creating compounds like sodium chloride (table salt). Covalent bonding involves the sharing of electron pairs, as in carbon dioxide (CO₂), where each oxygen shares two electrons with the central carbon atom. Students also learn to draw dot-and-cross diagrams and determine the chemical formula of simple ionic compounds, such as magnesium oxide (MgO), based on the charges of the ions involved.

## Electricity and Magnetism
This topic covers the relationship between current, voltage, and resistance in electrical circuits, described by Ohm’s Law (V = I × R). Students learn to apply this formula to calculate the missing variable in a circuit, such as finding the current (2.5 A) flowing through a 10 Ω resistor connected to a 25 V supply. The unit also explores the UAE’s role in large-scale projects, including the analysis of a solar farm in Abu Dhabi, where panels convert sunlight into electrical energy for the national grid, demonstrating practical applications of physics principles.

## Energy Resources and Sustainability
Students study renewable and non-renewable energy sources. They analyze the UAE’s rapid adoption of solar energy, particularly the Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum Solar Park in Dubai, which reduces reliance on fossil fuels. The curriculum covers the calculation of energy efficiency for solar panels and the environmental impact of fossil fuels. Students also learn about sustainable practices in the UAE, such as waste-to-energy plants in Sharjah, which convert 1,500 tonnes of municipal solid waste daily into electricity, contributing to the nation’s goal of zero landfill waste by 2050.

## Forces and Motion
This topic explains Newton’s laws of motion. Students apply Newton’s second law (F = m × a) to calculate the force (800 N) required to accelerate a 200 kg object at 4 m/s². The UAE is highlighted through an analysis of the Yas Marina Formula 1 circuit, where the relationship between speed, acceleration, and centripetal force is examined. Students also study the effects of friction and air resistance on motion, including how these forces influence the design of high-speed vehicles in the UAE, such as those used in the Dubai Police fleet.

## Acids, Bases, and Salts
Students learn the definitions and properties of acids, bases, and salts. They study the pH scale and identify common acids (e.g., hydrochloric acid, HCl) and bases (e.g., sodium hydroxide, NaOH) used in laboratories and industries. The UAE context is explored through examples, such as the use of desalination plants in the UAE to remove salts from seawater for drinking, and the analysis of alkaline soils in desert regions, which are treated with sulfuric acid to improve agricultural productivity.

## The Human Body and Health
This topic covers the structure and function of the human digestive and circulatory systems. Students learn about the role of enzymes in breaking down food and the transport of oxygen and nutrients by blood. The UAE is highlighted through examples like the prevalence of type 2 diabetes in the region, linked to diet and lifestyle. Students also study the importance of hydration in desert climates, where a person may lose up to 2 litres of water per day through sweat, and the role of balanced diets rich in dates and local vegetables in maintaining health.
