# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 3 Science

## Properties of Materials
Students learn to identify and group materials based on their observable properties like hardness, transparency, and conductivity. They distinguish between natural materials (wood, sand) and human-made materials (plastic, glass). In the UAE, a real-world example is comparing materials used in buildings; the reflective glass of the Burj Khalifa is transparent and hard, while the traditional mud-brick walls are opaque and strong. Students conduct simple tests to see if materials sink or float, heat up, or allow electricity to pass, understanding why specific materials are chosen for different purposes.

## Plants and Their Growth
Students explore the life cycle of flowering plants, learning about the functions of roots, stems, leaves, flowers, and fruits. They understand that plants need water, light, and air from the soil to grow. A UAE example is the date palm, a crucial local plant. Students can observe how its roots search for deep underground water in the desert. They also learn about photosynthesis in simple terms: plants use sunlight to make their own food. Activities include planting seeds, like a small flower in a cup, and watering it to observe the stages of growth from seed to sprout.

## Heat and Temperature
Students learn that heat is a form of energy that can make things warmer. They explore different sources of heat, such as the sun, fires, and electricity. They understand that heat travels from hotter objects to colder ones and can change the state of matter, like ice melting into water. A practical UAE example is feeling the hot sand at the beach on a sunny day and the cooler shade of a tree. Students use thermometers to measure the temperature in different places, like inside the classroom versus outside, to see how the sun heats the air.

## The Earth and Sky
Students identify the main components of the Earth: land, water, and air. They learn about the features of land, such as mountains, deserts, and valleys, and water features like seas and oases. In the UAE, they can relate this to the Hajar Mountains and the Arabian Gulf. They observe the day and night sky, identifying the sun, moon, and stars. They learn that the sun appears to move across the sky during the day, causing day and night, and the moon appears as a bright object at night, changing its shape over weeks.

## Forces and Motion
Students investigate pushes and pulls as forces that can make objects start moving, stop moving, change direction, or change shape. They discover that friction is a force that opposes motion, for example, a heavy carpet is hard to pull because of friction with the floor. In a UAE context, they can think about a camel being pulled by a rope (a pull force) to carry goods in a souk. They explore simple machines like levers and inclined planes, learning how they make work easier, such as using a ramp to pull a box into a pickup truck.

## Caring for the Environment
Students learn about the importance of keeping the environment clean and healthy. They identify different types of waste, such as paper, plastic, and food, and understand the problem of littering. A UAE focus is on protecting the desert and coastline. They learn the 3Rs: Reduce, Reuse, and Recycle. For example, they can reuse a water bottle to reduce plastic waste. Students discuss how pollution harms animals and plants and why it is everyone's responsibility, including their own, to help by not littering and encouraging others to recycle materials like aluminium cans.
