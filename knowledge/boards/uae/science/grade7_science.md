# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 7 Science

## Matter and its Properties
Students learn that matter is anything with mass and volume, existing as solids, liquids, or gases. They study the physical properties of materials, such as density, conductivity, and magnetism. For example, gold has a very high density of 19.3 g/cm³, which is why it feels heavy for its size. In the UAE's hot climate, understanding thermal conductivity is vital, as materials like sand heat up quickly due to low conductivity. They also explore chemical changes, like when iron rusts in the humid coastal air, forming a new substance with different properties.

## Forces and Motion
This topic covers Newton's laws of motion. The first law states an object at rest stays at rest unless a force, like a strong desert wind, acts upon it. The second law is described by the formula F = m × a, where a force of 50 newtons can accelerate a 5 kg object at 10 m/s². The third law explains that when a falcon dives, it pushes air down, and the air pushes the falcon up. Students calculate speed, using the example of a car travelling 150 km on the Sheikh Zayed Road in 1.5 hours, giving a speed of 100 km/h.

## Energy Transfers
Students investigate different forms of energy and their transformations. They learn that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted. A common example is a solar panel on a villa converting light energy from the sun into electrical energy to power air conditioning. Another is the chemical energy in food, such as dates, being converted into kinetic energy for a student running. The efficiency of these transfers is key, and they learn that friction often converts kinetic energy into unwanted heat, especially on sandy surfaces.

## The Particle Model of Matter
This section explains how all substances are made of tiny particles called atoms and molecules. In solids, like a camel's leather saddle, particles are tightly packed and vibrate in fixed positions. In liquids, such as water in a falaj irrigation system, particles are close but can move past one another. In gases, like the air filling a souk stall, particles are far apart and move rapidly. Students learn that adding heat increases particle energy, causing expansion, which is why desert roads can expand and crack in the high summer temperatures.

## Organisms and their Environments
Students classify organisms and study their relationships within ecosystems. They learn about the desert food web, where a date palm produces energy, which is consumed by insects, and then by birds like the houbara bustard. Adaptations for survival are studied, such as how the Arabian oryx can conserve water in extreme heat and survive on sparse desert plants. The impact of human activity, like construction near the Liwa Oasis, on local habitats is also examined, along with conservation efforts to protect biodiversity.

## Electricity and Magnetism
This topic introduces electric circuits and the components needed for a current to flow, such as a battery, wires, and a bulb. The relationship between voltage (V), current (I), and resistance (R) is defined by Ohm's Law: V = I × R. A student might calculate the current in a circuit with a 9-volt battery and a 3-ohm resistor to be 3 amperes. They also learn about magnetic fields around a bar magnet and the Earth's magnetic field, which allows a compass to always point north, a vital tool for navigation in the Gulf region.
