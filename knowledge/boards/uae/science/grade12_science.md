# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 12 Science
## Thermodynamics and Heat Engines
Students analyse the first and second laws of thermodynamics. The first law states that the change in a system's internal energy equals the heat transferred to the system minus the work done by it (ΔU = Q - W). The second law introduces entropy, a measure of disorder, stating that it always increases for a spontaneous process. They apply these principles to heat engines, calculating thermal efficiency (η = W/Qh), where W is work output and Qh is heat absorbed from the hot reservoir. An example is calculating the efficiency of a car engine operating with a reservoir at 600 K and exhausting to the ambient Dubai air at 310 K.
## Wave-Particle Duality and Quantum Theory
This topic explores the dual nature of light and matter. Students learn that light exhibits both wave-like interference and particle-like photon properties, described by E = hf, where E is energy, h is Planck's constant (6.626 x 10^-34 J.s), and f is frequency. They extend this to matter, understanding de Broglie's hypothesis that particles have wavelengths (λ = h/p). Real-world applications include the electron microscope, used in UAE laboratories to analyse materials at the atomic scale, and the photoelectric effect, which is fundamental to the solar panels powering many desert facilities.
## Electromagnetic Induction
Students investigate how a changing magnetic flux induces an electromotive force (EMF) in a conductor, as defined by Faraday's Law (EMF = -N ΔΦ/Δt). They apply Lenz's Law to determine the direction of the induced current. For example, they calculate the EMF generated in a coil of 1000 turns within a generator, where the magnetic field strength changes by 0.5 T over a period of 0.1 s. This principle is vital for the large-scale power generation in the UAE and the inductive charging systems used for electric vehicles near landmarks like the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque.
## Nuclear Physics and Radioactivity
Pupils delve into nuclear reactions, differentiating between alpha, beta, and gamma radiation. They learn about the law of radioactive decay, N = N₀ e^(-λt), where N is the remaining quantity, N₀ is the initial quantity, λ is the decay constant, and t is time. Calculations involve determining half-lives and activity (Becquerel). The UAE's peaceful use of nuclear energy for water desalination is a key example, where controlled fission reactions in reactors like the Barakah plant generate immense thermal energy to combat water scarcity in the arid climate.
## Organic Chemistry and Petrochemicals
This section focuses on the structure and reactions of hydrocarbons, crucial to the UAE's economy. Students study the IUPAC naming and properties of alkanes, alkenes, and areomers, as well as addition and substitution reactions. A core application is fractional distillation, where crude oil is separated in refineries. Students can calculate the percentage yield of a specific hydrocarbon, for instance, deriving octane (C8H18) for gasoline from a barrel of oil costing 80 dirhams, linking chemical principles directly to the nation's vast petrochemical industry.
## Genetic Engineering and Biotechnology
Learners explore modern genetic techniques and their applications. They examine the process of recombinant DNA technology, using restriction enzymes and plasmids to insert genes, for example, creating drought-resistant crops suitable for the Gulf-region's saline soil. The polymerase chain reaction (PCR) is studied to amplify DNA samples for forensic analysis, such as those used in environmental monitoring in the UAE's protected desert and marine areas, showcasing how science supports national conservation and sustainability goals.
