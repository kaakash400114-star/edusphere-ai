# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 4 English

## Reading Comprehension: Identifying the Main Idea
Students learn to find the central message or main idea in a text. The main idea is what the text is mostly about. It can be stated in a topic sentence, often at the beginning or end of a paragraph. To find it, students read the passage, identify who or what the text is about (the key subject), and then determine the most important thing said about that subject. For example, in a passage about visiting a souk in Dubai, the main idea might be "The Dubai souk is a lively place to shop for unique goods."

## Grammar: Using Past Tense Verbs
Students learn to correctly use past tense verbs to describe actions that have already happened. The regular past tense is typically formed by adding '-ed' to the base verb, for example, 'walk' becomes 'walked' and 'play' becomes 'played'. For irregular verbs, the past tense has a unique spelling, such as 'go' becoming 'went' and 'see' becoming 'saw'. In sentences, students practice using these verbs to tell stories about past events, like "We visited the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque last weekend."

## Vocabulary: Understanding Synonyms and Antonyms
Students learn that synonyms are words that have the same or very similar meanings. They use them to make writing more interesting. For example, 'big', 'large', and 'huge' are synonyms. Antonyms are words that have opposite meanings, like 'hot' and 'cold' or 'buy' and 'sell'. Students practice by creating pairs of antonyms to describe opposites found in the UAE, such as 'day' and 'night' or 'sand' (desert) and 'water' (sea).

## Writing: Structuring a Descriptive Paragraph
Students learn to write a descriptive paragraph with a clear topic sentence, several supporting sentences, and a concluding sentence. The topic sentence introduces the subject, such as "The camel is an important animal in the UAE." Supporting sentences use descriptive words (adjectives) to explain details like its appearance or how it helps people. Students use specific details related to the UAE, like describing the colour of a camel's coat or how it can travel long distances in the desert heat. The concluding sentence summarizes the main idea.

## Listening: Following Multi-Step Directions
Students practice listening carefully and following a series of two or three steps. This skill is crucial for understanding instructions in all subjects. To do this, they learn to listen to all the steps before starting, identify key action words (like 'first', 'next', 'then'), and carry them out in the correct order. For example, a teacher might give the directions: "First, draw a picture of a falcon. Next, write a sentence below it explaining what it can do." Students must complete both tasks correctly to demonstrate understanding.

## Spelling: Identifying Common Spelling Patterns
Students learn and apply common spelling patterns to help them write words correctly. They study words with the 'ight' pattern, which makes an 'ite' sound, like 'light', 'night', and 'sight'. They also practice the 'ai' pattern, as in 'rain', 'wait', and 'train', which is commonly heard in words related to weather. Students sort words into these pattern groups and practice using them in sentences about UAE life, for example, "We saw bright lights in the city at night."
