# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 7 English
## Literary Devices and Figurative Language
Students identify and analyse key literary devices. Similes use 'like' or 'as' for direct comparisons, such as 'the desert sands were as fine as gold'. Metaphors state a comparison directly, like 'the bustling souk was a river of humanity'. Personification gives human qualities to non-human things, for example, 'the rising sun painted the sky'. Students learn to spot these devices in texts and explain their effect on the reader, recognising how they enhance imagery and emotional impact in stories and poems.

## Narrative Structure and Genre Conventions
Learners deconstruct stories by identifying the five key stages: exposition (setting the scene in Dubai), rising action (a challenge at the camel market), climax (the decisive race), falling action (the aftermath), and resolution (the celebration). Students differentiate between genres, focusing on science fiction, adventure, and mystery, identifying their unique conventions, such as futuristic technology in sci-fi or the uncovering of clues in a mystery set in an old Sharjah fort.

## Argumentative Writing Techniques
Students learn to structure a persuasive essay using a clear thesis statement, for instance, 'Recycling should be mandatory in all UAE communities'. They support their argument with logical, evidence-based points, such as 'reducing plastic waste protects the desert ecosystem'. Counter-arguments are acknowledged and refuted. Formal language is used, and writing is organised into a clear introduction with a roadmap, body paragraphs with topic sentences, and a conclusion that summarises the main point and calls to action.

## Grammatical Accuracy and Complex Sentences
Pupils master the correct use of complex sentences, utilising subordinating conjunctions like 'although', 'because', and 'while'. For example: 'Although the summer in Abu Dhabi is very hot, people enjoy evening events at the Corniche'. Students also focus on subject-verb agreement with compound subjects and learn to correctly use active and passive voice to vary sentence structure, understanding how this affects the focus and flow of their writing.

## Reading Comprehension and Analysis
Students develop skills to infer and deduce meaning from texts. They read passages about Emirati culture or history and answer questions requiring them to interpret character motives, identify themes like generosity or resilience, and understand the writer's purpose. Vocabulary acquisition is key; students learn to define and use new words in context, such as 'majlis' or 'wadi', demonstrating a deep understanding of texts beyond literal interpretation.

## Spelling, Punctuation, and Vocabulary Building
This section focuses on formal spelling rules and commonly confused words, like 'desert' (the arid land) versus 'dessert' (the sweet course). Students learn to use a range of punctuation for clarity and effect, including semi-colons, apostrophes for possession and contraction, and commas in complex sentences. They expand their vocabulary by learning synonyms, antonyms, and specific terminology related to the UAE, enriching their written and spoken expression.
