# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 7 English

## Literary Devices and Figurative Language
Students identify and analyse key literary devices. Similes use 'like' or 'as' for direct comparisons, such as 'the desert sands were as fine as gold'. Metaphors state a comparison directly, like 'the bustling souk was a river of humanity'. Personification gives human qualities to non-human things, for example, 'the rising sun painted the sky'. Students learn to spot these devices in texts and explain their effect on the reader, recognising how they enhance imagery and emotional impact in stories and poems.

## Narrative Structure and Genre Conventions
Learners deconstruct stories by identifying the five key stages: exposition (setting the scene in Dubai), rising action (a challenge at the camel market), climax (the decisive race), falling action (the aftermath), and resolution (the celebration). Students differentiate between genres, focusing on science fiction, adventure, and mystery, identifying their unique conventions, such as futuristic technology in sci-fi or the uncovering of clues in a mystery set in an old Sharjah fort.

## Argumentative Writing Techniques
Students learn to structure a persuasive essay using a clear thesis statement, for instance, 'Recycling should be mandatory in all UAE communities'. They support their argument with logical, evidence-based points, such as 'reducing plastic waste protects the desert ecosystem'. Counter-arguments are acknowledged and refuted. Formal language is used, and writing is organised into a clear introduction with a roadmap, body paragraphs with topic sentences, and a conclusion that summarises the main point and calls to action.

## Grammatical Accuracy and Complex Sentences
Students must form complex sentences by correctly applying subordinating conjunctions such as 'although', 'because', 'while', and 'since' to establish clear logical relationships between clauses. This mastery extends to ensuring subject-verb agreement in sentences with compound subjects, using conjunctions like 'and' and 'or' to connect subjects correctly. A key concept is the proper use of active voice ('The student wrote the essay') to make the subject clear and direct, versus the passive voice ('The essay was written by the student'), which shifts focus to the object. Learners also learn to correctly form complex sentences with introductory clauses, such as 'While we were touring Dubai Creek, the falcon demonstration began'. Pupils will understand how varying sentence structure through these grammatical tools improves the focus and cohesion of their written communication.

## Reading Comprehension and Analysis
Students learn to deconstruct texts by applying explicit comprehension strategies such as summarising main ideas, identifying cause-and-effect relationships, and distinguishing between fact and opinion. They critically evaluate the author's craft by analysing literary devices, including metaphor and symbolism, to explain their impact on meaning and reader engagement. A core method involves analysing character development and thematic elements within culturally relevant texts to make valid inferences and support interpretations with textual evidence. Vocabulary is systematically acquired through contextual analysis, with students required to define, determine the connotation of, and correctly apply academic and domain-specific terms such as 'heritage' and 'tradition'. To demonstrate mastery, students formulate written and oral analyses that synthesise these skills to articulate a deep and comprehensive understanding of the text.

## Spelling, Punctuation, and Vocabulary Building
Students master formal spelling rules, such as the 'i before e except after c' principle, and differentiate between homophones like 'affect' (verb) and 'effect' (noun). Punctuation instruction covers the correct use of semi-colons to link closely related independent clauses, apostrophes for both possession (the teacher's book) and contractions (it's), and commas to correctly separate clauses and introductory phrases. Vocabulary building is a core component where students acquire synonyms and antonyms to enhance their written expression, alongside subject-specific terminology related to UAE history and geography. This foundational knowledge enables students to produce precise, grammatically correct, and stylistically sophisticated texts across all academic disciplines.
