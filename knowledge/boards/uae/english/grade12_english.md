# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 12 English

## Argumentative and Discursive Writing
Students learn to construct structured, persuasive arguments on complex global and local issues. They master techniques like logical deduction, inductive reasoning, and using reputable evidence, such as citing reports from UAE ministries or statistics from the Dubai Statistics Centre. A key focus is the Toulmin model, with students learning to establish a claim, provide grounds with data from sources like the UAE Vision 2031, and address counter-arguments, often using regional examples to strengthen their position.

## Analytical Reading of Unseen Texts
This module develops skills in close reading and comprehension of unseen, sophisticated texts. Students learn to identify and analyse linguistic devices, including rhetorical questions used in speeches by UAE leaders, imagery from descriptions of the desert climate, and symbolic language found in texts about souks. They must deduce the writer's purpose, tone, and intended audience, explaining how specific lexical and grammatical choices create meaning and influence a reader familiar with Gulf-region contexts.

## Literary Appreciation: Modern and Contemporary Prose
Students critically analyse modern prose to understand character development, thematic depth, and narrative techniques. They examine the use of setting, such as urban landscapes in Dubai or traditional villages in Ras Al Khaimah, to reflect themes of identity and change. Analysis focuses on a writer’s use of symbolism and characterisation to explore universal ideas like resilience, using texts where protagonists navigate the clash between heritage and modernisation in a rapidly transforming Gulf society.

## Critical and Creative Writing
This section blends critical thinking with imaginative expression. Students learn to deconstruct media narratives, such as those surrounding tourism in Abu Dhabi or sustainability initiatives in Masdar City. They then apply this understanding to create original, descriptive pieces. This involves using vivid imagery to evoke Dubai’s skyline or the tranquillity of Liwa Oasis, while employing figurative language, varied sentence structures, and a rich vocabulary to evoke specific emotional responses in the reader.

## Research and Academic Writing
Students are guided through the formal research process, culminating in a substantial academic paper. They learn to formulate a clear, focused research question, such as "The Role of Cultural Festivals in Preserving Emirati Heritage." Key skills include developing a thesis statement, structuring arguments coherently, integrating sources using in-text citations, and compiling a comprehensive bibliography in a standardised format, often drawing on resources from the Zayed University library or the National Archives.

## Public Speaking and Rhetorical Devices
This module refines the ability to deliver structured and persuasive oral presentations. Students learn to employ rhetorical devices, such as anaphora in a speech about national unity, or parallel structure when describing the rapid development of the UAE. They practice varying vocal tone, pace, and body language to engage an audience, often preparing to deliver a formal address on a topic like the achievements of Sheikh Zayed or the future of innovation in the UAE's diverse economy.
