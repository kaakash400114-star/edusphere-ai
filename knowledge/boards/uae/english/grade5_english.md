# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 5 English


## Parts of Speech and Sentence Structure
Students learn to identify eight parts of speech: nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, prepositions, conjunctions, and interjections. They practice classifying words in sentences from texts about the UAE, such as identifying 'desert' as a noun and 'beautifully' as an adverb. The curriculum focuses on constructing grammatically correct simple, compound, and complex sentences. Students learn to use coordinating conjunctions like 'and', 'but', and 'or' to join independent clauses, forming compound sentences to describe scenes from a souk or a camel trek.

## Reading Comprehension Strategies
Students develop skills to understand explicit and implicit meanings in texts. They learn to use strategies like predicting, questioning, and visualising to engage with stories and informational passages. For example, while reading about Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque, they predict the main idea and ask questions about its construction. Students learn to find main ideas, supporting details, and draw inferences. They practice summarising a paragraph about the UAE's desert climate in three to five sentences, identifying key facts like temperatures and rainfall.

## Vocabulary Development
Students expand their vocabulary through contextual analysis, word roots, and affixes. They learn to determine the meaning of unfamiliar words using clues within sentences and paragraphs. Lessons focus on thematic vocabulary related to the UAE, such as words for local currency (dirham), traditional markets (souk), and desert flora and fauna. Students are introduced to synonyms, antonyms, and homophones. They practice using new words in sentences, for instance, using the word 'mosaic' to describe the intricate designs on a building or in a piece of art.

## Writing for a Purpose
Students learn to write for different purposes and audiences, focusing on structure, tone, and style. They practice composing structured paragraphs with a clear topic sentence, supporting details, and a concluding sentence. For descriptive writing, they use sensory details to describe a scene at a souk, including sights, sounds, and smells. For narrative writing, they create short stories with a clear beginning, middle, and end, possibly using a setting in the UAE. Students also learn to write simple and formal letters, such as a thank-you note to a museum guide.

## Spelling and Punctuation Rules
Grade 5 students master spelling by applying rules for common consonant and vowel teams (such as 'ight' in 'light'), prefix meanings (e.g., 'un-' in 'unhappy'), suffix spellings (e.g., '-tion' in 'education'), and common irregular verbs like 'go/went/gone'. They apply these patterns to correctly spell UAE-specific vocabulary including 'Emirate', 'mosque', and 'Ramadan'. In punctuation, students learn to use commas to separate items in a series, as in 'The teacher showed us the flag, the map, and the book'. They correctly form contractions like 'don't' and possessives using apostrophes, such as 'The student's project'. Furthermore, they are expected to punctuate direct speech correctly, demonstrating the rule: 'The falconer warned, 'Hold the leash firmly.'

## Listening and Speaking Skills
Students develop active listening skills by following multi-step directions, identifying the main idea of a spoken message, and taking simple notes. They practice clear and expressive speaking skills through short presentations, oral reports, and group discussions. Topics are often linked to the UAE, such as describing a famous landmark like the Burj Khalifa or explaining a local custom. Students learn to participate in turn-taking conversations, ask relevant questions, and speak with confidence in front of their peers.
