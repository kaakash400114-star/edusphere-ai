# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 3 English

## Sentence Construction
Students learn to write compound sentences using coordinating conjunctions: 'and', 'but', 'or', 'so'. For example, 'My camel ran fast, but I couldn't catch it.' They practice writing declarative statements, questions, and commands. A declarative statement gives information: 'The falcon is a national symbol of the UAE.' A question asks: 'Where is the tallest building in the world?' A command tells someone to do something: 'Please take your shoes off before entering the mosque.'

## Spelling Patterns
Students learn spelling patterns for the long 'a' sound. The 'ai' pattern is used in words like 'rain', 'train', and 'sail'. The 'ay' pattern is used at the end of words, like 'day', 'play', and 'stay'. They learn common exceptions such as 'said'. For a UAE example, they would spell 'Dubai' correctly. These rules help students become more confident and accurate spellers when writing independently.

## Reading Comprehension
Students learn to identify the main idea and key supporting details in a text. The main idea is what the whole text is mostly about. Key details are the important pieces of information that explain the main idea. For a text about a souk, the main idea might be 'A souk is a busy market.' Key details could be 'You can buy spices and gold,' and 'People often bargain for the best price.'

## Nouns and Verbs
Students learn to differentiate between common nouns and proper nouns. A common noun names a general person, place, or thing (e.g., 'sheikh', 'mosque', 'car'). A proper noun names a specific person, place, or thing and always starts with a capital letter (e.g., 'Sheikh Zayed', 'Grand Mosque', 'Dubai'). They also identify action verbs (what something does, like 'run') and state verbs (what something is, like 'is').

## Writing a Description
Students write simple, descriptive paragraphs about their environment. They use sensory details to describe what they see, hear, and smell. A good topic is describing a desert scene: 'The sand is hot and golden. I can hear the wind blow past the dunes. At night, the sky is full of bright, twinkling stars.' This helps students learn to structure their writing and use vivid language to create a clear picture for the reader.

## UAE-Themed Vocabulary
Students expand their vocabulary with words related to their local environment. They learn the meaning of 'dirham' (the UAE currency) and use it in sentences, such as 'My father gave me 10 dirhams to buy juice.' They also learn words like 'falaj' (an ancient irrigation channel), 'wadi' (a dry riverbed), and 'souk' (a traditional marketplace). Understanding these terms connects their language learning to their cultural surroundings.
