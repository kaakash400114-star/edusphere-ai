# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 3 English


## Sentence Construction
Students learn to write compound sentences using coordinating conjunctions: 'and', 'but', 'or', 'so'. For example, 'My camel ran fast, but I couldn't catch it.' They practice writing declarative statements, questions, and commands. A declarative statement gives information: 'The falcon is a national symbol of the UAE.' A question asks: 'Where is the tallest building in the world?' A command tells someone to do something: 'Please take your shoes off before entering the mosque.'

## Spelling Patterns
Students are introduced to the primary spelling patterns for the long 'a' vowel sound. The 'ai' pattern is consistently used in the middle of words, as demonstrated in 'rain', 'train', and 'sail'. In contrast, the 'ay' pattern is used at the end of words, appearing in words such as 'day', 'play', and 'stay'. The curriculum also highlights common exception words that do not follow these patterns, like 'said'. Through this structured approach, students learn to apply these spelling rules, becoming more confident and accurate writers in various contexts.

## Reading Comprehension
Students learn to identify the main idea and key supporting details in a text. The main idea is what the whole text is mostly about. Key details are the important pieces of information that explain the main idea. For a text about a souk, the main idea might be 'A souk is a busy market.' Key details could be 'You can buy spices and gold,' and 'People often bargain for the best price.'

## Nouns and Verbs
Students learn to differentiate between common nouns naming general items like ‘car’, ‘desert’, and ‘student’ and proper nouns denoting specific entities such as ‘Emirates’, ‘Liwa’, and ‘Fatima’, which always require a capital letter. They further classify verbs into two distinct types: action verbs, which describe physical or mental activities (e.g., ‘write’, ‘think’, ‘build’), and state verbs, which express being, feeling, or possessing (e.g., ‘is’, ‘seem’, ‘have’). Pupils apply this knowledge by identifying these parts of speech within texts and constructing complete sentences that correctly use nouns and verbs. This foundational skill ensures clear and accurate communication by establishing the subject of a sentence and its corresponding action or state.

## Writing a Description
Students write simple, descriptive paragraphs about their environment. They use sensory details to describe what they see, hear, and smell. A good topic is describing a desert scene: 'The sand is hot and golden. I can hear the wind blow past the dunes. At night, the sky is full of bright, twinkling stars.' This helps students learn to structure their writing and use vivid language to create a clear picture for the reader.

## UAE-Themed Vocabulary
Students will expand their vocabulary by learning UAE-specific terms, focusing on both common and culturally significant words. They will develop a deep understanding of terms related to the UAE's geography, history, and economy, such as 'falaj' (traditional irrigation channels) and 'wadi' (dry riverbeds). Students will learn new nouns like 'dirham', the UAE currency, and 'souk', the traditional marketplace, to describe everyday life. They will demonstrate understanding by defining these words and using them in context-specific sentences. For example, a student might write, "I saw beautiful spices at the local souk with my mother," or "Water flows through the falaj to the farms." This thematic vocabulary approach reinforces language skills through authentic cultural application.
