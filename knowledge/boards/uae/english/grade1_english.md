# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 1 English

## Phonics and Letter Sounds
Children learn the 42 main letter sounds using the Jolly Phonics method, which uses an action for each sound. They practice saying the sound and doing the action simultaneously for 's' (a snake sound and movement), 'a' (open your mouth wide as if at the doctor), and 't' (a tennis swing motion). They begin blending these sounds to read simple consonant-vowel-consonant (CVC) words like 'cat', 'sun', and 'mat', focusing on the initial, middle, and final sounds in spoken words.

## High-Frequency Words
Students are introduced to a bank of 30 high-frequency, or 'tricky', words that cannot be easily decoded using phonics. They learn to instantly recognise these words by sight, such as 'the', 'I', and 'go'. Using flashcards, they play games to match the written word to its spoken form. For example, they will see the word 'she' and know it does not follow the 'sh-e' sound pattern but represents the single sound 'shee' immediately.

## Basic Sentence Construction
Children learn that a simple sentence must begin with a capital letter and end with a full stop. They practice constructing sentences using a subject (like 'The camel') and a verb (like 'walks'). For example, 'The camel walks slowly,' combining a subject, verb, and an adverb. They use pictures of UAE life, such as a man praying or a falcon in the sky, to create simple, factual sentences, reinforcing capitalisation and punctuation rules.

## Listening and Speaking Skills
Students develop the ability to listen for key details in short stories and poems. They practice answering 'who', 'what', and 'where' questions. For instance, after hearing a story about a boy buying dates at the souk, they can answer, 'Who bought the dates?' The boy. They also learn to speak in complete sentences, sharing their weekend experiences using sentence stems like, 'I went to the park with my family,' focusing on clear pronunciation and taking turns to talk.

## Reading Comprehension
Children learn to point to each word as they read to build one-to-one correspondence. They read short, decodable texts about familiar topics in the UAE, such as a story about a child visiting the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque. After reading, they demonstrate understanding by answering simple questions, for example, 'Where did the family go?' The mosque. They also identify the main character and the setting from the illustrations and text.

## Writing and Handwriting
Students begin to form lower-case and capital letters correctly, using a pre-cursive style that is smooth and flowing. They learn the correct starting point and direction for each letter, such as starting at the top for 'c' and 'o'. They practice writing their own name and simple high-frequency words, like 'in' and 'on'. They are encouraged to write about their own experiences, for instance, 'I like to eat mangoes,' linking their writing to their personal world.
