# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 11 English
## Advanced Literary Analysis and Critical Thinking
Students learn to analyze complex texts using formalist and post-colonial lenses. They examine themes, narrative structures, and authorial intent in works like *The Kite Runner*. Students practice identifying symbolism and archetypes, using textual evidence to support interpretations. For instance, they analyze the significance of the pomegranate tree as a symbol of friendship. They evaluate how historical and cultural contexts, such as pre-Taliban Afghanistan, shape the narrative. This fosters critical thinking and the ability to construct sophisticated, evidence-based arguments in essays.

## Argumentative and Persuasive Writing
Students master the structure and techniques of high-stakes argumentative writing for examinations. They learn to formulate a clear, debatable thesis statement and develop multi-paragraph essays using logical reasoning and rhetorical devices. A key method is the Toulmin model: claim, data/warrant, and qualifier. They write essays on local issues like sustainable tourism in the UAE, incorporating statistics and expert opinions. They also practice counter-argument and rebuttal to build a balanced and convincing case, adhering to formal academic conventions.

## Rhetorical Devices and Media Literacy
Students study advanced rhetorical devices, including anaphora, epistrophe, and synecdoche, to analyze and compose persuasive texts. They learn to identify and evaluate the techniques used in political speeches, advertisements, and news articles. For example, they analyze how Sheikh Zayed’s use of repetition in his speeches builds national unity. Students critically deconstruct media messages, distinguishing between fact and opinion and identifying bias. They examine local news reports on the economy to evaluate persuasive language, developing skills essential for informed civic participation.

## Academic Vocabulary and Disciplinary Terminology
Students expand their academic vocabulary to articulate complex ideas with precision. They study discipline-specific terms from fields like economics (e.g., GDP, inflation, dirham), environmental science (e.g., arid climate, desertification), and politics. Students practice incorporating these terms correctly into analytical and expository writing. They learn to define and differentiate between synonyms with subtle differences, such as 'critical' and 'analytical,' to enhance their academic expression. This foundational skill is crucial for success across subjects and in higher education.

## UAE Culture and Identity in Global Context
Students explore texts that reflect the UAE's heritage and its place in the modern world. They analyze themes of identity, tradition, and progress in Emirati literature and non-fiction. For example, they compare the traditional value of hospitality depicted in stories of the *majlis* with contemporary issues of globalization. They study how symbols like the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque represent national identity and spiritual values. Students engage with primary sources such as poems and essays to articulate the unique cultural narrative of the UAE within a global framework.

## Research and Information Literacy
Students learn to conduct advanced academic research using a variety of credible sources. They develop skills in formulating research questions, planning a research strategy, and evaluating sources for bias and authority. Students practice using both digital libraries and physical archives to gather information on topics related to the UAE, such as the history of souks or the impact of the desert climate on national development. They learn to synthesize information from multiple sources and properly cite them using a standardized format like MLA or APA, ensuring academic integrity.
