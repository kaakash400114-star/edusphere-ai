# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 2 English
## Phonics: Consonant Digraphs and Trigraphs
Children learn to identify and pronounce consonant digraphs, which are two letters that make one sound. They focus on 'ch' (as in 'chip'), 'sh' (as in 'ship'), 'th' (as in 'think'), and 'wh' (as in 'what'). They also learn the trigraph 'tch' (as in 'match'). Students practice by reading simple words and sentences. For example, in a sentence about the desert, 'The snake slithered through the sand,' they identify the 'sh' sound in 'snake' and 'sand'.

## Vocabulary: Themed Word Lists
Students expand their vocabulary by learning themed word lists. A key theme is 'At the Souk.' They learn words like 'spices', 'dates', 'incense', 'perfume', 'scarf', and 'lantern'. Another theme is 'Our Weather', where words include 'sunny', 'hot', 'windy', 'sandstorm', and 'cloudy'. Children match these words to pictures and use them in simple sentences, such as 'I bought a scarf at the souk for fifty dirhams.'

## Grammar: Simple Past Tense
Children are introduced to the simple past tense to describe completed actions. They learn to add '-ed' to regular verbs (e.g., walk becomes walked, play becomes played). They practice forming sentences like 'She walked to the mosque' or 'We played in the park'. Students also learn about common irregular verbs such as 'go' (went) and 'see' (saw), using them in sentences like 'Yesterday, I saw a big falcon.'

## Reading: Comprehension of Short Stories
Students develop reading comprehension by answering simple questions about short stories. Stories are often based on UAE life, like a visit to the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque or a trip to the beach. They learn to identify the main character and the setting. For a story about a camel race, they answer questions such as 'Where was the race?' (the desert) and 'Who won?' (the fastest camel).

## Writing: Writing a Simple Paragraph
Children learn to write a simple, coherent paragraph on a given topic. They are taught to structure their writing with a topic sentence, two or three supporting sentences, and a concluding sentence. For a topic like 'My Favourite Place in the UAE', they might write: 'My favourite place is the beach. The water is blue and the sand is soft. I like to build sandcastles. It is a fun place.'

## Speaking and Listening: Role-Playing in a Souk
Students practice speaking and listening skills through role-playing activities. One common scenario is acting out a visit to a souk. Children take on roles as customers and shopkeepers, using phrases like 'Hello, how much is this?' and 'That costs twenty dirhams, please.' This interactive play helps them build confidence, use new vocabulary, and practice polite conversation in a familiar, regional context.
