# UAE Curriculum (MOE) Grade 2 English

## Phonics: Consonant Digraphs and Trigraphs
Children learn to identify and pronounce consonant digraphs, which are two letters that make one sound. They focus on 'ch' (as in 'chip'), 'sh' (as in 'ship'), 'th' (as in 'think'), and 'wh' (as in 'what'). They also learn the trigraph 'tch' (as in 'match'). Students practice by reading simple words and sentences. For example, in a sentence about the desert, 'The snake slithered through the sand,' they identify the 'sh' sound in 'snake' and 'sand'.

## Vocabulary: Themed Word Lists
Students in Grade 2 expand their vocabulary by systematically learning themed word lists to build contextual understanding and descriptive language. A key method involves associating words with concrete images, for example linking 'spices,' 'dates,' and 'lantern' to visuals of a souk, and 'sunny,' 'windy,' and 'sandstorm' to pictures of weather. Children are explicitly taught to apply these new words in simple, structured sentences to demonstrate comprehension, such as writing 'The perfume at the souk smelled wonderful.' This structured approach ensures that vocabulary acquisition is meaningful and transferable to both written and spoken communication, grounding learning in familiar UAE contexts.

## Grammar: Simple Past Tense
In Grade 2 English, students learn to form the simple past tense for verbs. For regular verbs, they add the suffix '-ed' to the base word, as in 'walk' becomes 'walked' and 'play' becomes 'played'. These verbs are then used to form complete sentences about past events, such as 'We walked to school' or 'She played football'. They are also introduced to common irregular verbs, which do not follow the '-ed' rule; for instance, 'go' becomes 'went' and 'see' becomes 'saw'. Students practice these irregular verbs by constructing sentences like 'Yesterday, I went to the park' or 'He saw a tall building'. This foundation enables children to accurately describe actions that have already been completed.

## Reading: Comprehension of Short Stories
Students apply specific comprehension strategies to decode and understand the elements of short stories. They learn to identify key story components, such as the main character, the setting, and the sequence of events, by locating explicit details within the text. For instance, when reading a story about a falconry competition, students can pinpoint the main character as the young falconer and the setting as the mountains. They use context clues to answer factual questions like 'What did the falcon do?' by finding the answer 'It caught the rabbit.' Students also begin to infer simple character traits, such as determining that a character is 'brave' after reading about their actions during a desert journey.

## Writing: Writing a Simple Paragraph
Children learn to write a simple, coherent paragraph on a given topic. They are taught to structure their writing with a topic sentence, two or three supporting sentences, and a concluding sentence. For a topic like 'My Favourite Place in the UAE', they might write: 'My favourite place is the beach. The water is blue and the sand is soft. I like to build sandcastles. It is a fun place.'

## Speaking and Listening: Role-Playing in a Souk
Students will engage in structured role-playing scenarios to practice target speaking and listening skills within a UAE cultural context. The primary activity involves simulating interactions in a souk, with students alternating between the roles of customer and shopkeeper. Methodically, students will use a core set of functional phrases for greetings, inquiries, and transactions, such as "As-salamu alaykum," "How much is this, please?" and "That costs thirty dirhams." This requires them to actively listen for key information like prices and numbers to respond appropriately. This practical application reinforces proper turn-taking, eye contact, and the use of polite conversational formulas essential for effective communication.
