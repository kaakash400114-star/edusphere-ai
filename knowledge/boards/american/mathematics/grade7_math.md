# American Grade 7 Mathematics - Common Core

## Rational numbers operations

In seventh grade, you learn how to add, subtract, multiply, and divide rational numbers, which include fractions, decimals, and negative numbers. You use rules like keeping the same denominator when adding and subtracting fractions, and changing the sign when multiplying or dividing two negative numbers. For example, you might solve problems like -1/2 + 3/4 or -5 × (-2). You also practice converting between fractions and decimals to see how they are the same thing. Understanding these operations helps you solve real-world problems with money, measurements, and temperatures.

## Proportional relationships

In seventh grade, students learn that proportional relationships are when two quantities change at the same rate, meaning you can multiply one number by a constant to get the other. They use tables, graphs, and equations to spot these relationships, like figuring out how many apples you get for different amounts of money if each apple costs the same price. A key rule is that the ratios between the pairs of numbers must always be equal, such as 2 apples for $4 and 6 apples for $12, because 2/4 equals 6/12. Students also learn to write this relationship as an equation like y = kx, where k is the constant number that connects the two amounts.

## Percent problems

In seventh grade math, students learn to solve percent problems by understanding that percent means “per 100” and can be written as a fraction or decimal. They practice finding the part, the whole, or the percent in real-life situations, like calculating discounts or test scores, using the formula part = percent × whole. For example, if a shirt costs \$20 and is on sale for 25% off, students can find the discount amount by multiplying 0.25 by 20 to get \$5. They also learn to convert between percents, fractions, and decimals to make problem-solving easier. These skills help students understand how percents are used everyday, like when talking about tips or taxes.

## Expressions: expand-factor

In seventh grade math, students learn to expand expressions using the distributive property, which means multiplying a number outside parentheses by each term inside, like turning 3(x + 4) into 3x + 12. They also practice factoring expressions, which is the opposite—finding a common factor in terms and rewriting it as a product, like turning 6x + 9 into 3(2x + 3). Students use these skills to simplify expressions and solve problems by breaking them down into smaller, easier parts. By working with numbers and variables, they build a strong foundation for solving equations later on. These skills help them understand how numbers and letters work together in math problems.

## Multi-step equations

In seventh grade, you learn to solve equations that have more than one step, like 3x + 5 = 20, by using opposite operations to find the missing number. First, you subtract 5 from both sides to get 3x = 15, and then you divide both sides by 3 to find x = 5. These problems teach you to keep the equation balanced by doing the same thing to both sides every time. You also work with equations that have variables on both sides, like 2x + 7 = x + 10, by moving all the x terms to one side. By practicing these steps, you build a strong foundation for solving even harder math problems in the future.

## Inequalities

In grade 7, students learn to write and solve inequalities using symbols like < (less than) and > (greater than) to represent comparisons. They discover that solving inequalities is similar to solving equations, but when you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number, you must flip the inequality sign to keep the statement true. For example, turning -2x < 6 into x > -3 after dividing by -2. Students also learn to graph these solutions on a number line, using open circles for symbols that do not include the number and closed circles for symbols that do. This helps them visually understand all the possible values that make an inequality correct.

## Scale drawings

In seventh grade, students learn about scale drawings, which are pictures or maps that show real objects or places at a smaller or larger size but keep the same shape. They understand that a scale compares the lengths on the drawing to the actual lengths, like a scale of 1 inch to 10 feet meaning 1 inch on the drawing equals 10 feet in real life. Students practice using scales to find actual distances from a drawing, for example, if a playground map is drawn with a scale of 1 inch = 5 yards and the slide is 3 inches long, the real slide is 15 yards long. They also solve problems by creating scale drawings of objects or spaces, making sure all parts are the same size compared to the real thing. This helps them see how math is used in real life for building maps, houses, or toys.

## Angle relationships

In seventh grade, students learn about angle relationships by recognizing and using pairs of angles, like complementary and supplementary angles, which add up to 90 degrees and 180 degrees. They explore angles formed by parallel lines and a transversal, such as corresponding angles and alternate interior angles, which are equal in measure. Students practice solving problems by writing equations to find missing angle measures, like determining the value of an unknown angle in a triangle or a quadrilateral. For example, if two angles are complementary and one is 40 degrees, the other must be 50 degrees because 40 + 50 = 90. By understanding these rules, students can analyze geometric shapes and solve real-world problems involving angles.

## Area-volume of 3D shapes

In 7th grade, students learn to find the area and volume of three-dimensional shapes. They calculate the surface area by adding up the area of all the faces, like finding the total wrapping paper for a box. For volume, they multiply the length, width, and height to see how much space a shape holds, such as how many cubes fit inside a rectangular prism. They also explore cylinders, using formulas to find their surface area and volume, and compare how changing one side affects the whole shape.

## Random samples and probability

In seventh grade math, students learn about random samples and probability to understand how likely events are to happen. A random sample is a group chosen from a larger population where every member has an equal chance of being picked, like surveying 10 random students from the entire class to find the favorite color. Probability is shown as a fraction between 0 (impossible) and 1 (certain), such as the chance of flipping heads on a coin is ½. Students also practice comparing probabilities using words like "more likely" or "less likely" by analyzing real-life situations, like the chance of rolling a 6 versus a number greater than 2 on a die.
