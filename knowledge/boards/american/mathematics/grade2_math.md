# American Grade 2 Mathematics - Common Core

## Place value to 1,000

In Grade 2, children learn that place value helps us understand numbers up to 1,000. They explore how each digit in a number has a special value based on its place, like ones, tens, and hundreds. For example, in the number 342, the 3 is in the hundreds place, the 4 is in the tens place, and the 2 is in the ones place. Students practice breaking down numbers into hundreds, tens, and ones to see how they are built. This helps them read, write, and compare numbers more easily as they grow their math skills.

## Fluent add-subtract within 100

In this topic, children learn to add and subtract numbers within 100 using place value strategies, which means breaking numbers into tens and ones. They solve problems like 35 + 20 by adding the tens (3 tens + 2 tens = 5 tens) and keeping the ones (5 ones) to make 55. Students also use counting on or counting back to find answers, such as solving 50 - 3 by counting back three from 50 to get 47. They practice using mental math and writing equations to show their work, making sure they can explain how they got their answer. By the end, children solve word problems involving two-step addition and subtraction, like finding the total of 18 apples and 15 bananas, then subtracting 7 eaten pieces.

## Add-subtract within 1,000

In second grade, students learn to add and subtract numbers within 1,000, using strategies like breaking apart numbers and using place value. They practice adding two three-digit numbers by adding the ones, then the tens, and then the hundreds, often with regrouping, such as 456 + 278. Students also subtract three-digit numbers by subtracting the ones, tens, and hundreds, sometimes needing to regroup, like 512 - 345. They use concrete objects and drawings to make sense of these operations and build fluency. The goal is to understand the process of combining and taking apart numbers up to 1,000.

## Money: coins and dollar bills

In second grade, students learn to identify and count coins like pennies, nickels, dimes, and quarters, and know their values (1¢, 5¢, 10¢, 25¢). They practice counting groups of coins and dollar bills by adding the values together, such as finding the total of two dimes and three pennies. Students also learn to show different amounts of money in more than one way, like using 25¢ with a quarter or with two dimes and one nickel. They solve simple addition and subtraction word problems about money, such as figuring out how much money is left after buying a snack for 50¢. This helps them understand the basics of money and how to use it in everyday situations.

## Measurement: inches-feet

Second graders learn to measure length using both inches and feet. They understand that an inch is a small unit of measurement, like the length of a paperclip, and a foot is a larger unit, equal to 12 inches. Students use rulers marked with inches to measure shorter objects, such as their pencils or books. They also learn to measure longer objects, like a door or a table, by using feet and counting how many 12-inch segments fit into the item. This helps them choose the best tool and unit for measuring different things around them.

## Time to five minutes

In second grade, children learn to tell time to the nearest five minutes on both analog and digital clocks. They understand that the minute hand moves from one number to the next to show a five-minute pass, like when it points at 2, it’s 10 minutes past the hour. Students also practice reading times such as 3:15, where the hour hand is past the 3 and the minute hand is on the 3. This helps them connect the clock numbers to counting by fives, building a strong foundation for telling time accurately.

## Picture and bar graphs

In second grade, students learn to read and interpret picture graphs and bar graphs where each picture or square stands for one object. They practice creating their own graphs using data from simple surveys, like counting favorite fruits or types of pets. Students answer questions about the graphs by comparing how many items are in each category, such as finding which group has the most or least. They also learn to solve addition and subtraction problems using the information shown on the graphs. For example, they might add the number of students who like apples and oranges together.

## Shapes: sides, angles, faces

In second grade, students learn to name and describe two-dimensional shapes by counting their sides and angles. They identify triangles as shapes with three sides and three angles, and quadrilaterals as shapes with four sides, including squares and rectangles. Students also explore three-dimensional shapes, recognizing that they have faces, such as the flat square faces on a cube or the curved face of a cylinder. By comparing shapes, they notice that some shapes have equal sides and right angles, like squares, while others do not. This work helps them build a foundation for understanding geometry and the properties of shapes in the world around them.

## Arrays: equal groups

Students learn to arrange objects into equal rows and columns to form arrays, which helps them understand multiplication as repeated addition. For example, an array with 3 rows and 4 dots in each row shows 3 groups of 4, or 3 × 4. They also explore how arrays can be rotated, like turning a 2-by-5 array into a 5-by-2 array, to see that the total number of objects stays the same. By counting the objects in each row and column, students build a foundation for understanding the commutative property of multiplication. Concrete examples like arrays of stickers or tiles make these abstract concepts clear and engaging.
