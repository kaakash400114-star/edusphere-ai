# American Grade 10 Mathematics - Common Core

## Geometry: proofs-triangles

In Geometry proofs for triangles, you learn to show two triangles are the same (congruent) using rules like Side-Angle-Side (SAS) or Angle-Side-Angle (ASA). You also discover how to prove parts are equal with midsegments and the Triangle Sum Theorem, which says all angles in a triangle add up to 180 degrees. For example, if two sides and the angle between them match in two triangles, you can prove they’re congruent using SAS. You’ll practice writing step-by-step reasons to explain your thinking, using given information and postulates. These skills help you solve real-world problems, like showing why two window frames are the same shape and size.

## Congruence-similarity

In Grade 10, you learn to figure out if two shapes are congruent, meaning they are exactly the same size and shape, by checking if their corresponding sides and angles match up. You also study similarity, which means shapes have the same shape but not necessarily the same size, and you can find missing side lengths using ratios and proportions. For example, if one triangle has sides of 3, 4, and 5, and another has sides of 6, 8, and 10, they are similar because you can multiply the first set of sides by 2 to get the second. You’ll use transformations like translations, rotations, and reflections to see how they affect congruence and similarity in geometric figures.

## Right triangles-trigonometry

Students learn to use sine, cosine, and tangent to find missing sides and angles in right triangles. For example, if one angle is 30 degrees and the hypotenuse is 10, sine helps find the opposite side. They memorize SOH CAH TOA to remember which ratio to use: sine is opposite over hypotenuse, cosine is adjacent over hypotenuse, and tangent is opposite over adjacent. Students also explore special right triangles, like 45-45-90 and 30-60-90 triangles, which have predictable side ratios. This skill helps solve real-world problems, such as finding the height of a flagpole by measuring its shadow and the angle of the sun.

## Circles: arcs-angles

In 10th-grade math, you learn about arcs and angles in circles, discovering that the measure of a central angle equals the measure of its intercepted arc, like a 90° angle cutting off a 90° arc. You also explore inscribed angles, which are half the measure of their intercepted arcs, such as a 60° inscribed angle matching a 120° arc. Tangent lines form right angles with the radius at the point of tangency, and chords equidistant from the center are congruent. Finally, you use these rules to solve problems, like finding missing angle measures or arc lengths in real-world shapes.

## Area-perimeter of complex shapes

In 10th grade, you learn how to find the area and perimeter of complex shapes by breaking them into simpler rectangles, triangles, and circles. You add the areas of the simpler shapes together to find the total area, and for perimeter, you add the lengths of all the outer sides, being careful not to double-count shared edges. For example, you can find the area of an L-shaped figure by splitting it into two rectangles and adding their areas. You also use formulas like the area of a triangle (½ × base × height) and the area of a circle (π × radius²) for parts of the shape. This helps you solve real-world problems like finding the amount of paint needed to cover a wall with windows or the fencing needed for an irregular yard.

## Coordinate geometry proofs

In Grade 10, you will use the coordinate plane to prove geometric facts about shapes like triangles, quadrilaterals, and lines. You will learn to find the distance between two points with the formula d = √[(x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)²] and the midpoint with the formula M = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2). You will also use slopes to show that two lines are parallel (same slope) or perpendicular (negative reciprocal slopes). For example, you can prove a quadrilateral is a rectangle by showing its diagonals are the same length. These tools help you turn shape properties into algebra problems you can solve.

## Transformations

In Grade 10, students learn to move shapes around a grid using transformations like translations, rotations, reflections, and dilations. They figure out the rules for each one, such as sliding a shape left or right for a translation or flipping it over a line for a reflection. A dilation makes a shape bigger or smaller using a scale factor, like doubling its size. They also see how these changes affect a shape’s size, position, and orientation on a coordinate plane, practicing with examples like turning a triangle or stretching a rectangle.

## Solid geometry-volume-surface area

In Class 10, students learn how to find the volume and surface area of three-dimensional shapes like cylinders, cones, and spheres. They use formulas such as V = πr²h for the volume of a cylinder and V = (1/3)πr²h for the volume of a cone, while surface area involves calculating the area of curved and flat sides. For example, they might solve problems to find the amount of paint needed to cover a cylindrical tank or the space inside a spherical ball. Students also explore how changes in a shape’s dimensions affect its volume and surface area. These skills help them understand real-world applications, like building structures or measuring objects accurately.

## Probability

In grade 10, you will learn how to find the probability of events, which is a number from 0 to 1 that shows how likely something is to happen. You’ll use simple formulas like the probability of an event is the number of favorable outcomes divided by the total number of possible outcomes. For example, the probability of rolling a 3 on a standard die is 1 out of 6, or 1/6. You’ll also learn about independent events, where one event doesn’t affect another, like flipping a coin and rolling a die. Finally, you’ll explore compound events by using the rule that the probability of two independent events both happening is found by multiplying their individual probabilities.
