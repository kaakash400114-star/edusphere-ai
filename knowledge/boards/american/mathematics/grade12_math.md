# American Grade 12 Mathematics - Common Core

## Precalculus: functions-modeling

Students learn to interpret and build functions that model real-world situations, such as describing how the area of a square changes with its side length. They use linear, quadratic, exponential, and trigonometric functions to represent patterns and relationships, like calculating how a population grows over time or the height of a bouncing ball. Key concepts include identifying key features like intercepts and maximum values, and comparing different models to see which fits the data best, for example, deciding whether a linear or exponential equation better shows how savings increase with interest. Students also use graphs and tables to analyze and predict behavior, such as determining when two quantities will be equal.

## Trigonometry-advanced identities

In Grade 12, students deepen their understanding of trigonometric identities by proving and applying complex rules like the sum and difference formulas, such as sin(A ± B) = sin A cos B ± cos A sin B. They also learn to simplify expressions and solve equations using double-angle and half-angle identities, for example, rewriting sin(2x) as 2 sin x cos x to solve problems. Students explore how these identities help rewrite expressions in equivalent forms, making it easier to graph functions or find exact values, like using the Pythagorean identity sin²x + cos²x = 1 to rewrite tan²x + 1 as sec²x. These skills build a foundation for calculus by connecting trigonometric functions to their graphs and real-world applications like modeling periodic motion.

## Limits-preview to calculus

In Grade 12, students explore limits to understand how functions behave as they approach specific values or infinity, forming the foundation of calculus. They learn to find limits by direct substitution, factoring, and analyzing piecewise and rational functions, like finding the limit of (x² - 4)/(x - 2) as x approaches 2. They also interpret limits graphically, identifying holes, jumps, and asymptotes, which reveal whether a function is continuous. Students apply the limit definition to determine the slope of a tangent line, connecting limits to the concept of instantaneous rates of change. This work prepares them for defining derivatives and integrals in later calculus studies.

## Statistics-probability applications

In Grade 12, students explore advanced statistics and probability by analyzing complex data sets with measures of spread like standard deviation and interpreting margin of error. They build on their knowledge of probability by solving real-world problems with independent and dependent events, calculating conditional probabilities using formulas like P(A|B) = P(A and B) / P(B). Students also learn about probability distributions, including the binomial and normal distributions, to model and predict outcomes. For example, they might determine the likelihood of a certain number of successes in a series of trials or use the normal curve to find the percentage of data within specific standard deviations. Finally, they interpret results in context and make informed decisions based on statistical evidence.

## Financial math: interest-loans

In Grade 12 Financial Math, you will learn how to calculate simple and compound interest to understand how money grows over time. For loans, you’ll master formulas to find monthly payments, total interest paid, and the overall cost, using tools like the amortization schedule for a car loan or a mortgage. You’ll also explore how interest rates and loan terms affect the amount you pay back, comparing different financing options to make smart choices. Finally, you’ll apply these concepts to solve real-world problems, like determining the best payment plan for a large purchase or planning for future savings goals.

## Matrices-vectors

In grade 12 math, students learn how to multiply matrices by vectors and other matrices, following specific rules where the number of columns in the first matrix must match the number of rows in the second. They explore how to represent systems of linear equations as matrix equations like Ax = b, where A is a matrix of coefficients, x is a vector of variables, and b is a vector of constants. Students also discover the concept of the identity matrix, which acts like the number 1 in multiplication, leaving a matrix unchanged when multiplied. For example, multiplying a 2x2 matrix by a 2x2 identity matrix returns the original matrix. Finally, they learn to find the determinant of a 2x2 matrix to determine if it has an inverse, using the formula ad - bc for the matrix [[a, b], [c, d]].

## Discrete math: logic-set theory

In Grade 12, students explore the foundations of discrete math by learning about logic and set theory. They study logical connectives like "and," "or," and "not" to determine the truth value of compound statements, such as "If it rains, the ground is wet," and use truth tables to organize their findings. In set theory, they define sets, identify subsets, and perform operations like union and intersection, for example, finding all students in both the math club and the science club. Students also learn to analyze arguments using Venn diagrams to visually represent relationships between different groups of elements.

## Calculus preview: derivatives-integrals

In Grade 12, students explore calculus by learning the rules of derivatives, like the power rule to find the slope of curves, and how to use integrals to calculate the area under a curve. They practice finding derivatives for polynomial and exponential functions, such as determining how fast a car is accelerating from a position equation, and learn to compute definite integrals, like finding the area under a graph to solve real-world problems. Students understand that derivatives measure rates of change while integrals accumulate quantities, connecting these concepts to real-life applications like growth and accumulation. They also use the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus to link differentiation and integration, showing how these two processes are inverses of each other. This preview prepares students for deeper calculus concepts in higher education by focusing on problem-solving and understanding relationships between functions and their graphs.
