# American Grade 1 English Language Arts - Common Core

## Letter sounds A-Z

Students in first grade learn the letter sounds, or phonemes, for each letter from A to Z. They practice matching uppercase letters to their lowercase partners and blending sounds to form simple words. Children learn that letters work together to make new sounds, like /sh/ in "ship" and /ch/ in "chop". They identify the beginning sound of words, such as the /b/ sound in "ball" and the /k/ sound in "cat". This knowledge helps them read and write words by connecting letters to their sounds.

## Sight words

First-grade students learn to read and spell common sight words, which are high-frequency words that do not always follow regular spelling patterns. They memorize these words by sight to help them read more fluently and understand simple sentences, such as "I can see the dog" or "The cat is big." Students practice recognizing these words in books and everyday writing, helping them become faster and more confident readers. The list includes words like "the," "and," "is," "it," and "can," which are essential for building early reading skills. By mastering sight words, children can focus more on understanding the meaning of texts instead of sounding out each word.

## CVC words: cat-pin-sun

First graders learn to read and spell simple three-letter words called CVC words, which have a consonant, a vowel, and another consonant, like "cat," "pin," and "sun." They practice matching the letter sounds they know to the letters in these words to say the whole word, such as blending the /c/ sound, the /a/ sound, and the /t/ sound to read "cat." Students also learn to listen for the individual sounds in a spoken word and write down the correct letters to spell it, like hearing "sun" and writing s-u-n. This helps them understand how letters work together to make words.

## Naming words (nouns)

In first grade, students learn to identify and use naming words, or nouns, which are words for people, places, things, and animals. They practice sorting words into categories like person, place, or thing to understand that nouns name things they can see and talk about. For example, words like "teacher," "school," and "book" are all nouns. Students also learn that nouns can be singular, like "cat," or plural, like "cats." By using nouns in their speaking and writing, children build stronger sentences to share their ideas about the world.

## Action words (verbs)

In first grade, students learn to identify and use action words, or verbs, in their writing and speaking. Verbs tell us what someone or something is doing or being. Students practice finding verbs in sentences like "The cat runs" and "She jumps high." They also learn to use action words to make their own sentences, such as "I eat my lunch" and "They play outside." By using verbs, children can share who is doing an action and what that action is.

## Capitalization: names and sentence starts

First graders learn to capitalize the first letter of a sentence, like in "The cat sat." They also learn to capitalize the first letter of their own name and the names of people they know, such as their teacher or friends. Words like "I" are always capitalized when used alone. Students practice writing simple sentences correctly, making sure the first letter is uppercase and the rest are lowercase. For example, they write "Samanah plays ball" and "He is my friend." These rules help them understand how written language works and makes their writing easy to read.

## End punctuation: period-question mark

In first grade, you learn to use a period (.) to end a sentence that tells a statement or shares a fact, like "The cat is big." You also learn to use a question mark (?) to end a sentence that asks a question, like "Can you help me?" These marks are called end punctuation, and they help people understand when a sentence is finished. A sentence that tells something has a period, while a sentence that asks something has a question mark. By using these marks correctly, you make your writing easy to read for others.

## Rhyming words

Children learn to identify and produce rhyming words by listening for sounds that are the same at the end of words. A rhyme occurs when two words share the same ending sound, such as "cat" and "hat." Students practice this skill by playing games like matching pairs of rhyming words and generating new rhyming pairs, such as "sun" and "fun." They also begin to recognize that rhyming words often have the same spelling pattern for the ending sound. This helps build a strong foundation for phonological awareness and early reading skills.

## Opposites

First graders learn to identify and understand basic opposite pairs, which are words that have opposite meanings. They explore common antonyms like big/small, up/down, and hot/cold to describe the world around them. Students practice recognizing opposites in spoken words, pictures, and simple texts, building their vocabulary and descriptive language skills. This understanding helps them compare and contrast objects and ideas, a key skill for both reading comprehension and writing development. For example, they learn to describe a heavy box versus a light feather or a tall building versus a short tree.

## Speaking in complete sentences

First graders learn to express their thoughts and ideas by using complete sentences. A complete sentence has two main parts: a subject, which tells who or what the sentence is about, and a predicate, which tells what the subject does or is. They practice putting these parts together to make statements, such as "The cat sat." or "My friend is nice." They learn to speak in full sentences when sharing stories, asking questions, or giving information to others, helping everyone understand their ideas clearly.
