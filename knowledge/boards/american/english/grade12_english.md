# American Grade 12 English Language Arts - Common Core

## British-world literature analysis

In twelfth grade English Language Arts, students analyze British-world literature to explore complex themes, universal human experiences, and diverse cultural perspectives. They use close reading strategies to examine literary elements like symbolism, irony, and characterization in texts such as George Orwell's *1984* or Shakespeare's *Macbeth*. Students develop evidence-based arguments by crafting thesis statements and supporting them with specific textual examples from these works. They also evaluate how historical, social, and political contexts shape the meaning and impact of literature across British and global traditions. The goal is to build critical thinking and analytical skills while recognizing the interconnectedness of world literary traditions.

## Literary criticism approaches

Twelfth-grade students learn to apply distinct literary criticism approaches to deepen their text analysis. They study formalism, focusing on how elements like plot, structure, and literary devices create meaning in works such as Shakespeare's *Hamlet*. New Criticism teaches them to analyze the text in isolation, ignoring external context. Students also explore psychoanalytic criticism, interpreting characters’ motivations through Freudian concepts like the Oedipus complex, such as examining Victor Frankenstein’s psyche in *Frankenstein*. Additionally, they examine feminist and Marxist criticism, understanding how gender or class dynamics shape stories, like analyzing the societal pressures in *The Great Gatsby*. These frameworks provide diverse lenses to uncover and justify interpretations of literature.

## Advanced rhetoric-composition

In Grade 12, students learn to craft complex arguments by using advanced rhetorical strategies like logos, ethos, and pathos to persuade specific audiences. They master the formal research paper, which requires integrating multiple credible sources to support a clear thesis while properly citing them in MLA or APA format. Students analyze and emulate sophisticated argument structures from speeches and essays to develop their own persuasive, evidence-based writing. They also practice crafting concise, powerful language for rhetorical situations, such as writing a compelling op-ed or a logical policy proposal.

## Research-paper mastery: MLA format

In Grade 12 ELA, students master MLA format to create organized and credible research papers. They learn to format their paper with a one-inch margin, double-spaced text, and a header with your last name and page number in the top right corner. Each source used in the paper requires an in-text citation, like (Smith 45), placed right after the information that came from that source. Finally, students create a Works Cited page, listing each source alphabetically by the author’s last name, using proper punctuation and italics as shown in their handbook.

## Grammar-style polish

In Grade 12 English Language Arts, students master a sophisticated understanding of grammar-style polish to enhance the precision and professionalism of their writing. They learn to use advanced punctuation, such as semicolons and colons, to correctly link closely related independent clauses and to introduce clarifying lists or examples. This grade focuses on identifying and eliminating errors in subject-verb agreement, particularly with indefinite pronouns and compound subjects, and ensuring consistent verb tenses across complex sentences. Students practice applying these rules to refine their own work, for instance, by correcting a sentence like “The team of players are practicing their new plays” to the grammatically sound “The team of players is practicing its new plays.” This attention to detail elevates their writing for academic and real-world audiences.

## Creative writing workshop

In this creative writing workshop, twelfth-grade students develop advanced storytelling techniques by experimenting with complex narrative structures like nonlinear timelines and multiple perspectives, learning how to control pacing to build suspense or convey emotion. They practice crafting nuanced character development by creating backstories and internal conflicts, using dialogue to reveal personality and advance the plot without exposition. Students also explore the power of figurative language and vivid imagery, analyzing mentor texts to model how authors use symbolism and metaphor to deepen thematic meaning. Through peer review and revision, they apply grammatical conventions and stylistic choices to refine their work, ensuring clarity, cohesion, and a strong authorial voice in their original short stories or creative nonfiction pieces.

## Poetry-prose deep analysis

Students in Grade 12 learn to analyze how poets and authors use form, structure, and language to shape meaning and evoke emotion in complex texts. They explore how the specific choices in poetry, like line breaks, stanzas, and figurative language, create a unique impact compared to prose. This involves studying how prose techniques such as narrative perspective, character development, and plot structure are employed in both fiction and non-fiction to build themes. For example, students compare a Shakespearean sonnet’s volta to the emotional arc of a short story to understand how each form manages tension and resolution. The focus is on connecting formal elements to the overall artistic and communicative purpose of the work.

## Argument-debate mastery

Students in Grade 12 learn to construct and defend complex arguments using logical appeals, emotional appeals, and credible evidence. They master the structure of a formal debate, including opening statements, rebuttals, and closing arguments, while adhering to rules of respectful dialogue. Students also analyze an opponent's argument by identifying logical fallacies, such as ad hominem attacks or straw man misrepresentations, to strengthen their own position. A key skill involves synthesizing information from multiple reputable sources, like peer-reviewed articles and expert interviews, to build a well-supported claim on topics like environmental policy or digital privacy. This process culminates in demonstrating their ability to anticipate counterarguments and effectively refute them.

## College application essays

In Grade 12, students learn to write a college application essay that tells a personal story to highlight their strengths and experiences. They follow a clear structure, starting with a strong hook to grab the reader's attention, followed by body paragraphs that use specific examples from their life to show their growth, skills, or values. The essay must have a central message that connects their story to why they are a good fit for the college, ending with a memorable conclusion that summarizes their main points. Students focus on using a formal but authentic voice, avoiding slang and clichés, to create a polished piece that represents their best writing for an admissions committee.

## Professional communication

In 12th grade, students learn to craft and deliver professional communication, such as formal emails and business letters, using a clear structure with a subject line, greeting, body, and closing. They practice presenting logical arguments with evidence in formats like reports and proposals, making sure their ideas are organized and easy to follow. Students also develop skills for professional conversations, like interviews and meetings, by actively listening, asking clarifying questions, and responding respectfully. For example, they might write a letter to a local official about a community issue or participate in a mock job interview to demonstrate these skills. The goal is to prepare them for real-world communication in college and careers.
