# American Grade 10 English Language Arts - Common Core

## Literary analysis essays

In tenth-grade English, students learn to write literary analysis essays by stating clear claims about a text and supporting them with specific evidence from the story, poem, or play. They use transition words to connect their ideas and explain how the evidence proves their point, like showing how a character's actions reveal their personality. Students also learn to organize their essays with an introduction that presents their main idea, body paragraphs that each focus on one piece of evidence, and a conclusion that restates their argument. Finally, they practice analyzing the author’s craft, explaining how word choice or sentence structure affects the reader’s understanding of the theme.

## Theme-symbolism

In English class, students learn how themes are the big ideas or lessons in a story, like friendship or courage, and how symbolism uses objects or actions to represent those ideas. A red rose can symbolize love, or a long journey can symbolize a hard challenge. Readers look for clues, like a character’s repeated action or a special object that appears many times, to figure out what it stands for. By connecting these symbols to the story’s events and characters, students can better understand the author’s main message. This helps them see how writers use smaller parts to build a deeper, bigger meaning.

## Character development

In Grade 10 English, students analyze how characters evolve in stories by identifying their traits and motivations through their actions, dialogue, and internal thoughts. They learn to use textual evidence to support claims about a character's development, tracking changes over time to explain their growth or decline. For example, students might compare a character’s perspective at the beginning of a novel to their perspective at the end. Additionally, they explore how interactions with other characters and the plot influence a character's decisions, helping them understand the forces that shape a person. By the end of the unit, students can articulate how these elements contribute to a story’s theme.

## Rhetorical analysis

In English Language Arts, students learn to identify an author's purpose and main claim in a text. They analyze how authors use specific rhetorical strategies like pathos, ethos, and logos to build arguments and influence their audience. Students examine the structure of arguments, including the use of evidence and logical reasoning to support a claim. For example, they might study a speech and point out how personal stories appeal to emotions or expert opinions build credibility. Students also practice explaining how these techniques help the author achieve their goal.

## Grammar in context

Students analyze how grammar choices in writing shape meaning and style. They learn to use complex sentence structures, like those with subordinate clauses, to show relationships between ideas. For example, in the sentence, "Although the rain fell, the game continued," the subordinate clause "Although the rain fell" adds important background information. Students also practice correctly using commas with introductory phrases and independent clauses to improve clarity and flow. They explore how verb tenses shift to show time order in narratives, ensuring events are clear to readers.

## Sentence variety-syntax

In Grade 10, students learn to use different sentence structures to make their writing more interesting and clear. They practice combining simple sentences using conjunctions like “and,” “but,” and “so” to create compound sentences, such as “I wanted to go to the park, but it started to rain.” Students also learn to form complex sentences by adding dependent clauses, like “When the bell rang, the students quickly lined up.” They are taught to vary sentence length and structure to improve the flow and rhythm of their writing, for instance, by starting some sentences with prepositional phrases or adverbs. By mastering these techniques, students can better express their ideas and engage their readers.

## Evidence-based arguments

In Grade 10, students learn to build arguments by gathering strong, relevant evidence from credible sources like articles and research to support their claims. They practice structuring their writing with a clear claim, logical reasoning that connects the evidence to the claim, and counterarguments to address opposing views, creating a well-supported and convincing case. For example, an argument for longer recess periods would use research showing how physical activity improves focus and student behavior. Students are taught to evaluate evidence for bias, relevance, and reliability to ensure their arguments are factually sound and persuasive.

## Poetry analysis

Students will learn to analyze poems by identifying key elements like rhyme, rhythm, and figurative language, such as metaphors and similes, to understand the poet’s message. They will examine how stanzas and line breaks shape the poem’s structure and meaning, using examples to support their interpretations. Students will also compare and contrast different poetic forms, like sonnets and free verse, to recognize how style affects the reader’s experience. By exploring the speaker’s perspective and tone, learners will explain how word choices create mood and convey deeper themes. Finally, they will practice writing structured analyses that connect specific textual evidence to the poem’s overall purpose.

## Research-paper process

Students learn to research a topic by finding reliable sources, like books and websites, and taking notes to record important facts. They organize these notes into a five-paragraph structure: an introduction with a clear thesis statement, three body paragraphs with evidence and explanations, and a conclusion that restates the main idea. Students must cite their sources using a simple format, such as putting the author’s last name in parentheses, to show where their information came from. The final paper is written in a formal style to share their findings clearly and honestly.

## Presentation skills

In Grade 10 ELA, students learn to plan and deliver clear presentations by organizing ideas with a strong introduction, logical body, and memorable conclusion. They practice using a variety of visual aids, like slides or posters, to support their main points and engage the audience, ensuring the images are easy to see and directly related to their message. Students focus on speaking confidently with appropriate volume, pace, and eye contact, and are taught to anticipate questions and prepare thoughtful answers to strengthen their arguments. For example, after researching a topic such as environmental issues, a student might present a five-minute speech using key facts and one powerful image to persuade their peers. These skills help them communicate ideas effectively and build self-assurance for both academic and real-world situations.
