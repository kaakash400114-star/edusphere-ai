# American Grade 7 English Language Arts - Common Core

## Textual evidence-analysis

Students learn to find and share proof from a text to support their ideas. They practice reading closely to pick out important details like facts, quotes, and events that make their answers strong. For example, after reading a story, they can point to a sentence that shows a character is brave. Students also compare proof from different parts of a text to build a complete answer. This helps them explain their thinking using words from the story.

## Point of view-effect

In Grade 7, students learn how a story’s point of view shapes the narrative, including first-person (“I/we”) and third-person (“he/she/they”) perspectives. They examine how a narrator’s perspective influences the reader’s understanding of events and characters, such as noticing how a character’s feelings are more direct in first-person. Students identify whether the narrator is a participant in the story or an outsider, and how this affects the information shared. They also analyze how shifts in point of view can change the story’s meaning.

## Theme across genres

Students learn to find the main idea, or theme, of a story, poem, or play by asking what the author wants them to learn. They can find clues about the theme by looking at what happens in the story and how the characters feel and act. For example, the theme of a story might be about being brave, like a character who faces a fear, or about friendship, like characters who help each other. Students compare the theme of two different stories to see how it is the same or different. They use evidence from the text, like what a character says or does, to explain how they know the theme.

## Clauses: dependent-independent

Students learn to distinguish between independent clauses, which can stand alone as complete sentences, and dependent clauses, which need an independent clause to make sense. An independent clause has a subject and a verb and expresses a complete thought, like "The cat slept." A dependent clause begins with a subordinating word, such as "because" or "when," and cannot stand alone, like "because it was tired." Students practice combining these clauses to form complex sentences, such as "The cat slept because it was tired." They also learn to punctuate these correctly, using a comma before a dependent clause that starts a sentence.

## Simple-compound-complex sentences

In seventh grade, you learn to write and identify different kinds of sentences. A simple sentence has one subject and one verb, like "The dog barked." A compound sentence joins two simple sentences with a comma and a connecting word, such as "The dog barked, and the cat ran away." A complex sentence has one main part and a part that starts with a word like "when," "because," or "after," like "The cat ran away when the dog barked." You practice making your writing clearer and more exciting by using all three types of sentences.

## Commas with clauses

Seventh graders learn to use commas to separate two complete thoughts joined by words like and, but, or so. A complete thought has its own subject and verb, like "She finished her homework." When two of these thoughts are joined, a comma comes before the joining word: "She finished her homework, so she could play outside." They also use commas after words like yes, no, and well when they start a sentence. This helps readers understand the pauses between ideas.

## Figurative-connotative language

In seventh grade, students learn to identify and analyze figurative and connotative language to deepen their understanding of text. They study how metaphors compare two unlike things without using "like" or "as," such as "Her laughter is music," and how similes make comparisons using those words, like "He runs as fast as a cheetah." Connotative language refers to the feelings and ideas a word suggests beyond its dictionary definition, such as how "house" and "home" have different connotations. Students also explore how an author's word choice affects the meaning and tone of a text, identifying words with strong positive or negative connotations to understand the deeper message. By analyzing these elements, students gain insight into how language shapes and enhances communication.

## Argument essay

In Grade 7 English Language Arts, students learn to write argument essays to share their opinions and persuade others with reasons. A strong argument essay has a clear introduction with a claim, which is a statement of the writer's opinion, such as "School uniforms should be required for all students." The middle paragraphs must include reasons to support the claim and evidence like facts or examples to prove those reasons are true. Students must also explain how their evidence connects to their reasons and acknowledge that some people might disagree with their claim. Finally, the essay ends with a conclusion that restates the claim in new words and summarizes the main reasons.

## Narrative techniques

Students learn to identify and use key narrative techniques like dialogue, sensory details, and point of view to make stories more engaging. They practice using dialogue to show how characters talk and feel, and sensory details to describe what they see, hear, and smell. Seventh graders also explore how a story’s point of view, like first-person ("I") or third-person ("he/she"), changes how events are told. By analyzing texts, they recognize how authors build suspense and develop characters through these methods.

## Research process

In seventh grade, you will learn how to find good information for research using books, websites, and other sources. You must put information in your own words and tell where it came from to avoid copying. A good way to organize your research is by using facts and details to support main ideas, like writing about a historical event and its effects. You will also work to find answers to questions by reading carefully and checking that your sources are reliable. Finally, you will share your findings by writing a short report or making a presentation with clear facts and a strong topic.
