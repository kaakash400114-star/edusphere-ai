# American Grade 2 Science - NGSS/Common Core

## Plant and animal habitats

Plants and animals live in special homes called habitats that give them everything they need to survive. A habitat is a place where an animal or plant finds food, water, shelter, and space to live. Different animals live in different habitats; for example, frogs live in wet ponds and live in dry deserts. Plants also have habitats; for instance, cacti live in deserts and oak trees live in forests. If animals and plants cannot get what they need from a habitat, they will not be able to live there.

## How plants and animals survive

Plants and animals have special body parts to help them live in their habitats. Animals use their feet to walk, claws to climb, and beaks to find food. Plants use their roots to drink water from the soil and their leaves to catch sunlight to make food. A bird has wings to fly away from danger, and a cactus has thick skin to store water. These adaptations help living things survive, get what they need, and stay safe in their environments.

## Properties of materials

In second grade, students learn that different materials have properties that make them good for certain jobs. They can describe materials by how they look, feel, and sound, such as rough, smooth, hard, soft, loud, or quiet. They also learn that materials can be pushed, pulled, or twisted to change their shape, like molding clay or crumpling paper. Students observe how some materials, like wood and metal, sink in water while others, like plastic and cork, float. They discover that materials can be grouped by their properties, such as all the things that are shiny or all the things that bend.

## Changing matter: solids-liquids

Solids, liquids, and gases are the three main forms of matter, or stuff all around us. Solids keep their shape and feel hard, like a block or a toy car. Liquids can pour and take the shape of their container, just like water in a cup or juice in a bottle. We can see and touch these forms of matter every day in many ways, and even see things change, like ice melting into water.

## Weather patterns

Students learn to observe and describe daily weather using their five senses. They identify common weather conditions like sunny, cloudy, rainy, windy, and snowy, and match these to clothing choices like wearing a coat in cold weather. They recognize that the sun is a source of heat and light and can describe how weather changes from day to day, such as a clear sky becoming cloudy before it rains. Students also learn to use simple tools, such as a thermometer to measure air temperature, to gather information about the weather. Finally, they explore how weather affects plants and animals, like how rain helps flowers grow.

## Water on Earth

Water covers most of Earth’s surface and is found in oceans, lakes, and rivers. Some water falls from the sky as rain, snow, or sleet. Plants and animals need water to live, just like people do. Water can be a solid, like ice, or a liquid, like what we drink. The sun makes water from puddles and lakes go up into the air, and then it falls back down again.

## Forces: push and pull

Forces are pushes and pulls that can make things move, stop, or change shape. When you push a toy car, you make it go forward, and when you pull a wagon, you bring it closer to you. You can push or pull to open a door, lift a book, or squish a soft toy. Objects need a force to start moving, stop moving, or change how they are moving. Gravity is a pull that keeps things on the ground and makes apples fall from trees.

## Simple engineering design

Students explore simple engineering design by planning and building small solutions to everyday problems. They follow steps like asking a question, imagining ideas, planning with drawings, creating a prototype, and testing it to see if it works. For example, they might design a container to hold cotton balls or a bridge for a toy car to cross a gap. They use basic materials like paper, tape, and blocks and learn that designs can be changed to make them better. This helps students think like engineers by solving problems and building things step by step.
