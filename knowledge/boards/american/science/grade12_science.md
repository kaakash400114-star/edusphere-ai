# American Grade 12 Science - NGSS/Common Core

## Advanced physics: mechanics-electricity-magnetism

In Grade 12 physics, students learn Newton's laws of motion and how forces like gravity and friction affect objects, solving problems using equations like F=ma. They explore the principles of electricity, such as Ohm’s law (V=IR), and how circuits work with resistors and capacitors. Magnetism covers how magnetic fields are created by moving charges and how they interact with electric currents, seen in devices like electric motors. Students combine these concepts to understand how changing magnetic fields create electricity, known as electromagnetic induction, which is the basis for generators. The curriculum also includes the application of these principles to real-world technologies like power grids and communication systems.

## Advanced chemistry: organic-biochemistry

In Grade 12 Advanced Chemistry, students build on organic chemistry to explore biochemistry, learning the structure and function of large molecules essential for life. They will study how amino acids link to form proteins with unique shapes, and how simple sugars like glucose join to make carbohydrates. The class also examines how nucleic acids like DNA store and transmit genetic information through their sequence of bases. Students will see how enzymes act as biological catalysts, speeding up reactions by binding to specific molecules, and explore how lipids store energy and form cell membranes.

## Advanced biology: molecular-cellular

Students explore the molecular and cellular basis of life by examining the structure and function of DNA, including how its genetic code is replicated and translated into proteins. They learn about the central dogma of molecular biology, with a focus on transcription and translation, and investigate how mutations can alter protein function, such as in sickle cell anemia. The curriculum also covers the organelles of the eukaryotic cell, explaining how each contributes to overall cellular function, and how cells communicate through signal transduction pathways. Students analyze these processes using diagrams, models, and virtual lab simulations to reinforce key concepts.

## Anatomy-physiology deep dive

Students explore how the human body's systems work together to maintain life by studying the structure and function of organs, like the heart pumping blood, and the lungs exchanging oxygen and carbon dioxide. They learn about the nervous system’s role in sending signals throughout the body using neurons, and how the digestive system breaks down food into nutrients the body can use. Key concepts include homeostasis, where the body stays balanced—for example, by regulating temperature—and the importance of feedback mechanisms, like sweating when you get too hot. By comparing organ systems to teamwork, students understand how each part contributes to overall health and survival.

## Environmental science

In Environmental Science, students explore how human activities impact ecosystems and how we can live more sustainably. They learn about the cycling of nutrients like carbon and nitrogen through environments, observing how pollution from cars and factories disrupts these natural cycles. The study includes analyzing data on climate change, such as how rising temperatures affect polar ice and wildlife habitats. Students also evaluate different types of renewable energy, like solar and wind power, to understand how they reduce our reliance on fossil fuels. Finally, they explore the principles of conservation biology, discovering how protecting biodiversity helps maintain healthy ecosystems for all living things.

## Scientific research-methods

Students learn that scientific research starts with a testable question, like how fertilizer affects plant growth, and is guided by a hypothesis, which is a possible explanation. They explore different research methods, such as controlled experiments where one variable is changed while others stay the same to see the effect. Data is collected through observation or measurement, then organized using tools like tables and graphs to find patterns. Finally, students analyze this data to draw conclusions and communicate their findings, understanding that results must be repeatable and tested by others to be valid.

## Data analysis-statistics in science

In Class 12, students learn to analyze scientific data using key statistical concepts like mean, median, and mode to summarize information and identify central tendencies. They explore probability through simple experiments, such as flipping coins or rolling dice, to understand the likelihood of events occurring. Students also practice graphing data, like plotting temperature changes over time, to spot trends and relationships. By applying these methods, they can draw evidence-based conclusions from scientific observations. For example, they might compare test scores from different groups to see if a teaching method is effective.

## Engineering-design thinking

Students learn to apply engineering-design thinking to solve real-world problems by following a structured process that includes defining the problem, researching, brainstorming solutions, building a prototype, and testing and improving it. They use skills like creating detailed sketches and building simple models to test their ideas, such as designing a water filter to clean dirty water or a structure that can withstand a simulated earthquake. Students also learn to evaluate their designs using data from tests and make improvements based on feedback, similar to how engineers refine products before they are sold. This process helps them develop creative and practical solutions while learning to work collaboratively and think critically about challenges.

## Medical-health science foundations

In Grade 12 medical-health science, students explore the core concepts of human body systems and how they work together to maintain health, focusing on the structure and function of organs like the heart, lungs, and brain. They learn about the body’s defense mechanisms, including the immune response to pathogens like bacteria and viruses, and study how diseases can spread and be prevented. Students also examine the basics of genetics and heredity, understanding how traits are passed from parents to offspring and the role of DNA in carrying genetic information. Finally, they analyze how lifestyle choices—such as diet, exercise, and hygiene—affect physical and mental health, connecting scientific principles to real-world decisions.

## STEM career applications

In Grade 12 STEM career applications, students explore how scientific and engineering principles are applied in real-world professions, such as designing sustainable energy systems or developing medical devices. They analyze data using advanced statistical methods to solve problems in fields like environmental science or market research. Students also examine the engineering design process by creating prototypes for everyday products, considering factors like material selection and efficiency. Through case studies, they learn how computational tools model complex systems, from predicting weather patterns to optimizing traffic flow. This curriculum connects classroom learning to future careers, emphasizing innovation and problem-solving in technology-driven industries.
