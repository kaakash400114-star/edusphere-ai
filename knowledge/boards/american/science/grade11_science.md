# American Grade 11 Science - NGSS/Common Core

## Physics: motion-energy-momentum

In eleventh-grade physics, you will explore motion by learning to measure speed, velocity, and acceleration using formulas like speed = distance/time. You will discover how forces cause objects to move and stop, following Newton’s laws of motion, like a rocket launching because of a powerful push. Energy comes in forms such as kinetic (moving) and potential (stored), and it changes from one form to another, like a roller coaster converting potential energy at the top to kinetic energy speeding down. Momentum, which is mass times velocity, explains why it’s hard to stop a moving truck compared to a bicycle. You will see how collisions transfer momentum, like two bumper cars hitting and bouncing off each other.

## Electricity-magnetism-waves

Students explore the invisible forces of electricity and magnetism, learning how they are connected and can create each other. They discover that moving magnets can produce electricity and that electric currents can create magnetic fields, which is how generators and motors work. In waves, they study how energy travels through different materials, like sound moving through air or light traveling from the Sun. They use formulas to calculate how fast waves travel and how their frequency and wavelength are related, such as seeing how changing the pitch of a sound wave changes its sound.

## Chemistry: equilibrium-thermodynamics-acids-bases

Students learn how chemical reactions reach a balance point called equilibrium, where forward and reverse reactions happen at the same rate. They study thermodynamics to understand energy changes, such as heat flowing out of exothermic reactions and into endothermic reactions, like ice melting. The topic explores acids and bases, learning that acids donate protons (H+) while bases accept them, and using indicators like litmus paper to tell them apart by color change. Students write chemical equations to show how acids and bases react to form water and salt, and they investigate how concentration and temperature can shift a reaction's equilibrium point, affecting how much product is made.

## Organic-biochemistry

Students in grade 11 learn to identify the carbon-based compounds that make up all living things, focusing on the four main groups: carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids. They explore how these molecules are built from smaller units, like monosaccharides forming glucose, and how their specific shapes determine their functions in our bodies. For example, they will learn about amino acids linking to create proteins that build muscle and enzymes that speed up chemical reactions. Students also investigate the process of dehydration synthesis and hydrolysis, which builds and breaks down these large molecules. The curriculum connects these chemical structures to essential life processes, like how cells use energy from breaking down food.

## Biology: genetics-evolution

Students study how genetic information is passed from parents to offspring through DNA and how genes determine traits like eye color or height in humans. They explore the rules of inheritance, such as dominant and recessive alleles, using Punnett squares to predict the chances of traits appearing in offspring, like whether a child will have attached earlobes. The curriculum also connects genetics to evolution, explaining how changes in DNA create variation in a population over time, such as how bacteria can develop resistance to antibiotics through natural selection.

## Human anatomy-physiology

Students explore how the human body is organized from cells to organ systems. They learn the structure and function of major systems like the skeletal, muscular, and circulatory systems, understanding how bones provide support and movement, while the heart pumps blood. A key focus is on the digestive system and its role in breaking down food into energy for the body, which connects to the importance of healthy nutrition. Students also investigate the nervous system, identifying the brain as the control center and nerves as communication lines that send signals throughout the body to help you think, move, and feel.

## Ecology-environmental science

In eleventh-grade ecology, you will learn about energy flow through ecosystems, discovering how producers like plants capture sunlight and how energy is passed to consumers in a food chain. You will explore the key concept of carrying capacity, which is the largest number of individuals an environment can support with its available resources, such as the limited number of fish a pond can hold. The study also focuses on human impacts on the environment, including the causes and effects of pollution and how changes to one part of a food web can affect the entire ecosystem, like how removing a top predator can cause prey populations to grow too large. You will also identify factors that contribute to climate change, such as the burning of fossil fuels, and their effect on global weather patterns.

## Scientific method-research design

Students learn that the scientific method starts with asking a clear question, like "How does light affect plant growth?" Next, they form a hypothesis, which is a testable prediction such as "Plants will grow taller when placed near a window." Researchers then design an experiment by identifying variables, like keeping soil and water the same while only changing the light exposure. To collect reliable data, they measure plant height over several weeks and record results in a chart. Finally, they analyze the data to see if it supports their hypothesis and explain their findings, learning that repeating tests ensures results are not just a coincidence.
