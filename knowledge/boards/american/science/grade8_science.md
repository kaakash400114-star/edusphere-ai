# American Grade 8 Science - NGSS/Common Core

## Chemical reactions-conservation

Students learn that in a chemical reaction, atoms are never created or destroyed, they just get rearranged. This means the total mass of all the materials before the reaction is always the same as the total mass after. For example, when you burn a piece of wood, it looks like it disappears, but it actually turns into ash, smoke, and gases; if you could catch it all, it would weigh the same as the original wood. This rule is called the Law of Conservation of Mass. Scientists use chemical equations to show how atoms move from one substance to another, making sure the number of each type of atom is the same on both sides of the equation.

## Atoms-periodic table

Atoms are tiny building blocks that make up everything, like the wood in a desk or the air you breathe. Each atom is made of even smaller parts: protons, neutrons, and electrons. The periodic table is a chart that organizes all the different types of atoms, called elements, by the number of protons they have. For example, all hydrogen atoms have one proton, and all oxygen atoms have eight protons. Scientists use the periodic table to see how elements are alike and different, helping us understand why some things are gases and others are solids.

## Cell division-genetics

In eighth grade science, you will learn that living things grow and repair themselves through a process called cell division, where one cell splits to make two new identical cells. You will explore how tiny structures inside cells called genes carry instructions that determine traits, such as eye color or plant height, and that these genes are passed down from parents to offspring. Understanding genetics helps you see why family members share similar features and how traits can be inherited through patterns like those seen in pea plants. You will also learn how cells follow specific rules to divide correctly to keep organisms healthy.

## Natural selection-evolution

Natural selection is a process where organisms with helpful traits are more likely to survive and have babies. For example, in a population of beetles, green beetles might be harder for birds to see on green leaves, so more green beetles live long enough to reproduce. Over many years, more baby beetles inherit the green color, and the beetle population becomes mostly green. This change happens because nature selects the traits that help an organism live in its environment. It is a key reason why living things change slowly over time, which is called evolution.

## Forces-motion equations

Students learn how to use Newton’s laws of motion to figure out how things move. They discover that force equals mass times acceleration, which means the harder you push a shopping cart, the faster it goes. Students also explore how friction slows things down, like how a soccer ball stops rolling on the grass. They learn that every action has an equal and opposite reaction, such as a rocket pushing hot air down to blast itself up into the sky. By solving simple problems, students practice using equations to predict how objects will speed up, slow down, or stay still.

## Energy: kinetic-potential

In eighth grade science, you learn about two main types of energy: kinetic energy, which is the energy of moving things like a rolling ball or a flying bird, and potential energy, which is stored energy waiting to be used, like a ball held high in the air or a stretched rubber band. You discover that energy can change from one form to another; for example, when the held ball is dropped, its potential energy changes into kinetic energy as it falls and speeds up. A simple rule is that the higher an object is or the faster it moves, the more energy it has. Students also explore real-world examples, like how a skateboarder at the top of a ramp has potential energy that turns into kinetic energy as they roll down.

## Waves-electromagnetic

In eighth grade, you will learn that waves move energy from one place to another, and light is an example of an electromagnetic wave that can travel through empty space. You will explore how waves have different properties like their height (amplitude), how close together they are (frequency), and their speed, which all affect what we see and hear. A key rule is that electromagnetic waves, including radio waves and X-rays, all travel at the same super-fast speed in a vacuum: the speed of light. You will also discover that different types of electromagnetic waves are part of a big spectrum, and we only see a small part of it as visible colors, like the red, yellow, and blue of a rainbow.

## Earth's history-human impact

Students in eighth grade investigate how Earth’s history is recorded in rock layers and fossils, understanding that older layers are usually found deeper in the ground. They learn that the extinction of dinosaurs was a major event in Earth’s past and explore how human activities today can leave lasting changes on the planet. Key concepts include analyzing how burning fossil fuels and cutting down forests have increased carbon dioxide in the atmosphere, which is causing temperatures to rise. Students connect these changes to impacts like melting glaciers and extreme weather, studying how they affect plants, animals, and people. They also examine how recycling and planting trees can help reduce human effects on the environment, seeing how individual actions can make a difference for Earth’s future.
