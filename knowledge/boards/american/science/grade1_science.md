# American Grade 1 Science - NGSS/Common Core

## Living vs non-living things

First-grade students learn to observe and classify objects as living or non-living by checking for key signs. A living thing grows, eats, reproduces, and moves on its own, like a dog that walks to find food or a plant that grows taller from a seed. A non-living thing does not grow, eat, or reproduce, such as a rock that stays the same size or a toy car that needs a person to move. Children learn that people, animals, and plants are all living, while things like chairs, pencils, and water are non-living. Understanding the difference helps students see how living things need things like food, water, and air to stay alive.

## Five senses and body parts

Students learn to identify and use their five senses to observe the world around them. They understand that eyes help us see, ears help us hear, noses help us smell, tongues help us taste, and skin helps us touch and feel. Children explore how different body parts, like hands and feet, are used for specific actions, such as grasping or walking. For example, they might use their sense of sight to spot a red ball and their sense of touch to describe its smooth surface. This topic builds foundational skills for making observations and connecting body parts to their functions.

## Animals and their babies

Students learn that many animals have babies and identify common parent and baby animal pairs. They recognize that baby animals often have the same names, such as a cat and a kitten or a dog and a puppy, and that some babies have special names like a calf for a baby cow. Children understand that animals and their babies may look similar but babies are usually smaller and cannot do everything grown-up animals can do. This topic helps young children build vocabulary and observe the natural connections between living things in the world around them.

## Plants: needs and parts

Plants need sunlight, water, and air to grow big and strong, just like people need food and water. A plant has three main parts: roots that drink water from the soil, a stem that holds the plant up and moves water to the leaves, and leaves that catch sunlight to make food. We can see these parts on plants like a sunflower, where the roots are under the ground, the thick stem holds the flower high, and the green leaves point to the sun. Every plant has these parts and needs these things to live and grow.

## Weather and seasons

Students learn to observe and describe different types of weather, such as sunny, cloudy, rainy, and snowy days. They explore how weather changes with the four seasons: spring, summer, fall, and winter, by noticing the temperature and clothes people wear. Children identify seasonal patterns, like falling leaves in autumn or blooming flowers in spring. They also learn about common weather tools, like thermometers to measure heat and rain gauges to collect water. Students connect these observations to their daily lives, such as wearing a jacket on a cold day or using an umbrella when it rains.

## Day and night sky

First graders observe the sun as a star that appears during the day, providing light and warmth, and disappears at night. They learn that the moon and stars become visible in the dark nighttime sky and describe them as bright points of light. Children explore how the sun’s position changes the length of shadows during the day. They also identify objects in the sky, such as the sun, moon, and clouds, and note that they are different in size, shape, and brightness. Students understand that day and night are caused by the Earth’s rotation on its axis.

## Taking care of Earth

Children learn that Earth is our home and needs our help to stay clean. They discover simple rules like not littering and throwing trash in the bin to keep land and water safe. Students explore how plants and animals need clean air, water, and soil to live, and that pollution can harm them. By recycling materials like paper and plastic, children understand how to reuse things instead of wasting them. They also learn about how conserving water by turning off the tap helps save Earth’s important resources.

## Sound, light and movement basics

Students explore how sound travels through different materials, like when they hear a bell ring through the air or feel it vibrate on a desk. They learn that light helps us see by traveling from a source, such as the sun or a lamp, to our eyes, creating shadows when objects block the path. Movement is described as a change in position, like a ball rolling across the floor or a student walking from one spot to another. By observing everyday examples, children identify sources of sound, light, and motion and describe how these phenomena interact in their world.
