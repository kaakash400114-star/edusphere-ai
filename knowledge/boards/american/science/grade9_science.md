# American Grade 9 Science - NGSS/Common Core

## Biology: cell structure-function

In ninth-grade biology, students learn that all living things are made of tiny units called cells, which have parts called organelles that do special jobs. The nucleus is the cell's control center, holding DNA, which acts like instructions for the whole organism. Other organelles, like mitochondria, act like power plants to make energy, while the cell wall and membrane protect the cell and let materials pass through. Students explore how plant cells have a rigid wall and chloroplasts for making food, while animal cells have flexible membranes and no cell walls.

## Cellular energy: photosynthesis-respiration

Cells use energy to live and grow, and they get this energy in two main ways. Through photosynthesis, plant cells use sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide to make their own food, called glucose, and release oxygen. Animals and plants also break down glucose in a process called cellular respiration to release the energy they need for activities, like running or thinking. Both processes happen in the cells, with photosynthesis using light to create food and respiration using food to release energy, keeping all living things alive and moving.

## Cell division-genetics-DNA

In 9th-grade Science, students learn about cell division by exploring mitosis, the process where one cell splits into two identical copies, and meiosis, which makes four unique cells for reproduction. They discover how genes, which are tiny sections of DNA, act as instructions for traits like eye color or height, with DNA shaped like a twisted ladder called a double helix. Genetics teaches students about inherited patterns through Punnett squares, which show possible traits from parents, such as whether a plant will have smooth or wrinkled seeds. Students also understand mutations, or changes in DNA, that can affect traits, like a cat’s fur color changing. This unit connects how cells pass on information to why living things look and behave differently.

## Evolution-natural selection

In Grade 9, students learn that evolution is a change in living things over many years, and natural selection is how those changes happen. Animals and plants have traits, like a giraffe’s long neck or a cactus’s spines, that help them survive in their environment. Organisms with helpful traits are more likely to live long enough to have babies and pass those traits on, so over time, more and more organisms in a group will have those helpful traits. For example, in a group of beetles, green beetles might be harder for birds to see on green leaves, so more green beetles survive and have more green baby beetles, making the whole group more green over time.

## Ecology-ecosystems

In grade 9, students learn that an ecosystem is a community of living things, like plants and animals, interacting with their non-living surroundings, such as soil and water. They explore how energy moves through a food chain, starting from the sun to producers like grass, then to consumers like rabbits and foxes. Students also study how matter, such as water and carbon, is recycled through processes like the water cycle and decomposition. They discover that changes, such as pollution or the loss of a species, can disrupt the balance of an entire ecosystem and affect all the living things within it.

## Human body systems

Students learn about the human body’s major systems, including the skeletal, muscular, circulatory, and respiratory systems, and how they work together to keep us alive. They study that the skeletal system provides support and protection, the muscular system allows movement by contracting, and the circulatory system transports blood, oxygen, and nutrients throughout the body using the heart as a pump. The respiratory system works with the circulatory system by taking in oxygen and removing carbon dioxide through the lungs. For example, when you run, your muscles need more oxygen, so your heart beats faster and you breathe quicker to supply it.

## Chemistry basics: matter-atoms-bonding

In ninth-grade chemistry, you will learn that all matter is made of tiny particles called atoms, which are the building blocks of everything around you. Atoms join together through chemical bonds, like the ionic bond between sodium and chlorine in table salt, or the covalent bonds that hold two hydrogen atoms to one oxygen atom in water. You will explore how atoms can rearrange to form new substances, such as when hydrogen and oxygen combine to make water, and understand that chemical reactions follow specific rules, like the law of conservation of mass, which means no atoms are ever lost or created. The structure of a molecule, like the simple H₂O shape, determines its properties, such as why water is liquid at room temperature.

## Earth science: plate tectonics-climate

Students learn how Earth’s giant plates move and shape our planet’s surface, creating mountains and volcanoes. These plate movements also affect the climate over long periods of time by changing ocean currents and land formations. For example, when plates collide, they can push up tall mountains that block cold air or create warm ocean currents that bring milder weather to nearby areas. Over millions of years, these slow shifts can turn cold places warm or dry places wet, showing how the ground and the air work together. Scientists study these connections to understand why Earth’s climate has changed in the past.
