# American Grade 4 Science - NGSS/Common Core

## Plant and animal structures

Plants and animals have different parts that help them survive. Plants have roots, stems, leaves, and flowers to get sunlight, water, and nutrients. Animals have parts like legs for movement, beaks for eating, and fur or scales for protection. For example, a bird uses its wings to fly and its sharp claws to catch food, while a tree uses its long roots to drink water from the soil. Each part has a special job to help the plant or animal live and grow in its environment.

## Energy: light-sound-heat-motion

Fourth graders learn that energy can move things, make light, produce sound, and create heat. They explore how pushing or pulling an object transfers motion energy, like kicking a ball. Students discover that sound is made when things vibrate, such as a guitar string shaking, and that heat energy flows from warmer things to cooler ones, like warm hands melting ice. They also recognize that light travels in straight lines and can be blocked to make shadows. Finally, they identify different sources of energy, including the Sun, batteries, and food, which power these changes.

## Rocks and fossils

Students learn to classify rocks as igneous, sedimentary, or metamorphic based on how they form, such as granite cooling from magma or sandstone pressing together from layers. They explore how fossils are formed when plants or animals get trapped in sediment and turn to rock over millions of years, like a shell imprint in limestone. By observing rocks and fossils, students discover clues about Earth’s past, understanding how layers of rock show the sequence of events. They also investigate how weathering and erosion break down rocks into smaller pieces, which can become part of new sedimentary rocks. Through hands-on activities, they practice sorting and observing these materials to understand their properties and origins.

## Weathering-erosion

Weathering is when rocks break down into smaller pieces, like ice cracking a rock or wind blowing sand on it. Erosion happens when these pieces are moved somewhere else by water, wind, or ice, like a river carrying pebbles down its stream. When the moving materials slow down and settle, it is called deposition, which can build up new landforms over time. For example, wind can carry sand dunes to a new spot where they stop. These processes happen very slowly, changing the shape of the Earth’s surface little by little, just like water can smooth a sharp stone over many years.

## Earth's changing surface

Students learn about slow and fast changes that shape Earth's surface, such as mountains wearing down and rocks breaking into soil. Weathering and erosion are key ideas, with examples like wind wearing down sand or water carving a river valley. Volcanic eruptions can build new land, while earthquakes crack the ground, showing forces that change the surface over time. These natural processes follow rules, like gravity pulling rocks downhill, and they can happen quickly, like landslides, or slowly, like a cave forming over many years. The study helps children see how landforms are created and changed by these powerful forces.

## Electricity and magnetism

Fourth graders learn that electricity is the flow of tiny bits called electrons, which power things like lights and toys. They discover that circuits need a complete path for electricity to flow, such as when a wire connects a battery to a lightbulb. Students explore how magnets have invisible forces that can push or pull objects, like making a paper clip stick to them or move without touching. They also learn about different types of circuits, including open circuits that stop electricity flow when a path is broken, like a switch turning off a lamp. Finally, children investigate how magnets work with certain metals, like iron, but not others, such as plastic.

## Waves information transfer

Waves carry information from one place to another, just like a friend whispering a message across the room. We learn that sound waves travel through air, water, or even a desk to reach our ears, like when a bell rings and you hear it from another room. Light waves travel very fast and let us see things, such as when the sun’s light reaches Earth during the day. Some objects block light, creating shadows, which is how we know light moves in straight lines. These waves help us understand how we hear sounds and see the world around us.

## Engineering solutions

Fourth graders learn to solve problems by designing and building simple models using everyday materials. They follow an engineering process: ask, imagine, plan, create, and improve, just like real engineers. Students test their designs, like a bridge made of straws or a boat from foil, to see if they work. They learn that engineers choose materials based on properties, such as using strong wood for a frame or light plastic for wings. By sharing and testing ideas, children discover that solutions can be better when we work together.
