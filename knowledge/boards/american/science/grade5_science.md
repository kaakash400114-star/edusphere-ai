# American Grade 5 Science - NGSS/Common Core

## Matter and its interactions

Matter is anything that takes up space and has weight, like water in a cup or a toy truck. All matter is made of tiny particles called atoms that cannot be seen with just our eyes. Atoms combine to form different kinds of matter, such as solid wood, liquid juice, or invisible air. When matter changes, like when water turns to ice in the freezer, it is still the same kind of material, just in a different form. Scientists study how matter behaves, like how sugar disappears in water because the sugar atoms mix in with the water atoms.

## Ecosystems: food webs

In fifth grade science, students learn about ecosystems by exploring food webs, which show how living things get energy. A food web is a connected set of food chains, and every link starts with the sun helping plants make their own food. Animals are consumers that eat plants or other animals to survive, like rabbits eating grass and snakes eating rabbits. Decomposers, such as worms and fungi, break down dead plants and animals, returning nutrients to the soil for new plants to grow. This cycle of energy shows how all parts of an ecosystem depend on each other to stay healthy.

## Earth's systems: spheres

In fifth grade, students learn about Earth’s four major spheres: the atmosphere (the air around us), the hydrosphere (all the water in oceans, lakes, and rivers), the lithosphere (the solid ground and rocks), and the biosphere (all living things). They explore how these spheres interact—for example, rain from the atmosphere falls into the hydrosphere, and plants from the biosphere grow in the lithosphere. These connections show how Earth is a system where parts work together. Students also study how events, like a volcanic eruption, can affect all four spheres. Understanding these interactions helps explain changes in weather, land, and life on our planet.

## Water cycle

Water moves between Earth’s surface and the sky in a continuous cycle called the water cycle. The sun heats up water in oceans, lakes, and rivers, turning it into an invisible gas called water vapor that rises into the air. As this vapor cools high in the atmosphere, it turns back into tiny water droplets or ice crystals, forming clouds. When the droplets get too heavy, they fall back to Earth as rain, snow, sleet, or hail. Some water soaks into the ground to become groundwater, while other water flows over land into rivers and eventually returns to the oceans, ready to start the cycle all over again.

## Space systems: stars-solar system

Students learn that stars are huge balls of glowing gas that make their own light, like our Sun. They explore the solar system as a group of planets, moons, and smaller objects all moving around the Sun in space. Children discover that Earth is the third planet from the Sun and is one of eight planets that orbit in a set path, with Mercury being the closest and Neptune the farthest. They also learn that some planets have moons that travel around them, like Earth has one moon, and Jupiter has many moons. These ideas help students understand the Sun is the center of our solar system and everything in it moves in a special way.

## Conservation of energy

Energy can never be created or destroyed, but it can change from one form to another. When you drop a ball, its potential energy turns into kinetic energy as it falls, and back into potential energy as it bounces. Some energy turns into heat or sound that you can’t always see, so not all the original energy is used in the bounce. We use energy for many things, like when you turn on a light, and it changes from electrical energy to light and heat energy. Scientists always count all the energy before and after something happens to prove it is saved.

## Human impact on environment

Humans can change the environment by building cities and farms, which can harm animal homes. We can also help the environment by recycling, reusing items, and planting trees to clean the air and give animals safe places to live. Littering and pollution are bad for nature because they hurt plants, animals, and even our drinking water. The Earth gives us resources like water and wood, so we must use them carefully to make sure they last for the future. Everyone can make a small difference by turning off lights and saving water to protect our planet.

## Design and problem solving

Students learn to solve problems by thinking like engineers, which means asking questions and trying different ideas. They design and build simple solutions, like a model bridge that holds paper clips or a container that keeps an ice cube from melting fast. Using the engineering design process, they plan, test, and improve their creations to work better. For example, they might build a paper airplane that flies the farthest by changing its shape. Students also learn to share their ideas and work together to find the best answers.
