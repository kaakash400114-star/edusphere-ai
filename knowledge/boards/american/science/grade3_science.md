# American Grade 3 Science - NGSS/Common Core

## Animal adaptations

Animals have special body parts to help them live in their homes. For example, a beaver has strong teeth to cut down trees, and a duck has webbed feet to swim in water. Animals also change how they act to stay safe, like a rabbit staying very still to hide from a fox. Some animals grow thick fur for cold winters, while others move to warmer places when it gets chilly. These special traits help animals get food, stay safe, and raise their babies.

## Life cycles: plants and animals

All living things grow and change through a life cycle. A plant starts as a seed, grows into a seedling, and becomes a big plant. Some plants, like beans, make new seeds to start the cycle again. An animal, like a frog, begins as an egg, becomes a tadpole with a tail, and grows into an adult frog. Different animals have different life cycles, but they all grow up from babies.

## Fossils

Fossils are remains of plants and animals that turned to rock long ago. They can be bones, leaves, or footprints that are very old. Scientists find fossils in the ground to learn about creatures that lived millions of years before us. For example, we have found fossils of giant dinosaurs like the Tyrannosaurus rex. These discoveries help us understand how animals looked and lived in the past.

## Weather and climate

Weather is what the air outside is like right now, like sunny, rainy, or windy. We can measure weather using tools, such as a thermometer to tell us how hot or cold it is. Climate is the weather pattern in a place over a long time, like a place being hot and rainy all summer. Weather changes from day to day and from place to place, but climate stays the same for many years. For example, a desert climate is always hot and dry, while a place by the ocean might have a cooler, wetter climate.

## Forces and interactions

Forces are pushes and pulls that make objects move or stop moving. When you push a friend on a swing, you are using a force to make them go higher. Gravity is a force that pulls things down, like when a ball falls to the ground. Magnets can pull some objects, like paper clips, even without touching them. Objects can change speed or direction when a bigger force pushes or pulls them.

## Magnets

Magnets push and pull objects without touching them, and they work best with things made of iron, like a paperclip. Each magnet has two sides, or poles, called north and south, and the same poles push away from each other while different poles pull together. Magnets can be found in everyday items, such as a refrigerator magnet holding up a drawing, and they can make a magnetized needle in a compass point to north. Magnets can also pick up other magnetic objects through materials like a piece of paper, but they do not work on things made of plastic or wood.

## States of matter

Everything around you is matter that can be a solid, a liquid, or a gas. Solids have a shape and stay in one place, like your desk or a block. Liquids flow and take the shape of their container, like water in a cup or juice in a bottle. Gases are all around you, they have no shape and spread out to fill a space, like the air you breathe. You can see matter change its state, like when ice melts into water or water turns into steam when it gets hot.

## Engineering design process

Third-grade students explore the engineering design process, a method for solving problems by creating new things. They learn to ask questions, imagine solutions, and make simple plans to build their ideas, like designing a bird feeder to help feed hungry birds. Next, they gather materials such as cardboard and string to build a model of their design. After testing it, students observe if it works and make small changes to make it better, such as adding more perches. This helps them understand that engineers often improve their ideas through testing and fixing, just like real scientists and engineers.
