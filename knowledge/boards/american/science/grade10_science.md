# American Grade 10 Science - NGSS/Common Core

## Chemistry: atomic structure-bonding

Students learn that atoms are tiny particles made of protons, neutrons, and electrons, with electrons moving in areas called energy levels around the nucleus. Atoms bond together by gaining, losing, or sharing electrons to form stable compounds like water (H₂O), where two hydrogen atoms share electrons with one oxygen atom. They discover that ionic bonds form when metals give electrons to nonmetals, creating salts like sodium chloride (NaCl), and covalent bonds form when nonmetals share electrons to make molecules like carbon dioxide (CO₂). Students also explore how the number of electrons in an atom’s outermost shell determines how it will bond, helping us understand the structure of everyday materials.

## Stoichiometry-chemical reactions

In 10th-grade science, students learn to balance chemical equations to see how many molecules or atoms are needed to start and finish a reaction. They use ratios from these equations to figure out how much stuff is needed or made, which is called stoichiometry. For example, in the reaction where hydrogen and oxygen make water, they learn it takes two hydrogen molecules and one oxygen molecule to make two water molecules. Students practice calculating amounts of reactants or products using these balanced equations and simple math. This helps them understand that chemical reactions always follow specific rules and can be measured exactly.

## Thermochemistry

In Grade 10 Science, students learn about thermochemistry, which is the study of heat energy moving in chemical reactions. They discover that some reactions absorb heat, like when you mix baking soda and vinegar, while others release heat, like a campfire burning wood. Students use specific formulas, such as q = m × c × ΔT, to calculate how much energy changes in these reactions. They also explore important ideas like endothermic and exothermic processes to understand why some reactions feel cold and others feel hot. This helps them see how energy is a key part of everything that happens in the world around us.

## Gases-kinetic theory

In tenth-grade science, you learn that gases are made of tiny particles that are always moving and bumping into each other. The kinetic theory of gases explains that these particles are spread far apart and have no attraction to one another, which is why gases can fill any container. You will also explore how changing the temperature, volume, or pressure of a gas affects its behavior using scientific formulas. For example, pumping more air into a tire increases the pressure because the particles are hitting the inside walls more often. This understanding helps explain real-world things like why a balloon expands when it is heated.

## Solutions-acids-bases

Students learn about solutions, acids, and bases by exploring how substances mix and their chemical properties. They discover that acids, like lemon juice or vinegar, taste sour and release hydrogen ions (H+), while bases, like baking soda or soap, taste bitter and release hydroxide ions (OH-). The pH scale measures how acidic or basic a solution is, with numbers below 7 being acidic and above 7 being basic. Students also learn about neutralization, when an acid and a base react to form water and a salt, like when antacid tablets neutralize stomach acid. This helps them understand real-world applications, from cleaning to digestion.

## Physics: motion-forces

In Grade 10 physics, students learn the rules of motion and how forces change an object's speed or direction. They study Newton’s Laws, like how objects stay still or move steadily unless a force pushes or pulls them. Students use formulas to calculate distance, speed, and acceleration, such as figuring out how fast a car travels in a certain time. They also explore types of forces, like gravity pulling a ball to the ground and friction slowing down a skateboard. Through these concepts, they understand how everyday movements, from a thrown ball to a moving bike, follow specific physical rules.

## Energy-momentum

In 10th-grade science, you learn about energy and momentum and how they are related. Momentum is how hard it is to stop something moving, like a fast soccer ball, and you find it by multiplying an object’s mass by its speed (p = mv). Energy, which is the ability to do work, can be stored as potential energy, like a ball held high, or used as kinetic energy, like the ball falling. The law of conservation of energy says energy can’t be created or destroyed, only changed from one form to another, and momentum is also always conserved in collisions. For example, when two bumper cars hit, their combined momentum stays the same before and after.

## Waves-electricity-magnetism

Students learn about waves, like sound and light, and how they can move through different materials. They also find out about electricity, including how circuits work with batteries, wires, and lightbulbs, and how objects can become charged with static electricity. Magnetism is explored, discovering what materials are magnetic and how magnets can push or pull each other without touching. Students see how electricity and magnetism are connected when moving magnets create electricity in a process used to make power. They also learn that the Earth has a magnetic field, which helps compasses point north.

## Earth-space science

In Earth-space science, you will explore our planet’s place in the universe by learning how the sun, moon, and Earth interact to cause seasons, eclipses, and the phases of the moon. You will study the structure of Earth, including its layers like the crust and mantle, and how plate movements create mountains, earthquakes, and volcanoes. Additionally, you will analyze weather patterns and climate, understanding how the sun’s energy drives wind and ocean currents that affect our weather every day.
