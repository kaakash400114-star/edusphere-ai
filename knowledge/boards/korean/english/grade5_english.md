# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 5 English

## Living in Seoul
Students learn to describe their city and its environment using specific vocabulary and location prepositions. They practice forming simple and compound sentences to explain where places are. For example, 'The library is next to the station,' and 'You can cross the street at the traffic light.' They use maps of their local area to ask and give directions to key landmarks such as the market, the school, or the hanbok museum. The lesson reinforces understanding of positional language like 'near,' 'behind,' and 'across from' in a real-world context.

## Planning a Weekend Trip
This topic focuses on expressing future plans using 'going to' and 'will.' Students learn to write simple itineraries for a short trip. A key lesson involves using time expressions such as 'first,' 'next,' and 'at 3 PM.' They practice phrases like, 'We are going to visit Gyeongbokgung Palace first,' and 'We will buy souvenirs at the Insadong market.' Activities include creating a weekend schedule in Seoul, incorporating specific events and times, and sharing their plans with the class using future-tense verbs correctly.

## Exchanging Money
Students learn functional language for a common transaction. The lesson teaches vocabulary related to currency, including 'won' and specific coin/bill denominations. They practice dialogues for exchanging money, such as, 'I would like to exchange 100 US dollars, please.' Students also learn to ask and understand simple questions like, 'What is the exchange rate?' The unit focuses on polite expressions and correct form for a bank or currency exchange, preparing students for real-life interactions while reinforcing numbers and polite requests.

## Healthy Eating Habits
This unit introduces modal verbs like 'should' and 'must' to give advice about diet and health. Students learn to differentiate between 'should' for recommendations and 'must' for strong necessity. They practice forming sentences such as, 'You should eat more vegetables,' and 'You must wash your hands before a meal.' The lesson uses the example of kimchi to discuss traditional, healthy foods and how to describe their benefits, encouraging students to give and receive health-related advice using correct grammar.

## Family and Traditions
Students expand their descriptive vocabulary to talk about family members, celebrations, and traditional clothing. They learn to use possessive adjectives and possessive pronouns, for example, 'My grandmother's favorite food is kimchi' and 'That's hers.' The lesson uses the concept of a 'Chuseok' family gathering to practice describing people, events, and objects. Students write short paragraphs about their own family traditions and learn to compare different customs using comparative adjectives like 'older' and 'more important.'

## Describing Past Events
This topic covers the simple past tense, focusing on regular and common irregular verbs. Students learn to form past tense sentences using correct spelling rules, such as adding '-ed' to regular verbs. They practice with everyday verbs like 'walked,' 'ate,' and 'saw' to describe activities on a past day, for example, 'I went to a market yesterday,' and 'She saw a beautiful hanbok.' The lesson involves reading a short story about a trip to Seoul and answering comprehension questions using the simple past tense, solidifying understanding of completed actions.
