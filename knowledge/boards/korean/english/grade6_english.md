# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 6 English

## Narrative Writing Structure
Students will learn to compose multi-paragraph narratives using a clear plot structure. They will master the five key elements: exposition to introduce characters and setting in Seoul, rising action with a central conflict (e.g., losing a family heirloom), a climax, falling action, and a resolution. The writing must include descriptive details about a hanbok or local market to create vivid imagery. They will apply specific transition words to sequence events logically and conclude with a reflective statement.

## Grammatical: Complex Sentence Formation
Grade 6 students will learn to construct complex sentences by joining an independent clause with one or more dependent clauses using subordinating conjunctions. A dependent clause, such as 'when the sun sets' or 'because it was fascinating', contains a subject and verb but cannot stand alone as a complete thought. These clauses can be placed at the beginning, middle, or end of the sentence to add specific details, reasons, or conditions. For example, 'Although the museum was crowded, we enjoyed seeing the exhibits' or 'We completed the project when our teacher gave us extra time'. Mastery of this structure allows for greater precision and elaboration in writing.

## Reading Comprehension: Analyzing Textual Evidence
Students will learn to construct well-supported arguments by analyzing a variety of texts to locate relevant evidence. They will practice distinguishing between explicit details, such as a character’s dialogue, and implicit information, like motivations inferred from actions. The method involves directly quoting or accurately paraphrasing textual evidence to substantiate claims about themes, character traits, or plot developments. For example, when reading a passage about saving won, students must locate specific sentences proving the protagonist's determination. This systematic approach to evidence-based reasoning is essential for developing critical analysis skills and excelling on assessments like the CSAT.

## Vocabulary: Idioms and Cultural Context
The curriculum requires students to understand and use common English idioms related to emotions and situations. They will learn the meaning and appropriate usage of phrases like "spill the beans" or "cost an arm and a leg." For example, "A trip to Seoul's famous theme park can cost an arm and a leg." This includes recognizing their figurative nature versus literal meanings to improve comprehension in authentic English texts and conversations.

## Oral Presentation: Structured Argument Delivery
Students will learn to construct a structured argument using a problem-solution-benefit format, beginning with a compelling introduction that states a debatable thesis. The main body must consist of three distinct claims, each substantiated by a concrete example such as the health benefits of kimchi, economic data, or historical precedent, followed by logical analysis. Presentations require a formal speech structure, incorporating clear transitions between sections and a persuasive conclusion that restates the thesis. Delivery techniques must emphasize vocal clarity, a measured pace, strategic pauses for emphasis, and consistent eye contact with the audience to demonstrate confidence and engage listeners effectively.

## Research Project: Synthesizing Information
Grade 6 students will conduct a short research project to gather and synthesize information from multiple sources. The topic will relate to South Korea, such as the history of the Korean won or the traditions of wearing a hanbok. Students will learn to identify relevant facts, organize their findings into a coherent report, and cite their sources. This foundational skill is crucial for building the advanced research abilities needed for senior high school studies and the CSAT.
