# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 3 English
## Basic Sentence Structure
Students learn to build complete sentences with a subject and a predicate. A subject tells who or what the sentence is about, like 'My friend' or 'The dog'. A predicate tells what the subject does or is, like 'plays soccer' or 'is cute'. Every sentence must have both parts to be complete. For example, 'Ji-hun runs fast' is a good sentence because it has the subject 'Ji-hun' and the predicate 'runs fast'. They practice identifying subjects and predicates in simple sentences about everyday life in South Korea, such as 'My mother cooks kimchi'.

## Nouns and Verbs
Students identify nouns as words that name a person, place, thing, or idea. Common nouns include 'school', 'book', and 'Seoul'. Proper nouns, like 'Seoul' or 'Kimchi', name specific people or places and start with a capital letter. Verbs are action words that tell what someone or something does. Examples are 'eat', 'study', and 'wear'. Students practice by finding nouns and verbs in sentences like 'I wear my hanbok to the festival' and 'Children fly kites in the park'.

## Reading and Comprehension
Students read short stories and passages about daily life in South Korea. They focus on finding the main idea, which is the most important thought in a text, and identifying key details. For instance, after reading about a trip to a market, they should be able to state the main idea, 'Buying snacks at a market', and list details like 'buying tteokbokki and banana milk'. This skill helps them understand what they are reading and prepares them for more complex texts later on.

## Writing Descriptive Sentences
Students learn to write descriptive sentences using adjectives to describe nouns. Adjectives answer questions like 'What kind?' or 'Which one?'. For example, 'It is a big, red kite' or 'The spicy kimchi smells delicious'. They practice adding at least two descriptive words to simple sentences. A student might change 'I saw a mountain' to 'I saw a tall, green mountain near Seoul'. This makes their writing more interesting and detailed for others to read.

## Spelling and Sight Words
Students master spelling common third-grade sight words correctly and understand their meaning. Sight words are words that are frequently used in English and often do not follow standard spelling rules. These include words like 'because', 'friend', 'they', and 'said'. They also practice spelling simple vocabulary related to their culture, such as 'won', 'kimchi', 'hanbok', and 'Seoul'. Weekly spelling tests are used to check their progress in accurately spelling these important words.

## Speaking and Listening Skills
Students practice clear communication by speaking in complete sentences and listening actively to others. They learn to take turns speaking in group discussions. In class, they might participate in a simple show-and-tell, describing an object like a 'won coin' or a 'small toy'. For listening, they follow simple, step-by-step oral instructions, for example, 'First, pick up your pencil. Next, draw a picture of your favorite food.' This builds their confidence and ability to interact effectively.
