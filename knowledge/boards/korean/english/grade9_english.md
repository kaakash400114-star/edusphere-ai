# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 9 English

## Literary Analysis Techniques
Students apply specific methods to analyze literary texts, such as tracing a character's development through direct and indirect characterization. They identify elements of plot structure using the five-act framework and use textual evidence to support their analysis. A key technique is identifying contrasting foil characters to highlight the protagonist's traits, and analyzing internal and external conflicts to reveal thematic concerns. Students formulate claims about a text's theme and substantiate them by finding and explaining relevant textual examples. This structured approach to analyzing character, plot, and theme is foundational for critical thinking and success in examinations.

## Argumentative Essay Writing
Students construct formal arguments using a clear thesis statement supported by logical reasoning and credible evidence. They master the Toulmin model (claim, grounds, warrant, backing), learning to address counter-arguments effectively. A standard structure includes introduction, three body paragraphs (each with a topic sentence and examples), and a conclusion. For instance, an essay on cultural preservation might use the *Seoul* Metropolitan Government's policies as a specific case study to reinforce the argument.

## Understanding Dialects and Registers
Students will apply the principles of diglossia to distinguish between formal and informal registers, analysing how context dictates language choice through exercises comparing academic texts with casual conversations. They will systematically identify regional dialects, such as Southern British English, and sociolects, like AAVE, by analysing phonological and lexical features. Furthermore, students learn to map technical registers by extracting domain-specific terminology from subjects like science. The methodology involves a comparative analysis of standard English versus non-standard variations to understand social implications. This is reinforced by evaluating vocabulary choices in texts, like discerning formal terms for an essay on hanbok versus informal language in a social media discussion.

## Reading Comprehension of Complex Texts
Students develop strategies for understanding advanced English texts, including identifying main ideas, supporting details, and authorial intent. They practice skimming for gist and scanning for specific information, learning to infer meaning from context. Students analyze sentence structure, including complex and compound-complex sentences, to grasp sophisticated arguments. A key exercise involves summarizing dense articles about topics like the South Korean economy, using data points such as the value of the *won*.

## Public Speaking and Presentation Skills
Students will master the standard speech formula of introduction, body, and conclusion to build a logical argument. They will apply methods like the AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action) framework to structure persuasive presentations effectively. Practical exercises include practicing rhetorical devices such as anaphora and parallelism to enhance emphasis and memorability. To ensure audience engagement, students will learn techniques for managing vocal pacing, strategic pauses, and appropriate eye contact. Assignments will require the development of a formal oral report on a global issue, such as climate change, with the integration of concrete local examples like Jeju Island’s sustainable agriculture initiatives to ground abstract concepts in a familiar context.

## Media Literacy and Critical Evaluation
Students will apply rhetorical analysis techniques to identify propaganda methods such as glittering generalities, bandwagon appeals, and name-calling within advertisements and political messages. They will learn to systematically evaluate source credibility by cross-referencing information with reputable outlets and examining author credentials for potential conflicts of interest. Lessons will focus on distinguishing objective statements from subjective opinions by looking for verifiable evidence versus emotional language. Students will practice deconstructing media arguments to spot formal fallacies, like the either-or fallacy, found in commentaries on national policy. A core method involves identifying the target audience and demographic demographics by analyzing imagery, diction, and placement in a promotional campaign for a local beverage.
