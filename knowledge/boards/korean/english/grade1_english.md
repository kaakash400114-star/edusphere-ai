# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 1 English
## Hello and Goodbye
Students learn to identify and use basic greetings and farewells. They practice saying "Hello" and "Hi" to introduce themselves. They learn to say "Goodbye" and "Bye-bye" when leaving. The teacher uses visual aids, such as pictures of classmates, to model these phrases. Students repeat after the teacher in a call-and-response activity. They practice shaking hands while saying "Hello" and waving while saying "Goodbye". These simple interactions are the foundation for polite social communication. Example dialogue: Student A says, "Hello, my name is Min-ju." Student B says, "Hello, Min-ju. I am Ji-hoon."

## My Body Parts
Students learn to name and point to basic body parts. They learn the words "head", "shoulders", "knees", "toes", "eyes", "ears", "mouth", and "nose". The teacher leads the class in songs like "Head, Shoulders, Knees and Toes" to reinforce learning. Students touch the correct body part as the teacher says the word. They practice by saying "This is my [nose]". This activity connects language with physical action. It helps build a child's vocabulary for self-expression. For example, a student can point to their mouth and say, "This is my mouth."

## Counting to Ten
Students learn to count from one to ten in English. They practice saying the numbers aloud with the teacher. They use flashcards, counting blocks, or their fingers to represent each number. The teacher shows a card with the number 3, says "three", and the students repeat. They then hold up three fingers. This method connects the spoken word with the numerical symbol and quantity. They practice counting objects in the classroom, like "one, two, three books". This is a key mathematical skill taught through English.

## Colors and Shapes
Students learn to identify common colors and shapes. They learn the words for red, blue, yellow, green, and circle, square, and triangle. The teacher holds up a red square and says, "This is a red square". Students repeat the phrase. They play a game where the teacher calls out a color or shape, and students find an object matching it. For example, if the teacher says "yellow", a student might point to a pencil. This builds vocabulary for describing the physical world around them.

## Family Members
Students learn to name immediate family members. They learn the words "mother", "father", "sister", and "brother". The teacher might bring in a family photo and say, "This is my family". Students then practice with their own drawings or photos. They learn to say, "This is my mother" while pointing. This lesson connects language to a child's personal life and social context. It helps them describe their own family, which is a common topic for early conversation.

## Favorite Foods
Students learn to name common foods. They learn words like "rice", "kimchi", "apple", and "banana". The teacher brings in pictures or realia of these items. When showing a picture of kimchi, the teacher says, "This is kimchi". Students repeat. They practice asking and answering "What do you like?". A student might say, "I like apples". This introduces basic food vocabulary. It also allows students to express simple personal preferences using English words.
