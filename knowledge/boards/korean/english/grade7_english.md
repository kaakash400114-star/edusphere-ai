# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 7 English
## Narrative Writing Structure
Students learn to compose engaging narratives using a clear five-part structure: introduction, rising action, climax, falling action, and conclusion. They practice developing complex characters and vivid settings. A key rule is to 'show, not tell,' using descriptive details to convey emotions instead of stating them directly. For example, instead of "He was scared," they write, "His hands trembled as he walked through the dark alley near Seoul's historic gates." This method helps build immersive stories with authentic Korean elements, such as a character preparing for a Chuseok festival.

## Parts of Speech and Sentence Composition
This section refines understanding of parts of speech: noun, verb, adjective, adverb, pronoun, preposition, conjunction, and interjection. Students learn to construct varied and sophisticated sentences, combining simple, compound, and complex structures. A key rule involves subject-verb agreement, ensuring the verb matches the number of the subject. For example, "The student studies hard" is correct, while "The student study hard" is incorrect. They practice with contextual examples like "My favorite food is kimchi," and "We won the competition."

## Reading Comprehension Strategies
Students master specific strategies to improve reading comprehension, including predicting, summarizing, questioning, and visualizing. They learn to identify the main idea and supporting details in a text. A core method is the SQ3R technique: Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review. For example, when reading a passage about the Gyeongbokgung Palace, they first survey headings, then question what the palace's purpose was, read to find the answer, and recite the key facts to ensure understanding.

## Literary Devices and Figurative Language
Students are introduced to key literary devices, including simile, metaphor, personification, and onomatopoeia. They learn to identify these devices in texts and use them in their own writing. A rule for metaphor is that it directly compares two unlike things without "like" or "as." For example, "Her voice was music to his ears" is a metaphor. They analyze examples in Korean literature, such as comparing a hanbok's elegance to a blooming flower, and the sound of market vendors as a symphony of daily life in Seoul.

## Paragraph Development and Coherence
Students focus on writing well-organized, coherent paragraphs. They learn the TS (Topic Sentence), CD (Concrete Detail), and CM (Commentary) structure to build strong arguments or descriptions. A key method is using transitions (e.g., however, therefore, for instance) to connect ideas smoothly. For example, a paragraph about Korean culture might begin with the topic sentence, "Traditional games are an important part of Korean heritage," followed by a detail about yutnori and an explanation of its significance.

## Grammatical Conventions and Punctuation
This section covers advanced grammar and punctuation rules, including the correct use of colons, semicolons, and commas for complex sentences. Students learn to avoid common errors like comma splices and sentence fragments. A key rule is using a semicolon to join two closely related independent clauses. For example, "The students prepared for their CSAT; they knew their future depended on this exam." They practice editing sentences related to daily life, ensuring correct punctuation in a sentence like "The price of a bowl of bibimbap is around 10,000 won."
