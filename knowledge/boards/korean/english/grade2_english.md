# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 2 English
## Hello and Goodbye
Students learn to use common greetings and farewells in everyday situations. They will practice saying "Hello" and "Good morning" to teachers and friends. They will also learn to say "Goodbye" and "See you later" when leaving. For example, a student can greet their classmate by saying, "Hello, Min-ju. How are you?" and respond with, "I am fine, thank you. Goodbye." These phrases are essential for polite social interaction in South Korea, where respect for elders is important. Students will practice using these phrases with their teacher and classmates.

## My Family
Students will learn to name family members and use simple possessive phrases. They will learn words like "mother," "father," "sister," and "brother." They will practice saying "This is my mother" or "That is my father." Students will use Korean names as examples, such as "This is my brother, Ji-hoon." They will also learn to ask and answer "Who is this?" with "This is my [family member]." This helps students describe their immediate family and understand different family structures through simple conversation and picture cards.

## Counting and Numbers
Students will practice counting from one to twenty in English. They will learn to recognize the number symbols (1-20) and their corresponding English words. They will use counting in real-life situations, such as counting "five apples" or "ten pencils." The lesson can incorporate familiar Korean objects, like counting "three won coins" or "two pieces of kimchi." Students will practice simple addition problems using numbers up to ten, for example, "Two books plus three books is five books," building a foundation for mathematical concepts in English.

## My Body
Students will learn to name basic parts of the body, such as head, eyes, nose, mouth, hands, and feet. They will practice pointing to these parts on themselves and others while saying the correct English word. A key classroom activity is the song "Head, Shoulders, Knees, and Toes." For example, a student will say, "I have two eyes and one nose." They will learn simple action verbs to go with the body parts, like "I can clap my hands" or "I stomp my feet," linking physical movement to language learning.

## My School
Students will learn vocabulary related to school items and people. They will name objects like a pencil, book, desk, and bag. They will also learn to identify people like a teacher, friend, and classmate. Students will practice using the phrase "This is a..." for objects, for example, "This is a pencil." They can use Korean contexts, like "This is a book about Seoul." They will learn to say "I have a bag" or "My friend has a book," helping them describe their own school environment and possessions.

## My Favourite Foods
Students will learn the names of common foods they eat. They will practice saying "I like..." or "I don't like..." to express preferences. For example, a student can say, "I like kimchi" or "I like rice." They will learn about popular Korean foods, such as "bibimbap" or "tteokbokki." Students will also practice asking their friends, "Do you like noodles?" and answering "Yes, I do" or "No, I don't." This topic helps students communicate about daily life and cultural foods in simple English sentences.
