# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 12 English


## Advanced Argumentation and Persuasive Rhetoric
Students learn to construct sophisticated logical arguments using inductive and deductive reasoning. They master persuasive appeals: logos, pathos, and ethos. Lessons analyze rhetorical strategies in renowned speeches, like Martin Luther King Jr.'s "I Have a Dream," to understand their impact. Students are required to write structured argumentative essays on contemporary issues relevant to South Korea, such as the economic balance between chaebols and small businesses, incorporating evidence and a clear, counter-argument rebuttal to meet CSAT depth requirements.

## Literary Analysis of Modern Global Narratives
Students will analyze modern global narratives by applying the formalist method, focusing on how plot, setting, and style create meaning, and the post-colonial lens to examine how texts challenge cultural power dynamics. For character analysis, students will employ the archetype concept to compare figures like the individualistic protagonist in an American novel with the sacrificial character in a Korean text such as Han Kang's *The Vegetarian*. Comparative analysis will contrast narrative structures, such as the linear plot of a Western narrative versus the cyclical forms common in Asian literature. Through textual evidence and evidence-based argumentation, students will articulate how stylistic choices reflect and shape cultural perspectives, ultimately identifying the authorial intent behind these constructed narratives.

## Academic and Expository Writing for University Readiness
Students master the conventions of academic writing, including constructing a clear, arguable thesis statement and employing organized, logical paragraph structures. They learn to synthesize information from a minimum of three credible sources, such as academic journals and government reports, using signal phrases for integration. Formal citation methods are strictly applied, requiring either APA or MLA formats for both in-text citations and a complete reference list. The culminating assignment requires composing a well-structured research paper, such as an analysis of Seoul’s urban planning or the global impact of K-pop, to demonstrate university readiness.

## Listening and Speaking: Debates and Seminars
Students master the rules of academic debate, including structure (introduction, argumentation, rebuttal, conclusion) and time management. In seminars, they employ methods such as Socratic questioning and the use of evidence-based reasoning to analyze complex topics, for instance, evaluating the ethical implications of artificial intelligence. Through formal debate on subjects like the effectiveness of Korea's renewable energy policy, they practice persuasive techniques, anticipate counterarguments, and formulate rebuttals using logical fallacy identification. Active listening is essential for summarizing opposing viewpoints accurately before constructing cogent responses, demonstrating the critical analysis and articulate communication skills necessary for higher education and professional environments.

## English in a Korean Context: Business and Culture
Students master specialized English terminology and structured communication protocols for the global Korean workplace, focusing on formal email composition, meeting participation techniques, and cross-cultural negotiation strategies. Core concepts include navigating hierarchical decision-making and employing strategies to maintain harmony and save face. The curriculum explicitly teaches the rules of Korean business etiquette, such as formal introductions and the correct use of honorifics, contextualized within international corporate scenarios. Students practice concrete applications by drafting formal contracts and developing comprehensive business proposals that integrate traditional Korean cultural elements, such as a new venture featuring kimchi or hanbok, to target international markets.

## Critical Media Consumption and Digital Literacy
This module teaches students to critically analyze and evaluate various forms of media, including news articles, advertisements, and online content. They learn to identify bias, propaganda, and logical fallacies. Students examine the impact of media on society through a Korean lens, discussing topics like the influence of the Korean Wave (Hallyu) or the portrayal of social issues in domestic and international news. They practice creating responsible digital content and developing a discerning eye for credible information.
