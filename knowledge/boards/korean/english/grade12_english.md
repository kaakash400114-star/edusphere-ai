# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 12 English

## Advanced Argumentation and Persuasive Rhetoric
Students learn to construct sophisticated logical arguments using inductive and deductive reasoning. They master persuasive appeals: logos, pathos, and ethos. Lessons analyze rhetorical strategies in renowned speeches, like Martin Luther King Jr.'s "I Have a Dream," to understand their impact. Students are required to write structured argumentative essays on contemporary issues relevant to South Korea, such as the economic balance between chaebols and small businesses, incorporating evidence and a clear, counter-argument rebuttal to meet CSAT depth requirements.

## Literary Analysis of Modern Global Narratives
This module focuses on analyzing complex literary themes, character archetypes, and narrative structures in modern novels and plays from various cultures. Students apply formalist and post-colonial critical lenses. Comparative analysis is key, for example, contrasting the individualism in an American novel with the collectivist themes often found in Korean literature, such as Han Kang's "The Vegetarian, to develop a nuanced understanding of cultural perspectives and authorial intent.

## Academic and Expository Writing for University Readiness
Students develop proficiency in writing formal academic essays, research papers, and expository texts. The curriculum emphasizes clear thesis statements, organized paragraph structure, and proper citation formats, including APA or MLA. They practice synthesizing information from multiple sources, such as academic journals and official reports, to write a well-researched paper on a topic like the urban development of Seoul or the global influence of K-pop, preparing them for tertiary education.

## Listening and Speaking: Debates and Seminars
This hones skills for formal academic discourse. Students learn to deliver structured presentations, articulate arguments clearly, and respond to questions with cogent reasoning. The primary activity is formal debate on complex social, political, or ethical topics, such as the merits of universal basic income or Korea's demographic challenges. They must use persuasive language, active listening, and respectful rebuttal techniques, demonstrating high-level proficiency expected for the CSAT and university interviews.

## English in a Korean Context: Business and Culture
Students learn specialized vocabulary and communication strategies for the globalized Korean workplace. This includes writing formal business emails, participating in meetings, and understanding intercultural communication nuances. Topics cover Korea's unique business etiquette, such as the hierarchical structure and the importance of saving face. Real-world examples include negotiating a contract with a foreign company or presenting a business plan for a new venture involving traditional elements like kimchi or hanbok.

## Critical Media Consumption and Digital Literacy
This module teaches students to critically analyze and evaluate various forms of media, including news articles, advertisements, and online content. They learn to identify bias, propaganda, and logical fallacies. Students examine the impact of media on society through a Korean lens, discussing topics like the influence of the Korean Wave (Hallyu) or the portrayal of social issues in domestic and international news. They practice creating responsible digital content and developing a discerning eye for credible information.
