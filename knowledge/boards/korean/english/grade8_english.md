# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 8 English

## Reading Comprehension of Informational Text
Students apply the SQ3R method—Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review—to systematically process complex informational texts, using annotation to identify key arguments. They explicitly distinguish between factual reporting and subjective opinion to analyze an author's intent and persuasive techniques, such as hyperbole or an appeal to authority. Learners practice constructing concise multi-paragraph summaries that isolate the central thesis and its essential supporting details, directly supported by textual evidence. This skill is further developed through drawing reasoned inferences about economic data or social phenomena from given reports. As a core application, students compare the rhetorical strategies used in competing articles about Korea’s economic policy to understand varied perspectives on national development.

## Grammatical Structures and Error Correction
Students master advanced grammatical concepts, including conditional sentences (Types 1, 2, 3), passive voice, and complex sentence structures with subordinate clauses. They analyze grammar rules in context and apply them in sentence correction exercises. A key focus is distinguishing between similar structures, such as 'although' versus 'despite.' Students practice editing texts for grammatical and syntactical errors, often using examples related to Korean culture, such as "Although kimchi is famous worldwide, its traditional preparation method is complex."

## Critical Writing and Argumentation
Students master the Toulmin model of argumentation by constructing essays with a clear claim, grounds of evidence, warrants, and rebuttals to counter-arguments, using a five-paragraph structure as a foundational framework. They learn to formulate explicit thesis statements, a crucial method for establishing a focused argumentative purpose. Students practice finding and integrating credible sources, such as academic articles or government reports, as concrete evidence to support logical reasoning and the application of rhetorical appeals (ethos, pathos, logos). For example, they might employ statistical data (logos) and expert testimony (ethos) in an essay arguing that educational funding should be prioritized for rural schools. Writing tasks require synthesising evidence to persuade readers, analyzing topics like the efficacy of limiting smartphone use for teenagers or the ethical implications of autonomous vehicles.

## Vocabulary Expansion in Context
Students expand academic and thematic vocabulary through word roots, prefixes, suffixes, and contextual analysis. They learn to deduce word meanings from surrounding sentences in advanced texts. Weekly vocabulary is thematically organized, such as terms related to technology or Korean history. Students practice using new words in original sentences and writing paragraphs that incorporate vocabulary correctly, for example, describing the intricate architecture of Seoul's palaces using words like 'ornate,' 'symmetrical,' and 'majestic.'

## Listening and Note-Taking Skills
Students will learn to identify key concepts, main arguments, and supporting details within formal lectures, news reports, and academic documentaries. They will master advanced note-taking systems, including the Cornell method for structured recording of notes, cues, and summaries, and mind-mapping for visually connecting ideas. Authentic listening exercises require active processing, such as capturing data from a report on South Korea's digital economy or noting arguments in a panel discussion on the cultural significance of the Chuseok holiday. The curriculum emphasizes distinguishing between fact and opinion while interpreting nuanced verbal and non-verbal cues in the audio materials. Finally, students practice synthesizing information from their notes to produce coherent oral or written summaries, demonstrating full comprehension.

## Oral Presentation and Debate
Students refine public speaking skills by preparing and delivering structured presentations on researched topics. They utilize visual aids, such as slideshows, effectively. In formal debates, students learn to argue both sides of an issue, present evidence, and rebut opposing viewpoints with logical reasoning. Topics often integrate Korean culture, for instance, debating the pros and cons of the current CSAT system or discussing the influence of K-pop on the global spread of Korean language and traditions.
