# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 7 Mathematics
## Introduction to Rational Numbers
Students learn to extend their number system to include rational numbers. They master the comparison of positive and negative fractions and decimals on the number line, understanding their order and density. The absolute value of a number is defined as its distance from zero. Students perform arithmetic operations (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division) with rational numbers, applying rules for signs. For example, calculating the temperature difference in Seoul from -5°C in the morning to 2°C in the afternoon requires subtraction of -5 from 2, resulting in 7°C.

## Algebraic Expressions and Equations
Students formalize their understanding of algebra by simplifying and evaluating expressions with variables and constants. They combine like terms using the distributive property and the rules of integer arithmetic. Students learn to solve one-variable linear equations, understanding the balance principle of equality. For instance, an equation representing the total cost of `n` hanbok at 150,000 won each, with a 20,000 won tax, would be solved as `150,000n + 20,000 = 470,000` to find the number of items purchased.

## Proportion and Rate
This topic focuses on proportional relationships and their application in real-world contexts. Students identify direct and inverse proportionality and set up and solve proportions. They use the concept of a constant of proportionality. For example, if 5 kg of rice costs 10,000 won, students can find the cost of 12 kg by solving the proportion `5/10,000 = 12/x`. This skill is crucial for understanding unit pricing and scaling recipes for making kimchi for a larger family gathering.

## Basic Geometric Figures and Properties
Students analyze the properties of two-dimensional and three-dimensional shapes. They learn the formal definitions and properties of angles, triangles, quadrilaterals, and circles. The Pythagorean theorem is introduced and applied to find missing side lengths in right-angled triangles. For example, to find the height of a building that casts a 30-meter shadow when the sun's angle creates a right triangle, students can apply the theorem if they know the distance from the top of the shadow to the top of the building.

## Probability and Statistics
Students are introduced to the fundamental concepts of probability as a measure of likelihood. They calculate the probability of single events and understand the concept of sample space. In statistics, they learn to distinguish between population and sample, and how to calculate and interpret the mean, median, and mode of a dataset. For instance, they might analyze survey data on students' favorite weekend activities in Seoul, calculating the mean number of visits to a specific district.

## Introduction to Functions
Students are introduced to the concept of a function as a special relationship where each input has exactly one output. They learn to represent functions using tables, graphs, and simple algebraic rules. Students identify the domain and range of a function. For example, a function could represent the conversion of US dollars to Korean won, such as `f(x) = 1,300x`, where `x` is the dollar amount and `f(x)` is the equivalent value in won. This lays the groundwork for future CSAT problems.
