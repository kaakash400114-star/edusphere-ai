# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 3 Mathematics

## Numbers and Operations
Students understand the structure of numbers up to 10,000. They learn to read, write, and compare four-digit numbers. The focus is on mastering vertical addition and subtraction with regrouping (carrying and borrowing). For example, students solve problems like 3,482 + 1,567 and 5,201 - 3,845. They also explore the concept of rounding numbers to the nearest ten or hundred to simplify calculations, which is useful for estimating the cost of items in a traditional market in Seoul.

## Multiplication and Division Concepts
Students begin learning the fundamentals of multiplication and division. They understand multiplication as repeated addition and division as equal sharing. They memorize multiplication tables up to 9x9 and solve simple word problems. A practical example is dividing a package of 36 kimchi equally among 9 family members. Students use arrays and grouping to visualize these operations, connecting them to real-life scenarios like distributing party favors for a friend's birthday.

## Geometry and Measurement
In geometry, students identify and classify two-dimensional and three-dimensional shapes, such as rectangles, prisms, and pyramids. They learn about lines of symmetry in shapes like a hanbok's design. For measurement, they measure length using meters and centimeters, volume using liters, and mass using grams and kilograms. A key activity involves calculating the perimeter of a rectangular schoolyard or measuring ingredients for a traditional Korean recipe, linking math to cultural practices.

## Fractions
Students are introduced to the concept of fractions. They learn to read and write simple fractions, such as 1/2, 1/3, and 1/4. They compare fractions with the same denominator and understand fractions as parts of a whole. For example, they might identify that 3/4 of a plate of tteok (rice cake) has been eaten. Students use visual aids like fraction circles to build a concrete understanding of how fractions represent equal parts of a single object or group.

## Problem Solving and Patterns
Students develop problem-solving skills by analyzing word problems with multiple steps. They use addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division to find solutions. Additionally, they explore and create number patterns and sequences, such as counting by 50s or 100s when calculating prices in Korean won. This includes identifying missing numbers in a pattern like 200, 400, __, 800, which helps them recognize relationships in numerical data.

## Practical Applications of Mathematics
Students apply mathematical concepts to everyday situations. They practice calculating change when purchasing items, such as a notebook for 1,500 won paid with a 5,000-won bill. They also learn to tell time to the minute and schedule activities, like planning a trip to Gyeongbokgung Palace. These activities reinforce the relevance of math in daily life, from managing a small budget to understanding timetables for public transportation in Seoul.
