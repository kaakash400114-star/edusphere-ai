# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 3 Mathematics


## Numbers and Operations
Students understand the structure of numbers up to 10,000. They learn to read, write, and compare four-digit numbers. The focus is on mastering vertical addition and subtraction with regrouping (carrying and borrowing). For example, students solve problems like 3,482 + 1,567 and 5,201 - 3,845. They also explore the concept of rounding numbers to the nearest ten or hundred to simplify calculations, which is useful for estimating the cost of items in a traditional market in Seoul.

## Multiplication and Division Concepts
Students are introduced to multiplication as repeated addition, such as calculating 4 groups of 7, and to division as equal sharing, for example, distributing 35 stickers equally among 5 friends. They learn that multiplication combines equal quantities, while division separates a total into equal parts, mastering related facts like 8 x 7 = 56 and 56 ÷ 8 = 7. Using arrays and grouping, they visualize these operations to understand the concepts, such as seeing 3 rows of 6 donuts to represent 3 x 6. Students memorize multiplication tables up to 9x9 and solve word problems like dividing 36 pieces of tteok among 9 family members. This practical approach solidifies their foundational understanding of inverse relationships and prepares them for multi-digit calculations.

## Geometry and Measurement
In geometry, students identify and classify two-dimensional and three-dimensional shapes, such as rectangles, prisms, and pyramids. They learn about lines of symmetry in shapes like a hanbok's design. For measurement, they measure length using meters and centimeters, volume using liters, and mass using grams and kilograms. A key activity involves calculating the perimeter of a rectangular schoolyard or measuring ingredients for a traditional Korean recipe, linking math to cultural practices.

## Fractions
Students are introduced to the concept of fractions. They learn to read and write simple fractions, such as 1/2, 1/3, and 1/4. They compare fractions with the same denominator and understand fractions as parts of a whole. For example, they might identify that 3/4 of a plate of tteok (rice cake) has been eaten. Students use visual aids like fraction circles to build a concrete understanding of how fractions represent equal parts of a single object or group.

## Problem Solving and Patterns
Students will apply a multi-step method to solve word problems, selecting operations from addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division as required. They will identify patterns and rules in sequences such as counting by fifties or hundreds to calculate amounts in Korean won. For example, to find the missing number in the pattern 200, 400, __, 800, students will determine the rule of adding 200 each time to find 600. This process involves recognizing relationships and using logical reasoning to continue or extend numerical patterns.

## Practical Applications of Mathematics
Third-grade students apply multiplication and division to solve practical word problems involving equal groups and fair sharing. They learn to calculate change by subtracting the total purchase price from the amount paid, for example, finding the correct change from a 10,000-won bill after buying items totaling 3,400 won. In measurement, they solve problems related to length and area using standard units, such as calculating the perimeter of a rectangular classroom. Students also practice telling and writing time to the minute and solve elapsed-time problems, like determining the duration of a class that starts at 9:05 a.m. and ends at 10:20 a.m. These applications reinforce the use of mathematical operations to reason through and solve real-world scenarios.
