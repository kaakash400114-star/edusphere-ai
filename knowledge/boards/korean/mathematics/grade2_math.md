# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 2 Mathematics

## Numbers up to 100
Students understand numbers up to 100 and can read, write, and order them. They learn the composition of numbers based on tens and ones, for example, 47 is 4 tens and 7 ones. Students practice comparing numbers using greater than (>), less than (<), and equal to (=) symbols. They apply this by comparing quantities, such as the number of students in different classes in Seoul. Using number lines, they can identify a number's position and find missing numbers in a sequence up to 100.

## Addition and Subtraction within 100
Students master addition and subtraction within 100 using various methods. They learn vertical calculation (column addition and subtraction), starting from the ones place and moving to the tens place. Students also use mental math strategies like making tens, for example, 38 + 5 can be thought of as 38 + 2 + 3 = 43. They solve one-step word problems involving real-life situations, such as calculating the total number of kimchi jars or the remaining won after buying a school bag.

## Multiplication Concepts
Students are introduced to multiplication as repeated addition. They understand that multiplying means adding the same number multiple times, for example, 3 x 4 is the same as 4 + 4 + 4. Students use concrete objects like blocks or drawing pictures to model multiplication problems. They learn to read and write multiplication expressions and to find the product. They connect multiplication to everyday examples, such as counting multiple pairs of hanbok sleeves for a school event.

## Basic Geometry and Measurement
Students identify and describe two-dimensional shapes like circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles. They learn about their properties, such as the number of sides and corners. In measurement, students measure length using non-standard units (like paper clips) and standard units (centimeters and meters). They compare the lengths of objects, such as a pencil and a ruler. They also measure capacity using containers and understand concepts like 'full' and 'half-full' in relation to cooking recipes.

## Time and Money
Students learn to tell time to the hour and half-hour using an analog clock. They identify the hour hand and minute hand and write the time in digital format. For money, students recognize and use Korean coins (₩10, ₩50, ₩100, ₩500) and banknotes (₩1,000, ₩5,000, ₩10,000). They practice adding and subtracting amounts of money to solve simple problems, such as calculating the total cost of two items or finding the change received after paying with ₩5,000.

## Data and Patterns
Students collect, organize, and represent simple data using tally charts and pictographs. They interpret the data to answer questions like "Which fruit is the most popular?" Students recognize, describe, and extend simple patterns, such as ABAB or ABCABC sequences, using shapes, colors, or numbers. They apply this skill to identify patterns in their surroundings, like the repeating pattern on a traditional Korean quilt or the sequence of traffic lights in Seoul.
