# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 4 Mathematics
## Understanding Large Numbers
Students learn to read, write, and compare numbers up to one hundred million (100,000,000). They practice decomposing these numbers into values such as ten millions, millions, hundred thousands, ten thousands, thousands, hundreds, tens, and ones. For example, 45,678,902 is read as "forty-five million, six hundred seventy-eight thousand, nine hundred two." They use number lines and place-value charts to understand the magnitude of numbers, connecting large numbers to real-world contexts like the population of Seoul or the value of large currency amounts in Korean won.

## Four Operations with Larger Numbers
Students master addition, subtraction, multiplication, and subtraction of numbers up to five digits. They learn the standard algorithms for column addition and subtraction, ensuring they carry over and borrow correctly. For multiplication, they use the standard algorithm to multiply a three-digit number by a two-digit number. A practical application involves calculating the total cost for several items, such as the price of multiple hanbok sets. They also solve word problems involving all four operations, applying their skills to real-life financial calculations with Korean currency.

## Fractions and Mixed Numbers
Students deepen their understanding of fractions, learning to find equivalent fractions and simplify them to their lowest terms. They compare and order fractions with the same or different denominators using benchmarks like one-half and by finding a common denominator. They are introduced to mixed numbers, learning to convert between improper fractions and mixed numbers. For example, they might calculate the fraction of a day spent on different activities, such as 1/4 of the day at school and 1/8 for studying for the future CSAT.

## Geometric Shapes and Properties
Students study the properties of various two-dimensional and three-dimensional shapes. They classify quadrilaterals like rectangles, squares, rhombuses, and trapezoids based on their properties, such as parallel sides and right angles. For three-dimensional figures, they focus on prisms and pyramids, identifying their faces, vertices, and edges. A common task involves drawing these shapes and analyzing real-world examples, such as identifying the shapes found in traditional Korean buildings or the geometric patterns in a hanbok's design.

## Measurement and Units
Students expand their knowledge of measurement by learning the metric units for length (kilometer, meter, centimeter), mass (kilogram, gram), and volume (liter, milliliter). They practice converting between larger and smaller units by multiplying or dividing by powers of ten. For instance, they convert 3,500 grams into kilograms (3.5 kg). They apply these skills in practical problems, such as calculating the total weight of ingredients needed to make a large batch of kimchi for a community event or determining the distance between two cities in Korea using a map scale.

## Data Handling and Simple Probability
Students learn to collect, organize, and interpret data using tables, bar graphs, and line graphs. They find the mean (average) of a set of numbers to understand central tendency. For example, they might calculate the average temperature in Seoul over a week. They are also introduced to basic probability concepts, expressing the likelihood of simple events, like drawing a specific type of card from a small set or predicting the chance of rain for a school picnic, using simple fractions (e.g., 1/4 chance).
