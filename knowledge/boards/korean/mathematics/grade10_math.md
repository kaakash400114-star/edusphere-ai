# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 10 Mathematics
## Understanding Functions
Students analyze various types of functions, focusing on quadratic, rational, and irrational functions. They master methods for determining the domain, range, and asymptotes. Key topics include finding the maximum and minimum values of quadratic functions using the vertex formula, $f(x) = a(x-h)^2 + k$, and understanding the properties of composite functions. Practical applications involve calculating profit models for businesses in Seoul or analyzing population growth trends within South Korea's provinces.

## Equations and Inequalities
This section covers solving complex equations and inequalities, including quadratic, fractional, and absolute value forms. Students learn to solve systems of equations involving two or three variables using methods like substitution and elimination. They apply these skills to real-world problems, such as determining the break-even point for a company selling traditional hanbok or calculating the exchange rate fluctuations between the Korean won and the US dollar.

## Sequences and Series
Students study arithmetic and geometric sequences, learning to find the nth term and the sum of the first n terms. The curriculum introduces the concept of mathematical induction as a proof technique. Examples involve calculating the cumulative number of tourists visiting Jeju Island over a period or modeling the depreciation value of a new luxury car in Seoul using a geometric sequence, reflecting practical financial planning in South Korea.

## Plane and Solid Geometry
This topic explores the properties of two- and three-dimensional figures, including circles, polygons, prisms, and pyramids. Students learn to calculate the area of composite shapes and the volume of complex solids. Key theorems, such as the Pythagorean theorem and the properties of similar triangles, are applied. A practical example involves calculating the surface area of a traditional kim fermentation jar or determining the optimal dimensions for a new building in Gangnam.

## Probability and Statistics
Students calculate the probability of single and compound events using rules of addition, multiplication, and conditional probability. They study probability distributions, including the binomial distribution. In statistics, they analyze data sets to find measures of central tendency and dispersion, and learn to represent data using histograms and box plots. An example is predicting the success rate of a new food product, like fusion kimchi, based on market surveys.

## Trigonometric Functions
This section covers the unit circle, trigonometric ratios, and graphs of sine, cosine, and tangent functions. Students learn to solve trigonometric equations and apply the Law of Sines and Cosines to solve triangles. Real-world applications include calculating the height of a Seoul skyscraper using angle of elevation or determining the optimal angle for a traditional Korean archery (Gungdo) target to ensure accuracy.
