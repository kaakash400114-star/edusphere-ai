# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 5 Mathematics
## Numbers and Operations
Students deepen their understanding of numbers up to 100 million, focusing on reading, writing, and comparing large numbers. They master the four operations (addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division) with multi-digit numbers, including three-digit divisors. Mental math strategies are emphasized for quick calculations, such as using decomposition. For example, students calculate the cost of buying 12 hanbok at 350,000 won each, using multiplication to find the total price of 4,200,000 won. They also solve word problems involving money, distances between cities like Seoul and Busan, and quantities of items like kimchi jars.

## Fractions and Decimals
This topic covers the concepts of equivalent fractions and simplification to their lowest terms. Students learn to add and subtract fractions with different denominators by finding a common denominator, which can be the least common multiple. They also practice converting between fractions and decimals, particularly for monetary values in Korean won. For instance, they convert 0.75 won into 3/4 of a won. Word problems involve sharing resources, such as dividing 5 kilograms of kimchi equally among four families, resulting in each family receiving 1.25 kilograms or 5/4 kilograms.

## Basic Geometry
Students explore the properties of two-dimensional and three-dimensional shapes. They learn to calculate the perimeter and area of rectangles and squares using formulas (Perimeter = 2 × (length + width), Area = length × width). For example, they find the area of a traditional Korean square mat (jok) with sides of 3 meters, which is 9 square meters. They also identify and draw different types of triangles and quadrilaterals. The concept of volume is introduced for cubes and rectangular prisms, using the formula Volume = length × width × height, and applied to real-world objects like a box of Korean snacks.

## Measurement
Students learn to measure and convert units of length (km, m, cm, mm), mass (kg, g), and volume (L, mL). They solve problems involving measurement, such as calculating the total distance of a trip around Seoul or the weight of ingredients for a large batch of kimchi. Time is also covered, including reading analog clocks and calculating durations. For instance, students determine the time it takes to travel from Seoul to Busan by high-speed rail, which takes approximately 2 hours and 15 minutes. They solve word problems involving the conversion of units, like converting 3,500 grams into 3.5 kilograms.

## Data and Probability
Students learn to collect, organize, and interpret data using tables, bar graphs, and line graphs. They practice calculating the mean (average) of a set of numbers, such as finding the average number of visitors to a Seoul landmark over a week. The concept of probability is introduced through simple experiments, like determining the chance of picking a red token from a bag containing tokens of different colors. Real-life examples include surveying classmates on their favorite Korean food or analyzing the number of daily customers at a local market.

## Patterns and Problem Solving
Students identify and extend number and geometric patterns, such as sequences involving addition, subtraction, or multiplication. They develop problem-solving strategies, including working backward and making tables, to solve complex word problems. For example, they might solve a problem about the number of jars needed to store a large batch of kimchi or the total cost of purchasing items in a traditional market. Emphasis is placed on explaining the reasoning behind solutions and applying logical thinking to real-world scenarios, like planning an event or sharing resources fairly.
