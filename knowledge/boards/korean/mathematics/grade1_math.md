# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 1 Mathematics
## Numbers and Operations
Grade 1 students learn to read, write, and order numbers from 1 to 100. They understand place value, identifying the tens and ones in a two-digit number. Students master addition and subtraction facts within 20, using methods like counting on, counting back, and making ten. They solve simple word problems involving everyday items, such as finding the total number of kimchi jars in a fridge or calculating the change in won after buying a snack. Mental math strategies are emphasized for speed and accuracy.

## Patterns and Relationships
Children explore, identify, and create repeating patterns using shapes, colors, and sounds. They learn to recognize and extend simple numerical patterns, like counting by twos, fives, or tens. Students sort and classify objects based on one or more attributes, such as size, color, or shape, for example sorting different types of fruit. This foundational work helps them understand relationships and make predictions, which is a key step in developing logical thinking for future problems like those on the CSAT.

## Measurement
Students learn to measure and compare the length, weight, and capacity of objects using non-standard units. They compare two objects directly to determine which is longer, heavier, or holds more, such as comparing the length of two hanbok sashes. They are introduced to standard units, learning to read the hour on an analog clock to the nearest hour and name the days of the week and months of the year in sequence. This connects to daily routines and time management.

## Geometry and Spatial Sense
Grade 1 students recognize, name, and describe two-dimensional shapes like circles, squares, triangles, and rectangles. They identify these shapes in their environment, such as finding circles in a traditional fan or rectangles in a door. Students also explore three-dimensional figures like cubes, spheres, and cylinders. They use positional words to describe the location of objects, understanding terms like 'above', 'below', 'next to', and 'inside', which are essential for following directions on a map of Seoul.

## Data Handling
Children learn to collect, organize, and represent data using simple pictographs and tally charts. They conduct class surveys, for instance, asking their favorite fruit from a selection of apples, bananas, and oranges. Students then interpret the data to answer questions like 'Which fruit is the most popular?' This early experience with charts and graphs builds the skills needed to analyze more complex data sets in higher grades, a component of the quantitative reasoning section of the CSAT.

## Problem Solving
Students develop their ability to solve problems using a variety of concrete materials like blocks, counters, and number lines. They approach one-step addition and subtraction word problems using a four-step process: understand, plan, solve, and check. For example, a problem might involve sharing 20 candies among four friends equally. Students are encouraged to explain their thinking and strategies, fostering communication and logical reasoning skills that are crucial for academic success.
