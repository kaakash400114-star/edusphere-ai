# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 8 Mathematics
## Rational Numbers and Their Operations
Students master operations with rational numbers, including positive and negative fractions and decimals. They learn to find the greatest common divisor (GCD) and least common multiple (LCM) of two integers to simplify complex calculations. Rules for adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing rational numbers are rigorously applied. For example, to divide -3/4 by 1/2, one multiplies by the reciprocal: -3/4 * 2/1 = -6/4, which simplifies to -3/2. Proficiency in these operations is essential for solving algebraic equations and is foundational for advanced mathematics.

## Linear Equations with One Variable
Students learn to formulate and solve linear equations in one variable, including equations with variables on both sides. They understand that a solution is a value that makes the equation true. For instance, in the equation 2x + 5 = 13, students subtract 5 from both sides to get 2x = 8, then divide by 2, finding x = 4. They also apply these skills to solve real-world problems, such as calculating the cost of multiple items or determining the time required to travel a certain distance at a given speed.

## Functions and Graphs
This topic introduces the concept of functions as a relationship where each input has exactly one output. Students learn to represent functions using tables, graphs, and algebraic rules, primarily focusing on linear functions of the form y = ax + b. They practice plotting points on a coordinate plane and graphing straight lines. For example, a function representing the total price (y) of kimchi jars at 5,000 won each is y = 5,000x. Students interpret the slope as the rate of change and the y-intercept as the initial value.

## Geometric Figures: Triangles and Quadrilaterals
Students explore the properties of various triangles and quadrilaterals. They learn the conditions for congruence, such as Side-Angle-Side (SAS) and Angle-Side-Angle (ASA), and apply them to prove that two shapes are identical. For quadrilaterals, they identify properties of parallelograms, rectangles, rhombi, and squares, noting that a square is a special type of both a rectangle and a rhombus. They solve for unknown angles and side lengths in these figures, using the Pythagorean theorem for right-angled triangles within these shapes.

## Probability and Statistics
Students learn the fundamental principles of probability, understanding that the probability of an event is a value between 0 and 1. They calculate the probability of simple events and the probability of compound events using "and" and "or." For example, the probability of drawing a red card from a standard deck is 26/52. In statistics, they are introduced to bivariate data, learning to construct and interpret scatter plots to identify patterns and relationships between two variables, such as temperature and ice cream sales in Seoul.

## Practical Application with Proportions and Rates
Students apply their understanding of ratios, rates, and proportional reasoning to solve a variety of real-world problems. They convert units of measurement, such as converting the speed of a KTX train from kilometers per hour to meters per second. They solve problems involving scale, like finding the actual dimensions of a Gyeongbokgung Palace model. Students also analyze rates in a financial context, for instance, calculating the simple interest on a 10 million won savings account at an annual rate of 2% over three years.
