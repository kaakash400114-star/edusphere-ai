# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 5 Science
## The Water Cycle and Water Conservation
Water on Earth constantly moves through a cycle called the water cycle. It begins when the sun heats water in oceans, lakes, and rivers, causing it to evaporate and become water vapor in the air. This vapor rises, cools, and condenses into tiny droplets that form clouds. When the droplets become too heavy, they fall back to Earth as precipitation, like rain or snow. This water then flows over land as runoff into rivers and oceans. It is very important for people in South Korea, especially in busy cities like Seoul, to save water. We can help by turning off the tap while brushing our teeth or by reusing water for plants.
## Forces and Motion
A force is any push or pull on an object that can change its motion. The strength of a force can be measured using a unit called a newton (N). Friction is a force that happens when two surfaces rub against each other, which can slow down a moving object. For example, when you slide a book across a desk, friction stops it. Gravity is a force that pulls objects toward the center of the Earth, making things fall down. Motion is a change in an object's position over time. An object's motion can be changed by forces, like kicking a soccer ball to make it roll across a field.
## The Properties of Matter
All things are made of matter, which has mass and takes up space. Matter can be found in three main states: solid, liquid, and gas. Solids, like a wooden block or a kimchi jar, have a fixed shape and volume. Liquids, like water or milk in a bottle, have a fixed volume but take the shape of their container. Gases, like the air we breathe, do not have a fixed shape or volume and will spread out to fill any space they are in. The temperature of a substance can cause it to change its state of matter, like when water freezes into ice or boils into steam.
## Electricity and Circuits
Electricity is a form of energy that flows through materials called conductors. An electric circuit is a complete path that electricity can travel along. It needs three main parts: a source of electricity, like a battery, wires to connect the parts, and a device that uses the electricity, such as a small light bulb. In a closed circuit, the electricity can flow and the light bulb will light up. In an open circuit, where there is a break in the path, the electricity stops and the light bulb goes out. This is why we need to switch lights on and off to control them.
## Plant Growth and Photosynthesis
Plants are living things that need certain things to grow. They need water from the soil, carbon dioxide gas from the air, and energy from sunlight to make their own food. This process is called photosynthesis. During photosynthesis, the plant uses the sun's energy to combine water and carbon dioxide to create sugar (food) and release oxygen into the air. The roots of the plant, like those of a ginseng plant, absorb water. The leaves, which are often green, capture sunlight. This process helps all living things by providing food for us and oxygen for us to breathe.
## The Solar System and Earth
Our solar system is made of the Sun and all the objects that orbit it. The Sun is a star at the center that provides light and heat to the planets. Earth is one of the eight planets that travel around the Sun. Earth takes about 365 days, or one year, to complete one orbit around the Sun. As Earth orbits, it also spins on its axis, which is why we have day and night. The Moon orbits around Earth. We can see the Moon change its shape throughout the month as it reflects sunlight. This system of planets, including Earth, shows how gravity holds everything together in space.
