# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 10 Science
## Quantum Physics and Atomic Structure
Students explore the development of atomic models, from Dalton's indivisible spheres to the quantum mechanical model. They learn that Rutherford's gold foil experiment revealed a dense nucleus, while Bohr's model explained hydrogen's line spectrum with quantized electron orbits. The Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle is introduced, stating that one cannot precisely know an electron's position and momentum simultaneously. Electron configuration in multi-electron atoms follows the Aufbau principle, Pauli exclusion principle, and Hund's rule. This is foundational for understanding chemical bonding and the behavior of elements, which is crucial for the CSAT.
## Energetics and Thermodynamics
This topic covers the laws of thermodynamics as they apply to chemical and physical processes. The first law, conservation of energy, states that the total energy of an isolated system is constant. Enthalpy (ΔH) quantifies heat absorbed or released at constant pressure. Students learn to calculate the enthalpy change using Hess's Law and standard enthalpy of formation (ΔHf°). The second law is introduced through the concept of entropy (ΔS), a measure of disorder. The spontaneity of a reaction is determined by the Gibbs free energy equation, ΔG = ΔH – TΔS, a key concept tested in the CSAT.
## Electrochemistry and Redox Reactions
Students understand redox (oxidation-reduction) reactions as the transfer of electrons between species. Oxidation is the loss of electrons, and reduction is the gain. They learn to balance redox equations using the ion-electron method. In galvanic cells, a spontaneous redox reaction generates electrical current, like those in batteries. The electromotive force (EMF or E°cell) determines cell voltage. Students calculate cell potentials using standard reduction potentials. Electrolysis uses electrical energy to drive a non-spontaneous reaction, important for processes like electroplating and aluminum production in industrial applications near Busan.
## Molecular Structure and Chemical Bonding
This section explains how atoms bond to form molecules through ionic, covalent, and metallic bonding. Ionic bonding involves the transfer of electrons, creating cations and anions, as seen in table salt (NaCl). Covalent bonding is the sharing of electrons; students study Lewis structures and VSEPR theory to predict molecular shapes like linear, trigonal planar, and tetrahedral. The polarity of covalent bonds is determined by electronegativity differences. Intermolecular forces (London dispersion, dipole-dipole, hydrogen bonding) are examined, explaining why substances like water (H₂O) have high boiling points, a property essential for kimchi fermentation in traditional pots.
## Rates of Chemical Reactions and Equilibrium
Students explore the factors affecting reaction rates: concentration, temperature, surface area, and catalysts. The rate law, Rate = k[A]^m[B]^n, mathematically expresses this relationship. The concept of chemical equilibrium is introduced as a dynamic state where forward and reverse reaction rates are equal. The equilibrium constant (K) quantifies the position of equilibrium. Le Chatelier’s Principle predicts how a system at equilibrium responds to changes in concentration, pressure, or temperature. This is vital for industrial processes, like optimizing the yield of fertilizers, and for understanding buffer systems in biological chemistry.
## Waves and Energy Transfer
Students analyze the properties of mechanical and electromagnetic waves. Key concepts include amplitude (related to energy), frequency, wavelength, and the wave equation v = fλ. The Doppler effect explains the change in frequency due to relative motion, such as the changing pitch of a Seoul subway train passing a station. The electromagnetic spectrum ranges from radio waves to gamma rays, with visible light being a small portion. Students learn about wave behaviors: reflection, refraction, diffraction, and interference. These principles are fundamental to technologies like fiber optics in communication networks and the design of medical imaging equipment used in major hospitals.
