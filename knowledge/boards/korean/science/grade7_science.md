# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 7 Science
## The Structure and Properties of Matter
Matter is composed of tiny, indestructible particles called atoms. Each element is defined by its unique atoms, which have a nucleus of protons and neutrons surrounded by electrons. The arrangement and bonding of these atoms create molecules and different states of matter: solid, liquid, and gas. The kinetic theory explains how particles behave differently in each state. For example, a block of ice has water molecules vibrating in a fixed lattice, while liquid water in a Seoul reservoir allows molecules to flow past one another, demonstrating the increased kinetic energy required to change state from solid to liquid.
## Chemical Bonds and Chemical Reactions
Atoms form chemical bonds to achieve stable electron configurations, typically by sharing, gaining, or losing electrons to create a full outer shell. This results in ionic bonds, where a positive ion like a sodium ion from kimchi ingredients is attracted to a negative ion like a chloride ion, or covalent bonds where atoms share electrons, as in the water molecules essential for making kimchi. A chemical reaction occurs when bonds between atoms are broken and new bonds are formed, often visible by a colour change, temperature change, or the production of a gas, like the bubbles seen in carbonated drinks popular in Korea.
## Force and Motion
Newton's first law of motion states that an object at rest stays at rest and an object in motion stays in motion with the same speed and in the same direction unless acted upon by an unbalanced force. This is known as inertia. Force is a push or a pull, measured in newtons (N). The motion of a bus travelling from Seoul to Incheon is an example; its engine provides a forward force, while friction and air resistance provide opposing forces. When these forces are balanced, the bus moves at a constant velocity, demonstrating this principle in a real-world context.
## Work and Energy
Energy is the capacity to do work, and work is done when a force causes an object to move. The formula for work is Work = Force × Distance. Energy exists in many forms, including kinetic energy (the energy of motion) and potential energy (stored energy). When you lift a traditional hanbok, you are doing work against gravity, storing gravitational potential energy. If you then let it fall, this potential energy converts into kinetic energy. This transformation between forms of energy is a fundamental principle, like how kinetic energy moves a bullet train.
## Electricity and Magnetism
Electricity is the flow of electric charge, which is often the movement of electrons. An electric current can be direct current (DC), like from a battery powering a device, or alternating current (AC), which is the standard for electricity supplied to homes and businesses in South Korea. A circuit requires a complete path for the current to flow. Magnetism is the force exerted by magnets, which have a north and south pole. Like poles repel, and opposite poles attract, a principle used in electric motors that power countless machines, from home appliances to the high-speed trains of the KTX.
## The Structure and Function of Living Organisms
Living organisms are made of cells, which are the basic units of life. Cells are organized into tissues, tissues into organs, and organs into organ systems that perform specific functions, such as the circulatory system transporting blood. Plants and animals have different types of cells to suit their functions; for instance, root cells in plants are adapted for absorbing water and minerals. Just as a currency system requires different denominations of won to function effectively, an organism requires various cell types and systems to maintain life, from the cells in a single grain of rice to the complex human body.
