# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 1 Science
## Our Five Senses
Grade 1 students learn about the five human senses: sight, hearing, touch, taste, and smell. They identify the sense organ for each: eyes see, ears hear, skin touches, tongue tastes, and nose smells. Children explore through play-based activities, such as using magnifying glasses to see small details or listening to sounds in the schoolyard. They use descriptive words like 'crunchy' for taste when trying foods like apples and 'sweet' for the taste of traditional Korean rice cakes. They learn how these senses help them understand the world around them.
## Properties of Everyday Objects
Students identify and classify objects based on observable properties. They learn basic properties like color (red, blue, yellow), shape (circle, square, triangle), and size (big, small). They use simple comparisons, such as "The rock is heavy, and the feather is light." Students sort objects into groups, for example, placing all round blocks together or arranging items by color. They use familiar Korean objects like a *won* coin (round) and a *kimchi* container (square) as examples to make learning concrete and relatable to their daily life.
## Living Things and Their Needs
Children learn to recognize living things, like plants and animals, and identify their basic needs. They understand that living things need air, water, food, and a place to live. Students care for a class plant, such as a bean sprout, by giving it water and placing it near sunlight. They observe how classroom pets, like a fish, need food and clean water. They compare these needs to their own, understanding that just like a plant needs sun, they need food to grow strong.
## Changes in the Sky and Seasons
Grade 1 students observe daily and seasonal changes in the sky. They learn about day and night, recognizing that the sun appears during the day and the moon and stars appear at night. They observe and describe the basic characteristics of the four seasons: spring (warm, flowers bloom), summer (hot, sunny), autumn (cool, leaves fall), and winter (cold, snow may fall). They connect seasons to activities, like wearing a thick coat in winter or enjoying cherry blossoms in spring in Seoul.
## Care for Our Environment
Students are introduced to the importance of caring for their immediate environment, such as the schoolyard or park. They learn that pollution, like litter, can harm plants and animals. Through simple, concrete actions, they participate in keeping their space clean by putting trash into bins. They learn the value of saving resources, like turning off a light to save electricity or not wasting water. Teachers emphasize looking after shared spaces, just as families care for their homes, fostering a sense of responsibility from a young age.
## Fun with Magnets
Children explore the basic properties of magnets through hands-on experiments. They learn that magnets can attract certain materials like iron and steel, but not wood or plastic. They identify objects in the classroom that are magnetic (e.g., metal door handles) and non-magnetic (e.g., a plastic eraser). Using bar magnets, they discover that magnets have two poles, north and south, and that like poles push apart while opposite poles pull together. This simple, playful activity introduces them to the concept of a force they cannot see.
