# South Korean (NCCE) Grade 6 Science
## The Structure and Properties of Matter
Matter is composed of tiny particles called atoms. Atoms consist of a nucleus, containing protons and neutrons, surrounded by electrons. The number of protons defines the element, such as oxygen (8 protons). Matter exists in three states: solid (particles vibrate in fixed positions, like ice), liquid (particles are free to move but remain close, like water in the Han River), and gas (particles move rapidly and are far apart, like steam in a jjimjilbang). Changes in temperature can cause matter to change states, a physical alteration without changing its chemical identity.
## Force, Energy, and Their Transformations
Force is a push or a pull that changes an object's motion or shape, measured in Newtons (N). Newton's First Law states that an object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion, unless acted upon by an unbalanced force. For example, a bus speeding through Seoul requires a large force to stop. Energy is the ability to do work and cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed. A key transformation is seen in a power plant, where chemical energy from coal is converted into electrical energy that powers a Seoul residence.
## Living Things and Their Environments
An ecosystem is a community of living organisms interacting with their physical environment. Producers, such as ginseng plants, create their own food through photosynthesis. Consumers, like a tiger, obtain energy by eating other organisms. Decomposers, such as fungi, break down dead matter. The ecosystem of the Demilitarized Zone (DMZ) is a unique example, where human absence has allowed diverse species to thrive, creating a complex and protected food web demonstrating the delicate balance of nature.
## The Circulation System and Health
The human body's circulatory system transports essential substances like oxygen, nutrients, and hormones to cells and removes waste products like carbon dioxide. The heart acts as a muscular pump, pushing blood through two main circuits: the pulmonary (to the lungs) and systemic (to the rest of the body). This system carries oxygen-rich blood from the heart, through arteries to a toe, and returns deoxygenated blood via veins. A balanced diet incorporating kimchi, rich in vitamins, supports the production of red blood cells, which carry oxygen, and is crucial for maintaining circulatory health.
## Earth's Resources and Their Conservation
Earth's natural resources are materials or substances people use from the environment. They are categorized as renewable (like solar energy or forests) and non-renewable (like fossil fuels or minerals). In South Korea, a non-renewable resource is bituminous coal, historically vital for industry. Sustainable management is critical. For instance, the country invests heavily in technology to purify water and reprocess waste, minimizing environmental impact and ensuring that future generations have access to vital resources like clean water and clean air for cities like Busan.
## Light and the Solar System
Light travels in straight lines at a speed of 300,000 kilometers per second. When light hits a mirror, it reflects at an equal angle, the law of reflection, which allows us to see our image. The Earth is part of the solar system, which consists of the Sun and celestial bodies like the Moon, planets, and asteroids. The Earth rotates on its axis every 24 hours, causing day and night, and orbits the Sun every 365.25 days, creating the year. This orbit and the 23.5-degree tilt of the Earth's axis are responsible for the seasonal changes experienced in Korea.
